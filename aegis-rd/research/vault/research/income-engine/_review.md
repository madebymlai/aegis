# Editorial decision - convergent engine

## EDITORIAL DECISION

**Major Revision.**

Three referees say Major, one says Minor. I am not averaging. I am ruling Major because when the four
reports are stripped of their headline labels and read for what they actually ask of the plan, they
converge on a fact none of them states in these terms: every referee, including R3, agrees D1 through D8
are the right decisions and none needs to be reopened. What none of them can yet say is that the plan is
finished specifying those decisions. R1 wants three mechanisms disentangled where the plan currently
blends them under one payer-story label. R2 wants two rows of a five-row pre-registered table shown to
be buildable rather than assumed. R3 wants three already-correct decisions carried from a decision log
into the prose drafting will actually read. DA wants the plan's own sharpest negative finding faced
inside the argument rather than fenced into an appendix by a procedural rule. None of these is a request
to change what the plan decided. All of them are requests to finish building what the plan already
decided to build, and there are enough of them, several genuinely load-bearing, that handing this plan to
drafting today would produce a paper that overclaims in its opening pages and needs a second full pass
before it could be submitted anywhere. That is a Major Revision by any reasonable reading of the term,
even at plan stage.

**Most persuasive referee: R2.** R2's eight concerns are almost entirely a single move applied
consistently: hold the plan to bars the plan itself already set (the indeterminate criterion must
predate the number it excuses; the role-redundant row must be distinguishable from seat-earned in
practice, not only in table form; a re-test cadence must exist before a second look at ΔΘ̂ is taken; the
two downside-correlation figures the plan itself flagged as unreconciled must actually be reconciled).
That is the hardest kind of objection to wave off, because it never imports an external standard, it
only asks whether the document's own commitments are yet satisfied. R2 is also the most careful about
crediting `_argument.md` versus claiming originality, and the one report that directly answers the team
lead's framing question (does D7 leave the thesis empirically empty) with a clean, non-hedged "no, but
its central risk is unguarded and the summary language must say so." I would not have reached that
answer as cleanly without R2's concern 4. The single most consequential finding across all four reports,
however, is not R2's: it is the D5/D6.2 reconciliation that R3 and DA reached independently by different
routes, ruled on below.

**Why not Reject.** DA tests Reject directly and defeats it on four of six attacks, and the two that
survive (COI concentration, the rent-on-balance-sheet tension) are disclosure and framing problems, not
existential ones, by DA's own admission. Nobody else proposes it. Off the table.

**Why not Accept or Minor alone.** Accept fails because P0 items below are not stylistic; several
(the role-dead epistemic-route ambiguity, the unbuilt role-redundant benchmark, the segment-conditioning
precondition on the plan's only empirical exhibit, the cap's unit mismatch with what it exists to bound)
are places where the plan currently asserts machinery it has not yet shown works. Minor alone
undercounts because it can only be reached by scoping the review to R3's own lens (portfolio
construction), and the other three lenses (empirical asset pricing, inference architecture, adversarial
reading against the desk's own register) each surface real, separately-derived gaps R3's lens does not
reach. Aggregating across all four lenses, not any single one, is the editor's job.

## Panel summary, and how R3's dissent was adjudicated

R3 is not insufficiently critical. Read on its own terms, R3's report is as sharp as any of the other
three: its own major concern 1 (D5 and D6.2 unreconciled) is the same finding DA calls "the one attack I
could not fully defeat," reached independently, and its concern 3 (D8's segment-conditioning precondition
looks unmet in the one measurement the plan actually carries) sharpens `_argument.md`'s own prospective
warning into a finding about a thing that has now actually happened. Nobody should read R3's Minor
recommendation as a softer pass.

What explains R3's Minor verdict is a different, defensible axis: R3 is scoring **how much new work each
fix requires**, not **how much would break if the fix were skipped**. By that axis, three of R3's four
major concerns really are one-sentence-or-formatting-discipline fixes: a scoping clause the plan has
already written once (D6.4) and just needs to write again at the point of first assertion; a caveat the
plan already knows how to carry (D8 already carries the Tasche caveat and the smoothing caveat the same
way); a cap re-denominated in a unit the corpus's own §5.4 logic already supplies for the sibling tier.
R3's fourth concern, the D5/D6.2 reconciliation, is scored the same way ("this does not require naming an
implementation... it is asking for a sentence connecting two decisions the plan has already made") and
that scoring turns out to be too optimistic, which is exactly what the convergence ruling below
addresses: the sentence R3 asks for cannot yet be written, because the connecting mechanism does not
exist anywhere in the file. R3 correctly diagnosed the gap and slightly underestimated its size.

The other three reports are not committing the opposite error, holding a plan to a draft's completeness
bar. Check the remedies, not the recommendations. R1's remedies are "add one paragraph naming three
mechanisms," "reframe as payoff-shape-indexed," "state this explicitly as an open question." R2's are
"cite a commit hash," "specify the comparison or name it as owed," "state a re-test cadence." DA's
surviving attacks ask for disclosure passages and a confrontation inside the argument, not a rewrite.
Every one of these is a plan-appropriate remedy: decide, specify, scope, disclose. None asks for
polished prose. If the remedies had been about phrasing rather than specification, R3's Minor would be
the right aggregate call. They are not, so it is not.

**What the panel gets right, in aggregate, and should be told plainly.** All four reports independently
credit the same structural strengths: the D4 to D8 repair sequence as a real audit trail rather than
decoration (R1, R2, R3 all use nearly this phrase unprompted), the Tasche scope correction as precise and
checkable, the 37-item prohibition list as an unusually strong pre-commitment device, the discipline that
demoted Zeng's 92 percent fit and fenced Terstegge before either could become load-bearing, and the D7
move from indifference to admit-on-rationale-bound-by-cap as a genuine methodological improvement rather
than a hedge. This plan is being held to a high bar because the panel trusts it can clear one, not because
the panel doubts the underlying project.

## The convergence ruling

**Ruling: yes, these are one finding, and it does the work the team lead describes, but it must enter the
paper in a narrower form than the note that first found it, or the fix reintroduces exactly the error
`_synthesis.md` correctly excluded.**

### What the three routes actually found

[[the-premium-is-rent-on-a-balance-sheet]] surveyed durable, well-evidenced behaviours and found nearly
all of them compensate a party for supplying balance-sheet capacity that a rule bars someone else from
supplying, with one small exception. `_synthesis.md` attacked that note on its own initiative, kept its
real content (the two-class taxonomy: prohibition gives durability and non-substitutability together,
capital scarcity gives only durability and decays), and excluded one specific sentence by name: "an
unlevered account with no balance sheet to rent is trying to sell the one thing it does not have," ruled
out as "an access claim about this desk, not a claim about markets... screening research by
implementation." D5 inherited the surviving taxonomy but never inherited a reason the seat's own occupant
pool should skew toward the substitutable half of it, so D5 asserts "predominantly substitutable" without
deriving it. D6.2 then restored a durable, rule-compelled, non-substitutable cell that pays a real
occupant, sourced from Dick-Nielsen and Rossi's own finding that the dealer's compensation is "not
replicable by other investors in the economy," and called that cell "where a book should look for
occupants." Nothing in the plan connects D5's assertion to D6.2's restored cell. R3 found the gap from
inside the plan's own logic; DA found it from the excluded note; neither saw the other's route, and
neither one, alone, supplies the missing sentence.

### The missing mechanism, stated at the right altitude

Read D6.2's own source language again: the dealer is compensated for warehousing a deteriorating credit
through an extended hold, and the compensation is **not replicable by other investors in the economy**.
That is not a claim about what this desk's account can trade. It is a claim about what kind of party the
mechanism pays: a party performing a financing and inventory-warehousing function, which is a capacity
service, not a party merely bearing the price risk of holding a security. Run the same test on D6.1's
restored window-dressing cell and it holds there too: the compensation flows to "whoever is both
unconstrained and already present in the market" to supply repo financing at the reporting date, which
is the identical capacity-service shape. Both of the taxonomy's restored durable-and-pays-this-seat cells
are, on inspection, capacity-rent payments, not risk-bearing-allocation payments. That is not a
restatement of the excluded desk-access sentence. It is a claim about **what each mechanism pays for**,
generalizable to any occupant regardless of that occupant's own balance sheet, exactly the economics
side of the line the team lead drew.

This supplies a second, independent test the plan's admission criterion is currently missing. D6.1
already established the first test: does the behaviour genuinely sell convexity, or does it satisfy the
loss-placement clause vacuously (a repo spread with almost no loss to place)? That test alone does not
settle D6.2, because D6.2's cell passes it: the plan's own three-feature derivation (slow-clearing
inventory, exposure to further deterioration, compensation attributed to the riskier and longer hold)
shows the dealer genuinely carries crash-correlated inventory. What D6.2's cell fails is the second test:
is the compensated function risk-bearing, which a portfolio seat performs by definition, or
capacity-provision, which it does not, because a seat is an allocation of risk capital inside a paired
book, not a financing or warehousing desk. A seat can hold the same downgraded bond D6.2 describes and
bear the same price risk. It cannot supply the dealer's market-making relationships, inventory financing
lines, or distribution network, which is what the compensation is actually for on the source's own
account. That is why the return is "not replicable by other investors in the economy": not because other
investors lack permission, but because the compensated service is not one a risk-bearing allocation
performs at all.

### What this does to the paper

1. **It supplies D5's missing mechanism.** "The durable behaviours mostly do not pay this seat" is no
   longer asserted; it follows from a structural fact about the role's own definition. Rule-compelled
   durability arises from non-substitutability, and the corpus's evidenced instances of non-substitutable
   compensation are, so far, uniformly capacity-service compensation. A four-clause portfolio role,
   admitted on the four-clause contract in `_plan.md`'s own thesis paragraph, is a risk-bearing occupant
   by construction. The intersection of durable-and-risk-bearing is therefore structurally narrow, not
   because durable occupants are rare in the economy, but because the same mechanism that grants
   durability (a rule disqualifying the natural, capacity-holding arbitrageur) tends to route the
   resulting compensation to whichever party supplies that capacity, which is usually not a risk-bearing
   allocation. This is a narrower and more defensible claim than "durable occupancy is nearly impossible
   for anyone without a balance sheet," which is the survey-based overgeneralization `_synthesis.md`
   correctly kept out. It does not foreclose a future durable, risk-bearing, non-substitutable cell from
   turning up. It explains why none has yet.
2. **It reconciles D6.2 with D5.** "Predominantly substitutable" survives contact with D6.2's restored
   cell because that cell, though real and durable, is not a risk-bearing cell. Nothing about the
   taxonomy's classification changes. What changes is that the taxonomy needs the second test stated
   alongside the first.
3. **It vindicates D3 and D7 more strongly than before.** D3's claim that fair pricing is the *designed*
   equilibrium, not a disappointment, previously rested on four data points about where capital happens
   to sit. It can now rest on a structural reason: the seat's own definition excludes it from the
   capacity-rent cells regardless of who occupies it, so an occupant pool drawn from the substitutable,
   circumstance-compelled class is what this role should expect to find, by what the role is, not by
   where the evidence happened to point this year.
4. **It corrects D6.2's directive language, which is currently wrong.** "The durable-and-pays-this-seat
   cell is where a book should look for occupants" should not survive into the paper. The cell is real
   evidence for the taxonomy's direction term (rule-compelled classes need a direction, and this is
   evidence the direction can favour a warehousing party rather than a responder), but it is not a source
   of candidate occupants for this seat, because the compensated function there is not this seat's
   function. D6.2 should be rewritten to make exactly this distinction rather than pointing drafting
   toward an unoccupiable cell.

### The line that must be held, and where R3's own remedy needs sharpening

The team lead is right that the exclusion may have conflated two different claims, and having read
`_synthesis.md`'s actual exclusion text, the conflation risk is real but the original exclusion itself
was correctly scoped: it targeted one self-referential, survey-based sentence about this desk's own
access, not the general capacity-rent mechanism, which the taxonomy had already absorbed in weaker form.
The risk is downstream, at drafting time, where a careless pass could read "the rent thesis is excluded"
and throw out the capacity-rent distinction along with the desk-access sentence it was actually attached
to. The plan should say explicitly, at the point D5 and D6.2 are stated, that these are two different
claims: what a mechanism pays for is economics and belongs in the derivation; what a specific account can
reach is implementation and stays in `_buildability.md`.

R3's own proposed remedy for concern 1 needs one word-level correction before it is used. R3 suggests
stating that the durable-and-pays-this-seat cell "is not investor-reachable on the source's own account."
That phrasing sits one step from re-importing the excluded framing, since "reachable by an investor" reads
as an access claim about who happens to hold an account. The correct phrasing is functional, not
access-based: the cell is not **occupiable by a risk-bearing role**, because the compensation is for a
capacity-service function the role does not perform, regardless of which investor or account is asking.
Same conclusion, but only the functional version survives contact with the standing prohibition against
screening research by implementation.

### The candidates actually on the table

Four, drawn from the plan and the panel, not invented here.

- **C1. The two-class behavioural cut.** Rule-compelled versus circumstance-compelled, cut by
  substitutability rather than by mechanism label (D5).
- **C2. The finding built on the cut.** Durable, rule-compelled behaviours mostly do not pay this seat
  (D5, repaired by D6.1 and D6.2).
- **C3. The derivation.** Fair pricing is the seat's designed equilibrium, not a disappointment, because
  its occupants are drawn predominantly from the substitutable class (D3 via D5).
- **C4. R2's proposal.** The contribution is the falsification protocol built around ΔΘ̂ for a specific
  claim shape (does a role exist, evidenced by at least one admissible occupant), not ΔΘ̂ itself.

### What survived the panel, candidate by candidate

**C1 survived intact.** `_argument.md`'s own stress test, run specifically to attack D5, concluded "the
two-class reduction itself... is real content, not a relabelling." No referee disputes the classification.
DA's attack on novelty (attack 2) targets D6.2's direction-term refinement layered on top of the cut, not
the base cut itself, and DA is explicit that the refinement, not the classification, is "the standard
limits-to-arbitrage asymmetry between liquidity demanders and suppliers, applied to two already-published
mechanisms." That DA, whose brief is to find the strongest case against, did not extend the attack to C1
itself is evidence C1 is the most defensible candidate on novelty grounds, standing alone.

**C2 was defeated once and repaired twice, and closed the panel still short a mechanism.**
`_argument.md` found the original claim asserted rather than derived, with two counterexamples (window
dressing, the fallen-angel case). D6.1 repaired the first by restoring ①'s convexity clause. D6.2 repaired
the second by adding a direction term. Both repairs held under the integrity gates. But R3's own major
concern 1 found that even after both repairs, D5's restatement of C2 ("predominantly substitutable") and
D6.2's own directive language ("where a book should look for occupants") were never reconciled, and DA
reached the same tension from the excluded rent-on-balance-sheet note. That reconciliation gap is what the
convergence ruling above closes. Before that ruling, C2 was a repeatedly-patched empirical claim. After
it, C2 is a derived one.

**C3 previously sat beside C2, not on it.** Both drew on the same four data points (Kargar, window
dressing, FX carry decay, ILS/Tomunen decay) without an argument connecting them: C2 said the durable
class mostly doesn't pay this seat, C3 said fair pricing happens to be the equilibrium, and nothing in the
plan explained why the second follows from the first rather than being a separate favourable observation
about the same four data points.

**C4 is real if finished, and is not finished.** R2's own framing is conditional: "a real and useful
contribution if it is finished. It is also, precisely, the part still under construction." Three of the
docket's P0 items are exactly the unfinished joints R2 names (RM-7, the role-redundant benchmark; RM-8,
the role-dead epistemic route; RM-9's sibling RM-18, repeated-look discipline), and DA's attack 1 adds a
fourth from the opposite direction: the admission gate the protocol would need to apply has no computable
observable for three of the seat's four candidate venues. A protocol whose existence-refuting row is
unreachable in practice and whose gate cannot be computed for most candidates is not yet a protocol that
does the thing it would be credited for. C4 is not disqualified as a future contribution. It is
disqualified as **this plan's** contribution, today, because naming it would commit the paper to proving
something the plan cannot currently show works.

### Ruling on the collapse

**It holds.** Read C2 and C3 through the convergence ruling and they stop being two correlated
observations and become one claim with a mechanism. C2, properly derived, states that durable
(non-substitutable) compensation is capacity-service compensation, which a risk-bearing role cannot
supply regardless of who occupies it. C3 is no longer a second, separately-argued fact about the same four
data points; it is what C2 entails once C2 is derived rather than observed: if occupancy is structurally
confined to the substitutable class, an occupant compressing toward the marginal arbitrageur's cost of
capital is not a lucky or unlucky draw, it is the only kind of outcome the role's own definition permits.
One claim, one mechanism, two consequences (C2's admission behaviour, C3's pricing behaviour) rather than
two claims resting on the same coincidence.

This collapsed claim is also a different, and better-defended, object than the piece of it DA's attack 2
already tested. DA's attack targets D6.2's direction term: which side of an already-known
forced-buyer/forced-seller asymmetry gets paid, applied to two published mechanisms neither this desk
discovered. The collapsed claim is a level above that: not which side of a known asymmetry is paid, but
**why a risk-bearing portfolio role can never occupy the compensated side of that asymmetry, structurally,
across every rule-compelled venue examined**, and what that entails for how the role's fair-pricing
outcome should be read. That is not stated in Dick-Nielsen and Rossi, Bassi et al., or any other single
cited source; it is assembled from four things that exist independently in the corpus (the vault's own
rent-on-balance-sheet survey, the limits-to-arbitrage substitutability literature, Dick-Nielsen and
Rossi's specific "not replicable by other investors" finding, and this paper's own four-clause role
definition), and none of them states it. That assembly is the paper's own work, which is what a
contribution claim needs to survive DA's kind of scrutiny.

### The named claim

The paper's contribution is the following, offered as language the paper may carry close to verbatim:

> A portfolio role that earns its seat by bearing risk, rather than by supplying the balance-sheet
> capacity a rule bars someone else from supplying, is structurally confined to compensation that
> compresses toward the marginal arbitrageur's cost of capital. Durable, non-substitutable premia in this
> corpus are payments for a capacity-provision function a risk-bearing allocation does not perform,
> whoever occupies it; a risk-bearing seat's fair-pricing equilibrium is therefore not an empirical
> accident of where capital presently sits, but a consequence of what kind of party a portfolio role is.

**What this commits the paper to proving, and where.** Two obligations, not one.

1. That the two-test admission criterion (D6.1's convexity test, plus the capacity-service test the
   convergence ruling adds) correctly classifies every rule-compelled instance the paper actually
   discusses, not only the two it currently reconciles. This is checkable and should be checked, not
   assumed: see RM-28 below, which names one register entry (the crypto venue-wedge friction in
   [[the-premium-is-rent-on-a-balance-sheet]]) that reads as a possible risk-bearing, not
   capacity-providing, durable case, and has not yet been tested against the new criterion.
2. That a portfolio role, as this paper's own four-clause contract defines it, genuinely does not perform
   a capacity-provision function. This needs one paragraph of defence, not an assumption: a large book
   could in principle also run financing or securities-lending operations, so the claim should be stated
   at the level of what the four-clause contract admits a candidate *on* (risk borne and priced, clauses 1
   through 4), not at the level of what any given firm happens to be capable of, which returns to the
   economics-versus-implementation line the convergence ruling already draws.

**Section map consequence.** §2 should state three tests together, since they are one drafting move: the
three-mechanism payer-story distinction (RM-10), the rule-versus-circumstance operational test (RM-14),
and the capacity-service test (RM-2). §3 then applies the combined criterion to reach the derived version
of C2. §4 should state C3 as an explicit corollary of §3's derivation rather than as a claim argued
independently from the same four data points, which shortens §4 and removes the "sits beside" seam a
referee could otherwise still find.

that has not yet been checked against the rest of its own evidence base is not a reason to relax the
revision requirement; it is a reason the requirement now has a clear target.

## Contested and answerable

Sycophantic acceptance of every referee point is its own failure mode, so two items are flagged here as
places the authors may reasonably push back, with the evidence already in the file.

**R1's Gospodinov-Robotti concern, against Tomunen (RM-12).** Partially answerable, not fully. The
critique targets spurious cross-sectional fit: a model can explain differences in average returns across
a small, correlated set of test assets through misspecification rather than a true priced factor, which
is exactly what sank the He-Kelly-Manela reading and is exactly why Zeng's 92 percent fit is already
correctly demoted to corroborating. But the paper's actual load-bearing citation of Tomunen, stated
plainly in `_synthesis.md` §3.1 and §3.2, is not the 71 percent fit. It is that the estimated price of
constraint risk is positive in all sixteen independently re-estimated years, nine of them after Tomunen's
own documented structural break, and that the model zeroes the premium only in a limiting case the paper
does not claim has occurred. Sign persistence across sixteen independent annual re-estimations is a
different and more robust statistical object than a single cross-sectional R² snapshot, and it is harder
to produce by the specific spurious-fit mechanism Gospodinov-Robotti demonstrate, which was shown on one
test, one sample, one methodology. The plan's own discipline (demoting Zeng for exactly the reason R1
raises) shows the authors already apply the relevant distinction; they have just never written down that
they are applying it to Tomunen specifically. R1's concern is not fully closed, since nobody has shown the
critique's misspecification mechanism cannot also produce spuriously persistent sign across years in a
structural model, and the paper should say that much rather than claim immunity. But R1's own framing,
that this "is currently the single most load-bearing empirical anchor," slightly overstates the exposure:
the anchor is narrower and better-defended than the 71 percent figure R1's concern is built around. RM-12
reflects this: fix the sentence, not the argument.

**R3's framing of "not investor-reachable."** Answerable and already resolved above in the convergence
ruling. R3's diagnosis is correct; R3's proposed phrasing risks reintroducing the access-claim framing
`_synthesis.md` correctly excluded. Use the functional version (capacity-service versus risk-bearing),
not the access version (reachable versus not), when RM-2 is drafted.

**DA's attack that the contribution may not be new.** Not fully answerable yet, because it is downstream
of RM-1, which is not this panel's decision to make. Worth stating plainly for whoever does make it: if
the eventual contribution claim rests on D6.2's direction term alone, DA's challenge stands, since sorting
already-published forced-buyer and forced-seller findings by direction is not obviously more than a
label. The convergence ruling's capacity-service test is the part of that cell DA's pass did not have
access to test, since it postdates DA's own review, and it is a stronger candidate for surviving DA's
challenge than the direction term alone. That is offered as information for the decision, not as the
decision.
