# Convergent Income Engine - Stage 1 synthesis

Cross-source integration over the seven folded articles, the cited-not-folded three, and the
backbone notes. No new literature search was run: every source named here is already in the vault,
and every flag a source note carries is carried forward. Where this document departs from
[[research/income-engine/_brief|the brief]] it says so and gives the reason. Paper ① is treated
as correct and complete within its own scope, and is **cited, never folded**; the interfaces it
defines for this seat are set out in §2.

Section numbers written bare (§1, §3.1) refer to this synthesis. Sections of the paper's provisional
outline are written "paper §N". Sections of ① are written "① §N".

> [!warning] Two findings that cut against the brief
> 1. The brief proposes promoting [[carry-is-not-one-premium]]'s aside - that several rows may load on
>    the same intermediary constraint - from a footnote to a spine. **The evidence that killed the
>    single intermediary factor is the same evidence that forbids that unification.** Kargar's
>    resolution is *plurality*: at least two constrained intermediary types moving in opposite
>    directions. The paper must adopt a plural, venue-by-venue constraint and explicitly decline the
>    one-premium reading. This strengthens [[carry-is-not-one-premium]]'s own thesis and weakens the
>    brief's unifying instinct. It is a refinement of the brief, not of ①: ① §2.2 fixes the definition
>    and leaves identification to the seat papers.
> 2. The desk's rent-on-a-balance-sheet synthesis **does not survive in the form it is written**. Its
>    load-bearing clause is *prohibited from holding*, and its durability follows only when the
>    constrained party is both the natural arbitrageur and non-substitutable by arriving capital. That
>    holds for the repo reporting snapshot and fails for the corpus's two measured decay cases.
>    Details in §1, §3.1 and §5.

## 1. The thesis, sharpened

The convergent seat is a **portfolio role**, not a mechanism. Its job has three clauses and no
fourth: earn a positive expected return in ordinary markets, place its loss where the convex pole
does not also fail at the horizon the book is measured on, and be ranked on what it contributes to
the paired book rather than on how smooth it looks alone. The economic content of the role is already
fixed by ① §2.2 and this paper **adopts it rather than re-deriving it**: the short pole is paid for
bearing unhedgeable risk where the intermediary's capacity binds, and the size of that payment tracks
the constraint rather than a fixed preference, with inelastic insurance demand necessary and not
sufficient because a price-insensitive buyer facing abundant sellers pays little. Read forward into
construction, which is this paper's half of the division of labour, that supplies exactly the positive
content the brief says the thesis line is missing, and it converts "carry is a candidate, not the
definition" from a negative claim into a structural one: carry is one occupancy of a job that also
admits credit spread, received option premium, and catastrophe risk transfer, and each candidate is
admitted or excluded by whether its own venue's constraint still binds. The seat's negative skew is
the inventory it sells rather than a risk to minimise, and the reason the inventory is worth keeping
is portfolio-level and only portfolio-level: the crash-share literature says the crash is a *minority*
of the premium, so the inventory cannot be defended by its own income. It is defended by what its
absence does to the book. Finally, the seat does not require an above-fair price to do its job. Being
paid the market price for crash-risk bearing delivers both the ordinary-market return and the loss
placement, so where the premium has compressed to fair the seat is a **beta allocation and must be
labelled one**, which is admissible for the role and is not an alpha claim.

**The role spec is ①'s, and it is more specific than our brief.** ① §4.1 defines the Floor as two
sleeves of opposite sign on the convexity axis: a convergent income engine that sells convexity and
earns the calm-market carry, and a persistent-crisis responder that buys convexity and pays in
protracted drawdowns. The formulation the paper should carry verbatim in substance is that **one sleeve
funds the wait and the other pays for it once the drawdown lasts**, and that neither is asked to do the
other's job. The Floor is diversified on the axis that governs stress rather than on the axis that
governs calm, which is what defends it against the conditional-correlation trap: two sleeves chosen for
low average correlation can still be short the same convexity to the common shock, while two chosen for
opposite convexity are constructed to diverge precisely where average correlation misleads. ①'s
evidence for the pairing is Bhansali, Davis, Dorsten and Rennison (2015), PIMCO affiliation disclosed,
on carry-trend complementarity across roughly twenty markets over half a century with the
**diversification concentrated in the extremes rather than the middle**; Olszewski and Zhou (2013) in
currencies; and for the responder half Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen (2017), AQR disclosed, plus Asif,
Frömmel and Mende (2022) from outside the managed-futures industry on trend's crisis alpha coming from
cutting exposure quickly, under fifteen days for the composite indices they study. **These are cited as
①'s and must not be re-derived, and ①'s roster argument must not be folded into ours.** Our thesis
agrees with ①: ① never identifies the seat with carry as a mechanism, so "carry is a candidate for the
role, not its definition" is the position ① already holds, and our contribution is construction rather
than correction.

Three sharpenings are worth stating separately because the drafting stage will be tempted to drop them.

**"Selling insurance" must not smuggle in "above fair value."** The brief's thesis line risks that
reading. [[the-payer-did-not-leave-the-supply-arrived]] establishes that a near-zero risk-adjusted
alpha is fully consistent with a large loss concentrated in rare states, which is what fairly-priced
risk-bearing looks like. The role survives fair pricing; the alpha claim does not. Building the thesis
on the weaker premise is what makes the small-scale beta concession honest rather than embarrassing.

**"Where the intermediary's capacity binds" is plural.** See §3.1. ①'s definition is sound and this
paper adopts it; what fails is the single-factor apparatus that would have measured it, so the
construction paper must identify the binding constraint venue by venue and never assume one factor
common across venues.

**Durability requires the constrained party to be both the natural arbitrageur and non-substitutable.**
This is the synthesis's own contribution and it reconciles the corpus's two opposite observations
inside ①'s own third-decay-mode framing. [[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]] states the
first half: the behaviour survives because the party best placed to arbitrage it is the party the rule
constrains, so arriving capital is the wrong kind. The second half is what the corpus's decay cases
add: where the constraint is capital scarcity rather than a prohibition, **arriving capital is a
perfectly good substitute for the constrained specialist**, and the premium decays. A new institutional
allocator substitutes for a specialist ILS fund, which is Tomunen's measured result; a covered-call ETF
substitutes for a dealer's short-volatility inventory, which is the supply mechanism in
[[the-payer-did-not-leave-the-supply-arrived]]; unlevered capital partly substitutes for a
funding-constrained FX carry investor, which is why that premium tracks differential width. A dealer
forbidden the balance sheet on the reporting date has no substitute, because the rule disqualifies the
natural holder rather than starving it. One statement, four data points, both signs explained, and it
gives the seat's admission gate its actual content: ask not only whether a constraint binds but whether
arriving capital can relax it.

**And the substitutable case decays toward a floor, not to zero**, which is the limits-to-arbitrage
prediction §3.1 flags as load-bearing: a constraint premium compresses toward the marginal arbitrageur's
cost of capital. The corpus's one measurable instance now supports both halves of that sentence. Tomunen's
year-by-year price of cat risk is positive in **all sixteen years** of his sample including nine after his
own 2010 break, and his model zeroes it only for a fully unconstrained specialist sector, which he does
not show has occurred (§3.2). So substitutability predicts what is observed: compression, desensitisation
to shocks as capacity refills faster, and persistence at a lower level. This matters for the seat because
it is the difference between "the premium may return" and "the premium is now thinner and the crowded side
is ours", and only the second is defensible.

### The ① interface: what ① leaves to us

Two interfaces, both explicit in ①'s own text, and one of them is a commission the section map has to
discharge as a first-class obligation rather than a closing remark.

**The §4.4 commission, verbatim.** "Showing that the ordered roster does real work beyond an unordered,
equal-risk-contribution blend of the same sleeves, and that each role holds on its own terms, is the
task of the seat papers rather than a claim settled here." ① adds the two constraints under which the
realisation must happen: "what remains is to realize them by sizing and holding the sleeves **without
budgeting skew and without timing returns**." Both bind our construction section. Skew is a mandate the
seat must satisfy, not an objective to push, which is also `what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine`'s
own first deliberate non-choice; and the return-timing line is ① §5.5, which §3.1 below discharges for
the constraint gate.

**That commission has two halves and our seat can discharge only one and a half of them.** This needs
saying plainly because it is tempting to read $\Delta\hat\Theta$ as discharging both.

- **"Each role holds on its own terms" is squarely ours and $\Delta\hat\Theta$ is the instrument.** The
  role has the three clauses in §1, so the role holds when all three hold simultaneously for at least
  one candidate: positive held-out ordinary-market return, loss placed away from the responder's failure
  at the declared horizon, and a positive contribution to the paired book. The falsifier is specific and
  the corpus has already produced an instance of what a failed test looks like: the 27-cell composite
  re-rank in which every cell had $\Delta\hat\Theta < 0$ and downside correlation to trend in
  [+0.38, +0.49]. Critically, that instance **did not falsify the role** - the diary's own pivot reads it
  as "no floor partner on this universe and window", not "no such role" - so the pre-registration must
  state in advance which observation supports which conclusion, or the test cannot distinguish an empty
  candidate set from a dead role.
- **"The ordered roster beats an unordered equal-risk-contribution blend" is a book-level test the seat
  paper cannot run alone.** The comparator ① names is the *same sleeves* (convergent, responder, tail,
  gated breadth) blended by equal risk contribution with no ordering. $\Delta\hat\Theta$ as defined is
  $\hat\Theta$ of a two-leg blend minus $\hat\Theta$ of trend alone: the Target and Expansion appear in
  neither term, so the metric cannot see the ordering claim at the roster level. That test needs all
  four streams, which is more than this seat owns.
- **The restricted half the seat genuinely can discharge.** At the Floor level the ordering claim reduces
  to a claim about *how the convergent weight is determined*: the role sets it (fund the wait) whereas an
  unordered blend would set it by equal risk contribution. $\Delta\hat\Theta$ evaluated at the mandated
  weight and again at the equal-risk-contribution-implied weight is exactly that comparison and needs only
  two streams. The paper may specify it and pre-register the sign. It may **not** claim to settle the
  weight question, which `what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine` explicitly assigns to the
  floor: the metric measures marginal contribution at a *fixed* weight, not the optimal weight.

So paper §10 should state the commission, discharge the "role holds" half and the restricted Floor-level
ordering half by specification and pre-registration, and hand the full four-sleeve roster comparison
back as a cross-paper obligation with the comparator named. That is an honest discharge of an explicit
assignment, and it is more useful to ①'s revision round than a claim the seat cannot support.

### Per-article detail

**[[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]] splits in two.** It carries two separable jobs:
the role definition (positive drift in ordinary markets, loss placed away from trend's failure) and
the evaluation contract ($\hat\Theta$ standalone, $\Delta\hat\Theta$ composite, the robust skew panel,
the paired time-series inference). Those belong at opposite ends of the paper, because the ranking rule
is only intelligible once paper §6 has established what the ranker is defending against. *Contributes:* the
role, the Sharpe-is-the-adversary's-objective argument (Goetzmann et al., Spurgin, Leland, and the
Eling-Schuhmacher counter with its elliptical-universe boundary), the manipulation-proof measure and
its Doubt-Ratio trust check, the two deliberate non-choices (do not rank on skew depth, do not rank on
coskewness), and the pre-registration machinery. *Cut or demote:* the credit-specific construction
material (richness versus level, the defensive-versus-carry quality tilt, the corporate-bond factor
zoo) is a **credit sleeve construction argument, not a role argument** and should be compressed to a
single worked example or cited out. The Dickerson-Robotti-Rossetti zoo result must keep its
not-peer-reviewed flag if it is used at all.

**[[the-skew-is-the-product]]** is the paper's strongest single fold and needs no demotion. *Contributes:*
the one-way dial (every standalone lever spends skew), the Firoozye-Koshiyama theorem that positive
skew is an essential feature of every positive-Sharpe linear-filter dynamic strategy, the DTS
mechanism giving credit's crash-beta an address in the BB-B belt, the rebalancing band as a
payoff-shape lever rather than a fee lever, and the Bae-Elkamhi correlation-innovation factor that
prices carry and momentum jointly. *Cut:* the DTS material is credit-specific; keep the *general* form
(income and crash-beta get their own axes, pin the crash axis) and let DTS be the worked instance.
Keep the bridge to the cited-not-folded convexity paper explicit rather than re-arguing it.

**[[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]** supplies two halves that go to different sections. *Contributes
to §2:* the funding-unwind account and its three independent corroborations (Menkhoff et al. on global
FX volatility, Dobrynskaya on the downside-beta premium matching its equity-market value, Zeng on
interest-rate volatility tightening dealer constraints), Ilmanen's cross-asset selling-insurance-is-paid
result, and the wrapper hierarchy. *Contributes to §4:* the single load-bearing sourcing fact, that
the crash skew is a **currency** phenomenon, triply sourced, with equity carry positively skewed and
bond and commodity signs construction-dependent across two decompositions. *Demote:* the "premium may
have compressed" section must be rewritten, not deleted. As written it reads the compression as the
payer leaving; the correction note says the break date moved between the authors' own drafts, every
structural explanation offered predicts the wrong sign, and the mechanism with the right sign is new
sellers arriving. *Also demote:* Zeng's 92% cross-sectional fit is exposed to exactly the inference
critique in §3.1 and must be cited as corroborating, never load-bearing.

**[[carry-is-not-one-premium]]** is promoted in position and demoted in ambition. *Contributes:* the
invariant definition (carry is the return under unchanged market state, so expected return equals
carry plus expected price appreciation, and uncovered interest parity is precisely the claim that the
second term cancels the first), the six honesty distinctions, the return-decomposition identity, and
the payer-and-failure-state table. Critically, it supplies the argument that **stacking two carry
mechanisms concentrates one pole rather than adding an independent one**, which the paper needs in
paper §7 to stop ILS being read as a second premium. *Demote:* the table's own caveat that "several rows can
load on the same intermediary constraint" must be presented as a hypothesis the evidence in §3.1
currently disfavours, not promoted to a spine.

**[[commodity-carry-constructions]] becomes a worked instance inside §3, not a section.** It is a
construction-methodology article: roll yield is not a roll-day cash flow, the curve holds a spot
premium and a term premium with opposite signs (Szymanowska et al.: roughly 5 to 14 percent spot
against 1 to 3 percent term), and the eight-row construction-versus-contaminant table. Read as a
section it overweights roll mechanics that a portfolio-role paper does not need. Read as an instance
it does one thing better than anything else in the corpus: it **explains why the commodity skew sign
disagrees across studies** (directional time-series portfolios, market-neutral basis ranks and
calendar spreads do not own the same spot beta, maturity, or liquidation exposure), which is the
strongest available support for the paper's central negative claim that the concave shape is a
property of the construction rather than of the asset class. That is why it earns its fold, and it is
also why it should be one subsection.

**[[insurance-linked-securities-as-the-orthogonal-income-pole]] is retitled and cut hard.** See §3.2.
*Contributes:* crash-factor substitution (credit's crash factor is the recession by construction,
because what credit is paid to bear is corporate default; a cat bond's is meteorological), Froot's
who-pays result (premium-to-expected-loss multiples of fair value, with only supply-side explanations
surviving out of eight tested), Froot and O'Connell on the hard/soft cycle as a supply shift rather
than a revision to actuarial risk, and Patton's result that orthogonality rather than anti-correlation
is the right target for a short-sales-constrained book. *Cut:* the orthogonality headline, the "only
truly uncorrelated asset class" manager framing, the CATB proposal (§3.4), and the cycle-aware sizing
hypothesis (§3.1, it is on the wrong side of the return-timing line and is skew-liquidating besides).
*Reframe:* Froot plus Tomunen make ILS the **purest instance of the constrained-capacity thesis in the
corpus** and simultaneously its only measurable compression case. Both readings belong in the paper, and
the compression reading is *compression*, not extinction (§3.2). *Add, from
[[research/income-engine/_buildability|_buildability]]:* the crowding dynamic, since the UCITS inflows
that grant access are the documented cause of the compression, which is a sizing and durability point
rather than a wall.

**[[short-horizon-reversal-in-small-cross-sections]] is repurposed as the falsifying case.** The brief's
mapping ("execution overlay first, standalone sleeve second") is an implementation verdict and this
paper is not an implementation paper. The stronger use: reversal has the right *shape* (measured
concave on our own universe: negative quarterly skew, non-positive $\beta_2$, negative
crisis-conditional return, non-negative bear-regime beta), the right *payer story* (Nagel: reversal
returns are compensation for supplying liquidity, time-varying and VIX-predictable, so the seller of
immediacy earns most when immediacy is dearest), and **still fails the seat**. It fails because the
payment is statistical rather than contractual: per
[[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]], the liquidity provider's income is the bid-ask spread and the
reversion only lets them exit flat, so a price taker takes the provider's risk and pays the provider's
income to someone else. That is a sign inversion, not a cost problem, and no turnover control repairs
it. Reversal is therefore the case that proves shape is not the role, which is the paper's thesis
stated from the negative side. *Cut:* the Robeco conditioned-reversal cluster can be compressed to one
sentence with its single-manager-COI flag; the cross-asset overnight-to-intraday working paper is
unrefereed after a decade and should not be leaned on.

## 3. Contradictions resolved

### 3.1 The intermediary spine: plural and local, never a single factor

**Verdict.** The paper may not rest on a single intermediary capital factor, and must not inherit one
silently. What it may rest on is a weaker and better-evidenced claim: **constraints bind locally and
plurally, and the premium's sign and size in each venue track the constrained capacity of the
specialists who bear that venue's risk.** Identification is venue by venue.

The attack is decisive against the factor and not against the mechanism. Gospodinov and Robotti (*JFE*
2021) re-run He-Kelly-Manela's own sample with misspecification-robust inference and the capital
factor's significance disappears in nearly every asset class, with a placebo flagging an unrelated
industry factor as priced in **39 of 40 cases**. Adrian-Etula-Muir (*JF* 2014) and He-Kelly-Manela use
reciprocal measures and obtain opposite-signed risk prices. Nobody has re-estimated the seven-asset
cross-section past 2012-13.

Kargar (*JFE* 2021) is the resolution, and it is positive content rather than damage control:
broker-dealers cut leverage 47 percent in 2008-09 while bank holding companies raised it 72 percent,
so there are **at least two distinct constrained intermediary types moving in opposite directions**.
That is not a defective single factor; it is a correct plural description. Two consequences the paper
must carry:

1. **The unification the brief proposes is forbidden by this evidence.** "The folded articles may be
   one premium observed in several venues, discriminated by whether the constraint still binds" is
   exactly the single-factor reading in prose form. Plurality says the opposite: several constrained
   types, several venues, several failure states. That is [[carry-is-not-one-premium]]'s thesis, and the
   contradiction evidence *supports* it. The paper should say so and take the win, rather than trying
   to keep both.
2. **Plurality is also the paper's diversification argument.** If there were one constraint, a
   multi-mechanism convergent book would be one position and [[carry-is-not-one-premium]]'s warning
   would be a theorem. Because there are at least two, whether stacking mechanisms diversifies is an
   open empirical question - which is precisely what $\Delta\hat\Theta$ exists to answer and precisely
   what the paper may not answer.

**What survives as load-bearing** (in descending order of independence from the attacked test class):

- **Gârleanu, Pedersen and Poteshman (2009).** Option prices move with end-user net demand because
  market makers cannot hedge perfectly and must be paid to absorb the imbalance; end users are net
  buyers of index options and net suppliers of single-stock options, and the premium's sign follows.
  This is a demand-pressure model tested on option prices, not a cross-sectional test of a capital
  factor, so the Gospodinov-Robotti critique does not reach it. It is the single most useful source in
  the corpus for the paper's spine because it fixes a **sign by venue**, which is what
  `research/README` requires ex ante.
- **Santa-Clara and Saretto (2009).** Margin requirements cap how much a seller can write and margin
  calls force liquidation exactly at the loss; the 10 percent out-of-the-money put-write Sharpe falls
  from 0.304 to 0.095 once margins bind. The constraint measured inside the trade rather than inferred
  from a factor.
- **Tomunen (2026, *RFS*).** Published, verified in
  [[research/budgeting-convexity/_challenge-verification|budgeting-convexity/_challenge-verification]]:
  the premium is proportional to the intermediary constraint parameter and vanishes when it is zero,
  with 71 percent of test-asset expected-return variation explained by a theoretically motivated
  measure of those intermediaries' marginal rate of substitution.
- **Bassi, Behn, Grill and Waibel (*JFI* 2024)**, via
  [[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]]. The constraint observed directly in confidential ECB
  repo transaction data with a cross-jurisdictional natural experiment (period-average reporting in
  the US and UK against point-in-time in the euro area, Switzerland and Japan) rather than estimated
  from returns. This is the vault's best exhibit that intermediary constraints price anything at all,
  and it does not depend on the factor test surviving. **Carry the flag:** the sample period is
  unconfirmed and the note says to close that before citing the 12.5 and 25 percent figures in a
  paper.
- **The limits-to-arbitrage prediction that constraint premia compress to the marginal arbitrageur's
  cost of capital, not to zero.** Load-bearing for this paper: it is the theoretical reason a
  compressed premium is still a premium, which is the same conclusion the variance-premium correction
  reaches from the data side.

**What is demoted to corroborating.** Zeng (*JFQA* 2025) explaining 92 percent of the carry and
momentum cross-section with global interest-rate volatility tightening FX dealer constraints is a
high-$R^2$ cross-sectional fit on a small test-asset set, which is exactly the object the
Gospodinov-Robotti placebo attacked. It is corroborating and it may not be presented as independent
confirmation of the intermediary spine. This is a downgrade against how
[[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]] currently uses it.

**The §5.5 obligation, discharged.** The brief warns that a positioning or flow variable used to
decide whether a premium is available sits close to the return-forecasting line that
[[research/budgeting-convexity/budgeting-convexity|①]] draws in §5.5, and that ① had to remove exactly
that contradiction once (the Uysal and Mulvey concession, which drives an overlay from a supervised
estimate of the probability of a contraction and therefore falls on the far side). The argument for
which side of the line a constraint gate is on is **function and frequency, not the variable**:

- An **admission gate** answers "does this seat have an occupant at all", is evaluated at the cadence
  of a research decision, and carries a pre-registered kill condition. §5.5 does not reach it, because
  it is not a weight. The template already exists in the vault:
  [[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]] pre-registers "treat a consultation on period
  averaging as the kill signal rather than waiting for realized decay". That is an existence test with
  a declared falsifier, which is what `research/README` demands and what §5.5 permits.
- A **positioning scalar** answers "how much today" and is on the far side of the line whenever the
  variable proxies an expected return.

Applying that test finds one thing in the folded corpus already over the line and it should be struck:
the ILS article's cycle-aware sizing hypothesis, which scales the sleeve by the market's
spread-over-expected-loss multiple, up when multiples are rich and down when compressed. That is
valuation-based factor timing in Asness's sense, and it is doubly disqualified because de-risking a
concave sleeve is a skew-raising operation, so it liquidates the inventory the seat is hired to hold.
It may appear in the paper only as a rejected candidate with both reasons given.

### 3.2 ILS orthogonality: demoted to conditional, but the section is not a negative result

**Verdict, revised after the buildability agent's primary-source check
([[research/income-engine/_buildability|_buildability]], provenance chain independently checked
against [[research/budgeting-convexity/_challenge-verification|_challenge-verification]]).** Two claims
travel under one label and only one falls. **Trigger-level orthogonality survives unconditionally** - a
hurricane is not caused by a recession, and none of the challenge evidence touches what causes the
covered peril. **Unconditional return orthogonality falls**, through three separately verified channels
that all activate in the same state. And the **premium is not gone**, so the ILS section must not be
written as a negative result.

**The premium first, because the brief demands that order.** Tomunen's price of cat risk is estimated
year by year over 2003-2018 and is **positive in all sixteen years**, nine of them after the 2010 break
he himself documents, ranging 1.02 to 7.62 percent with a Fama-MacBeth average of 2.06 percent, and
robustness cuts (dropping callables, earthquake-only, parametric-trigger-only) leave it around the 2
percent level. His model zeroes the premium only in the limiting case of a fully unconstrained
specialist-fund sector, which the paper does not show has occurred. So the honest reading is
**compression tied to a named and still-operating mechanism, not extinction**, and it is exactly the
functional form §3.1 already flags as load-bearing: constrained-capacity premia compress toward the
marginal arbitrageur's cost of capital rather than to zero. *Two flags travel with this.* The
year-by-year figures come from the November 2019 job-market-paper draft rather than the published *RFS*
39(3) text, so the direction is well supported and the exact figures are pre-publication; and no formal
test of whether the post-2010 or post-2017 subsample premium differs from zero was located, so the
point-estimate series is suggestive rather than a direct test of the asymptote.

This also does not revive CATB. Mechanism and vehicle are separate questions, which is the distinction
[[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]] itself draws, and the vehicle-half kill stands untouched (§3.4).

**The three channels that fall, and the state they share.**

1. **Pricing.** Gürtler, Hibbeln and Winkelvos: cat-bond secondary spreads co-move significantly
   positively with the reinsurance cycle, and their dependency on corporate credit spreads
   **strengthens significantly after the Lehman bankruptcy**. A verified direct finding, not an
   inference.
2. **Return.** Carayannopoulos and Perez: cat bonds are zero-beta assets only in non-crisis periods,
   and with Lehman's collapse returns became significantly market-correlated, **averaging 0.29 from
   September 2008 through the end of 2009, reverting to insignificance by early 2011**. The mechanism is
   forced liquidation by the asset's own holders, not a change in the insurance trigger. **Both halves
   must travel together:** the same paper finds the correlation change was far smaller than for any other
   asset and normalised post-2009, and that cat bonds remained a net-valuable diversifier over the full
   period. This is a bounded and temporary breach, not credit carry's structural recession beta.
3. **Counterparty structure.** The four 2008-vintage deals using Lehman Brothers Special Financing as
   total-return-swap counterparty (Ajax Re, Newton Re, Carillon, Willow Re) were downgraded when the
   swaps terminated. **Keep the precision: these were rating and collateral impairments, not confirmed
   principal losses**, and no contemporaneous record establishes realised principal loss on the four.
   The failure mode was the wrapper's financing plumbing breaking alongside everything else's, not the
   peril turning out to be financial.

Three different mechanisms, none of them touching the trigger, all activating in a **systemic
funding-liquidity crisis**. That state assignment is the finding with the largest downstream
consequence and it is worked out in §3.5.

**A fourth line the corpus supplies and neither article states: the orthogonality number is measured on
an instrument that will read spuriously diversifying.** The ILS funds are weekly or fortnightly liquid,
their NAVs move on model-based catastrophe marks, and those marks overstated final principal losses
roughly two-fold in the largest event on record (the Swiss Re index fell about 10 percent on Hurricane
Ian against realised principal losses of 1 to 2 billion dollars).
[[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]] supplies the econometrics: Getmansky, Lo and Makarov
show a smoothed stream's **contemporaneous correlations are biased toward zero**, with the true
co-movement displaced into lagged terms, worst in fixed-income categories. So the "correlation near zero"
that the orthogonality claim rests on is exactly the statistic that stale, model-marked NAVs manufacture.
The ILS article reads the slow cadence as helpful because it enforces the right measurement horizon; the
role article says the slow cadence is what biases the measurement. **The paper must resolve this in the
role article's favour**: the correlation must be measured on multi-month compounded windows with the
lagged terms re-absorbed, and until it is, the orthogonality figure is not evidence. Note the direction
of this bias relative to channel 2: a de-smoothed series would show the crisis co-movement **larger**
than 0.29, not smaller, so the bounded-and-temporary reading is the optimistic end of the range.

**What the paper may claim.** Use the vocabulary the article's own sources already supply and stop
saying "orthogonal": cat bonds are an effective **diversifier against all asset classes, a poor hedge,
and a safe haven against extreme equity declines only in the post-crisis period**. The trigger stays
meteorological in every state, which is a genuine and useful substitution because it is not the
recession credit is structurally paid to bear; returns stay uncorrelated in normal and most crisis
states; and a bounded, temporary, liquidity-driven co-movement channel opens in a Lehman-scale funding
seizure and closes within roughly two years. The premium is compressing on a named and still-operating
mechanism rather than dying. The right framing for paper §7 is therefore a **conditional,
better-than-credit-carry, still-imperfect concave companion**, not an architectural decoupling. The
model-risk caveats stand as the ILS article states them: the global-warming undervaluation result is a
single peer-reviewed study, the secondary-peril warnings are practitioner testimony against the
speakers' own book, and the structure ordering (per-occurrence, peak-peril, index-trigger over aggregate
and indemnity) is a data claim from a manager source.

**And one dynamic that belongs in the argument rather than in a footnote.** The same UCITS inflows that
give retail access to the mechanism are the documented cause of the premium's compression: the sector
grew about 5.3 billion dollars, roughly 39 percent, in 2025 to about 19.12 billion, and Tomunen names
institutional inflow into the specialist funds as the specific cause of the post-2010 decline. **The
vehicle that solves access is the channel eroding what is accessed.** That is not a wall, since no
prohibition is involved and the premium has stayed positive through the inflow, but it bounds how
durable the current level may be assumed to be, and it is the sharpest instance in the corpus of §1's
substitutability test: here the buyer *is* the substituting capital. It belongs in the paper's sizing
discussion and in any claim about durability.

### 3.3 Shadow gamma: resolved, with two verdicts that must stay apart

**Verdict on the source.** Found. An author surnamed **Terstegge**, "Intermediary Option Pricing",
working paper on CBOE dealer position data 2011-2023, presented at FMA Derivatives 2025 and circulated
via AFA program materials. **Full given name and publication status are not independently confirmed**
per [[research/budgeting-convexity/_challenge-verification|_challenge-verification]], so it is a
**corroborating source and may never be load-bearing**. Anything the paper needs to be true must be
true without it. The brief's task is discharged: it exists, it is located, and it is fenced.

**Verdict on the substance.** It does **not** rebut Dew-Becker and Giglio, and the paper must not
present it as a rebuttal. His own *local* dealer gamma is flat too, which confirms their measurement.
His contribution is that **scenario gamma evaluated at a large downward move stays strongly negative,
concentrated in deep out-of-the-money puts**, across the same window in which the alpha test reads
zero. The reconciliation is arithmetic and it is the most important single line in the backbone:
**a near-zero average alpha is entirely consistent with a large loss concentrated in rare tail
states.** Zero alpha does not mean no premium; it means the seller is paid about fairly for bearing
crash risk. By [[what-is-a-strategy]] that is compensation for risk rather than someone else's
systematic behaviour, so it is an allocation and not an edge, and the paper must label it that way.

**The two verdicts the vault has been merging.** `graveyard.md` line 70 kills the shadow-gamma
*payoff* as a trade on our own out-of-sample data: the sign goes the wrong way at daily cadence, with
the top-gamma tercile's lag-1 slope mildly positive while the strong reversion sits in the stressed
bottom tercile, weekly null, European prints null, sub-window signs flipping, and an implied edge of
0.3 to 3 basis points per event against a 15 to 75 basis point cost wall. The second moment survives
strongly and GEX is kept as break-condition instrumentation. So: **real as a risk-model input, dead as
a trade.** For this paper the consequence is exact and it is the same line as §3.1: dealer
crash-scenario positioning may be used to argue that the *exposure* still exists, and may not be used
as a *signal*. Existence claims and harvestability claims are different claims.

**Carry the cautions forward.** The break date moved between the authors' own drafts (near 2010 in the
November 2023 NBER version, August 2012 in the current draft, with Bates 2022 placing it at 2017 on
separate methodology), so what survives is the direction and not a date. The supply mechanism has the
right sign and four sources behind it (BIS Quarterly Review March 2024; Calvet, Célérier, Liao and
Vallée on roughly 1.3 trillion dollars of structured products; Park and Kurucak on N-PORT data; the
Chicago Fed variance-premium paper), but the desk's own throwaway cross-market prototype cuts against
it: Australia flattened hardest and cleanest in the panel and is the only market distinguishable from
the US, in the direction of *more* compression, while Australia's option-income vehicle market is far
smaller than America's, which is the opposite ordering a supply story predicts. The panel is four
markets, the measure is the raw gap rather than alpha, and the US answer is itself unstable across US
benchmarks (S&P 500 p=0.088, Nasdaq p=0.0025, Dow p=0.82 on one identical specification). **The paper
may state the supply mechanism as the only candidate with the right sign that was found. It may not
state it as established.**

### 3.4 CATB is dead on both economics and vehicle

**Verdict.** Struck. The ILS section may not propose it, and two live vault documents that still do
are stale: the ILS article's "one to watch" line and [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]]'s "the ETF
stays a watch item".

Economics: Tomunen. Vehicle: 128 basis point TER, traded at roughly a 12 percent discount to NAV in
June 2026, holds about 12 to 14 million dollars, is USD-based despite the EUR line, and faces a
pending ESMA proposal to cap or exclude cat bonds from UCITS. Read through the two-wall taxonomy in
[[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]], the AUM and the discount are **size** walls that would dissolve
with the vehicle's own growth, while the ESMA eligibility question is a **structural** wall that does
not dissolve at any size. It is the structural wall that closes it. The paper may state that a roughly
19.12 billion dollar UCITS cat-bond *fund* universe exists at end-2025, about 31 percent of outstanding
cat-bond risk capital; it may not name a listed vehicle as the route.

**Wrapper dilution is now computed, and it is not a wall for the fund route.** The synthesis previously
asked for this as an owed computation; the buildability agent has done it. ESMA's own data-gathering
exercise finds UCITS cat-bond funds allocate **88 percent of NAV to direct cat-bond exposure** in
aggregate, with consultation respondents reporting dedicated funds holding only cat bonds and cash at
typically 85 to 97 percent and up to 100 percent of NAV, corroborated by two of the largest funds' own
material. So a position buys roughly its full notional in the intended mechanism, against TFIF's EUR 181
out of EUR 2,450. Wrapper *inversion* is likewise not a wall here, because the inversion law concerns
rules-based passive wrappers and these funds are actively managed.

**One correction to this synthesis's earlier scoping, and it runs against the vehicle route more broadly
than the ETF.** I attributed the ESMA eligibility question to CATB specifically. It is broader: ESMA's 26
June 2025 technical advice on the UCITS Eligible Assets Directive review names catastrophe bonds
explicitly among alternative exposures it conceptually believes belong under AIFMD rather than UCITS, and
the funds that would be caught are exactly the concentrated, direct-holding structures that make dilution
a non-issue. So the structural wall applies to the **fund** universe too, not only to the listed vehicle,
and it is pending and contested rather than erected, with ESMA itself recommending long transitional
provisions. ESMA's own data also notes only 72 UCITS in the EU invest in cat bonds and that these are
typically aimed at professional rather than retail investors even today. The mechanism-versus-vehicle
distinction still holds and CATB is still dead on its own vehicle facts; what changes is that the fund
route cannot be presented as regulatorily settled.

### 3.5 Failure states and who owns them: a ranking discipline, not an architecture gap

**Verdict, and a correction to how this synthesis first framed it.** The fast-spike joint failure is
**not a gap in the Floor and not something this paper reports against ①.** ① §4.2 opens by stating that
the Floor's responder has a structural weakness, that trend is late by construction so a crash
completing in days is over before the responder reverses, and that the Target tier exists as the
immediate defence covering the fast segment. The caution box in
[[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]] and ① §4.2 are the same observation, and ① discharged it by
adding a tier and sizing that tier by the speed gap it closes. There is nothing here for the seat paper
to report.

What remains ours is narrower and it is a **ranking discipline**, taken from the caution box's operative
sentence: *any candidate admitted on loss-placement grounds owes a check on this specific state, not
just on the trend tercile.* That is a constraint on how this seat admits candidates. Three things follow,
and they belong in paper §9 and paper §10 rather than in an architecture claim.

**(a) The attribution boundary.** A candidate must not be credited for diversifying the responder on
the strength of a state the Target is in fact covering. The boundary is set by ①'s own division of
coverage: the responder is paid to cover the **protracted** drawdown, and AQR (2020a) as cited in ① §4.2
supplies the asymmetry that makes this the load-bearing segment, because slow drawdowns inflict more
cumulative damage than fast ones and that is why trend is the workhorse and the tail the supplement. So
the seat's loss-placement claim is a claim **against the responder over the protracted segment**, and
diversification measured in the fast segment is not the seat's to claim, because the pairing was never
asked to supply it there. Operationally: report the placement statistics conditioned on the responder's
protracted-drawdown windows, and report the fast-window behaviour **separately and as a cost**, not
netted into the same number.

**(a-bis) The ILS failure state lands inside the boundary, and that is the sharpest test of the
discipline the corpus offers.** §3.2's three channels all open in a **systemic funding-liquidity
crisis**, and Carayannopoulos and Perez date the episode from September 2008 to the end of 2009 with
reversion by early 2011. That is not the fast vertical spike ① §4.2 assigns to the Target. It is a
protracted crisis, squarely inside the segment where ①'s Floor division of labour is the operative
mechanism. **The Target does not cover it**, and cannot be expected to: ① sizes the Target as the smaller,
faster supplement precisely because the protracted drawdown is the responder's job. So an income pole
that becomes correlated in that state is not failing at another tier's job.

The tempting conclusion is that it is therefore failing at the Floor's job. That conclusion is too quick,
and untangling it produces the ranking rule this section exists to state. **The Floor does not ask the
convergent pole to be uncorrelated in the protracted drawdown.** ① §4.1's formulation is that one sleeve
funds the wait and the other pays once the drawdown lasts; the funding sleeve is *supposed* to lose there,
because that loss is the inventory it sold, which is [[the-skew-is-the-product]]'s whole argument. What
matters is therefore **which claim a candidate was admitted on**:

- Admitted on **loss placement** (its loss lands where the responder does not fail), correlation in the
  protracted drawdown is a **direct falsification**, because that is the state the claim was about.
- Admitted on **budgeted concavity** (it collected calm income, and it loses a bounded amount in the
  drawdown while the responder pays), correlation in the protracted drawdown is **the contract
  performing**, and the only live question is whether the loss stayed inside its budget.

Applied to ILS, this is exactly the demotion §3.2 reaches, arrived at from the portfolio side: the crisis
correlation does not disqualify cat bonds from the seat, it disqualifies the *orthogonality* claim the
article makes for them, which is a placement claim. Strip that claim and ILS is admitted on the same
terms as credit carry - budgeted concavity - with a real but narrower advantage: the trigger is not the
recession, so the unconditional loss frequency is genuinely decoupled, and the residual crisis
co-movement is bounded and temporary rather than structural.

**The rule that follows.** A candidate may be credited on loss-placement grounds only for states in which
its placement has actually been measured, and the measurement must cover the **responder's own failure
state**. For ILS those two states overlap, so a placement credit requires measurement inside the overlap,
on a de-smoothed basis, which is precisely the measurement the corpus does not have (§6, gap 5). A
**budgeted-concavity credit** requires only a tail-budget number and evidence the calm income was
collected, both of which are measurable now. The seat should therefore admit candidates on budgeted
concavity plus segment-conditioned placement, and hold any orthogonality claim to a higher evidential bar
than the corpus currently meets.

**(a-ter) A second interface, to the responder rather than the Target.** §3.5(b) below sends the seat's
fast-segment loss to the Target's budget. The protracted-segment loss goes somewhere else: to the
**responder's sufficiency**. If the funding sleeve also loses while the drawdown lasts, the responder must
cover its own bleed *and* the funder's loss, which is where ① §4.1's qualitative "one funds, one pays"
becomes a quantitative Floor condition the seat paper can state. §4.2's finding is what makes it
tractable: since the crash is a minority of the premium, the funding sleeve's calm income is most of what
it delivers, so the Floor holds when the protracted-state loss is bounded and the calm income was
collected for long enough to have pre-paid it, and fails when either half is missing. That is a stateable
condition, it is additive to ① rather than corrective of it, and it is the natural home for the ILS
crowding dynamic in §3.2: a compressing premium shortens the period over which the wait is funded.

**(b) What the seat owes the tail budget (a cross-paper interface, not a correction).** ① §5.4 makes the
Target a budget line: a cost paid continuously, recovered only in stress, sized against the alternative
of simply de-risking, and designed to be earned back through monetisation. ① §4.2 sizes the tier by the
speed gap it closes. Those two together define a real interface running **from** this seat **to** the
Target: the size of the gap the Target must cover depends on how much the Floor loses in it, so **the
convergent seat's fast-window loss is a claim on the Target's sizing, and the Target's continuous
premium is paid out of the convergent seat's income.** What the seat owes, therefore, is a stated
magnitude rather than a qualitative caveat. The corpus already has the reporting instrument and the
horizon discipline: `convergent_tail_budget` as the actual mean loss in the worst decile of windows,
**never an annualised projection**, and
[[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]'s own pre-registered hypothesis that the floor be tested
separately on one-day gaps, one-month shocks and multi-quarter bears. The synthesis's recommendation is
to report the tail budget at **three horizons matched to the tiers' coverage segments** (gap, fast shock,
protracted bear) so the Target's sizing input is legible as a number and the seat cannot quietly
externalise its fast-window loss onto a tier it does not pay for.

**(c) Is the check redundant with $\Delta\hat\Theta$? No, on three grounds, and one is a specification
finding about the metric as configured.** The metric does price joint-loss states: power utility with
$\rho>1$ is convex in losses and $\hat\Theta$ is computed on the blended book, so a pole whose losses
land in the responder's gains scores high and one that co-crashes scores low, smoothly and without a
threshold. That is real and it is not sufficient.

1. **Identification, not weighting.** A fast spike is a handful of daily observations. The convex
   utility amplifies them if they are present, but `what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine`
   already states the boundary: a sample with no contractual crash cannot identify crash severity from
   realised returns however careful the resampling, and scenario or index-history evidence must be
   reported separately. The check is therefore a check on **whether the state is in the sample at all**,
   which no scalar computed on that sample can perform on itself.
2. **It prices the joint loss but cannot attribute it.** $\Delta\hat\Theta$ returns one number for the
   book and cannot say which segment the loss landed in. A candidate that co-crashes only in fast spikes
   is doing its job under ①'s division of labour; one that co-crashes in protracted drawdowns is not.
   Both can produce the same $\Delta\hat\Theta$. This is the same argument the role article makes for why
   a standalone measure cannot see placement, applied one level down, and it is why (a) requires
   segment-conditioned reporting rather than a single conditioning window.
3. **The metric's baseline excludes the Target, and the error runs both ways.** As defined,
   $\Delta\hat\Theta = \hat\Theta(\text{blend of responder and convergent}) - \hat\Theta(\text{responder})$.
   The Target appears in neither term. So a fast-spike joint loss is priced against a book with no fast
   defence, which **overstates the penalty relative to the actual roster** and can reject a candidate for
   a loss the roster has already covered, exactly as the mirror error would credit a candidate for
   coverage the Target supplied. This is a finding about our metric's configuration, which is ours to
   fix, and the fix is the segment conditioning in (a) plus an explicit statement of what the baseline
   book contains.

**Verdict: genuinely additional, not redundant.** The check survives, re-scoped as a reporting and
attribution requirement on the seat's own ranking rather than as a claim about the Floor.

The corpus supports the underlying fast-spike fact from five independent directions, which is more than
the caution box itself claims, and the paper may use them to *size* the segment rather than to argue
that a segment exists:

- **Bae and Elkamhi (*Management Science* 2021)** is the strongest published support and it is already
  in [[the-skew-is-the-product]]: innovations in global equity correlation carry a robust negative price
  of risk and price the carry and momentum cross-sections *jointly*, with fit near 90 percent in FX.
  Both paid legs lose when correlation spikes. The two poles are therefore **two claims written on the
  same calm** rather than opposite bets against a correlation shock. Transfer caveat: their momentum
  leg is cross-sectional FX, not cross-asset time-series trend, so the transfer is by analogy.
- **The mechanism from the convex side.** In fast sell-offs trend "lacks sufficient time to adapt and
  the crisis alpha characteristic disappears". *Flag:* Man Group source with the positioning-conditional
  return numbers unverified to a primary document; only the qualitative claim is citable.
- **[[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]'s own section** already says trend is a delayed offset and
  not a contractual hedge: puts protect sudden gaps, trend has historically worked better in prolonged
  declines and can remain long risk through the first shock. The paper's own fold corpus concedes the
  point; it should be promoted rather than left as an aside.
- **The documented episode.** August 2024: roughly 40 trillion yen of yen-funded carry unwinding in
  days, the funding currency appreciating sharply while equities crashed and the Nikkei had its worst
  day since 1987, amplified by procyclical deleveraging and margin increases. A joint fast failure on
  the record.
- **The reconciliation, which prevents overcorrection.** [[carry-diversifies-only-a-divergent-trend]]
  decomposed an apparent trend-carry co-crash to a **single fast-crash window read at daily
  frequency**, with the pairing diversifying at monthly frequency. And the broadest published
  unconditional estimate is only mildly positive (carry-momentum +0.24 time series, +0.15
  cross-sectional, concentrated in commodities), which is why the gate must be conditional rather
  than full-sample. *Flag:* that estimate is a Man Group working paper's number, tables verified to
  the primary document, and no independent estimate exists for a cross-asset TSMOM-versus-carry
  pairing specifically.

**What the paper may claim.** The seat places its loss away from the responder's failure **over the
protracted-drawdown segment at monthly-or-slower horizons**, which is the segment the responder is paid
to cover, and it co-fails with the responder in fast volatility spikes, which is the segment ① §4.2
assigns to the Target. Both halves are the roster working as designed. Two consequences the drafting
stage must carry. First, the $\Delta\hat\Theta$ pre-registration in paper §9 must report
**segment-conditioned** placement statistics rather than a single worst-decile-of-responder conditioning,
and must state what the baseline book contains. Second, **paper §7's rebalancing-premium argument for
orthogonality fails in the fast segment specifically**, because rebalancing into a fast spike requires
liquidity that weekly or fortnightly cat-bond funds do not have. The ILS article praises the slow cadence
for enforcing the correct measurement horizon; in the fast segment that same cadence is a liability.
That one is a construction question about our own candidate instruments, unaddressed in the folded
corpus, and it is carried to §6 as a gap of ours rather than as anything owed by ①.

## 4. Income versus crash

The brief's first deepen obligation. The corpus establishes that optimisers will split the income from
the crash; what the separation *is*, and where it belongs, is unworked. Here is the worked version.

### 4.1 The separation is a fact about construction, not about the premium

Every instance of "income without the crash" in the corpus is produced by a **choice of construction**,
and the choices are enumerable:

| Lever | What it does to the inventory | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Which currencies (exclude AUD, JPY, NOK) | higher Sharpe, flat-to-positive skew | Bekaert and Panayotov |
| Which selection statistic (option-implied skew) | similar returns, positive realised skew, Sharpe +0.12 | Torok (working paper, 0 citations) |
| Which leg of the decomposition | dollar-neutral leg holds the deep skew and downside beta; the diversified dollar-carry leg earns a *higher* Sharpe with minimal skew and no measurable downside exposure | Daniel, Hodrick and Lu |
| Which maturity bucket (defensive credit tilt) | a duration-times-spread cut in disguise: most of the income survives, the skew does not | [[the-skew-is-the-product]] |
| Which sizing rule | vol-managed carry Sharpe up ~40 percent with realised skew flipping sign; timed carry Sharpe 0.76 to 1.07 with skewness -0.76 to +0.97 | Almeida (thesis); Dupuy (*JBF* 2021) |
| Which rebalancing cadence | fixed-weight rebalancing is a short straddle on relative value; drifting is an embedded momentum position | Granger et al.; Hoffstein |

Two properties make this a result rather than a list.

**The dial turns one way.** No study in the corpus deepens the skew while improving the standalone
statistics. The dose-response is graded rather than binary (mild covariance-based risk rebalancing
compresses skew from about -0.5 to -0.3 while aggressive inverse-own-variance timing flips the sign
outright), but the direction never reverses.

**There is a theorem behind the direction.** Firoozye and Koshiyama derive the first four moments of
any linear-filter dynamic strategy on Gaussian returns in closed form and show positive skewness is an
*essential* feature of every positive-Sharpe dynamic strategy. So the moment a static premium stream
is wrapped in a responsive sizing rule, the wrapper drags the payoff toward positive skew by algebra.

Stated as the paper should state it: **the separation is not a discovery an optimiser makes about the
premium. It is a description of the search space the optimiser is handed.** Sharpe-gaming needs
payoff-shaping freedom; skew-shedding needs far less, because it is not an attack on the measure but
the honest optimum of every standalone objective, with the moment algebra on its side. An optimiser
pointed at any standalone objective will walk monotonically up the dial until the inventory is gone,
and every step will improve the score it was given.

### 4.2 The separation's price, which reverses the burden of proof

This is the part the corpus does not state and the paper needs, because it decides what the inventory
argument is allowed to rest on.

If the crash component is at most about **one third** of the G10 carry excess return (Jurek: crash-neutral
carry stays significantly profitable at 3.18 percent per year, t=3.13, to 5.31 percent, t=3.69,
depending on hedge aggressiveness), and the skewness-specific component is nearer **10 to 15 percent**
(Jurek and Xu, working paper, magnitudes provisional), then the seat that keeps its inventory is
giving up **very little income** relative to the seat that sheds it. The shed version keeps most of
the money.

Therefore the argument for keeping the inventory **cannot be that the crash rent is where the income
is**. The crash-share literature forecloses that defence. The inventory is worth holding for exactly
one reason: what its absence does to the *paired book*. Standalone, the inventory is worth close to
nothing and possibly less than nothing. That is why the paper's ranking rule must be relational, why
"the skew is not a risk to minimise" is a portfolio claim and not an asset-pricing claim, and why the
brief is right that the seat must be ranked on contribution rather than standalone smoothness. The
sharpened form: **the negative skew is not the product because it is well paid; it is the product
because it is the only thing the seat sells that the convex pole cannot supply itself.**

One undecidability must be declared alongside this. If Burnside, Eichenbaum, Kleshchelski and Rebelo
are closer to right - at-the-money-hedged carry has a risk-adjusted payoff statistically
indistinguishable from zero, the feared event being a high-marginal-utility state rather than a large
in-sample loss - then the entire premium is payment for a state the sample never shows, and **no
realised-stream verification, $\Delta\hat\Theta$ included, can distinguish it from alpha.** And if
Caballero and Doyle are closer to right, much of the apparent carry premium is a volatility premium
already captured by an option sleeve, so stacking both sells the same insurance twice. The crash-share
question is open three ways and the paper must not pick a winner. It must instead note that the
separation is *undecidable on realised returns* in the peso case, which is a limit on the paper's own
method.

### 4.3 Where the separation belongs

Three candidate placements. Order matters and the paper should give the order.

**First, in the instrument: remove the dial.** Prefer instruments whose income cannot shed its skew
because the two are fused in the contract, so the only way to reduce crash-beta is to exit the
position. [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]]'s addendum names the class: protection-selling on a
credit index, subordinated bank capital, collateralized put-write, catastrophe risk transfer. This is
architecturally strongest because there is nothing left for an optimiser to walk up.

**Second, in the mandate: pin the dial where it cannot be removed.** Give income and crash-beta
**separate axes**, pin the crash-beta axis by mandate, and let the search improve income *at* the pin.
[[the-skew-is-the-product]] works this out for credit (pin book duration-times-spread, so a defensive
tilt must buy proportionally more of the shorter bucket rather than silently shrinking the product)
and the general form is the concave mirror of pinning the volatility target by mandate and sweeping
shape only. Note the procyclicality objection and its answer: measured DTS spikes after spreads widen,
which is a bad exposure gauge, but for this seat pinning it forces the book to re-buy crash-beta as
spreads compress, which is exactly the concave discipline the pin exists to enforce.

**Third, in the evaluation: audit.** Rank on $\Delta\hat\Theta$ so the composite prices the placement.
Necessary and **not sufficient**, and the corpus already measured why: a composite-gated re-rank of a
27-cell grid **promoted nothing**, with every cell subtracting book certainty-equivalent and downside
correlation to trend in [+0.38, +0.49]. Ranking can only choose among the candidates it is offered; it
cannot manufacture a diversifying one. Putting the separation *only* in the evaluation is the mistake
the corpus has already made and measured.

That ordering is the answer to the brief's question. The separation belongs in the **mandate**, stated
as a constraint on the search space rather than as an objective inside it, with instrument choice as
the enforcement mechanism where available and the composite ranker as the audit. And there is a
corollary worth one line in the paper: this is why the seat's construction decisions are **governance
decisions**, not parameter decisions. Every one of the six levers in §4.1 was adopted somewhere in the
corpus for a legitimate local reason (a fee, a maturity, an execution cadence) by a party that was not
the owner of the inventory being sold. The rebalancing band is the cleanest instance: at the sleeve
level the fee arithmetic is right, and at the composite level the band's cost is the pole's role, which
no per-sleeve fee comparison prices.

### The rent thesis, attacked

The note asks to be attacked rather than assumed. Here is the result of contact with the folded
corpus.

**Where it survives and is strengthened.** Froot's catastrophe-risk result is the cleanest instance in
the corpus: premium-to-expected-loss ratios that are multiples of fair value, and of eight candidate
explanations tested, the survivors are supply-side (capital-market imperfections facing reinsurers,
and reinsurer market power). Tomunen then models exactly that constraint and fits it. Gârleanu,
Pedersen and Poteshman establish the same shape at the venue level for options. Santa-Clara and
Saretto measure it inside the trade. Window dressing shows it in transaction data with a natural
experiment. The thesis is a real description of the payer in these cases.

**Where it fails.** The note's load-bearing clause is *balance-sheet capacity that somebody else is
prohibited from holding*, and the prohibition is what supplies the durability, because arriving capital
is then the wrong kind. Two separate things have to be true for that to work, and the note only checks
the first. It is not enough that the constraint bind the party best placed to arbitrage the premium; the
constrained party must also be **non-substitutable** by arriving capital. A dealer forbidden the balance
sheet on the reporting date satisfies both, because the rule disqualifies the natural holder outright. A
capital-scarce specialist satisfies only the first, because a new entrant with capital is a perfectly
adequate substitute for it, and the premium then decays on inflows. Nothing else in the corpus's venues
even reaches the first test cleanly: a leveraged FX investor is not prohibited from holding the carry
position, it chooses the position and is forced out when funding tightens; a hedging commodity producer
is not prohibited; index option end users are net buyers by preference and mandate, mixed. And the
corpus supplies the demonstration on the substitutability half directly: capital arrived in the
catastrophe-bond market and the premium decayed.

**And the note's own best instance is a test of the durability clause, which fails in its strong form and
survives in its weak one.** The note observes that of the whole family, exactly one member has a public
multi-decade return series - cat bonds - and that is the one documented to have decayed, and reads this as
a selection effect. It is better read as a **test result**, and this synthesis initially read the result
as more damaging than it is. The primary-source check in
[[research/income-engine/_buildability|_buildability]] shows Tomunen's price of risk positive in all
sixteen sample years including nine post-break, so the measurable case does **not** show a
constrained-capacity premium dying. It shows one compressing and persisting. The strong durability clause
("does not dissolve as capital arrives, because arriving capital is the wrong kind") is refuted for a
substitutable constraint. The weak clause ("compresses toward the marginal arbitrageur's cost of capital
rather than to zero") is confirmed by the same evidence. The paper should state the weak clause, which is
the one §1 adopts, and should not repeat either the note's unqualified durability framing or an
extinction reading the data does not support.

**One clause must not enter the paper at all.** "An unlevered account with no balance sheet to rent is
trying to sell the one thing it does not have" is an *access* claim about this desk, not a claim about
markets. Admitting it as an economic premise would be screening research by implementation, which the
standing constraints forbid. It belongs in the buildability work, not in the argument.

**The form the paper should adopt** is the one in §1, which is ① §2.2's definition plus one construction
test: the seat is paid where the intermediary's capacity binds, and the premium is durable only where the
constrained party is both the natural arbitrageur and non-substitutable by arriving capital. Prohibition
gives both; capital scarcity gives only the first, and therefore gives a cycle. That version is weaker
than the note's, better evidenced, explains all four of the corpus's data points rather than two, and
turns the admission gate into a question with an answer: not only whether a constraint binds, but whether
arriving capital can relax it.

## 6. Gaps

What the argument still needs and the corpus does not supply. Ordered by how much the paper depends on
them.

**1. The seat has a definition but no instrument-level observable.** This is the biggest gap and it is
structural rather than an effort gap. The constraint framing supplies an admission criterion, and
§3.1 argues it sits on the permitted side of the §5.5 line as an existence test with a declared
falsifier. But the corpus supplies no **observable** for the venues this paper actually folds. The one
behaviour with a rule-dated, directly observable constraint is the repo reporting snapshot, and its
income is a funding rate rather than this seat's income. So the paper can define the gate and cannot
instantiate it for FX carry, credit spread, or received option premium. It must state the gate as a
research obligation with the observable named as missing, not present a gate it cannot compute.

**2. Nobody has re-estimated the He-Kelly-Manela seven-asset cross-section past 2012-13.** The spine's
single-factor version is contested on a sample that stops fourteen years ago, and the eSLR relaxation
effective 1 April 2026 dates a loosening that nothing in the literature has measured against.

**3. No structural-break alpha test exists on a deep-out-of-the-money-only slice, or on any non-US
index.** Both are publishable-grade gaps. Qiao, Xu, Zhang and Zhou cannot close them: they measure a
different object (the raw implied-minus-*expected*-realised gap, where the expected leg is itself a
statistical forecast) and they ran **no break test** in eighteen years of twenty-market panel data,
so their US full-sample average straddles the 2012 break and is fully consistent with both hypotheses.
Nobody on this desk has read that paper. Note also the unresolved discrepancy: a separate published
paper on SSE 50 ETF options reports a *negative* China variance premium against Qiao et al.'s positive
figure.

**4. Terstegge's identity and publication status.** Needed before the corroborating citation is even
safe to make. Anything post-2023 needs a venue check by standing rule.

**5. No de-smoothed ILS series exists in the corpus, at any frequency, and §3.5(a-bis) makes this
load-bearing rather than tidy.** §3.2's fourth line means the ILS placement claim has no admissible
measurement behind it, and the placement rule now says a candidate may be credited on placement only where
the measurement covers the responder's own failure state - which for ILS is the same protracted funding
crisis in which its co-movement channel opens. So this single missing measurement is exactly what stands
between ILS being admitted on budgeted concavity and being admitted on placement. What is owed: the
cat-bond funds' own smoothing profile (the Getmansky-Lo-Makarov MA(k) fit, or the cheaper first-order
autocorrelation plus Doubt Ratio pair), and correlations recomputed on multi-month compounded windows over
the 2008-2010 window specifically. Note the expected direction: de-smoothing should raise the crisis
co-movement above the reported 0.29, so this measurement can only weaken the placement case, never
strengthen it.

**6. The seat has no measured fast-segment loss to hand the Target's budget.** This is a gap of ours,
not a gap in ①. §3.5(b) establishes the interface: ① §4.2 sizes the Target by the speed gap it closes and
① §5.4 makes that tier a budget line, so the seat's fast-window loss is an input to somebody else's
sizing and is owed as a number rather than as a caveat. The corpus has the reporting instrument
(`convergent_tail_budget` as a realised worst-decile window loss, never annualised) and no measurement at
the gap and fast-shock horizons for any candidate. Sub-gap from the same section: **whether a
weekly-liquid income pole can be rebalanced at all in the fast segment**, where the ILS article reads
the slow cadence as a feature and the segment analysis reads it as a liability. Both are live design
questions rather than literature questions.

**7. No independent estimate of cross-asset TSMOM-versus-carry correlation.** The broadest published
number is a Man Group working paper's, and Bae-Elkamhi's momentum leg is cross-sectional FX rather
than cross-asset time-series trend, so the joint-pricing transfer is by analogy.

**8. Nobody tests whether skew-lite "good carry" retains diversification value against a trend book.**
Bekaert-Panayotov and Torok both stop before the question the paper cares about most.

**9. No study measures trend-gated high-yield skew against buy-and-hold high yield.** The
[[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] conclusion that a timing mute un-hires the pole rests on
convergent mechanism evidence including top-journal work, and on no direct measurement. Stated as a
strong prior, which is what it is.

**10. Bassi et al.'s sample period.** Named in [[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]] as a gap
to close before the paper cites the figures.

**11. The four-sleeve ordered-versus-unordered comparison has no owner yet.** ① §4.4's commission names
the comparator (an unordered, equal-risk-contribution blend of the same sleeves) and this seat can only
run the restricted Floor-level version (§2). The full test needs the responder, Target and Expansion
streams alongside ours, so it is a cross-paper obligation rather than a literature gap, and it should be
handed to ①'s planned revision round with the comparator and the predicted sign stated rather than left
implicit. Flagging it is the honest half of the discharge.

### The buildability interface, now that the note exists

[[research/income-engine/_buildability|_buildability]] is delivered and answers the ILS half of what
the argument was waiting on. Its verdict is cited, not re-derived: **buildable today, not durably so, and
not orthogonal in the state that would matter most.** What it settled for this synthesis:

- **Dilution and inversion are not walls for the dedicated cat-bond funds** (§3.4), so the mechanism is
  reachable and the ILS section stands.
- **The premium survives** (§3.2), which is why paper §7 is not a negative result.
- **The failure state is the protracted funding crisis**, which is the input that produced §3.5(a-bis) and
  is the most consequential single item the note contributed.

Three inputs remain open, and one of them the note explicitly declines:

1. **Whether any undilutable-crash-rent instrument is reachable beyond ILS.** §4.3's ordering puts
   instrument choice first precisely because it removes the dial rather than constraining it. ILS now
   qualifies on dilution grounds; the credit-index-protection, subordinated-capital and put-write legs of
   that class were out of the note's ILS scope. If ILS is the only reachable member, the separation
   argument still holds but rests on one instrument, which the paper should say.
2. **The cadence answer from §3.5.** Whether a weekly or fortnightly-liquid pole can be rebalanced in the
   fast segment where the rebalancing-premium argument is being claimed. Unaddressed by the note, and it
   determines whether that argument can be made at all.
3. **Whether the seat at achievable scale degenerates to a beta allocation.** The note deliberately stops
   short of NAV-level sizing and of broker-level tradability for the named share classes, both of which it
   lists as preconditions rather than findings. The paper must label the beta case per the standing
   constraint, and labelling it correctly requires knowing whether it is the actual case. This input may be
   *used only for labelling*: it may not narrow the candidate set, because the paper is forbidden from
   screening research by implementation.

Everything else the note produces (specific vehicles, tickers, TERs, share-class minimums, listings,
PRIIPs and gateway checks) stays in the buildability note. Where the paper cites its numbers it carries
its flags: the share-class figures are aggregator-sourced pending the fund's own KID, and the ESMA
outcome is undecided.
