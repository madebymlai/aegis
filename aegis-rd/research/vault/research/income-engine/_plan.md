### D2. The seat's inventory is bounded by what the book already bought, derived from ① rather than asserted

**The behaviour-level claim, which is the whole of D2 that enters the paper.** The convergent seat is paid
to sell insurance; the Target tier pays to buy it. A seat that sells the same deep crash protection the
Target holds puts the book on both sides of one exposure, so the premium received offsets the premium paid
and the book keeps only the costs. **A behaviour that pays only by selling the crash the book already
bought is therefore inadmissible**, whatever its standalone return looks like. That is an admission
criterion on behaviours, and it is what makes paper §6 a result rather than an observation.

**Why the boundary is ours.** It is not a number ① could have written down: how far a seat may sell depends
on **which occupant holds it**, and ① never picks occupants. ① can assign the deep fast segment to the
Target; only the seat paper can say what a given occupancy may therefore sell.

> [!note] Where the implementation detail went, and why it is not here
> This decision was originally written up through a specific instrument case - option-selling capital
> gates, a tail-sleeve collision, a numeric wing - because it was raised by a question about whether
> options are capital-gated like futures. They are not, but none of that belongs in a paper about market
> behaviours. The answer to the original question, and the NAV thresholds, wall taxonomy and venue detail
> behind it, live in [[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]] and are **not** carried into the body. What
> survives at the paper's altitude is the criterion above, stated over compelled behaviour rather than
> over strikes.

**The derivation runs entirely through ①'s own text**, so it does not correct ①: §4.2 assigns the fast
deep segment to the Target, §5.4 makes it a budget line, therefore the income pole must not sell into
what the book already bought, therefore the inventory is bounded above the Target's wing, and the exact
bound is occupancy-specific and ours.

**Consequences:**

- **Paper §6 becomes a result.** Not "you can separate the income from the crash" but "you must,
  because the tier above already owns the crash." Derived from the roster, not observed in data.
- **Paper §3 gains a consequence.** If how much crash a seat may sell depends on which behaviour occupies
  it, `carry-is-not-one-premium` stops being a taxonomy and acquires a decision.
- **Assert the rule, not the constant.** State the constraint and its derivation; leave the numeric
  wing to the occupant's own analysis. This is the mitigation for the one real cost, which is that the
  paper becomes responsible for a boundary interacting with a tier it does not own, and an error would
  surface only when ①'s revision round reconciles.

### D3. Fair pricing is sufficient for the role, stated as a claim rather than a concession

The seat does not require an above-fair price. Being paid the market price for crash-risk bearing
already delivers both the ordinary-market return and the loss placement, so the role survives fair
pricing intact. Written as **sufficiency, not necessity**: the role does not forbid alpha, it declines
to lean on it, so an excess return found in some venue is surplus rather than a revision.

**Why load-bearing rather than a fallback.**

- The vault already settled it. [[the-payer-did-not-leave-the-supply-arrived]] holds that fairly priced
  risk-bearing still pays in ordinary markets and still places its loss away from where the trend sleeve
  fails, and that this **sharpens** the rationale rather than removing it.
- It stops the paper fighting its own evidence base. A minority crash share, the compressed S&P variance
  premium, decayed cat-bond premia and arrived supply are each bad news for an alpha claim and neutral
  for a role claim. A paper needing the premium to be excess is built on ground its own citations erode.
- It converts an undecidability into indifference. If Burnside, Eichenbaum, Kleshchelski and Rebelo are
  closer to right, no realised-stream test including ΔΘ̂ can separate fair pricing from alpha. A paper
  indifferent to that answer is better placed than one needing it resolved.

**The obligation it creates.** [[what-is-a-strategy]] does not license holdings: a constant exposure
qualifies only where the payer is named, the sizing specified, and the premium's existence falsifiable.
So the seat must go through the five-part test **as a specified exposure**, not wave at a risk premium.
Carver's constant-forecast rule is the corpus's own proof this is passable. This is what stops "labelled
beta allocation" collapsing into "we bought a thing."

> [!important] Amended by D7 - the indifference claim covers the wrong pair
> D3 as stated claims indifference to whether the premium is fair or **excess**. That is sound and it is
> incomplete: the case that hurts is a premium that is **inadequate**, and realised returns cannot
> distinguish inadequate from fair any better than they distinguish fair from excess. **D7 supplies the
> repair** - admit on ex-ante rationale, bound by a forward cap, never claim verification - and the paper
> must carry D3 in D7's amended form rather than in the form written here.

### D4. The failure clause is joint unsatisfiability, not a negative book contribution

The role is an **existence** claim, so no single failure falsifies it. "It does not benefit the book" is
the observation that already occurred - the 27-cell composite re-rank had every cell at ΔΘ̂ < 0 with
downside correlation to trend in [+0.38, +0.49] - and was read as "no floor partner on this universe and
window." That reading is defensible, and it is unfalsifiable if always available, so the contract needs
three verdicts mapped to observations **in advance**:

| Verdict | Observation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Indeterminate** | ΔΘ̂ point estimate off zero but the interval spans it | **no verdict.** Not evidence of contribution, not evidence of harm. The default state, and the one the live measurement actually returned |
| Candidate fails | this candidate's ΔΘ̂ < 0 **with an interval excluding zero** | role untouched |
| Role redundant | ΔΘ̂ > 0 **with an interval excluding zero**, but below simply holding more responder | not dead, not worth a seat |
| **Seat earned** | ΔΘ̂ > 0, **interval excluding zero** | **clauses 2 through 4 hold**, never clause 1. One admissible candidate is sufficient, because the role is an existence claim, so this verdict permanently forecloses *role dead* |

**Role dead is not a row in this table, and moving it out is RM-8's fix.** It was previously listed here as
a fifth verdict, which mixed two incompatible epistemic routes. The empirical route - accumulate candidate
failures until the role is exhausted - has **no stateable stopping rule**, because the corpus's own
quantifier holds that the candidate space cannot be enumerated. No finite accumulation of non-results
establishes a universal non-existence claim. The structural route - that clauses 2 and 3 are provably
incompatible given this roster - is a **proof, not a measurement**, and belongs in the argument at §1 and
§6 rather than in a table of statistical outcomes.

So: **role dead is governed by the structural route only**, and the paper must say plainly that no
accumulation of empirical non-results establishes it. The rider, per the editorial ruling: the Devil's
Advocate's implicit demand for a stronger falsifier than the reachable verdicts supply is **WONTFIX**. A
universal non-existence claim over an unbounded space admits no finite protocol, for this paper or for ①,
and the fix is disclosing which verdicts are reachable rather than inventing a fourth.

> [!note] Rows 3 and 4 need a second comparison the metric does not supply on its own
> **Role redundant** and **seat earned** are distinguished only by comparing the blend against the
> responder **scaled up to fill the convergent weight**, because ΔΘ̂ already nets out the responder at its
> own weight. That is a second statistic and the paper must specify it rather than assume the delta
> supplies it. Stated as a specification requirement of the verdict structure, which is a theory question.
>
> Whether any particular codebase currently implements that comparison is an implementation question and
> is **not the paper's content**; see [[research/income-engine/_implementation-state|_implementation-state]].

**The seat-earned row was added at the third gate pass, and its absence was the more serious of the two
gaps.** The original table named three ways to fail and no way to succeed. Under D4's own logic the role
is an **existence** claim, so a single admissible candidate is exactly what settles it - which made the
missing row the most consequential outcome the table could return. A verdict scheme that enumerates only
failures is not neutral; it can only ever ratchet toward pessimism.

**The indeterminate row was added after the first Stage 2.5 gate and it is a repair to the taxonomy, not a
concession to a number.** A verdict scheme that cannot classify "no evidence either way" is broken on its
own terms, and this one could not: the original three rows silently assumed every measurement resolves.
The row would be correct even if nothing had ever been measured. It also fixes a latent error in the
candidate-fails row, which read ΔΘ̂ < 0 as sufficient when a point estimate without an interval decides
nothing.

The third is the real falsifier and it lands in D2's territory. **Paper §6 therefore carries the role's
survival**, not merely a result: the role lives or dies on whether income can be separated from the
crash. Without this mapping stated ex ante, every failure gets reassigned to the candidate list after
the fact.

### D5. The behavioural spine: rule-compelled versus circumstance-compelled

The user rejected two successive framings as too implementation-specific, then rejected a six-class
behavioural taxonomy as too low. Both rejections were correct. Instrument families (FX carry, credit
spread, received option premium) are trades, not behaviours; the behaviour is what the **other side**
does, and it is always someone acting for a reason that is not a view.

Six classes of compelled behaviour reduce to two, and the reduction is the theory:

| | Classes | What compels | Arriving capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rule-compelled** | snapshot measurement, capital charge, mandate rebalancing | a rule **names who may not hold** | wrong kind - premium survives |
| **Circumstance-compelled** | an exposure already held, leverage, an access barrier priced in cost | a need **anyone with capital can serve** | substitutes - premium compresses toward the marginal arbitrageur's cost of capital |

This cut **is** the non-substitutability test rather than a proxy for it, and it explains what an
enumeration could only list: segmentation splits across both classes, so covered interest parity
compressed because its barrier was cost while the crypto venue wedge persists because a wrapper that
genuinely cannot trade when the asset does is a closed window, which is a rule.

The classification **survived** the Step 3 stress test on four independent data points and on Kargar's
plurality finding as positive evidence.

> [!warning] The finding built on the classification is SUPERSEDED by D6.2 and D6.3
> D5 originally held that the durable behaviours "do not pay this seat" because window dressing pays a
> funding rate rather than ordinary-market income and mandate rebalancing is contrarian. The stress test
> defeated both exclusions. Read D6.1 to D6.3 for the repaired version: the claim is
> **majority-with-named-exceptions**, the window-dressing exclusion now rests on the restored convexity
> clause rather than on the funding-rate label, and durability splits into two failure modes rather than
> one axis. The paragraph below is retained only so the repair is legible.

**The original finding, superseded.** The durable behaviours are the rule-compelled ones, and they mostly
do not pay this seat: window dressing pays a funding rate rather than ordinary-market income, and mandate
rebalancing is contrarian so it pays whoever buys what rose, which is the responder. The behaviours that
do pay this seat - hedging demand, funding constraint, scarce specialist capital - are all
circumstance-compelled, hence substitutable, hence decaying by construction.

**What survives unchanged, and is why D3 is right.** The seat's occupants are drawn predominantly from the
substitutable class, so fair pricing is the **equilibrium the seat should be designed for** rather than an
unlucky outcome or a fallback. Compression is the mechanism working as predicted. The beta labelling
becomes a derivation instead of a concession.

**D2 restated at the correct altitude.** Not a strike or a wing, which is construction. At behaviour
level: *a behaviour that pays only by selling the deep crash is inadmissible, because the book already
bought it.* That is an admission criterion on behaviours and it is what carries paper §6.

### D6. Resolutions from the Step 3 stress test

The stress test ([[research/income-engine/_argument|_argument]]) attacked D5 as instructed and found
the taxonomy sound but the finding under-derived, with two counterexamples drawn from the corpus itself.
Five resolutions, all taken.

**D6.1 - Restore the fourth clause. The thesis line "three clauses and no fourth" was wrong.** It is what
let the gerrymandering in. `window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot` satisfies the three clauses as
stated - earned in ordinary markets, calendar-driven so uncorrelated with anything trend-shaped, and
observed in confidential ECB transaction data rather than inferred from the cross-sectional test
Gospodinov-Robotti's placebo defeats 39 times in 40. D5 excluded it on the ground that it "pays a funding
rate rather than ordinary-market income," which is a label rather than an argument, and which **inverts
the source**: that note treats funding-rate-shaped income as a positive, being accrual-shaped and
surviving a cost floor that kills statistical strategies of similar size.

The repair is a clause dropped from ① rather than a requirement invented here. ① §4.1 defines the seat as
one that **sells convexity**. Its absence made the loss-placement clause satisfiable **vacuously**: a repo
spread across a reporting date passes it by having almost no loss to place. Lacking an inventory is not
the same as placing one well.

**D6.2 - Rule-compelled classes need a direction, not just a compulsion.** The stress test defeated the
blanket claim that mandate rebalancing pays the responder. The repair is not an exception bolted onto the
taxonomy but a **missing dimension inside it**: within a rule-compelled class, *which side the rule
compels* sets the sign.

| Compelled action | Who is paid | Which pole |
|---|---|---|
| A glidepath rule compels selling what rose | whoever buys strength | the responder |
| An index rule compels selling what a downgrade excluded | whoever warehouses weakness | **this seat** |

Both are mandate rebalancing, both are rule-compelled and therefore durable against arriving capital, and
they have opposite signs. The second is evidenced by Dick-Nielsen and Rossi (2019, *RFS* 32(1)) on
corporate-bond index exclusions: the tracker is the forced **seller**, the dealer the buyer who warehouses
the excluded bond, earning a return the paper states is "not replicable by other investors in the economy."
**That the warehousing position carries the convexity clause's inventory is derived, not assumed.** Three
features of the mechanism in Dick-Nielsen and Rossi's own account establish it: downgrade-exclusion
inventory clears far more slowly than the low-maturity exclusions in the same study, with roughly a third
still held after 100 days; the dealer is therefore exposed to further deterioration in a credit whose
deterioration is precisely what forced the sale; and the paper attributes the dealer's higher compensation
specifically to that riskier and longer hold. A position paid for holding a deteriorating credit through an
extended window is crash-correlated inventory, which is what clause 2 requires, and not a funding spread
turned quickly, which would not supply it.

So the finding is **majority-with-a-direction-term** rather than majority-with-exceptions, and the
consequence is directive: the durable-and-pays-this-seat cell is where a book looks for occupants, and it
is reached by asking which way a rule pushes rather than by enumerating asset classes. Seeded as a
hypothesis, with no reachability claim attached, because the paper does not screen by implementation.

### D7. The peso problem is answered by bounding rather than by indifference, and the bound is owed

This closes the third of the stress test's three weakest points, which D6 left open.

**The threat, restated precisely.** D3 claims the paper is indifferent to whether the premium is fair or
excess, because the role needs only fair. That indifference covers the wrong pair. If Burnside,
Eichenbaum, Kleshchelski and Rebelo are closer to right, the feared event is a high-marginal-utility state
the sample never shows, and realised returns cannot distinguish fair pricing from **inadequate** pricing
any better than they distinguish fair from excess. Being underpaid for crash risk is **not** acceptable
for the role, so the indifference framing does not reach the case that actually hurts.

**The resolution is [[research/README|the vault's own constitution]], not a new argument.** README already
holds that a backtest *estimates* an effect we already expect and never *discovers* one, and that every
claim must carry an ex-ante economic rationale fixing the sign from first principles before any backtest.
Realised returns were therefore never the warrant for this seat's claim. The peso problem does not
undermine the paper; it exposes that the argument had been implicitly leaning on a verification it never
possessed, and it vindicates the method that never asked for one.

**The structure this forces, in three parts:**

1. **Admit on rationale.** The seat is warranted by the ex-ante story - a constraint binds, a party is
   compelled, the sign follows - which is checkable without a return series. This is also why D5's
   two-class taxonomy is load-bearing rather than decorative: it *is* the rationale.
2. **Bound by a forward cap, not by proof - and the corpus does not currently supply one.** Since adequacy
   is unverifiable on realised returns, the response is not better measurement but a cap on the loss the
   book will accept, so that underpayment costs **return, not solvency**. ① §5.4 supplies the *principle*
   by making the tail a convexity-premium budget line.

   **But the instrument this decision first named cannot do the job, and that is a finding rather than a
   detail.** `convergent_tail_budget` is the actual mean loss in the worst decile of realised 2 to 6 month
   windows. It is a backward-looking report, and a peso event is by definition one the sample has not
   contained, so a realised worst decile is structurally blind to exactly the loss the cap must bound.
   Bounding an unrealised tail with a realised statistic is the same circularity D7 exists to escape.

   What the seat therefore requires is a **forward cap denominated in the loss a bad state would produce,
   not in position size** (RM-5). ① §5.4 already supplies the template by sizing the Target as a
   convexity-premium budget line, so the seat's cap should be stated the same way: a scenario-loss budget at
   the role level, with any notional limit understood as one implementation of that budget rather than as
   the budget itself. A notional cap cannot substitute, because different constructions lose different
   multiples of notional in the same state, which is precisely what a short-convexity role guarantees.
   **State this as a design requirement with the gap named, exactly as the paper handles its other open
   obligations, and do not present the realised tail budget as discharging it.**
3. **Never claim verification.** ΔΘ̂ ranks candidates and estimates contribution at a fixed weight. It
   cannot certify that the price is right. The paper must state this where it pre-registers the test, or
   the pre-registration implies a warrant the metric cannot supply.

**Consequence for the contract.** The first clause, "earn a positive expected return in ordinary markets",
is an **ex-ante** claim and not a measured one. State it as such in paper §1, or the contract reads as
though a measurement stands behind a clause that no measurement can reach.

### D9. The capacity-provision test, and the leverage claim it replaces

Raised by the user at the Stage 3 checkpoint, and it corrects a factual error in a backbone note that had
already been litigated upstream.

**The error.** [[the-premium-is-rent-on-a-balance-sheet]] closed its abstract with "an unlevered account
with no balance sheet to rent is trying to sell the one thing it does not have." **This book is levered.**
The sleeve pays USD margin interest on a USD debit, the sleeve budget carries a 1.75 gross-up, and paper
①'s own re-review records that its §5.2 "correctly declines to describe the book as unlevered (not the
book's use of leverage, which it runs up to that cap)." ① was reviewed on this exact point and corrected;
the newer note regressed against it. Now fixed at source.

**Why it matters beyond accuracy.** Leverage was never the operative axis. A margin-levered account still
cannot be a repo counterparty, warehouse a bond inventory, or act as an authorised participant. What
separates the compensated party from an outside allocator is **institutional function**.

**The test, which the Stage 3 editor reached independently from the panel.** Alongside D6.1's convexity
clause, a second admission test: **is the compensated function risk-bearing, or capacity-provision?** A
seat bears risk. A dealer provides financing and warehousing. Applied to both restored D6 cells it gives
the same answer: window dressing pays whoever supplies repo financing across the reporting date, and
index-exclusion warehousing pays for financing and distribution capacity, which is precisely why those
returns are "not replicable by other investors in the economy."

**What it does, in four places at once.** It supplies the causal mechanism D5 was asserting without one;
it reconciles D6.2's durable-and-pays-this-seat cell with D5's predominantly-substitutable claim; it
sharpens D3 and D7, because an entity that can only bear risk is structurally confined to the substitutable
class and fair pricing is therefore its equilibrium by derivation rather than by observation; and it
**kills D6.2's "where a book should look for occupants"**, since a capacity-provision cell is not
occupiable by an entity that cannot provide capacity.

**Two boundaries to hold.** The test cuts on **function, not access**, so it states what the mechanism pays
for rather than what any account can reach and does not reintroduce screening by implementation. And R3's
proposed phrasing "not investor-reachable" **must not be used** - it reads as access and would smuggle back
exactly what `_synthesis.md` correctly excluded.

### D10. RM-28's result, and the bars-versus-prices scope condition. ADOPTED

**The sweep did not confirm the generalisation and did not counter it.** Of the eight compelled actions
tested, five fail an earlier gate before the capacity question is reached (Treasury financing and the
cross-currency dealer behaviour fail durability; issuance premium, dash-for-cash and foreign-ownership
limits fail the convexity clause), one is out of scope as D6.2's direction term, one is indeterminate, and
one is contested. So the capacity-provision test still rests on **two** confirmations, and the honest
reading is that it is **under-evidenced rather than supported**: the register contains very few cases that
reach the test at all, which revives the Devil's Advocate's original question of whether the criterion is
true by construction of the taxonomy rather than empirically.

**And the contested case is a counterexample.** Bank significant risk transfer:

- The sweep found, on ESRB, ECB and BIS primary sources, that its compensated function is **unambiguously
  risk-bearing** - a funded credit-linked note, with no financing, warehousing or distribution service.
- The sweep kept it out of the population by classifying bank capital charges as **circumstance**-compelled.
- But [[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]] states the wedge is "a **capital rule** rather than a capital
  shortage", which is rule-compelled by this vault's own reading.

**The classification evidence argues against the sweep's own conclusion.** It cited the Solvency II
cost-of-capital rate being cut by legislative fiat as evidence of a policy dial. But D6.3 already
establishes that rule-compelled premia fail **by rule change** rather than by capital arrival, so a dial a
legislator moves is a property of rules, not evidence of circumstance. Likewise the ECB finding that
less-capitalised banks use the channel most shows a rule binding harder nearer the constraint, not a
circumstantial need. **Rule-compelled plus risk-bearing is exactly what the named contribution says cannot
exist.**

**The proposed scope condition, which is sharper than the claim it repairs.** The discriminating question is
not rule versus circumstance but **what the rule does**:

| The rule | What the compensated party must do | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **bars holding** - a dealer may not carry the balance sheet on the reporting date | supply the balance sheet | capacity-provision |
| **bars holding** - a tracker may not hold an excluded bond | warehouse it | capacity-provision |
| **prices holding** - a capital charge makes the junior slice expensive to keep | simply bear the risk at a lower capital cost | **risk-bearing** |

Where a rule **bars** holding, the compensated party supplies capacity the barred party cannot. Where a rule
**prices** holding, a third party can bear the risk itself and no service is required. This makes the
contribution claim **falsifiable rather than universal**, and it supplies a mechanism rather than a
correlation.

**What it costs and what it buys.** It weakens the claim from universal to conditional. It also means
significant risk transfer is a durable, risk-bearing cell, and per the register a reachable one, which under
D4 is a **positive** finding for the role's existence claim even as it bounds the contribution claim.

> The alternative was to argue significant risk transfer is circumstance-compelled against this vault's own
> text, which needs an argument the sweep did not supply and that D6.3 appears to foreclose. Defending a
> universal claim by reclassifying the single case that breaks it is the move the Devil's Advocate already

### D10a. The distinction is derived, not induced from three cases

**This is the improvement that makes the contribution defensible.** As of RM-28 the scope condition rested on
three observations: window dressing and index exclusion on the capacity side, significant risk transfer on
the risk-bearing side. Three cases will not carry a structural claim, and the sweep established that the
register holds few further cases that even reach the test. **So the distinction must be argued from first
principles rather than counted.**

It can be:

- A rule that **bars** a party from holding removes that party from a position which **still has to exist
  somewhere**. Someone must therefore *supply the holding itself*. That is a service, and it is why the
  compensation is for capacity rather than for risk. The barred party's willingness to pay is not a
  preference about risk; it is the price of a function it is forbidden to perform for itself.
- A rule that **prices** holding leaves the party perfectly able to hold, only expensively. The position can
  migrate to whoever bears it at the lowest capital cost. **Nothing is supplied**; risk is transferred to a
  cheaper bearer, and the compensation is for bearing it.

The three cases then **follow from** the argument rather than generating it, which is what a referee will
test. Note what this buys: the claim stops depending on how many register entries were checked, so the
sweep's thin yield stops being a weakness in the contribution and becomes what it actually is, a limit on
how much corroboration exists.

**And it yields a falsifier**, which the paper did not previously have. See D11a.

## The thesis, as it now stands

Crystallized by D1 to D3, repaired by D6.1, and **amended by D16c on the pairing's asymmetry**.

The convergent seat is a portfolio role, not a mechanism, and its job has **four** clauses: earn a
positive expected return in ordinary markets; **sell convexity, so that it carries a crash inventory at
all** (① §4.1); place that inventory's loss where the convex pole does not also fail at the horizon the
book is measured on; and be ranked on its contribution to the paired book rather than on standalone
smoothness. The second clause is not decorative - without it the third is satisfiable vacuously by a
behaviour with no loss to place. The economic content is ① §2.2's and is adopted rather than re-derived:
the short pole is paid for bearing unhedgeable risk where the intermediary's capacity binds. The role
requires only fair pricing, so it is indifferent to whether the premium is excess. Its negative skew is
the inventory it sells, and the inventory is worth holding not because it is well paid - the crash share
is a minority, and venue-indexed - but because it is the only thing the seat sells that the convex pole
cannot supply itself. **Within this roster**, the seat's inventory is bounded above the Target tier's wing,
because the book has already bought the deep crash and selling it back is incoherent; the bound holds
because the Target owns the fast-deep segment and creates the collision, so a book without a
Target-equivalent tier is not bound by it (RM-3, per D6.4).

**Scope of the ranking clause.** ΔΘ̂ tests clauses 2 through 4 - the inventory's presence, its placement,
and the paired-book contribution. It does **not** test clause 1: adequate income is admitted on ex-ante
rationale alone, pending the forward cap D7 names as owed. So "the role holds" always means clauses 2
through 4 hold, never all four (RM-6).

## Part 2. Decisions about the map and the sections

### D11. The ranking section is cut. Eight sections, not nine

The old §8, "Ranking the seat: the paired-book test", is **removed**. Three reasons, and the first is the
one that decides it.

**The paper does not own the metric.** ΔΘ̂ is derived, justified and validated in
[[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]], which this paper folds. Respecifying it here would
restate a source rather than fold it, and the fold's whole discipline is that folded articles **serve**
sections rather than being reprinted as them.

**With the measured result gone, the section was a measurement protocol inside a paper about what a role
is.** How one would score candidates is engineering downstream of the role's definition. The paper's
subject is the definition.

**RM-8 had already dismantled it.** Moving *role dead* out of the verdict table and into the structural
argument at §1 and §6 removed the row that made the table load-bearing; what remained were rows describing
outcomes of a measurement the paper no longer reports.

**What must survive the cut, and where it goes.** [[what-is-a-strategy]] requires a declared **failure
state** as part of the contract, and unfalsifiability was the Devil's Advocate's sharpest attack. So §1
carries the failure condition **alongside the four clauses**, as part of the contract rather than as a
protocol: the role fails structurally if the clauses are jointly unsatisfiable, and no accumulation of
empirical non-results establishes that. §1 also states that any candidate ranking requires a comparison
against the responder scaled to the convergent weight, which is a specification requirement of the
contract, not a section's worth of material.

**What is deliberately given up.** The paper no longer tells a reader how to score candidates. That is a
loss only if the paper's job were to supply a scoring procedure, and it is not: ① §4.4 commissions a
demonstration that the role holds on its own terms, which §8 now discharges as an argument, with the
metric cited rather than rebuilt.

### D11a. The named falsifier, stated in §1 with the contract

D10a's derivation supplies something the paper previously lacked and that the Devil's Advocate attacked it
for lacking. The falsifier:

> **A bar-type rule whose compensated party is a pure risk-bearer refutes the claim.** If a rule removes a
> party's ability to hold, and the party paid on the other side supplies no financing, warehousing,
> distribution or market-presence function but simply bears the risk, then barring holding does not create a
> capacity need and the derivation in D10a is wrong.

State it in §1 alongside the four clauses and the failure condition. Three properties make it worth having:
it is **specific**, so somebody could go looking; it is **findable**, since regulatory bars are documented
rather than inferred; and it does not require enumerating an unbounded candidate space, which is what made
the old *role dead* verdict unfalsifiable in practice.

**This answers the Devil's Advocate directly rather than by disclosure.** Its surviving objection was that
assembling the verdict structure, the peso concession and the measurement fencing left nothing the paper
would accept as refutation. The editorial ruling accepted that diagnosis and prescribed disclosure of which
verdicts were reachable, because no better answer existed at the time. D10a supplies a better answer: the
paper now carries a refutation condition on its central claim, not merely an honest account of what it
cannot test.

**Word count.** Revise down from 7,900, which was priced for ten sections. Eight sections without an
empirical chapter suggests **6,000 to 6,500**, and §1 gains a little from absorbing the failure clause and
the falsifier while §3 and §4 lose the material that duplicated between them.

### D12. §4 keeps its empirical exhibit, and gains the second one it was missing

**The ILS exhibit stays, and the line that justifies it is not "ours versus theirs".** D7 forbids using
realised returns to certify **adequacy**, because the peso state has not fired. Tomunen's result is not that:
it is evidence about a premium's **decay path**, sixteen years of positive but declining price of risk. That
is what published empirical work legitimately establishes and what a theoretical paper legitimately cites.
The removed D8 crossed the line because it offered *this desk's* measurement of *this desk's* configs as
evidence that the role works, which is a demonstration rather than a citation.

**Under D10a it is also the right exhibit rather than merely a surviving one.** Cat-bond premia are rent on
specialist funds' capital **scarcity**, so the compulsion is circumstantial and not a rule barring holding.
That makes Tomunen the cleanest available instance of §4's first limb: a circumstance-compelled premium
compressing toward the marginal arbitrageur's cost of capital **without vanishing**, which is exactly the
predicted path and not the extinction reading the vault's shorthand once invited.

**The gap this exposes.** §4 has two limbs and was illustrating one. The seat is confined where compulsion is
circumstantial *and* where a rule bars holding; it is **not** confined where a rule prices holding. Written
with only the ILS exhibit, §4 demonstrates the confinement and merely asserts the release, which inverts the
emphasis: the release is the part a reader can act on.

**So §4 carries both.** Tomunen for the circumstance-compelled limb, and **significant risk transfer as the
price-type contrast** - a capital charge that prices holding rather than barring it, whose compensated
function is risk-bearing on primary sources, and which is therefore a durable premium a risk-bearing role can

**What does not travel with it.** The wrapper machinery from
[[research/income-engine/_buildability|_buildability]] - the ESMA reclassification question, share-class

cited at most once. §4 needs the decay path and the contrast, nothing about who can buy what.

### The named contribution claim

**Editor-proposed at the author's explicit request. Not author-formed.** Full ranking and reasoning in
[[research/income-engine/_review|_review]].

> Where a rule **bars** the natural holder from holding, the compensated party must supply **capacity** that
> a risk-bearing allocation cannot provide regardless of occupant, so a risk-bearing role is confined there
> to compensation that compresses toward the marginal arbitrageur's cost of capital. Where a rule merely
> **prices** holding, no service is required and a risk-bearing role can occupy the durable cell directly.
> Fair pricing is therefore a consequence of **which kind of rule creates the premium**, not an observation
> about where capital happens to sit.

**Revised 2026-07-25 after RM-28, from a universal claim to a conditional one with a mechanism.** The
original form asserted that durable and non-substitutable premia pay for capacity-provision *regardless of
occupant*, full stop. Bank significant risk transfer refutes that: it is rule-compelled by this vault's own
reading ("a capital rule rather than a capital shortage") and its compensated function is risk-bearing on
primary sources, a funded credit-linked note with no financing, warehousing or distribution service. See
D10 for the full derivation.

**The revision improves the paper in three ways rather than conceding ground.** It is **falsifiable**, since
a bar-type rule whose compensated party is a pure risk-bearer would refute it. It supplies a **mechanism**
where the original had a correlation. And it is **directive**: the original told an allocator it was
structurally confined to compressing premia, which is nearly useless, while the revision says *look for
price-type rules and avoid bar-type ones*, which is actionable at the behaviour level.
