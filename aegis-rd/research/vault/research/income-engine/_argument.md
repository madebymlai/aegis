# Argument stress test - convergent engine

Adversarial pass over [[research/income-engine/_plan|_plan]]. The job here is to find where the
argument breaks, not to write the paper. Every claim below is checked against
[[research/income-engine/_synthesis|_synthesis]], [[research/income-engine/_sources|_sources]],
[[research/income-engine/_buildability|_buildability]], the backbone notes, and ① itself where the
plan's own citation trail points there.

## 1. Is D5 circular?

**Verdict: not a pure tautology, but the specific finding it produces - "the durable behaviours mostly
do not pay this seat" - is asserted rather than derived, and the corpus contains at least two
counterexamples the plan does not address.** D5 is the paper's most exposed load-bearing claim.

### What survives

The two-class reduction itself (rule-compelled versus circumstance-compelled, cut by substitutability
rather than by mechanism label) is real content, not a relabelling. It rests on four independent data
points across [[the-premium-is-rent-on-a-balance-sheet]] and the synthesis: a dealer forbidden the
balance sheet on a reporting date has no substitute (rule disqualifies the holder), while a
capital-scarce specialist fund, an FX carry investor, and a dealer's short-vol inventory all decay on
arriving capital. Kargar's plurality finding is genuine positive evidence for a plural, venue-local
constraint structure, not a repaired single factor. And the plan is honest that the cut leaks -
segmentation splits across both classes, which is exactly what a real (non-gerrymandered) taxonomy
should do at its edges. So the classification survives.

### Where it breaks

The finding built on top of the classification is a different, stronger claim, and it is where the
argument actually does its political work: it is what lets D3 say fair pricing is the seat's *designed*
equilibrium rather than a disappointment. That claim needs the durable class to be mostly absent from
the seat's occupant pool, and the two exclusions offered are not derived from the paper's own stated
admission test.

**The three-clause test (D4) has no skew requirement, but the exclusions apply one.** D4 states the
role's job as three clauses "and no fourth": positive expected return in ordinary markets, loss placed
away from the convex pole's failure, ranked on portfolio contribution. Nothing in that test says the
income must arise from selling optionality rather than lending balance sheet. Apply the stated test,
not an implicit fourth clause, to window dressing. [[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]] states
plainly that the premium is earned in ordinary markets, is uncorrelated with anything trend-shaped
because it is calendar-driven rather than market-driven, and is - in the synthesis's own words - "the
vault's best exhibit that intermediary constraints price anything at all... it does not depend on the
factor test surviving" (`_synthesis.md` §3.1). That is a stronger empirical anchor than most of what the
paper actually wants to admit: FX carry and the He-Kelly-Manela-style single factor are the objects
Gospodinov-Robotti's placebo attacks (39 of 40 false positives), while window dressing is observed
directly in confidential ECB transaction data, not inferred from a cross-sectional test. On the letter of
D4's three clauses, window dressing is a *better* candidate than most of the seat's actual occupancy.

D5 excludes it anyway, on the ground that it "pays a funding rate rather than ordinary-market income."
That line is asserted in the plan, not derived from D4, and its source is traceable: the "funding rate,
not a price move... it should survive a cost floor that kills statistical strategies of similar
magnitude" framing comes from [[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]]'s own strategy-hypothesis
section, which is written from this desk's execution-cost perspective, not from the seat's abstract
three-clause test. Importing that framing into D5 risks smuggling an implementation-shaped distinction
into a theoretical admission criterion - exactly the move the standing constraints forbid ("do not screen
by implementation"). If the paper wants to exclude window dressing on the ground that it is not "selling
convexity," it needs to say so and add that as an explicit fourth clause to the role, which contradicts
D4's "three clauses and no fourth," or it needs to argue the exclusion some other way. As written, the
exclusion is a label, not an argument.

**"Mandate rebalancing is contrarian" does not survive the corpus's own catalogue.** D5 states mandate
rebalancing "is contrarian so it pays whoever buys what rose, which is the responder," generalizing from
one instance - target-date glidepaths, which [[the-premium-is-rent-on-a-balance-sheet]] does correctly
call contrarian and trend-paying. But the same register lists a second rule-compelled rebalancing flow
that is not contrarian in that sense: fallen-angel index rebalancing, where the ETF is the *forced buyer*
of a bond that has just fallen, and the compensated party is the dealer supplying it, with returns
[[income-must-accrue-not-be-captured]] describes as "not replicable by other investors in the economy."
That is a rule-compelled flow paying a warehousing/supply function adjacent to this seat's own occupancy,
not paying the responder. Index reconstitution more broadly (Pedersen 2018, in the register) is often
pro-cyclical - a stock enters an index because it grew large enough to qualify - which if anything pays
the *contrarian* liquidity-supplying side, not trend. D5's blanket "mandate rebalancing pays the
responder" does not hold across the corpus's own examples; it holds for one instance the plan generalizes
from.

**D5's flagship durable case is not obviously more durable than a circumstance-compelled one.**
[[window-dressing-at-the-regulatory-snapshot]] states its own kill condition in the sharpest terms in the
whole corpus: "move the metric from a snapshot to a period average and the behaviour disappears by
construction... Half the relevant world has already removed the incentive." The US and UK already report
on a period-average basis. That means the rule-compelled/durable label rests on continued regulatory
choice by the euro area, Switzerland, and Japan specifically, which is a policy dial exactly like the
Solvency II cost-of-capital rate the team lead flagged (cut from 6% to 4.75% by legislative fiat) and
exactly like the eSLR relaxation effective 1 April 2026. A rule that half the relevant regulatory universe
has already chosen not to have is not obviously more durable than a capital constraint that compresses as
capital arrives; both are contingent on a party (a regulator, in one case; the marginal arbitrageur, in
the other) whose behaviour the paper is asserting rather than modelling. D5's clean two-class durability
story is weaker for its own best example than the plan presents it.

**Why this matters more than a taxonomy quibble.** If a durable, rule-compelled occupant commonly *does*
pay this seat, D3's claim - that fair pricing is the equilibrium the seat should be designed around,
because its occupants are drawn from the substitutable class - loses its foundation. A rule-compelled
occupant, by the corpus's own limits-to-arbitrage logic, earns a *non-compressing* rent, which is exactly
the case D3 needs to be rare or absent. D5 is doing the work of protecting D3 from its most dangerous
counterexample, and the mechanism by which it does that (the funding-rate exclusion, the always-contrarian
generalization) is precisely where the argument is least derived from the paper's own stated test. That
is the shape of definitional gerrymandering even though the underlying two-class reduction is real.

**What would fix it, not what the paper currently does.** State D5 as a majority claim with named
exceptions rather than a clean dichotomy - "most rule-compelled behaviours identified in this corpus do
not pay this seat; window dressing is a documented exception, and its exclusion (or admission) turns on
whether income-in-kind-for-balance-sheet counts as this seat's income, a question the three-clause test as
stated does not resolve." That is a weaker, more defensible claim than the one currently in the plan, and
it is available without abandoning D5's real content.

## 2. The three weakest points, ranked

### 1. D5's unearned exclusions (see above)

**Cost if a referee lands it.** D3's central claim - fair pricing as designed equilibrium rather than
disappointment - loses its support. The paper would need to fall back to "fair pricing is *sufficient*
for the role" (which D3 already states as the primary claim) without the stronger "and it is what the
role should expect," which is D5's contribution. Recoverable, but it costs the paper's most quotable line
and reopens the question of why the seat should be designed around compression rather than treated as
having drawn a lucky, possibly temporary hand.

### 2. §6's mandated separation is a dependency dressed as a derivation

D2/§6 claims the income pole must not sell the deep crash because the Target tier already bought it. The
plan itself half-admits this: "the derivation runs entirely through ①'s own text... therefore the
inventory is bounded above the Target's wing" (D2), and separately concedes "the paper becomes
responsible for a boundary interacting with a tier it does not own." But nowhere does the plan state the
converse plainly: if the Target is unfunded, undersized relative to the fast segment, or simply absent
from a book that adopts this seat as a role, the collision D2 describes does not occur, and the strike
boundary is not merely unenforced but *wrong* - an unbounded seat might then be the correct construction.
A hostile referee reads §6, titled "the separation is mandated, not merely available," and asks: mandated
by what, independent of a specific three-tier roster this paper does not own and is forbidden from
correcting? The honest answer is "by ①'s roster, which this paper adopts but does not defend," which is a
dependency, not a portable derivation. This sits uncomfortably against the paper's own self-description as
a theoretical paper about market behaviours rather than about one architecture.

**Cost if a referee lands it.** §6 is not a "result" in the sense D4 claims for it ("paper §6 becomes a
result... derived from the roster, not observed in data"). It is a conditional design rule. That does not
kill the section, but it does mean §6 cannot carry "the role's survival" (D4's own phrase) without also
stating, prominently, the condition it survives under.

### 3. The peso-problem undecidability is bigger than the paper's "indifference" framing admits

D3 treats the Burnside-Eichenbaum-Kleshchelski-Rebelo undecidability as a reason for productive
indifference: if at-the-money-hedged carry is statistically indistinguishable from zero because the
compensated state has not fired in-sample, the paper is fine not knowing whether the premium is fair or
alpha, because it only claims sufficiency. That framing understates the problem. If the compensated state
has not fired in-sample, a *positive* measured ΔΘ̂ for some future candidate is equally consistent with
"this occupant is fairly priced" and with "this occupant is a peso-problem trade that has not yet paid its
bill," and D4's own falsification protocol (candidate fails when ΔΘ̂ < 0) has no complementary defence
against the second reading when ΔΘ̂ is positive. The paper's "we don't need to know if it's alpha" answer
addresses a narrower question than the one the undecidability actually raises, which is whether a positive
in-sample result means anything at all about the *true* expected contribution when the tail state is, by
construction, rare relative to any available sample window. This is not fatal to the paper - no realised-
return methodology fully escapes it - but stating it as "indifference is a strength" oversells a limitation
the paper shares with every other realised-stream test in the corpus, D4 included.

**Cost if a referee lands it.** Undercuts confidence in D4's third verdict machinery specifically (see §3
below, section 8's stress test) rather than the paper's overall thesis. Recoverable by stating the limit
explicitly rather than converting it into "indifference," which reads as more resolved than it is.

## 3. Per-section stress test

### §1. The seat is a role, stated as a contract with a failure clause

- **Core argument.** A convergent seat earns a roster place only if it passes a falsifiable five-part
  test (state/action/exit/payer/failure per [[what-is-a-strategy]]) collapsed into three clauses, with a
  pre-registered failure clause distinguishing candidate failure, role redundancy, and role death.
- **Weakest point.** "Role dead" requires the three clauses to be jointly unsatisfiable *for every
  candidate, permanently*. That is a universal claim over an open-ended, unbounded candidate space. There
  is no finite protocol that establishes it. The corpus's own instructive case - the 27-cell composite
  re-rank where every cell scored ΔΘ̂ < 0 - was read as "no floor partner on this universe and window," not
  as role death, which shows that in practice, researchers facing a clean negative result reach for
  "candidate fails" or "role redundant" rather than "role dead," because the third verdict can never be
  positively established from a finite experiment. The three-verdict table is logically coherent but the
  third row is operationally a verdict nobody will ever actually render. A hostile referee calls this
  unfalsifiable-in-practice wearing falsifiable clothing, exactly the framing the team lead's brief
  anticipates.
- **Reversal test.** If the failure clause instead had only two verdicts (candidate fails / role holds),
  does the paper's conclusion change? Barely - the third verdict is never reached in the sections that
  follow, since no section claims role death. The three-verdict table does real rhetorical work
  (pre-registering against post-hoc reassignment) but no *empirical* work is shown depending on the third
  row existing. It survives its own removal about as well as it survives its own reversal, which is a sign
  it should be presented as a discipline rather than as a result with content.
- **Evidence status.** [[what-is-a-strategy]]'s five-part test rests partly on practitioner blogs
  (Villahermosa's six-component anatomy, Longmore's payer framing) flagged UNCONFIRMED in `_sources.md`
  §5, corroborated only by consistency with Carver, who is himself a practitioner source. The core
  argument (payer clause, falsifiability) is otherwise CITED-CONSISTENT (Sharpe, Pedersen, Lopez de Prado,
  Harvey-Liu-Zhu).
- **Proposed word count.** 900.

### §2. The other side of compelled behaviour: two classes

- **Core argument.** Six candidate classes of compelled behaviour reduce to two - rule-compelled and
  circumstance-compelled - cut by whether arriving capital can substitute for the constrained party.
- **Weakest point.** This section is where ILS, `carry-is-not-one-premium`, and
  `commodity-carry-constructions` land as "instances" per the rebuilt map, but the map does not say where
  the Stage 1 synthesis's most carefully verified material - the ILS orthogonality demotion, the buildability
  verdict, the ESMA wall, the crowding dynamic - now lives. If it is compressed into "an instance" inside
  §2, either §2 balloons well past a setup section's proportion or that verification work gets dropped on
  the floor at drafting time. This is a structural gap in the plan, not just a word-count risk.
- **Reversal test.** If the classes were assigned in reverse (rule-compelled decays, circumstance-compelled
  is durable), the assignment would directly contradict Kargar, the window-dressing natural experiment, and
  Tomunen's substitutability finding - so the *classification* does not survive reversal, meaning it is
  doing real, evidence-anchored work. This is the section's strength, separate from the finding built on
  it in §3.
- **Evidence status.** Kargar and Gospodinov-Robotti are VERIFIED-IN-VAULT with primary-text figures
  confirmed this session (`_sources.md` §2.7). Zeng's 92% fit is explicitly demoted to corroborating and
  must not be treated as independent confirmation - a discipline the plan states but drafting will need to
  enforce line by line. `carry-is-not-one-premium`'s own promoted-then-demoted footnote (several rows may
  load on one constraint) must stay demoted per the synthesis's §3.1 verdict, not silently re-promoted.
- **Proposed word count.** 700, with an explicit decision at drafting time about where ILS's verified
  material actually lands (see §6 of this document).

### §3. The durable behaviours do not pay this seat

- **Core argument.** The D5 finding: rule-compelled behaviours are durable but mostly do not pay this
  seat (window dressing pays a funding rate, mandate rebalancing pays the responder); circumstance-
  compelled behaviours do pay this seat but are substitutable and decaying, which is why fair pricing is
  the designed equilibrium rather than a disappointment.
- **Weakest point.** This is D5 itself. See §1 of this document. The section's central move - two clean
  exclusions - does not survive contact with the corpus's own window-dressing note or its own
  fallen-angel example.
- **Reversal test.** Fails its own reversal in the sense that matters most: if "durable behaviours mostly
  DO pay this seat" were true instead, D3's claim that the seat's occupants are drawn from the
  substitutable class and fair pricing is therefore the designed equilibrium collapses, because a durable
  occupant would earn a non-compressing rent. This section is maximally load-bearing for D3 and, per §1
  above, maximally exposed.
- **Evidence status.** The window-dressing evidence is VERIFIED-IN-VAULT for the mechanism but the
  paper-specific figures (12.5%/25% contraction) are still formally NEEDS-VENUE-CHECK per `_sources.md`
  §2.1 - the sample period is confirmed only against the 2023 working-paper predecessor, not against the
  final paywalled *JFI* text. If §3 leans on window dressing as its flagship durable-but-excluded example
  (which the counterexample analysis above suggests it should, to be honest about the tension), that
  citation gap becomes load-bearing rather than a footnote-level flag.
- **Proposed word count.** 1,100 - larger than its current implied share, because doing this honestly
  (stating the finding as a majority claim with named exceptions, per §1's recommended fix) takes more
  words than the clean dichotomy currently drafted, not fewer.

### §4. Fair pricing is the equilibrium, so the seat is a labelled allocation

- **Core argument.** The seat does not require an above-fair price; being paid the market price for
  crash-risk bearing delivers both the ordinary-market return and the loss placement, so a compressed
  premium is a beta allocation and must be labelled and sized as one.
- **Weakest point.** The "why the seat rather than more responder plus cash" question the team lead poses
  directly. The plan asserts the negative skew is "the only thing the seat sells that the convex pole
  cannot supply itself," but never tests this against the cheapest alternative. Cash trivially satisfies
  "positive return, uncorrelated with the convex pole's failure" (clause a and b) at essentially zero tail
  risk; it fails only because it earns no *excess* return over the risk-free rate, which is not what
  either D4 clause states. The real argument - that excess return over cash, absent an actively-shaped
  payoff (which the crash-share literature says destroys most of the income, §4.1 of the synthesis), comes
  packaged with negative skew - is implicit in the corpus (Firoozye-Koshiyama's theorem) but is never
  stated as the answer to "why not just cash." Nor is the case made against generic market beta, which
  also carries negative skew but (unlike the claimed FX/short-vol occupancy) correlates with trend's own
  failure states rather than diverging from them - the comparison the paper needs is empirical
  (differential crash correlation) and explicitly belongs to the ΔΘ̂ test D4 has not run.
- **Reversal test.** If the seat required alpha to justify itself, the argument would need Burnside et
  al. resolved in the paper's favour, which it explicitly declines to claim. The section is built to
  survive this reversal by design (sufficiency, not necessity) - but see weak point 3 in §2 of this
  document: the survival may be narrower than claimed, because the peso-problem issue threatens more than
  the alpha-versus-fair question specifically.
- **Evidence status.** [[the-payer-did-not-leave-the-supply-arrived]] is the backbone here and is well
  sourced (four independent sources for the supply mechanism, though the paper must state it as "the only
  candidate found with the right sign," not as established, per the note's own caution and its
  Australia-flattens-hardest counter-instance). Terstegge must remain corroborating only, never
  load-bearing, per the explicit flag carried through `_sources.md` §2.4 and `_synthesis.md` prohibition
  15.
- **Proposed word count.** 800.

### §5. The skew is the inventory, and only the book can justify it

- **Core argument.** Every construction lever that removes negative skew also removes most of the seat's
  distinguishing value; the dial only turns one way (Firoozye-Koshiyama's theorem), so the inventory
  cannot be defended standalone and must be defended at portfolio level.
- **Weakest point.** The section rests almost entirely on the strongest fold in the corpus (`the-skew-is-
  the-product`) and is well evidenced, but its generalization from FX (the one venue with reliably
  negative skew, per D1) to "the inventory" as a general category risks smuggling back the single-venue
  crash-share problem D1 was written to fence. The section needs to keep restating that skew sign is only
  reliably negative in FX and reliably positive in equity carry, with rates and commodities
  construction-dependent - otherwise a referee reads "the skew is the inventory" as a universal claim the
  corpus explicitly forbids (`_synthesis.md` prohibition 25).
- **Reversal test.** If positive skew were the target instead, the section becomes close to vacuous by
  construction (short convexity is defined as negative skew), so the interesting reversal is not
  sign-flipping but testing whether skew-shedding constructions genuinely destroy value versus merely
  improving a gameable metric - which the corpus does test (Barroso-Santa-Clara, Moreira-Muir) and the
  section correctly uses. Passes.
- **Evidence status.** Strong. Firoozye-Koshiyama, Barroso-Santa-Clara, and Dupuy are CITED-CONSISTENT or
  VERIFIED-IN-VAULT. Almeida and the Man Group rebalancing-band cluster remain single-source or
  practitioner-COI and must keep their flags (master's thesis; Man Group affiliation on four of the
  section's rebalancing sources).
- **Proposed word count.** 900.

### §6. Income versus crash: the separation is mandated, not merely available

- **Core argument.** D2: the income pole must not sell the deep crash, because the Target tier already
  bought it, so the inventory's admissible range is bounded above the Target's wing.
- **Weakest point.** See §2 of this document, item 2. The mandate is conditional on the Target existing
  and being sized to cover the fast segment; absent that, the "must not" collapses to "need not," and the
  paper's claim that this is "a result... derived from the roster, not observed in data" overstates a
  claim that is actually derived from *one specific roster* the paper does not own or defend.
- **Reversal test.** If the Target tier were absent, does §6's conclusion still hold? No - this is the
  clearest failure of any reversal test in the ten sections, because the mechanism explicitly requires the
  other tier's existence to generate the collision that forces the boundary. A claim that fails this
  cleanly under reversal is a dependency, and the section should say so in its own text rather than only
  in the discussion column of a synthesis document.
- **Evidence status.** Derived entirely from ① §4.2 and §5.4 (cite-only, correctly not re-argued). The
  strike/wing language ("above -20%", "the wing") risks reading as instrument-specific rather than
  behavioural - a live risk given the user has twice rejected implementation-specific framings - and needs
  to stay illustrative (a footnote pointing to the occupant's own analysis) rather than load-bearing in
  the main line, which the plan states as its intent ("assert the rule, not the constant") but does not
  yet show executed.
- **Proposed word count.** 1,000, explicitly including a stated dependency clause rather than a bare
  assertion of mandate.

### §7. The falsifying case: right loss placement, wrong income source

- **Core argument.** Short-horizon reversal has the right shape (measured negative skew, the right payer
  story per Nagel) and still fails the seat, because the payment is statistical rather than contractual - a
  price taker captures the liquidity provider's risk without earning their income.
- **Weakest point.** The team lead's own framing is right to press here. [[income-must-accrue-not-be-
  captured]]'s language - "sign inversion, not a cost problem, so no drift-band width or turnover control
  can repair it" - is asserted rather than demonstrated as distinct from a cost failure. Mathematically, a
  "sign inversion" caused by paying the bid-ask spread on entry and exit is difficult to distinguish from
  "the gross edge is smaller than the round-trip cost," which is exactly a cost problem, unless the paper
  can show the taker's *gross* edge (before any transaction cost) is itself zero or negative because takers
  structurally never bear the adverse-selection risk the liquidity-provision premium compensates. The
  source note does not make that structural argument; it reports negative net drift with cost-shaped
  statistics (terciles, an implied edge in basis points against a cost wall) and asserts the sign-inversion
  reading. If a referee presses this, the paper's claim that reversal "proves the definition has teeth"
  weakens to "reversal fails, and we believe but have not shown the failure is structural rather than a
  cost artefact of this desk's execution."
- **Reversal test.** If reversal had instead been killed purely on cost grounds (turnover control repairs
  it at a lower cost floor), does §7's claim survive? No - that is exactly the ambiguity above. The section
  survives only if the structural reading is correct, and the paper's current sourcing does not clearly
  establish which reading is correct.
- **Evidence status.** Nagel is CITED-CONSISTENT and is the payer-story anchor. The specific in-house kill
  (2026-06-13, chop/trend terciles) is explicitly flagged in `_synthesis.md` prohibition 2 as "our own
  prior runs on a specific universe and window, usable as demonstrations of a mechanism and not as evidence
  for this paper's general claims" - the plan must not let §7 lean on it as more than an illustration. The
  Robeco reversal-survives-costs cluster is COI-concentrated (three papers, one practitioner evidence
  cluster per the article's own self-correction) and correctly demoted.
- **Proposed word count.** 700, with the sign-inversion-versus-cost distinction argued explicitly rather
  than asserted by adjective.

### §8. Ranking the seat: the paired-book test, pre-registered with three verdicts

- **Core argument.** ΔΘ̂ prices joint-loss states smoothly via convex utility on the blended book, but
  cannot by itself identify whether a crash state is even in-sample or attribute a joint loss to the right
  segment, so ranking requires segment-conditioned reporting alongside the composite score.
- **Weakest point.** The metric's baseline excludes the Target (`_synthesis.md` §3.5(c)), which means a
  fast-spike joint loss is priced against a book with no fast defence, overstating the penalty relative to
  the actual roster - a finding about the paper's own metric configuration that the plan correctly owns as
  a fix rather than a defect, but which is easy for drafting to lose given how technical it is. If it drops
  out, §8's ranking numbers (whenever they exist) will misprice exactly the segment §6 spent its whole
  argument establishing as the Target's job, not the seat's.
- **Reversal test.** If pre-registration were absent and verdicts were assigned after seeing results, the
  27-cell re-rank precedent shows exactly what happens: a clean negative result gets read as "no partner
  found" rather than any of the three stated verdicts, because nothing forced the choice in advance. This
  section does not survive its own reversal, which is the right outcome - it means the pre-registration
  requirement is doing real work rather than ceremony.
- **Evidence status.** The manipulation-proof measure (Goetzmann-Ingersoll-Spiegel-Welch) and Tasche's
  Euler-allocation formalism are CITED-CONSISTENT, but the claim that "ΔΘ̂ is the Tasche marginal
  contribution" is a **category error** - Tasche's Proposition 2.2 requires homogeneity of degree 1 and Θ
  is not homogeneous, so only the arithmetic form transfers and none of the Euler/RORAC guarantees do. The
  source article has since been corrected (`aegis-rd-v1k7`, commit `110507ad`, which added the scope
  condition as a footnote plus two limitations); the paper must not reinherit the stronger claim.
- **Correction, applied after the Stage 2.5 gate.** This entry previously also asserted that the metric's
  "scale convention was found to be misdescribed and sign-flipping under a vol-matched convention." **That
  is withdrawn: it was a live concern that has since been resolved against the argument.** `aegis-rd-600y`
  is closed **WONTFIX** against the governing spec: the floating-volatility convention is the
  specification, not a defect, because
  [[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]] states that each leg is pinned to book vol before
  blending and the blend's volatility then floats with the correlation structure, **which is the
  diversification signal**. A controlled measurement on the matched-marginal fixture scores the floating
  convention 12/12 correct placement decisions at every drift tested, against 9/12 vol-matched at the live
  sleeve's own +4.22 percent per year. The convention that produced both the 27-cell precedent and the
  2026-07-25 loader result is the correct one. The stale warning is struck rather than softened.
- **Proposed word count.** 900, and see `_plan.md` D8: §8 is no longer a pure pre-registration, since it
  must report the measured instance and then pre-register only what would move it off *indeterminate*.

### §9. ①'s §4.4 commission, half-discharged and handed back

- **Core argument.** ① commissions two things: that each role holds on its own terms, and that the ordered
  roster beats an unordered equal-risk-contribution blend. The seat paper discharges the first fully and
  the second only at a restricted Floor-level (mandated weight versus equal-risk-contribution-implied
  weight), handing the full four-sleeve comparison back as a named cross-paper obligation.
- **Weakest point.** Genuinely the most honest section in the plan, not the weakest in the adversarial
  sense - but it is thin as a *section*. Its content is bookkeeping and scoping ("here is what we can and
  cannot claim, and here is who owns the rest"), which is exactly conclusion-shaped material, not an
  argument that needs its own word budget separate from §10.
- **Reversal test.** If the paper claimed to discharge both halves of the commission, would that be
  stronger? No - it would be a false claim the synthesis explicitly warns against ("it is tempting to read
  ΔΘ̂ as discharging both"). The section survives its own reversal precisely because the reversed version is
  worse, which confirms the current framing is correct but does not show it needs a dedicated section
  rather than a subsection.
- **Evidence status.** Entirely a scoping argument against ① §4.4 (cite-only, correctly not re-derived). No
  independent citations carry this section; it is pure logical bookkeeping over what $\Delta\hat\Theta$ can

## 6. What the paper should drop

The user has twice rejected survey drift. Two concrete cuts, not a general trim:

**Fold §9 into the conclusion.** Its content - state the commission, discharge what can honestly be
discharged, hand the rest back with the comparator named - is forward-looking scoping, which is what a
conclusion does. Giving it a standalone section number implies it carries independent argumentative weight
it does not have; the per-section stress test above found no citation or claim in §9 that is not either
already established in §8 (what ΔΘ̂ can and cannot see) or purely a statement of future obligation. Cost of
cutting: none identified. This also directly reduces the section count from ten toward the user's
repeatedly stated preference for fewer, and closer to ①'s six.

**Resolve, before drafting, where ILS's verified material lives - and if it stays compressed to "an
instance" in §2, drop the parts that no longer earn their keep.** The Stage 1 synthesis and the dedicated
buildability note did substantial, carefully verified work: the orthogonality-to-conditional demotion, the
Tomunen compression-not-extinction reading, the ESMA wall, the crowding dynamic, the failure-state
attribution in §3.5(a-bis) that the team lead separately flagged as the sharpest test of the ranking
discipline. Under the D5 rebuild, none of this has an explicit section home. Two honest options, not both:
either give the compression evidence its promised weight inside §4 (it is the corpus's second-best example
of constrained-capacity compression after Dew-Becker-Giglio, and belongs where fair-pricing-as-equilibrium
is argued) and let §2 mention ILS only as a one-paragraph taxonomy instance, or accept that ILS is now
minor to this paper's argument and cut the CATB/vehicle-level detail, the crowding-dynamic magnitudes, and
the ESMA regulatory-timeline material down to a single sentence with a citation to the buildability note,
since none of that detail serves a *behavioural* argument once ILS is no longer a load-bearing section.
The current plan does neither - it demotes the section without stating which of these two paths the
material takes, which is exactly how survey drift re-enters: material with no assigned home tends to get
kept in full "just in case" rather than cut on purpose.

**What should not be cut**, despite being the section this stress test attacks hardest: §3 (D5's finding).
Cutting it would remove the paper's central contribution rather than fix its weakest point. The fix is to
argue it more carefully and at greater length (see §3 and §5 above), not to shorten it.
