# The Orthogonal Non-Equity Trend Universe

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> Breadth and convexity are answers to different questions, and this book conflated them. Adding orthogonal non-equity markets is a **Sharpe** lever - the information ratio climbs on $\sqrt{\text{independent bets}}$ and flattens somewhere between 20 and 50 futures markets - but the *convex shape* the floor seat is hired for is beta-timing on a handful of large macro betas, not breadth, so the campaign's own convexity-ranked champion correctly collapsed to three names. The marginal breadth that does pay lives almost entirely in **commodities** (the financial axes saturate at one bet per family), one liquid instrument captures a whole family because asset-class trend is a latent factor, and on a EUR-5k UCITS book the fee floor and ETC borrow cap the *economic* count well below the futures ceiling. This note is the per-axis map: one bare orthogonal instrument per macro family, tagged by whether it adds **convexity** (a candidate to graft onto the atalanta floor) or only **Sharpe/breadth** (a challenger universe, not the floor).

## Two questions wearing one word

"How many markets should the trend book trade?" is really two questions that the breadth literature keeps welding together. One is a **Sharpe** question: how many *independent bets* can the universe carry before the risk-adjusted return stops climbing? The other is a **convexity** question: where does the long-gamma, positive-skew payoff the floor seat is hired for actually come from? They have different answers, and the campaign has now hit both from opposite directions.

Grinold's law sets risk-adjusted skill at $IR \approx IC \times \sqrt{BR}$, where breadth is the number of *independent* bets, not the nominal asset count.[^grinold] For a single-signal trend program the information coefficient is roughly fixed by the signal, so breadth is the lever, and the universe is where you pull it. That is the Sharpe question, and the empirical answer is well-mapped (below). But the seat's ranker is not Sharpe - it is `trend_convexity_payoff`, an intrinsic own-horizon skew ([[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]) - and against *that* objective the 2026-06 champion arc ran breadth **backwards**: it dropped equity (a convexity drag, drop-one-name +0.052), then dropped everything but the three orthogonal crisis engines, lifting held-out convexity from -0.04/-0.23 to +0.447 on a **three-name** book (`IDTL` US long duration, `IGLN` gold, `ICOM` broad commodity). Equity-heavy breadth realized *short* gamma under a trend label; the minimal orthogonal-macro set realized the long-gamma shape. The reconciliation is Fidelity's decomposition: ~76% of a trend book's risk and most of its crisis alpha is *beta-timing* a few large macro betas, only ~24% is cross-sectional market selection ([[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]).[^fidelity] Breadth improves the Sharpe and the robustness of the sleeve; it is not the source of the convex shape. So the two questions pull apart, and the universe design has to serve them separately.

## The Sharpe lever and where it saturates

The breadth-adds-Sharpe claim is real and quantified, and the number is a plateau, not a line. Graham's long-running exercise (Tricker and Mitchell, refreshed May 2026) adds markets to a generic (20,120) trend model in order of liquidity and plots IR against count: the IR climbs steeply at first, **flattens between roughly 20 and 50 markets**, and past ~50 the additions "do not capture risk factors that are not already contained in the portfolio."[^graham] Their earlier note put the diminishing-returns knee at 40-60 markets and built a Market Diversification Index - the effective number of *independent* macro markets - that routinely collapses below 4 in crises and shifted structurally lower after 2008.[^graham] The mechanism is Grinold's, read through the effective-bet count: under equicorrelation a book of $N$ assets at pairwise correlation $\rho$ carries $N_{\text{eff}} = N / (1 + (N-1)\rho)$ independent bets, so at an equity-book $\rho \approx 0.85$ fifty tickers are ~1.2 bets, while at a non-equity $\rho \approx 0.20$ they are ~4.6.[^resolve] Measured on a real futures book, ReSolve finds 10.3 independent bets under inverse-vol weighting, rising to 16-22 under optimization - a Sharpe multiplier of ~1.5-1.9 over the naive book.[^resolve] And the best trend *years* run on even fewer axes: Quantica needed only 8 statistical factors to replicate the industry's 2022 return (its best year), against 33 in 2023 (its worst), an inverse-linear relationship between factor count and performance.[^quantica24q1] So the honest target is a low-teens effective-bet count, occupied once each - not a fifty-name sprawl whose marginal names are correlation, cost, and turnover.

## The marginal breadth is all in commodities

The decisive refinement - and the one that reshapes the per-axis budget - is *where* in a non-equity book the marginal bet still pays. It is not the financial axes. When Graham splits the additive markets, "the commodities have diversifying potential, the financial markets do not": beyond the essentials, adding rate and FX markets barely moves the IR while adding commodities keeps lifting it.[^graham] Quantica quantifies the same asymmetry inside commodities: the ten most-liquid commodity futures carry an average trend-return correlation of 0.30, but across the full 69 that average falls to **0.06**, taking the diversification multiplier from 1.65 to 3.6 - and crucially the *less-liquid* single commodities are what carry it, with no evidence that they trend better, only that they trend *less correlated*.[^quantica25q1] Auspice makes the intuition concrete: sugar, crude and milk are far less correlated to each other than the S&P, ACWI and Euro Stoxx are, so fifty commodity markets buy more independent bets than a hundred financial ones.[^auspice]

The engine underneath is a quadratic. In *trend* space, correlation is roughly the square of the underlying correlation: two assets that co-move at $\rho$ produce trend streams that co-move at ~$\rho^2$, because when one rises and the other falls you are long one and short the other and the P&Ls align.[^gresham] A 0.4 underlying correlation is a 0.16 trend correlation - which is why a trend book diversifies better than an equity book of the same count, and why *selecting low-underlying-correlation legs (commodities) has a quadratic payoff* in breadth. The same algebra warns that no genuinely negative-correlation market exists in trend space (the sign is squared away), so breadth caps out: past the orthogonal core, added names are near-duplicates that concentrate the book on the "macro trend" factor rather than diversifying it - many markets, one bet.[^gresham] The construction rule that falls out is asymmetric: spend exactly **one** bare name on each financial family (they saturate at ~one effective bet), and spend the remaining budget on **single commodities**, where the marginal orthogonality actually lives.

## One bare instrument per axis is enough

Why one name per financial family, not several? Because asset-class trend is a *latent factor*. Carver's point from replication work is that trend-following a broad index captures most of the P&L you would get from trend-following its constituents, so a single liquid representative of a macro family delivers most of that family's trend return.[^carver] It is also how the ETF industry already builds these products: the standard managed-futures replication set is ~20 contracts, five per sector (equity, fixed income, FX, commodity).[^kaminski] The families themselves are low-dimensional - a rate curve is level-plus-slope (the level factor alone is ~80% of curve variance), the FX complex is dominated by a single dollar factor (~60-80%), and even the commodity complex reduces to an energy factor and a metals factor at the top - so a second long-Treasury line or a second G10 cross is a near-duplicate in trend space, not a new axis.[^pca] This is the licence for the minimal design: the book is a set of *axes occupied once*, and depth within an axis is redundancy, not breadth.

## The wrapper transfers the math but taxes it

The breadth arithmetic is wrapper-agnostic - $\sqrt{BR}$ does not care whether a bet is a future or an ETF - but three taxes make a EUR-5k UCITS book saturate *lower* than the futures ceiling. First, replication tax: managed-futures ETFs capture only about a quarter of the trend-divergence signal that the futures indices capture (roughly 50%+), delivering "less trend beta and less crisis alpha" from slower systems and *selective, smaller* market exposure.[^rpm] Second, the fee floor: at small AUM the frictional cost of each added name dominates its diversification benefit, and the optimal count collapses to a handful - Scherer's framework puts a low-single-digit optimum on a small, cost-sensitive account, exactly the regime of a EUR-5k book paying a EUR 1.25 per-order floor ([[trend-following-under-a-fixed-per-order-fee]]).[^scherer] Third, the borrow constraint: single-commodity UCITS lines are **ETCs** (debt securities), buyable long but carrying little-to-no short inventory - the instrument-level cause of the campaign's short-side kill (held-out 0.62 long/short -> 0.89 long-only, aegis-rd-167; [[graveyard]]), so the commodity breadth is available only as *long* legs. The US-listed ETF proxies that map these axes cleanly are worse than un-shortable: a EUR-retail account cannot even buy them (PRIIPs/KID rejection, IBKR error 201, verified live), so they are evidence about the axes, never a tradeable book. The consequence is that the UCITS book's economic saturation sits below the futures 20-50 - the binding constraint is the fee floor and the ETC borrow, not the axis count.

## The per-axis map

Each macro family gets one bare, orthogonal, IBKR-tradeable UCITS line, tagged by what it adds. **[convex]** = a scarce/haven asset or a beta-timing crisis axis whose *own* shape is long-gamma, a candidate to graft onto the atalanta floor. **[breadth]** = genuinely orthogonal but a Sharpe/robustness lever whose standalone shape is not convex, and which *dilutes* a convexity-ranked book - the material of a Sharpe-ranked challenger, not the floor. Three names (`IDTL`, `IGLN`, `ICOM`) are the live champion and are gateway-verified; the rest are the LSE UCITS/ETP line for the axis and need the same what-if probe the three passed before any run.

| Macro axis | Bare UCITS line | What makes it orthogonal | Adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| US long duration | `IDTL.LSEETF` (held) | Real-yield / flight-to-safety; the deflation-crisis leg | **[convex]** beta-timing crisis alpha, scarce-asset haven |
| EUR long duration (Bund bloc) | iShares € Govt 15-30y | Distinct ECB policy cycle; Bund is the *institutional*-crisis haven where UST is the *economic* one | **[convex]** second orthogonal duration bloc, haven |
| Gold | `IGLN.LSEETF` (held) | Monetary scarce asset, no cash flow; geopolitical-crisis haven | **[convex]** the campaign's strongest single engine (drop-one +0.105) |
| The dollar | WisdomTree long-USD ETP | The FX *level* factor (DOL, ~60-80% of FX variance); strengthens in economic/institutional stress | **[convex]** scarce-asset haven, beta-timing |
| Funding-haven FX (JPY) | `SJPY` long-JPY ETP | Carry-unwind risk-off leg, distinct from the dollar | **[convex]** risk-off convexity uncorrelated to USD |
| Real rates (US TIPS) | `ITPS.LSEETF` | Inflation-vs-nominal axis; decouples from nominal only on an inflation shock | **[weak-convex]** inflation-shock hedge, but co-crashes with nominal on a growth shock |
| Broad commodity | `ICOM.LSEETF` (held) | Reflation / growth-inflation basket, optimized roll | **[convex-ish]** held as an inflation-crisis engine (drop-one +0.119); straddles the tag |
| Energy (crude/Brent) | `BRNT` ETC | Supply-shock axis; asymmetric spike in energy-driven inflation (2022) | **[breadth]** orthogonal supply risk, weak crisis convexity |
| Natural gas | `NGAS` ETC | Weather / local storage; ~0 trend-correlation to crude - the marginal-breadth carrier | **[breadth]** highest orthogonality per name (Quantica: gas/power ~40% of alt-market edge) |
| Industrial metals (copper) | `COPA` ETC | Global-growth cycle metal | **[breadth]** pro-cyclical, co-crashes - Sharpe not convexity |
| Agriculture (grains/softs) | `AIGA` / `WEAT` ETC | Weather-driven, macro-orthogonal supply shocks | **[breadth]** genuinely uncorrelated to G10 macro, low crisis convexity |
| Carbon (EUA) | `CARB` ETC | Cap-and-trade policy; correlation ~0 to equities, bonds *and* commodities | **[breadth]** cleanest orthogonality, but a weak crisis hedge and thin (~EUR 50M) |

The financial families each take one line; the commodity block is where the count can grow toward the ~20 cap by splitting into its non-redundant single names (a second precious metal, a second grain, gasoline alongside crude), because that is the only region where the marginal name still lowers the average correlation. Silver is the cautionary case: as a second "precious" it looks like a second gold, but its industrial-demand load means it does *not* inherit gold's clean haven convexity,[^pca] so it is breadth, not a convex engine.

## What actually gets added to the atalanta floor

The floor seat is ranked on convexity, so the map has a sharp reading: the champion already holds the three strongest convex engines (`IDTL`, `IGLN`, `ICOM`), and the honest expansion is **only the other [convex] axes**, not the breadth block. Three candidates are convex *as assets* and absent from the current book:

- **EUR long duration (Bund bloc)** - a second orthogonal duration engine on a different central-bank cycle, and the institutional-crisis haven that US Treasuries are not.
- **The dollar** - the FX level factor and a scarce-asset haven that strengthens in exactly the economic and institutional stresses where gold and duration can lag.
- **A JPY funding haven** - a risk-off leg whose convexity is uncorrelated to the dollar's.

The pre-registered test is a drop-one-*in* on held-out `trend_convexity_payoff`: each candidate earns its slot only if it *lifts* the champion's convexity (or holds it while cutting drawdown), the same bar `IDTL`/`IGLN`/`ICOM` cleared. The breadth block (energy, gas, metals beyond gold, ags, carbon) is explicitly **not** for the floor - on a convexity ranking it dilutes the shape, exactly as the 2026-06-13 saturation run showed (own-gamma trend quality peaked at ~20 names then *degraded* through the refinement tiers, and realized corr(book move, SPY move) rose 0.50 -> 0.58 as credit/EM/silver/copper were added). Those axes are the universe for a *Sharpe*-ranked challenger - a separate sleeve, budgeted separately ([[budgeting-the-divergent-seat]]) - where the √BR lever is the right objective and the commodity breadth pays.

## Limitations

- **The two objectives can conflict in sizing.** Grafting a convex axis that also raises correlation to a held leg can lift standalone convexity yet cut the *book's* effective breadth; the drop-one-in test measures combined convexity, not each axis in isolation, so a candidate that helps alone can still be redundant in the book.
- **The effective-bet count is regime-dependent and rises toward one in crises.** Static full-sample $\rho$ overstates the breadth available in stress - exactly when the payoff is wanted - so the realized plateau sits lower than the calm-market estimate.[^graham]
- **The commodity breadth is long-only on this substrate.** Single-commodity UCITS are ETCs with no borrow, so the Sharpe-challenger universe cannot run the short side that carries part of trend's skew; the futures wrapper that reclaims it (a per-root what-if sweep found 30 of 31 macro roots short-tradeable on the account) is blocked on a clean back-adjusted continuous-contract panel and is a separate build, not this book.
- **The dollar and JPY legs are FX-ETP proxies.** The bare dollar line is a WisdomTree long-USD ETP (a basket or single-cross approximation), not a true DXY future; its roll, tracking and USD denomination separate it from the futures axis, and the EUR-investor currency overlay is a distinct question from the instrument test ([[the-multi-asset-substrate-for-the-roster]]).
- **Carbon is orthogonal but marginal.** EUA correlation to equities, bonds and commodities sits near zero and its intra-family correlation to California allowances is only ~0.23,[^carbon] but its crisis-hedge effectiveness is below precious metals and agriculture, its own return is low, and the UCITS line is thin - a clean Sharpe diversifier, not a convex engine.
- **The per-axis PCA structure is a literature estimate, not a decomposition on our data.** The level/slope, dollar-factor and energy/metals figures come from published factor studies; the realized correlation structure of *this* UCITS book at *this* horizon is what the drop-one tests measure.[^pca]
- **The UCITS remap has a measured anchor but the expanded book does not.** An 18-name single-calendar London remap of the working core produced held-out Sharpe +0.126 with zero mismatch warnings, and a Tier-1 A/B settled two slots (`IEGE` + `NGAS` beat `IBGE` + `LEUR`, +0.571 vs +0.385 held-out Sharpe); the convex-axis expansions above are candidates to test, not measured results.
