# The UCITS-Constrained Carry Sleeve

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> Under a long-only, no-derivatives, EU-retail constraint the carry pole's credible upgrades sort into two kinds: legs that deepen income-per-tail while *keeping* the pole's negative skew (a short-maturity tilt inside HY; an EM local-currency leg as the only investable FX-carry skew source), and tilts that quietly dissolve the pole's identity (a trend gate flips its skew convex; a fallen-angel tilt smuggles back the rates duration the sleeve exists to exclude). The quality edge inside HY is largely the fallen-angel technical wearing a different label - and since no BB-only USD HY UCITS fund exists, it is bought through maturity-bucket index choice or not at all.
>
> **Correction (2026-07-03, same day)**: the defensive tilt passed every standalone test and still failed the pole - measured in the floor composite it had cut the book's duration-times-spread and with it the crash-beta the pole is hired for (qskew -1.83 to -0.49, corr-to-trend flipped positive; [[runs/floor/2026-07-03]]). The mechanism and the fix (pin DTS, gate on the composite) are in [[the-skew-is-the-product]]; the investable levers that carry undilutable crash-rent are in the addendum below.

The concave income pole has a metric article ([[what-makes-a-carry-sleeve-an-income-engine]]) and a sourcing article ([[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]); this one is about *construction* - which levers strengthen a sleeve that must stay long-only, physically-replicated, and UCITS-wrapped, the constraint set that already forced the tail tier's redesign in [[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]]. The governing rule carries over from the roster ([[the-tiered-strategy-roster]]): the pole is hired for negative quarterly skew, near-zero rates beta (orthogonality to the trend book's duration), and calm income. Any lever is judged twice - does it add income per unit of budgeted tail, and does it leave the pole's identity intact? Several popular levers pass the first test and fail the second, which is the finding this article exists to record.

## The defensive tilt is a maturity bucket, not a rating screen

The cleanest crisis evidence for defensive construction inside HY is the 2022 sell-off decomposed by maturity: issues with 0-5 year maturities returned -5.65% while 5-8 year returned -12.62% and >8 year -18.35%, against -11.10% for the broad index - and CCC-rated paper underperformed at -15.63% with the top ten worst issuers alone accounting for ~40% of the rating bucket's loss.[^ct22] The year was rate-driven, which is exactly the point for us: short-maturity HY is the long-only approximation of the duration strip, cutting the rate factor without derivatives. The pull-to-par mechanism (default risk decays as maturity approaches, and HY spread curves are often too flat to pay for extension) is the practitioners' account, and a European short-maturity implementation outperformed its broad benchmark in the 2022 drawdown (-4.3% vs -6.0%) while roughly matching it in the 2023 recovery.[^ctmat] The caveat the vendors state less loudly: the tilt gives up the recovery convexity - the weakest issuers bounce hardest in the rally phase, so the defensive book lags exactly when the pole's losses are being repaired. That is an acceptable trade for a sleeve whose job is budgeted bleed, but it must be priced on our own window, not taken from marketing decks whose "X% of return at Y% of drawdown" figures visibly drift year to year.

On the rating axis, the underlying anomaly is real but its interpretation is contested. Verdad's long-sample decomposition (developed-market corporates ex-financials, 1997-2019) finds risk-adjusted return *peaks at BB* and declines monotonically through B and CCC - "fool's yield": beyond a threshold, default losses overtake the coupon and realized return falls below the yield at purchase.[^verdad] Their later work sharpens the mechanism: B and CCC price returns are persistently negative and never reclaim pre-drawdown levels, so the quoted yield systematically overstates the achievable return in a way BB yield does not.[^verdad2] But an index-level decomposition attributed to Bloomberg's research group reportedly finds ~90% of the BB bucket's edge over adjacent buckets is the fallen-angel and rising-star *technical* (forced flows around the IG/HY boundary), not an independent quality premium - a claim we could not verify to a primary document and hold as provisional (see Limitations). The practical conclusion does not depend on resolving it: **no BB-only or quality-screened USD HY UCITS ETF exists** (the BB-only fund is US-domiciled '40 Act; the quality-tiered UCITS funds are EUR-denominated), so the only investable expression of "defensive inside HY" is the short-maturity bucket - iShares SDHY (iBoxx USD Liquid HY 0-5 Capped, since 2013) or PIMCO STHY (ICE BofA US HY Constrained 0-5yr, since 2012), both LSE-listed.

## Fallen angels: a real premium the mandate cannot hold

The fallen-angel reversal is among the better-documented technicals in credit: downgraded-to-HY bonds underperform matched HY around the downgrade (forced selling by capital-charged insurers and IG-mandated funds) and then reverse for up to two years, with the recovery proportional to the initial underperformance.[^bendor] The forced-selling mechanism is confirmed by independent insurer-level work, which also bounds it - the abnormal-return gap between constrained and unconstrained holders is modest and fully reverts within months, a bounded mean-reversion, not a crash premium.[^ache] And the premium has *not* decayed at the index level: FTSE Russell's post-Covid sequel finds fallen angels carrying a superior Sharpe ratio to both IG and HY since 2020, while stating plainly that "the cliff-edge effect of the formal downgrade from IG to HY is diminishing" post-Dodd-Frank as downgrades are pre-priced and mandates get flexible - the index-level premium persists even as its textbook mechanism thins, and the fallen-angel share of US HY has meanwhile fallen to a 23-year low near 3%.[^ftse25]

So why reject the tilt? Because the vehicle fails the identity test. Fallen angels are downgraded *investment-grade* issuance - long original maturities - so the FA index runs roughly 6.7-7 years of duration against ~3-4 for broad HY. A long-only UCITS wrapper has no way to strip it: the rate-hedged wrapper exists for IG (LQDH) but not for HY or FA, and the whole point of the duration-stripped pole (`carry.yaml`'s redesign) was to kill the rates factor that overlaps the trend book. A fallen-angel leg would re-import, unhedged, the exact exposure the sleeve was rebuilt to exclude - at 2016-inception instrument history (iShares WING) that barely predates our window. The premium is real; the mandate cannot hold the vehicle. A short-maturity fallen-angel index would resolve the conflict, and none was found to exist.

## The trend gate would un-hire the pole

Tactical credit - gating the HY leg to cash or Treasuries on a moving-average or momentum signal - has genuine peer-reviewed support: a simple MA overlay on OAS-sorted HY and IG portfolios produces abnormal returns that survive transaction costs and an Asness-Moskowitz-Pedersen factor control, robust across formation windows.[^br18] It also has an unusually honest independent decomposition: across ~1,800 specifications, the timing edge concentrates almost entirely in 2000-03 and 2008-09 - strip those two windows and annualized return falls from 2.9% to 1.6%, of which roughly half is just carry differential harvesting, leading the authors to doubt there is "sufficient juice to squeeze" net of costs outside systemic crises.[^hoffstein]

But the disqualifying evidence is about shape, not returns. De-risking into declines is mechanically a skew-raising operation: the same construction applied to momentum cuts the strategy's maximum drawdown from -97% to -45% and roughly doubles Sharpe precisely by truncating the left tail,[^bsc] and the momentum-crash literature frames such risk management as converting a negatively-skewed stream into a neutral-to-positive one.[^dm] The trend article reached the identical conclusion from the opposite direction - trend-following's positive skew is *manufactured by* exactly this respond-to-price mechanism ([[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]). Layer a trend gate on the carry pole and the book's two poles converge on the same shape: the concave income anchor becomes a weak convex hybrid, the floor's skew budget loses its short leg, and the roster owns two long-gamma sleeves again - the construction error [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] opens by warning against. No source directly measures trend-gated HY skew against buy-and-hold HY (a literature gap), but the mechanism evidence is convergent and includes top-journal work, so the prior is strong: **a timing mute belongs in a separate, convex-budgeted sleeve, never inside this one.** This also supersedes the vol-regime-mute hypothesis pre-registered in [[what-makes-a-carry-sleeve-an-income-engine]] - the FX evidence for regime avoidance is real, but on this pole the cure amputates the payoff shape the sleeve is hired for.

## EM local currency: the investable FX-carry skew

The sourcing article's central unfinished business is that carry's crash skew lives in *currencies*, which a no-derivatives retail book cannot hold directly. EM local-currency sovereign bonds are the loophole: BIS work shows local-currency sovereign yields and spreads move with the bilateral dollar exchange rate - the bond return genuinely embeds the FX risk premium rather than sitting beside it,[^bis474] with the post-2013 "risk-taking channel" strengthening the linkage (a stronger dollar tightens EM local financial conditions and widens local spreads together).[^bis775] The distributional signature is documented independently: EM local-currency bond returns carry high variance and *negative skewness* pronounced enough to measurably deter US investor participation - the crash asymmetry is real and priced, not an artifact of one episode.[^bw] Year-level index behavior confirms FX as the asymmetric driver: the GBI-EM's bad years are its currency years (2013, 2015, 2018), and the clean in-window illustration is the taper tantrum, where local-currency EM fell ~10% while developed HY fell low single digits. For a pole hired to be short-gamma, this is the right kind of ugly: a UCITS local-currency tracker (iShares IEML, since 2011) is a long-only, physically-replicated claim on the FX-carry premium *with its skew attached* - the first investable test of the sourcing article's "source the skew to FX" hypothesis.

Two disciplines on sizing it. First, its duration is EM local rates, not the DM duration atalanta rides - so it does not break rates-orthogonality in the sense that matters, but it is not duration-free either. Second, the diversification it adds is calm-market only: the definitive cross-asset carry study finds carry strategies' worst drawdowns cluster across asset classes in global recessions even though their unconditional correlations are low,[^kmpv] and EM sovereign risk has grown *more* entangled with the US credit factor since the GFC, not less.[^bis541] The leg should lift income and deepen skew at modest weight; it must not be sized as if it hedged the crash - in the states the skew budget prices, all the carry legs go down together, which is precisely what the pole is paid for.

## Addendum (2026-07-03): levers whose income cannot shed its skew

The floor re-verification ([[runs/floor/2026-07-03]]) showed the cash-bond levers above optimize the pole out of its role: every defensive improvement is a DTS cut in disguise ([[the-skew-is-the-product]]). A second instrument sweep therefore asked a different question - not "which cash-bond tilt pays best per unit of tail" but "which UCITS instruments pay income that is *structurally* crash-rent", so the skew cannot be tilted away without exiting the position. Four candidates survive verification, one gap is confirmed, and one old kill is reaffirmed.

**Pure credit spread, sold as protection: DBXM.** The Xtrackers II iTraxx Crossover Swap UCITS ETF (LU0290359032, TER 0.24%, launched 2007, ~50m AUM, synthetic/funded swap, Xetra-listed) holds the protection-*seller* side of the iTraxx Crossover 5y CDS index: its return is cash rate plus CDS running spread, minus spread-widening and defaults.[^dbxm] This is the duration-stripped credit-carry exposure the pole has been approximating with SDHY+LQDH, delivered directly - no rates leg to hedge, no maturity bucket to defensively tilt, income and crash-beta fused in one instrument at the exact BB-B belt where credit's short-put character concentrates ([[the-skew-is-the-product]]). Costs of admission: small AUM, synthetic counterparty structure (the rented-premium warning of [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]] applies in milder form - this one is unlevered and collateralized), an unconfirmed LSE listing, and 2020/2022 drawdown figures still unverified. The Tabula/Janus Henderson iTraxx Crossover ETFs that once competed here were liquidated in 2021-22;[^tabula] DBXM is the last pure-spread vehicle standing.

**Subordination as crash-rent: AT1s and corporate hybrids.** The Invesco AT1 Capital Bond UCITS ETF (EUR-hedged Dist class XAT1, IE00BFZPF439, TER 0.39%, ~440m AUM) buys the bank-capital layer that is contractually first to absorb stress - its March 2023 stress test is on the public record (a ~13% drawdown around the Credit Suisse AT1 write-down, with only ~2.9% direct CS exposure).[^at1] The income is high because the skew is real and event-shaped; no defensive tilt inside the wrapper can remove it. The Invesco Euro Corporate Hybrid Bond ETF (EHBD, IE00BKWD3966, TER 0.39%, EUR-native) is the same theme one notch milder in non-financial corporates.[^ehbd] Sizing discipline: AT1 events cluster with credit crises (that is the point), and the instrument is a single-sector bet - a modest satellite leg, sized off the pole's tail budget, never a core holding.

**The variance risk premium, finally investable: covered-call UCITS.** The sourcing article crowned VRP the deepest concave pole and left it uninvestable; that has changed. JPMorgan's equity-premium-income range (global JEGP, IE0003UVYC20, TER 0.35%, ~1.2bn AUM; US JEPI-UCITS since late 2024) and Global X's covered-call trackers (QYLD, IE00BM8R0J59, ~650m AUM; XYLU, IE0002L5QB31, both TER 0.45%) sell index calls against long equity books and distribute the premium monthly.[^ccall] No put-write UCITS ETF exists - confirmed gap - so covered calls are the only wrapper. The identity caveat is structural and must be priced before any test: these books are concave *in equities* - long equity minus a call, so they eat most of an equity crash net of a modest premium - which means their crash factor may overlap the states the trend pole already monetizes. Whether a covered-call leg deepens the pole's skew *without* raising corr-to-trend past the composite gate is exactly the kind of question that can only be answered in the composite, per [[the-skew-is-the-product]].

**Watchlist, not investable yet: cat bonds.** Europe's first cat-bond UCITS ETF (KRC CATB/C47B, IE000UWJUW87, TER 1.28%, launched Dec 2025) is the purest insurance-selling income in existence and the only leg here whose crash states are uncorrelated with financial crises - but it is ~$10m AUM, six months old, and under an explicit ESMA challenge to cat bonds' UCITS eligibility now sitting with the European Commission.[^catb] Re-visit if it survives the ruling and grows.

**Reaffirmed**: no rate-hedged HY UCITS and no BB-only USD HY UCITS exist (2026 re-check); dedicated FX-carry UCITS ETFs survive only as ~10m-AUM zombies from 2008 (Xtrackers DBX1AY), so EM local bonds remain the sole practical FX-carry claim.[^fxzombie]

## Limitations

- The short-duration-HY and CCC-avoidance evidence is practitioner-grade throughout - no peer-reviewed paper isolates either as a standalone anomaly, every vendor cited sells the product, and headline capture ratios drift between editions of the same claim. The 2022 maturity-bucket decomposition and Verdad's long-sample rating curve are the least conflicted pieces.[^ct22][^verdad]
- The claim that ~90% of the BB quality edge is fallen-angel/rising-star technicals is secondhand (attributed to Bloomberg index research; no primary document verified). If true it merges the quality and fallen-angel levers into one mechanism; the investable action (maturity-bucket choice) is unchanged either way.
- The trend-gate skew-destruction verdict is a mechanism inference from adjacent literatures (risk-managed momentum, trend construction), not a direct measurement on gated HY - strong convergent prior, unmeasured instance. A cheap diagnostic on our own data could close it, but is low priority since both the returns decomposition and the shape argument point the same way.[^hoffstein][^bsc]
- No independent net-of-cost backtest of an EM-local UCITS leg over 2018-2025 exists; we build our own (the run this article seeds).
- Post-publication decay applies to every premium here (the fallen-angel case shows a mechanism can thin while its index-level premium persists - the reverse can also happen).[^ftse25]
- The addendum's instrument facts (tickers, ISINs, TERs, AUM, listings) were verified against issuer/justETF pages on 2026-07-03, but crash-behavior numbers are patchy: DBXM's 2020/2022 drawdowns and the AT1 ETF's March-2020 drawdown could not be pinned to a primary document (research hit a fetch rate limit), and the Credit Suisse-episode figures are secondhand press numbers. Build drawdowns from our own data before sizing any new leg.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **The defensive tilt pays per unit of tail.** On the duration-stripped credit book, shifting the HY bucket from broad (IHYU) toward short-maturity (SDHY) raises held-out `carry_income_utility` at a flat-or-shallower `carry_tail_budget`, with the known cost showing up as recovery-year lag rather than crisis-year loss. Swept as the `defensive` axis of `carry_sota.yaml`. **Confirmed 2026-07-03** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03#20260703T174905661970Z_demeter_eu_credit_carry_sota - the construction sweep|diary]]): monotone along the axis (utility +0.0100 -> +0.0106 -> +0.0110 at defensive 0/0.5/1.0), tail budget -12.4 -> -11.6%/yr, maxDD 7.69 -> 7.21%, income flat (+2.00 -> +2.04) - the predicted recovery lag did not even show in aggregate - and concavity preserved (per-split qskew {-1.49, +0.80} vs incumbent {-1.63, +0.92}). The full tilt (SDHY replaces IHYU) is the sweep champion and the promotion candidate. **Amended same day** ([[runs/floor/2026-07-03]], [[the-skew-is-the-product]]): the confirmation was standalone-only. In the floor composite the tilt (with the band) had shaved the pole's crash-beta - qskew -1.83 to -0.49, corr-to-trend flipped to +0.17 - because a short-maturity shift is a duration-times-spread cut, and DTS is where credit's short-put character lives. The tilt's income economics stand; its promotion without a composite gate was the error. Re-test under the composite-gated re-rank with DTS pinned.
- [x] **The EM local leg deepens the concave signature.** A modest (10-30%) IEML sleeve lifts held-out income-per-tail *and* moves the pole's quarterly skew more negative (the FX-carry signature imported into a UCITS book) - the first investable test of "source the skew to FX" from [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]. Swept as the `fx_weight` axis of `carry_sota.yaml`; pre-register that in crisis splits the leg co-crashes with credit (that is expected, not disqualifying). **Killed on the unbanded book, then partially reinstated under live mechanics - both 2026-07-03** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03#20260703T174905661970Z_demeter_eu_credit_carry_sota - the construction sweep|construction sweep]], [[runs/demeter/2026-07-03#20260703T183523549479Z_demeter_eu_credit_carry_sota - live-mechanics re-sweep (bands + fixed fee)|live re-sweep]]): on the daily-rebalanced book the leg was utility-negative at every weight (fx 0.15 collapsed utility -70%) - the dollar-strength window's crash states without the income. Under the drift band (turnover 254 -> ~3 trades/yr) the economics flip: fx 0.15 is the live champion at utility parity with fx 0 and a clearly shallower tail (-9.6 vs -11.2%/yr budget, maxDD 6.02 vs 7.08) for ~0.3%/yr income. So the leg earns its place as a *tail-shallower under banded mechanics*, not as an income source - and the skew-deepening half of this hypothesis remains supported only in that conditional form. fx 0.30 stays dead on this window.
- [x] **Upgrades must beat the incumbent, shape-ranked.** The sweep's champion must dominate the nested incumbent cell (defensive 0, fx 0 = the current IHYU+LQDH lock structure) on held-out `carry_income_utility` without deepening `carry_tail_budget` - ranked on a shape-only grid with the vol target pinned by mandate, per the ranker A/B lesson in [[what-makes-a-carry-sleeve-an-income-engine]] ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03|diary]]). **Confirmed 2026-07-03**: the champion (defensive 1.0, fx 0) clears both bars against the nested incumbent, on a plateau rather than a spike, with rho-stability by dominance (higher mean AND shallower tail).
- [ ] **(Negative control, low priority.) Trend-gating the credit legs flips the pole's skew.** If ever run: pre-register that a MA-gated variant *raises* quarterly skew toward zero-or-positive and fails the concave family gate of [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]] - confirming the identity argument above on our own data rather than by mechanism inference.
- [ ] **The pure-spread leg restores the skew (DBXM).** Adding a protection-seller iTraxx Crossover leg (DBXM) to the book deepens held-out qskew past the -0.5 gate and holds corr-to-trend <= 0 at flat-or-better `carry_income_utility` - crash-rent income the defensive axis cannot tilt away. Precondition: verify IBKR tradability (LSE listing unconfirmed) and pull 2020/2022 drawdowns from our own data window before sizing; pre-register that the leg's crisis splits co-crash with HY (expected, budgeted). **BLOCKED 2026-07-03, untested**: the IB gateway has no security definition for the instrument - by symbol (DBXM/EUR, SMART) or by ISIN (LU0290359032) - so the last pure-spread UCITS vehicle is not tradable on our broker at all ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03]] AT1-leg preamble). Not a kill (the hypothesis was never tested); re-open only if IBKR lists it or another pure-spread UCITS vehicle launches.
- [x] **The subordination leg pays event-shaped crash-rent (XAT1).** A modest (10-20%) AT1 leg lifts income and deepens qskew with corr-to-trend intact; pre-register its March-2023-style event risk as within tail budget at that weight, and kill if the leg's income arrives without deepening the pole's skew (that would mean we bought bank beta, not insurance premium). **Killed 2026-07-03 (this construction)** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03#20260703T200118213807Z_demeter_eu_credit_carry_at1 - the AT1 leg|diary]]): with the leg's ~5%/yr coupons fully credited (after fixing the distribution-store defect the test itself uncovered), every at1 > 0 cell loses held-out utility AND income to the control - under the book vol-target the high-vol leg shrinks total exposure by more than its coupon adds. The instrument is fine; the *sizing architecture* taxes it away. Re-entry rides the DTS-pinned construction hypothesis of [[the-skew-is-the-product]], where a high-vol crash-rent leg is not automatically scaled out.
- [ ] **(Composite-gated only.) The covered-call leg is tested in the floor, not standalone.** A VRP leg via covered-call UCITS (QYLD/JEGP) may deepen skew while raising corr-to-trend (its crash factor is equities); it is admissible only through the composite gates of [[the-skew-is-the-product]] - never promote on standalone income-per-tail.
