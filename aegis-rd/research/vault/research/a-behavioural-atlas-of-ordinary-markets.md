# A Behavioural Atlas of Ordinary Markets

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> In the quiet, range-bound states where the trend sleeve bleeds, the four asset classes we can reach are close to linearly unpredictable at the daily-weekly horizons the cost wall already disfavours - what remains predictable is the second moment (volatility and correlation regimes), a handful of asset-class-specific dependence structures at monthly scale (bond policy gradualism, commodity-index flow propagation, gold's state-switched randomness), and the behaviour of a short list of named participants who transact for non-price reasons (delta-hedging dealers, buyback programs, index rebalancers, central banks in gold). Confidence: **medium** - the shape of the answer is multi-source and stable, but several load-bearing specifics rest on single working papers or have never been measured on ETF-wrapped, European-listed substrate, and the atlas names each such gap.

This article fills the research seat defined by [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] and [[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]]: the convergent ordinary-market engine is still empty, and two framings for filling it have already been retired - the mechanism-and-payer search that ended in a freshly published, already-decaying flow alpha ([[falsifying-the-mandated-rebalancing-alpha]], killed 2026-07-17), and the hunt for a second century-class premium, rejected in [[finding-a-buildable-convergent-engine]]. What remains is the path professional desks actually walk: design proprietary rules from structural understanding of the specific markets traded. This atlas is that structural understanding, gathered in four parallel evidence sweeps (phenomenology and horizon structure; participants and flows; failure boundaries and measurement tools; substrate and costs), each run wide-to-narrow across academic and practitioner sources. It delivers behaviours, reasons and evidence - not rules.

## What "ordinary" means, and how much of the time it is

The field has no standard operational definition of a range-bound state. The practitioner toolbox - Kaufman's Efficiency Ratio, Wilder's ADX, the Choppiness Index - uses thresholds that vary from author to author (ER < 0.3 or < 0.4; ADX < 15 up to < 25; Choppiness above 61.8), and the same practitioner who backtests them finds different "best" cutoffs per instrument and per strategy.[^qs] The folk constant that "markets trend only ~30% of the time" turns out to be untraceable: forum threads state it as received wisdom without citation, recent blogs cite each other in a closed loop with percentages wandering from 20% to 40%, and no named systematic-trading authority owns a quantified version of the claim.[^folklore] The one informal measurement found (an ADX-and-ER filter study on SPY/QQQ) lands at ~20-23% time-in-trend, directionally consistent but from a single unreviewed source with a restrictive definition. The consequence for us is blunt: the time-in-range fraction for our instruments is a number we must derive ourselves, with a pre-registered definition, because the literature does not have it - for any asset class, let alone our four.

What the literature does establish is that regimes exist and persist, with duration estimates that depend entirely on the model's granularity. A weekly three-state SWARCH on the S&P 500 (1928-2018) gives a low-volatility regime near 9% annualized vol lasting ~80 weeks on average and covering ~45% of weeks, a medium regime near 14% lasting ~50 weeks (another ~45%), and a high-vol regime near 31% that is rare (~10%) and much less persistent.[^ecb] A monthly four-state joint stock-bond model gives a slow-growth state of ~7-16 months (~40% steady-state probability) and a bull state of ~8-15 months, with crash and recovery states of only 2-3 months.[^guidolin] Multi-year bull/bear cycle models give ~5-year bulls and ~1.5-year bears. All three agree that calm states are long-lived and dominate calendar time; they disagree by an order of magnitude on "how long", which means state duration is a modelling choice we must make and defend, not a fact we can import.

Two phenomenological anchors complete the picture of the ordinary state. First, Cont's stylized facts - near-zero linear autocorrelation of returns beyond ~20-minute timescales, volatility clustering with power-law decay that stays significant over days to weeks, and the leverage effect - remain the best-replicated regularities in empirical finance (8 of 11 confirmed on modern SEC intraday data), though they were established mostly on equities and FX and have not been separately re-verified for bonds, gold and broad commodities at daily-to-monthly cadence.[^cont] [^contrep] Second, ordinary states are *dispersed*, not synchronized: implied pairwise equity correlation falls in step with the VIX in quiet periods, and cross-asset correlations (stock-commodity averaging +0.13, bond-commodity -0.09 over 1962-2012 weekly data) spike in economic weakness and sit low in calm - so a quiet market is one where idiosyncratic, asset-specific news dominates the common factor.[^twosigma] [^dunsby] Whipsaw in the trend book and dispersion in the cross-section are the same state seen from two sides ([[trend-following-in-whipsaw-regimes]]).

## The horizon structure of dependence, per asset class

The single best cross-asset quantification is old but exact. Cutler, Poterba and Summers measured 1-month autocorrelations of +0.101 for equities (13 markets, 1960-88), +0.238 for bonds, and +0.020 (SE 0.075, indistinguishable from zero) for gold, with the 13-24-month averages turning weakly negative for equities and bonds - the short-run-momentum, long-run-reversal shape their feedback-trader model predicts.[^cps] That sample ends in 1988, and the era since has not been kind to the equity number: century-long variance-ratio and spectral evidence shows US equity-index predictability was materially higher before ~1980 and decays toward zero afterwards, with the remaining predictability concentrated in crashes and crises - exactly the states this atlas assigns to the defence sleeve, not the convergent seat.[^kim] [^limbrooks] For ordinary states specifically, the honest reading is that equity-index linear dependence at daily-weekly horizons is the weakest it has ever been.

The other three classes each have their own structure:

**Long-duration government bonds** keep the strongest documented positive dependence at the monthly scale. Time-series momentum holds across bond futures in the canonical 58-instrument study (1-12 month persistence, partial longer-horizon reversal),[^mop] and the mechanism decomposes informatively: bond momentum is primarily autocorrelation in *yield changes*, tied to the deliberate gradualism of policy rates, and is strongest for longer maturities.[^ycmom] The competing fact is that after duration-adjusting, *cross-sectional reversal* along the curve (slope mean-reversion) is reported as stronger and more robust than momentum on 40+ years of Treasury security-level data.[^martellini] Both camps predate the 2022 hiking cycle; no post-2022 re-test was found, which makes this a mandatory in-house measurement rather than a borrowable fact.

**Gold** is best described as state-switched. The careful large-sample evidence (28 countries, 1968-2014) cannot reject weak-form efficiency for developed-market gold, and the classic estimate is statistically zero;[^ntim] [^cps] yet a narrower LBMA study rejects the random walk on 2000-2020 daily data,[^bhuyan] and there is a documented mechanism for when dependence switches on: feedback/chartist trading that scales with gold's speculative ownership share, strongest in the 2002-2012 bull run, plus variance ratios that reject randomness in stress windows but not calm ones.[^baur] The reconciliation - near-random in calm developed-market conditions, dependent in bull runs and stress - is itself the useful behavioural fact, because it says a dependence-harvesting rule on gold has no edge precisely in the ordinary states this seat targets.

**Broad commodities** carry the sharpest wrapper-relevant split in the whole atlas. Storage theory gives *individual* commodities high positive autocorrelation in normal, non-stockout periods (inventories smooth prices),[^deaton] but the *index* our ETFs track flipped sign with financialization: daily autocorrelation of the GSCI and BCOM declined steadily from 2004, went negative around 2005 and significantly negative from 2006, while a non-indexed commodity portfolio showed no trend at all - attributed to index flows propagating non-fundamental shocks across basket members and producing overshoot-and-reversion at the index level.[^datang] Since our access route is the index wrapper, the index-level fact is the operative one - but it rests on one working paper, is documented at daily frequency only, and the cost wall makes daily harvesting implausible. Whether it survives at weekly cadence is unknown and testable.

**Volatility**, across all four classes, is the dependable dimension: clustering and regime persistence are the one structure that decades of arbitrage have not removed, because linear return predictability is what gets competed away (Mandelbrot's "whitening of the spectrum" - trend-followers and stat-arb attack the first moment, nobody can directly arbitrage the second).[^cont] This asymmetry - dead first moment, alive second moment - is the atlas's most reliable single observation.

## Who transacts in ordinary states, and why

The brief values a regularity with a named, constrained participant over a statistical one. The sweep produced a short list per class, with an important negative result attached.

**Equity index.** The dominant quiet-state actors are option dealers who must delta-hedge mechanically. When dealers are net long gamma - the normal state when customers are net option sellers in calm markets - their hedging leans against every move, and measured gamma imbalance predicts the sign of intraday autocorrelation: a one-standard-deviation rise in dealer gamma is associated with a ~20bp reduction in absolute index moves, with the effect economically stronger at the index level than for single names.[^barbon] The practitioner ecosystem (SqueezeMetrics' GEX and its descendants) distributes daily estimates of this state, which makes the raw signal partially crowded, but the *regime description* is what matters for us: ordinary equity-index states are dealer-dampened states, and the flip to negative gamma is a measurable break condition. The confounder is 0DTE options, now 40-50% of SPX volume, whose net effect on volatility is genuinely contested across three working papers and the exchange's own data (destabilizing / stabilizing / negligible) - unresolved, and flagged as such.[^0dte] Corporate buybacks are the second mechanical quiet-state flow: firms enter on down days, pay a premium that rises as prior-day returns fall, exit when returns turn positive, and measurably reduce daily volatility and order-imbalance price impact - robust across 20+ years and several markets, bounded by authorization size, earnings blackouts and safe-harbor volume caps.[^brownpinder] [^keswani] The single-name effect is well-known to desks; an *index-level aggregate* of Europe's mandatory daily buyback disclosures has, as far as the sweep could find, never been tested - operationally awkward, capacity-limited, and therefore exactly the obscure corner the brief asks for.

**Long-duration bonds.** The futures complex is dominated by a symbiotic pair - asset managers net long ~$700bn, leveraged funds net short up to ~$1trn in the cash-futures basis trade, the two tracking each other at R² = 92% since 2015 - which is institutional plumbing, systemically monitored, leverage up to 70x, and inaccessible to us by construction.[^tbac] [^feds] The eurozone version of the basis trade is documented by the ECB as much smaller and "more contained" because arbitrage costs are higher - a diffuse hint that the EUR government complex is a less-arbitraged corner than Treasuries. Month-end index-extension and insurer flows remain real and mechanically driven (NY Fed data shows last-day volumes elevated through 2024-25),[^nyfedeom] [^schwarz] but our own falsification separated the flow from the edge: the published front-running expression reproduced at Sharpe 1.16 gross in-sample and decayed to ~0 post-publication, with a one-session execution delay alone destroying it ([[falsifying-the-mandated-rebalancing-alpha]]). The flow is ecology; the edge is dead; the general lesson - mandate-driven flows persist while their front-running premia decay - is the load-bearing one.

**Gold.** Central banks are the structurally price-insensitive participant: ~1,000t/yr of net buying over the last four years (double the prior decade), 13% of above-ground stock held, executed deliberately through OTC and domestic channels to avoid market impact, with survey evidence that tactical trading motives are declining.[^wgc] This is a slow demand floor, not a harvestable cadence-scale flow - and honesty requires saying the sweep found *no* rigorous causal evidence that gold ETF flows or central-bank buying predict returns at our horizons (the one flow-to-price causality test found is an unreplicated master's thesis, failing the two-source rule). Price discovery sits in New York futures despite London's larger volume.[^hauptfleisch]

**Broad commodities.** Commodity index traders roll on pre-announced schedules with no informational content; commercial hedgers trade far more than pure risk-reduction implies; and the modern premium decomposition says speculators earn a liquidity-provision premium that roughly offsets the insurance premium hedgers pay - meaning who is paid depends on the horizon of the position change.[^krt] [^chengxiong] The exploitable version of the roll flow (the "Goldman roll" front-run, in-sample Sharpe above 4 pre-2012) is confirmed dead by two independent teams, and the indices our UCITS wrappers track have been redesigned (longer roll windows, multi-contract spreading) specifically to de-fang it.[^irwin] [^mou]

**Two corrections the sweep forced.** First, vol-target and risk-parity rebalancing - the flow most often blamed for quiet-state footprints - is better described as a *transition-state* participant: vol-scaling's measurable effects concentrate in risk assets and in volatility extremes, not the calm middle, so it belongs to the failure boundary, not the ordinary-state map.[^harveyvt] [^bongaerts] Second, the CTA-crowding narrative fails its best measurement: trend-followers hold under 10% of directional open interest and under 1% of directional volume across futures markets, flat despite 10x industry AUM growth, with maximum equity-futures participation of 3.5% over any 5-day window even in March 2020.[^quantica] Neither vol-target unwinds nor CTA herding should be treated as dominant ordinary-state forces in our instrument set.

## How ordinary states end

The failure boundary matters because any rule designed for ordinary states must declare its break condition ([[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]] makes the convergence-break state part of the seat's definition). Four findings structure it.

**Range compression predicts expansion, not direction.** Volatility clustering guarantees that squeezes precede expansions - that much is ARCH physics. But the directional trading claim built on it fails the strictest test available: under false-discovery-rate correction across 7,846 technical rules on a century of DJIA data, zero breakout/squeeze rules survive costs, and the Bollinger-squeeze effect that was predictive in 9 of 14 international markets before 1983 fell to 1 of 14 after 2001.[^bajgrowicz] [^marshall] The counter-evidence is that FX and commodity channel breakouts tested profitable net of costs in older peer-reviewed work - possibly a genuine cross-asset difference, never resolved by an FDR-corrected multi-asset study.[^taylor] For us: expansion timing is knowable, expansion direction is not, and any break condition should be volatility-triggered rather than direction-triggered.

**The bond-equity correlation regime is the master break condition for this book.** Its sign is set by the relative volatility of growth versus inflation news and their correlation - a three-factor model explains ~70% of long-run variation - not by the inflation level; the post-1951 relation (roughly +0.17 of correlation per +1pp of joint inflation/real-rate rise) exists only in the countercyclical-policy era and only for safe-haven sovereigns; and the reliably negative correlation of 2000-2020 is the historical exception, not the norm.[^brixton] [^molenaar] Real-time transition variables with documented information: the short rate, the yield spread, and the VIX (high VIX pushes correlation negative through flight-to-safety even when inflation pushes it positive). This regime also decides how much diversification the quartet actually contains ([[havens-at-regime-turning-points]]).

**Early-warning instruments are coincident more often than leading.** The turbulence index's stress-detection and multi-week persistence replicate out-of-sample; the absorption ratio's *pre-crisis direction* does not - the original reports it rising before crises, a careful replication finds it falling, and only the coincident spike plus post-crisis persistence is agreed.[^kritzman] [^absorption] The best-graded early observable in this family is the correlation-surprise decomposition, which is mathematically orthogonal to volatility and carries incremental forward information beyond it.[^kinlaw] Market-breadth timing at the aggregate level shows near-zero lead at 1-21 days with apparent power concentrated in 2008-09 - the textbook coincident-mislabeled-as-leading failure.

**Gold's own boundary observable is contested at the sign level.** The canonical real-rate anchor (-0.82 correlation of real gold price with 10y TIPS yield, US 1997-2011) is flagged as fragile by its own authors (-0.31 in the longer UK sample; a bare time trend fits better), and a peer-reviewed regime-switching study finds the opposite sign in recessions.[^erb] Do not build a gold break condition on the real-rate correlation without re-deriving it on the current sample.

## The measurement toolbox, graded

The atlas will be used through instruments, so their failure modes are first-class facts. Condensed verdicts, each with the key citation:

- **Variance ratios**: trustworthy as a formal randomness test at one pre-chosen horizon; the textbook asymptotic z-statistic is badly non-normal in small samples and multi-horizon batteries inflate false rejections - use Chow-Denning joint tests or wild-bootstrap variants on ETF-length samples.[^charles]
- **Hurst exponents**: trustworthy on *volatility* series (long memory in squared returns is real), untrustworthy on *return* series - no long-range dependence survives short-range controls in the canonical test, the estimator is biased upward (~0.55 expected for a truly uncorrelated 1000-observation series), acutely sensitive to fitted-lag choice (the same SPY series flips 0.43 to 0.67), and shows no year-over-year persistence.[^lo91] [^lobato] [^robotwealth]
- **Efficiency Ratio / ADX**: acceptable as transparent, non-directional "is there a trend at all" gates; no peer-reviewed validation as measurement instruments exists, and what rule-based evidence exists is efficiency-gated (works in less-efficient markets, not US/UK).[^lam] Our own runs already found ER gates either flatline or fee-storm a thin daily book ([[graveyard]]).
- **Markov/HMM regime models**: trustworthy for post-hoc regime counting; three real-time traps - smoothed probabilities embed look-ahead (only filtered probabilities are backtest-safe), label switching across re-estimations, and a structural detection lag with no tuning escape.[^ang] [^kniyer] The econometric horse race between abrupt-switching and gradual-drift descriptions of regime change is live, and no study benchmarks HMMs against simple vol-percentile thresholds on a small multi-asset universe - the burden of proof is on the fancier model.
- **Turbulence/absorption/correlation surprise**: see the failure-boundary section - coincident detection robust, leading claims contested, correlation surprise the strongest candidate.[^kinlaw]
- **Realized-vol regime classification**: sound, with one nuance - volatility's own persistence is regime-dependent (anti-persistent in calm, persistent in crisis), and the "normal" vol level is nonstationary across decades, so fixed thresholds decay; percentile thresholds age better.[^caporale]

## The substrate translation and the cost wall

Every fact above must survive the wrapper. Five substrate facts gate the design space:

1. **Commodity roll policy lives inside the licensed index, not in our hands.** UCITS broad-commodity trackers are synthetic, tracking BCOM-class indices whose roll has been optimized (and de-fanged against front-running); optimized roll recovers roughly +2-3% p.a. versus front-month but the improvement is partly commodity-mix, not methodology.[^quanticaroll] Behaviours documented on spot or front-month series must be re-verified on the tracked index.
2. **Physical gold ETCs have no futures curve.** They track the LBMA auction; any signal derived from COMEX term structure has no expression in the wrapper, and the futures-spot link itself can dislocate (~$70/oz in March 2020 versus a normal <$5).[^invescogold] Hard boundary, not a friction.
3. **Bond ETFs are total-return baskets, not futures.** Tracking of EUR government lines is tight (0.00-0.18% p.a. tracking difference); futures-specific behaviours (CTD switches, calendar roll) have no ETF analogue.[^trackdiff]
4. **European listing hours truncate the information day.** A London-listed US-underlying line trades 03:00-11:30 ET and inherits the last ~4.5 US hours as an opening gap; the one behaviour tailor-made for those hours - the overnight drift that concentrated the entire US equity premium into the 2-3am ET European open - decayed to zero after 2021 via a >50% compression in end-of-day order-imbalance dispersion, and the fund built on it closed in 14 months.[^boyarchenko] [^libertydrift] The general asynchronous-hours finding (ETF prints during the underlying's closed hours are echo, not information) comes from the mirror case (US-listed, foreign underlying) and transfers by inference only.[^levy]
5. **The cost wall confines cadence, parametrically.** At an order size where the fixed minimum dominates (around €2,000/order), commission alone is 125-300bp round trip - unreachable by any documented edge. At ~€10,000/order the all-in round trip is ~12-77bp depending on asset class (measured spreads: equity index ~4bp, gold ETC ~3-10bp, broad commodity ~7-17bp, long-duration Treasury line ~15bp on snapshot); at €50,000+ the commission floor vanishes and spread dominates.[^xetra] Weekly harvesting must clear that hurdle per event with no cross-sectional netting; monthly amortizes it comfortably - the arithmetic independently confirms the brief's weekly-to-monthly prior. Short-side feasibility is asymmetric: equity-index lines borrow easily (~1.2-1.5%/yr), bond lines thinly (~2-3.4%), and gold ETC / commodity ETF borrow could not be confirmed at all - some issuers prohibit lending outright - so short-side designs are realistically equity-first.[^ibkr]

## The five behavioural facts most worth designing against

1. **The second moment is alive where the first moment is dead.** Volatility clusters and volatility/correlation regimes persist for weeks to quarters across all four classes; linear return dependence at daily-weekly horizons is the most arbitraged dimension in the set. *Why it persists*: arbitrage attacks return forecasts, not variance forecasts; nobody can trade the second moment away in a whole-share, no-derivatives book, and even institutionally it survives a century of measurement. *Failure boundary*: regime transitions are faster than regime lives, and volatility persistence itself switches character at the transition (anti-persistent calm, persistent crisis). *Erasure observable*: none plausible for clustering itself; for any rule built on it, a fixed volatility threshold silently decays as the nonstationary vol level drifts - use percentiles and monitor them.
2. **Long-duration bonds carry monthly-scale positive dependence with a named non-commercial cause - policy gradualism - but that dependence is the trend family's own premium, so its value to this seat is inverted.** The divergent sleeve already harvests exactly this structure (slow time-series momentum on the long-duration leg), so a convergent-seat rule built on it would pay in the trend sleeve's paying states and bleed in its bleeding states - the opposite of what the seat is hired for, and worth least under the decorrelation ranking. The fact enters the atlas twice: as a family-membership warning - a directional bond rule at weekly-monthly cadence is a correlated copy of the trend sleeve, not a convergent engine ([[verifying-strategy-family-membership]]) - and as a pointer to the channel that is convergent on the same substrate, the duration-adjusted slope mean-reversion that the security-level evidence finds stronger than the momentum. *Why the underlying dependence persists*: the autocorrelation source is the central bank's own smoothing of rate moves; the participant is constrained by mandate, not profit. *Failure boundary*: rapid regime discontinuities (2022-style). *Erasure observable*: a reaction function shifting to large discrete moves; no post-2022 re-test exists for either channel, which makes both an urgent in-house measurement.
3. **The broad-commodity index (not its members) mean-reverts at short horizon because index flow propagates non-fundamental shocks.** *Why it persists*: the flow is mechanical and pre-announced; the wrapper guarantees we hold exactly the index that exhibits it. *Failure boundary*: documented at daily frequency only - likely below the cost wall - and single-commodity storage dynamics push the other way. *Erasure observable*: commodity-index AUM / CFTC index-trader positions shrinking; the on-index versus off-index correlation spread closing.
4. **Ordinary equity-index states are dealer-dampened (positive-gamma) states, and the gamma flip is a measurable break condition.** *Why it persists*: dealers must hedge; customers structurally sell options in calm markets. *Failure boundary*: the flip to negative gamma when hedging demand surges - which is precisely the hand-off line to the defence sleeve, giving the convergent seat a principled, observable exit trigger rather than an arbitrary stop. *Erasure observable*: option OI mix flipping toward net customer buying; 0DTE growth (contested) shortening the persistence of any day's gamma state.
5. **The bond-equity correlation regime - driven by the growth/inflation news mix, conditional on safe-haven status - is the boundary condition for the whole book.** *Why it persists*: it is a property of the macro policy regime, not an arbitrageable flow. *Failure boundary*: it is itself the boundary; the design obligation is to declare rules' behaviour under both signs. *Erasure observable*: the growth-inflation correlation sign, the sovereign's haven status, and the short-rate/yield-spread/VIX transition variables - all public and cheap to monitor.

Ranked by the brief's own decorrelation rule - a payoff is worth most when it arrives in the states where the divergent sleeve bleeds, because lower blended volatility is what the allocator's vol-targeting converts into scale - only facts 3 and 4 are payoff-shaped for this seat: both are reversion structures that earn inside ranges and break when trends start. Facts 1 and 5 are conditioning and boundary knowledge, correlation-neutral by nature, and fact 2's payoff half already belongs to the divergent sleeve; only its reversal channel is candidate material.

> [!warning] Post-measurement update (2026-07-17)
> Both payoff-shaped facts failed their pre-registered gates on our substrate the same day ([[measuring-the-ordinary-market-payoff-gates]]): no weekly commodity-index reversion exists on the UCITS line (and monthly dependence is trend-family positive), and the gamma conditioning goes the wrong way at tradable cadence. The macro-quartet convergent lane is closed for payoffs; facts 1, 4's second moment, and 5 remain live as instrumentation, and the seat's search continues in the event/single-stock lane.

A sixth fact frames all five: the fixed-cost wall is the design constraint that decides which of these are reachable, and it points every design toward weekly-to-monthly cadence, long-side-first, with the equity line as the only comfortable short.

## Where the camps disagree

- **Gold's randomness**: developed-market efficiency (large multi-country studies) versus LBMA-sample rejection; reconciled here as state-dependence, which both sides' data supports.
- **Commodity-index dependence sign**: storage theory (positive, single commodity) versus financialization (negative, index level) - different objects, both real; the index-level side rests on one working paper.
- **0DTE and volatility**: destabilizing, stabilizing and negligible all have credible sponsors; unresolved; the exchange's own "negligible" data carries a conflict of interest.
- **Absorption ratio's pre-crisis direction**: rises (original, OFR) versus falls (replication); only the coincident spike is settled.
- **Gold versus real rates**: the canonical negative correlation is fragile by its own authors' admission and sign-flips under other methodologies.
- **Breakout profitability**: dead under FDR correction in equities; alive in older FX/commodity tests; no unified study exists.
- **CTA crowding**: the transparent footprint measurement (small, flat) beats the crowding narrative (unmeasured, promotional).
- **Regime change, abrupt or gradual**: the Markov-switching narrative versus the forecasting horse race that favours smooth parameter drift; live, methodological, unresolved.

## Limitations

The atlas's biggest structural gap is that almost nothing above was measured on ETF-wrapped, European-listed substrate: Cont's facts, the horizon-structure table, the FDR breakout null and the gamma-regime evidence all live on US futures, indices or single stocks, and the asynchronous-hours literature covers the mirror configuration. Three load-bearing specifics rest on single sources (commodity-index negative autocorrelation; the gold flow-to-price causality claim, which accordingly is *not* used; the exact synthetic-versus-physical tracking dispersion figures). Two cost inputs are snapshots rather than series (long-duration and commodity ETF spreads), and gold/commodity borrow availability is unconfirmed. Any of these could move a conclusion; the hypotheses below are ordered to close the gaps that matter most.
