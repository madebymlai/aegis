# Budgeting the Divergent Seat

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> "What is the best divergent payoff a UCITS/IBKR-IE book can own" is the wrong question, and the newest work says why: hedge quality is not one number but several near-independent axes (conditional convexity, tail reliability, non-stress carry, drawdown persistence), and a divergent sleeve's worth is its *overlay capacity* - how much a risk-budget can hold, set by its left-tail conditional correlation, not its standalone Sharpe. On both measures no single mechanism wins, which is why [[where-convexity-still-lives]] could not crown a challenger. But the answer is not to pile several convex instruments into one seat either, because each sleeve must own a *distinct* instrument set: the fast-gap axis and the short-variance premium already live in the tail and carry sleeves, so the divergent seat cannot double-book a gold sliver or a long-VIX overlay it would be *buying while carry sells it*. The axes are covered *across* the roster's tiers - slow trend (reliability, persistence, positive carry), the tail sleeve (the single-day gap), carry (the concave side) - each sized by left-tail correlation; and the one genuinely new, instrument-orthogonal line the divergent seat itself can budget in is a winter-cover complement (cross-asset value or economic trend) on its own signals, to pay down the trendless-winter bleed price trend cannot design away.

[[where-convexity-still-lives]] answered the seat's selection question - among trend, bought convexity, cross-sectional and network momentum, macro-divergence and scarce assets, which single mechanism *wins* the divergent slot - and the answer was that none displaces slow trend outright: bought convexity is the tail tier's job, the concave income is carry's, and the convex non-trend systematics are things to combine with trend, not replace it. That verdict has a sequel the selection frame cannot see. If no single source wins, the seat is not a mechanism to be chosen but a *budget* to be sized, and the 2024-2026 literature has quietly rebuilt the vocabulary for sizing it. This article is that sequel: it takes the menu as settled and argues how to compose it.

## Hedge quality is not one number

The selection frame implicitly ranks candidates on a single "how convex is it" scale. The sharpest recent theory says that scale does not exist. Noguer i Alonso and Al Fallouji, modelling a put sleeve and a trend sleeve together in a continuous-time CVaR framework, decompose hedge quality into four axes that do not move together: *conditional convexity* (how sharply the payoff bends in a shock), *tail-event reliability* (how often it actually delivers across events), *non-stress carry* (what it costs to hold in calm), and *drawdown persistence* (whether it keeps paying through a protracted decline).[^cvar] Trend and options sit at opposite corners of this space. The put has the highest conditional convexity (it reprices on jump impact) but the worst carry; trend has the highest tail-event reliability and the best carry but is late to the first shock, because its signal must cross zero before it is positioned. Their optimum is neither pure sleeve but an interior put-plus-trend hybrid - in their Monte Carlo the equal-weight hybrid cut tail-CVaR to 0.28 against 0.47 for trend-only and 0.63 for the put-only, while keeping skew near the trend leg's +1.2.[^cvar] The result matters less for its exact numbers - it is a stylized model - than for its shape: a payoff can be excellent on one axis and poor on another, so "best divergent payoff" is a category error the moment more than one axis binds. This is the theoretical form of the same fact our own metric already half-encodes, measuring the trend pole's intrinsic own-horizon skew rather than a single equity-beta convexity ([[verifying-strategy-family-membership]], [[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]).

The four axes also explain, rather than merely assert, the division of labor the roster already runs. Trend's weakness is the *first* axis at short timescales (it sleeps through a one-day gap) and its strength is the *fourth* (it harvests protracted bears); the tail overlay is the mirror. Reading the seat as a single scalar hides that the two are complements on orthogonal axes, not rivals on one.

## A diversifier's worth is its overlay capacity, not its Sharpe

The second reframing is about sizing, and it is the more actionable of the two. The instinct is to rank convex sleeves by standalone Sharpe and hold the best. Quantica's overlay-capacity work inverts that. Under a fixed marginal-risk budget - "you may add at most +1% to a 100%-equity book's volatility" - the binding constraint on how much of a diversifier you can hold is its *correlation to equities in the left tail*, not its Sharpe.[^quanticastack] On that measure the ranking scrambles: an equity-market-neutral sleeve with a standalone Sharpe near 0.87 admits only a ~10-25% overlay because it is not negatively correlated when it matters, while trend at a standalone Sharpe near 0.40 admits a ~48-56% overlay and has held that capacity stable over the last decade, precisely because its left-tail correlation to equities is negative.[^quanticastack] Gold admits ~38-40%, and Treasuries have fallen from ~58% to ~34% as their crisis diversification eroded after 2022.[^quanticastack] Long-vol scores the highest raw convexity of all but the lowest deployable weight, because its negative carry makes any large allocation too expensive to run.[^quanticastack] The lesson is blunt: a divergent sleeve is worth what a risk budget can *hold* of it through the cycle, and a lower-Sharpe, reliably-crisis-negative sleeve beats a higher-Sharpe one that goes flat or positive in the drawdown.

Read together, the two reframings convert the seat from a selection into a construction. Hedge quality is multi-axis, so you want more than one source to cover more than one axis; overlay capacity is set by left-tail correlation, so you size each source by how much a budget can carry, not by its Sharpe. That is a budget, and it is why the honest answer to the issue's question is a small stack rather than a winner.

## Covering the axes across sleeves, not inside one seat

The reframings do not license stacking every convex instrument into the trend sleeve, and a hard book-design rule blocks the naive reading: each sleeve owns a *distinct* instrument set, both to keep the sleeves' realized streams orthogonal and to stop the book paying two spreads to hold offsetting positions. That rule turns "budget the divergent seat" into a question about the *roster*, not a shopping list for one sleeve. Priced on hedge-quality axis, overlay capacity, and the UCITS constraint, the axes are covered as follows - and only the last line is a genuinely new addition *inside* the divergent seat.

*Slow price trend is the backbone*, and remains so for the reason [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] gives: it is the one convex payoff the market *pays* you to hold rather than charging you for, an anomaly rather than an insurance premium.[^lemp] That gives it the best non-stress-carry axis and the highest stable overlay capacity, and its durability now has fresh support - crowded momentum shows *lower* crash probability than uncrowded, the opposite of the crowding fear, because more participants reinforcing a directional move is stabilising for a trend position.[^crowd] It is UCITS-native either self-built from cross-asset ETFs or bought wholesale through the EUR-listed managed-futures UCITS ETF ([[trend-following-under-a-fixed-per-order-fee]], [[the-multi-asset-substrate-for-the-roster]]). Its known deficits - the trendless-winter bleed and the single-day gap - are what the other lines cover, but crucially *not all inside this seat*.

*Gold's left-tail convexity is real, and it is already budgeted elsewhere.* The 2025 evidence is that gold's negative equity correlation *rises* as risk assets sell off - convex in the left tail rather than merely uncorrelated on average - and unlike Treasuries it has kept that property since 2022, which is why its overlay capacity sits above duration's.[^gold][^quanticastack] But the book already owns that convexity twice over: the trend backbone holds gold (a UCITS physical gold ETC) *directionally* whenever gold trends, and the tail sleeve's physical fallback is a Swiss-franc-and-gold-led haven basket ([[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]]). Under the one-instrument-per-sleeve rule an always-on gold sliver bolted onto the divergent seat would double-book the tail sleeve's haven and the trend leg's own position, so gold does not earn a *separate* divergent-seat line - its convexity is captured where it already lives, and the design task is to size that existing exposure by its left-tail correlation, not to re-add it.

*The single-day gap is the tail sleeve's axis, and it is emphatically not a long-VIX overlay in the trend seat.* The physics is not negotiable: a single-asset trend P&L equals the long-horizon minus the short-horizon realized variance, a variance swap that pays the short-term realized variance as its entry cost, so a shock completing inside one day is paid by an option and slept through by trend.[^cfmvar] Covering it means buying convexity and paying a premium - and three constraints stack to keep that line out of the divergent seat. First and decisively, instrument-orthogonality: the book's variance exposure already belongs to the carry sleeve, which *sells* variance to harvest the risk premium ([[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]], the `demeter.vol_carry` work), so a long-VIX overlay in the trend seat would be buying the very instrument carry is short, netting to a spread-paying wash and breaking the non-overlap rule. Second, cost: the always-on short-term VIX-ETP floor was already killed on our book, its roll decay an equilibrium premium too dear to carry at any crash-moving weight ([[always-on-convexity-from-listed-instruments]]). Third, UCITS: the cheapest gap instruments - VIX and VSTOXX ETNs - are unsecured notes, KID-blocked for EEA retail and out on tradability grounds, and OTC variance swaps are not retail-holdable.[^rebuffed] So the gap axis is delegated to the separate tail sleeve, cost-budgeted and sourced from the physical haven basket, not folded into the trend pole.

*A cross-asset value or economic-trend leg is the one genuinely new, instrument-orthogonal line the divergent seat can add.* It is orthogonal because it runs on a different signal (relative fundamentals or price-vs-fundamental dislocation), not on a convex instrument some other sleeve already owns. Cross-asset value is time-series trend's structural mirror - value and momentum run about -0.60 correlated within and across asset classes, so a value leg pays roughly when trend fades, by construction - and the corroboration is peer-reviewed rather than practitioner, which the vault's earlier LOIM read lacked.[^vamom] Economic (macro-fundamental) trend corroborates from the other side: it correlates only ~0.3-0.4 with price trend and is positive in the fundamental-catalyst drawdowns where price trend is starved.[^econtrend] The honest limits are three and each is load-bearing. Value covers *trendless-chop* winters but co-crashes in *acute liquidity* events - value, momentum, carry and defensive all broke down together in February-March 2020.[^styleslump] Much of what is sold as "global macro divergence" decomposes into trend plus equity beta once you regress it out, so a candidate must show residual non-trend, non-equity alpha before it earns a budget line.[^macroalpha] And the UCITS vehicles that exist blend carry into the same sleeve, which risks smuggling the concave pole into the divergent seat and violating the |corr| < 0.10 mandate to the credit-carry pole - carry itself is a concave, co-crashing premium, not a divergent payer, so a carry-dominant "diversifier" fails the seat on shape.[^macroalpha]

## There is no free convexity, so the budget is the answer

The tempting objection is that a cleverly-engineered single sleeve could still dominate the budget. Every rigorous source in the sweep closes that door from a different side, and their agreement is the strongest claim here. Bouchaud's variance-swap identity says you *choose which premium you pay* - the whipsaw and chop bleed of harvested convexity via trend, or the option premium of paid-for convexity via long-vol - but never whether.[^cfmvar] The primary-grade decay evidence sharpens which premium is cheaper over time: index-option alphas have become statistically indistinguishable from zero over the past fifteen years, and the historical option overpricing was an intermediary-frictions phenomenon now eroding as households increasingly *sell* crash insurance through structured products - so the paid-for side is thinning while trend's harvested convexity, an anomaly rather than a friction, is the more durable premium.[^vrpdecay] And the live proof that no combination escapes the winter is a manager running the exact long-vol-plus-trend construction this article recommends: One River's Risk Responders posted its largest and longest drawdown ever in 2025, in a trendless tape with no panic, and its own research warns that shortening the models to defend the initial drawdown carries a meaningful long-term cost.[^oneriver] The winter cannot be designed out; its depth can be bounded by spreading the payoff across axes that fail at different times.

That is what a budget buys. The convergent recommendation across otherwise-competing houses is the same shape - harvest cheap convexity through slow-to-medium trend, and cap the residual with a small always-on paid-for overlay - stated by Man (trend plus a ~25% options overlay replicates put downside convexity without the carry), by CFM (a 10% defensive sleeve of short-term trend plus a liquid VIX-future long-vol leg), and by the quantified diversification-term-structure result that an equal blend of trend, bonds and gold reached a Sharpe of 0.72 against 0.49 for equity alone and beat any single divergent sleeve.[^manconvex][^cumulus][^termstructure] None of these is a silver bullet; all of them are budgets. One caveat separates their form from ours: these houses run the overlay *inside a single product*, so their VIX-future or options leg sits beside their trend leg in one book. A roster split into distinct sleeves under the one-instrument rule covers the same axes *across* sleeves - the gap-hedge instrument is the tail sleeve's, the short-variance premium is carry's - so the illustrations validate the budget principle while their specific long-vol instrument stays out of the divergent seat. The peer-reviewed capstone reads the same way: overlaying trend *and* an option tail hedge on a full-equity book improved every one of nine crisis episodes tested and added about 0.25% per month after factor controls, cutting max drawdown from roughly -34% to -19% - two axes covered, neither sleeve asked to be the whole answer.[^schwalbach]

## Limitations

- The two reframings rest on a small number of sources each. The multi-axis hedge-quality result is one stylized continuous-time model[^cvar] and the overlay-capacity result is one manager's closed-form-plus-empirics with a clear conflict of interest (Quantica sells trend).[^quanticastack] They are conceptually strong and mutually reinforcing, but neither is a replicated empirical law; treat "size by left-tail correlation, not Sharpe" as a well-motivated design principle, not a proven theorem.
- Left-tail conditional correlation is itself a few-observation tail estimate and therefore unstable ([[skewness-in-asset-returns]]), so a budget sized on it must be built with wide error bars and revisited on a slow cadence, not re-optimised on every new drawdown.
- The one-instrument-per-sleeve rule is what makes this a roster problem rather than a single-sleeve stack, and it is the binding constraint on the whole argument: it is why gold (already a trend leg and a tail haven) and long-variance (already carry's short-side) cannot be re-added inside the divergent seat, and why the only new internal line is a signal-orthogonal value/economic-trend complement. Relax that rule and the article collapses back into the naive "stack every convex instrument in one seat" it argues against.
- The budget's components still interact even across sleeves. A value/economic-trend complement, the existing directional gold exposure, and the tail sleeve can displace each other under a fixed book-vol target (the roster's own allocator-displacement caveat), so combined convexity-per-bleed must be measured jointly on held-out data, never assembled from standalone convexity numbers.
- This article is a construction argument built on [[where-convexity-still-lives]]'s selection verdict; if that verdict were wrong and some non-trend source did dominate on every axis at once, the budget would collapse back to a single mechanism. Nothing in the sweep suggests that, but the whole argument is downstream of "no single winner."
- **The budget is smaller in practice than in principle on our actual book.** All three off-sleeve levers this article proposes for the divergent seat have now been tested or scoped and none is currently deployable on the UCITS/IBKR-IE 5k substrate: left-tail-correlation sizing of the existing legs is a clean null (tail-parity, [[runs/atalanta/2026-07-07#atalanta_tailparity|2026-07-07]] - the sleeve's drawdown is a signal/whipsaw property no sizing tilt moves), the winter-cover value/economic-trend complement is substrate-blocked as a clean instrument (hypothesis 2 above), and the gap/long-variance axes are boundaries owned by other sleeves. So on this book the divergent seat's budget reduces to *the trend backbone plus the cross-tier coverage already in the tail and carry sleeves* - the construction argument stands as roster design, but the *new-line* ambition inside the seat is presently blocked by the substrate, not by the economics. That gap is itself the strongest case for the broader/futures-capable substrate ([[trend-following-under-a-fixed-per-order-fee]], [[the-multi-asset-substrate-for-the-roster]]).

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [ ] **Sizing the trend backbone's existing legs (and the book's convex tiers) by left-tail conditional correlation (overlay capacity) beats equal-risk and Sharpe weighting on combined held-out convexity-per-bleed.** The direct test of the Quantica reframing on our own book, and it respects instrument-orthogonality by *re-weighting exposures the book already holds* rather than adding instruments (e.g. the trend leg's own directional gold and rates positions). Negative control: Sharpe weighting, expected to over-weight the higher-Sharpe, less-crisis-negative legs. Lands as: a sizing rule across the divergent seat's own legs. **The robust proxy - tail-risk parity - was tested first and is a clean NULL, 2026-07-07** ([[runs/atalanta/2026-07-07#atalanta_tailparity|2026-07-07]]): sizing the champion's three legs by inverse Expected Shortfall instead of inverse volatility (the data-efficient, conditioning-bias-free version the asymmetric-correlation literature points to, chosen over the fragile equity-crisis-conditional tilt) left held-out worst-split drawdown *flat* (10.17-10.20% vs the champion's 10.18%) while shaving convexity (0.4451 -> 0.4303 as the tilt rises), so no dose Pareto-improves the champion. The mechanism lesson generalises against the whole hypothesis: this sleeve's worst-split drawdown is a *signal/whipsaw* property (the trend latch caught wrong-footed in a reversal), not a leg-sizing property - and the pyramiding arc reached the same wall from the response-function side. Re-weighting three curated, near-Gaussian, already-inverse-vol-sized legs cannot move a drawdown the signal itself creates, so the equity-conditioned "overlay capacity" version is **downgraded** (premise undercut; far more estimation-fragile) rather than a live next step. Where left-tail-correlation sizing plausibly still bites is *across the roster's tiers* (trend vs carry vs tail, whose streams genuinely differ in crisis) or on a *broad* book, not inside a 3-leg sleeve.
- [ ] **A cross-asset value / economic-trend UCITS leg, on its own signals and decomposed clean of equity beta and the credit-carry pole, lifts the divergent seat's trendless-winter (2023) return at |corr| < 0.10 to both poles.** Pre-register the Kaminski-style decomposition gate first (residual non-trend, non-equity alpha required) and a realized-skew >= 0 gate, then the 2023 held-out improvement; reject any carry-dominant vehicle on shape. Lands as: the one new instrument-orthogonal complement inside the divergent seat. **SUBSTRATE-BLOCKED as a clean instrument on the UCITS/IBKR-IE 5k book, 2026-07-07** (Exa design + availability sweep, [[runs/atalanta/2026-07-07]]): the *economics* are well-supported - cross-asset value is negatively correlated with momentum (Asness et al. "Value and Momentum Everywhere"), macro/economic momentum correlates ~0.4 with price trend and pays at the turning points trend misses (AQR "A Half Century of Macro Momentum"), and Lombard Odier's "finding value when trend fades" is precisely this profile (trend suffers in macro *uncertainty*; value and carry are resilient) - but no clean instrument exists EEA-retail. (1) The only UCITS value ETFs are *long-only equity* value-factor funds (iShares MSCI World/Europe Value, STOXX/World multifactor, Ossiam Shiller CAPE), all ~1.0 beta to equities: they fail the non-equity / decomposed-clean-of-equity-beta gate outright and would re-stack the decayed equity-trend corner. (2) No pure cross-asset value or macro-momentum UCITS ETF exists for EEA retail - those live in institutional ARP funds/swaps. (3) Self-building cross-asset value (long cheap / short expensive across asset-class ETFs) either *overlaps the roster* (it trades the same rates/gold/commodities atalanta already holds) or hits the *shorting + EUR-5k fixed-fee-floor* walls that killed [[runs/atalanta/2026-07-06#atalanta_cs_equity_momentum (v1) - the convex non-trend complement|cross-sectional momentum]]. The same UCITS-substrate ceiling, reached a third time. Re-entry: only on a broader / futures-capable substrate ([[trend-following-under-a-fixed-per-order-fee]] build-vs-buy), or bought wholesale via a carry-blended macro/ARP UCITS accepting the decomposition gate is failed.
- [ ] **The single-day-gap and long-variance axes stay in the tail and carry sleeves, not the divergent seat (a boundary, not a run).** Instrument-orthogonality, the cost-killed always-on VIX-ETP floor ([[always-on-convexity-from-listed-instruments]]), and demeter's ownership of the short-variance premium together fix this; the divergent seat's job is the slow-crisis and trendless-winter axes only. Recorded so any future "add a long-vol overlay to trend" proposal must first clear the non-overlap rule and the prior cost kill.
