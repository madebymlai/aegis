# The UCITS-Constrained Tail Sleeve: Convexity Without Account-Level Derivatives

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> For an EU-retail investor who cannot trade futures or options, the binding question for a tail sleeve is not *which instrument* but *where the derivatives live* - at the account (forbidden) or inside the fund wrapper (permitted) - and that one ruling decides whether a genuine immediate-gamma tail is even reachable: once it is, the only instrument that is both genuinely fast *and* backtestable on daily retail data is a UCITS VIX-futures ETF, which re-poses the standard VIXY question (spike versus uncarryable bleed) on an instrument a EU-retail book can finally hold and test, while the pure-physical fallback is a Swiss-franc-and-gold-led haven basket, not a dollar anchor.

This note builds the target tier of [[the-tiered-strategy-roster|the roster]] - the tail/defensive sleeve - under a constraint few backtests take seriously: UCITS-eligible, EU-retail-tradable, long-only, **no futures and no options traded at the account level**. That box kills the entire US-listed tail-hedge universe (PRIIPs blocks EU retail from US ETFs) and every instrument a US-domiciled backtest would reach for (VIXY, TAIL, BTAL, EDV, and any account-level detection trigger), leaving the question apparently airless. The resolution is that the constraint is less binding than it first reads, and where it *does* bind it points at a small, testable instrument set.

## The constraint reframed: where do the derivatives live

The decisive distinction is not the instrument but the level at which a derivative is held. UCITS itself permits funds to use derivatives internally - global exposure to 100% of NAV under the standard commitment approach, and well beyond that for the absolute-VaR cohort that runs systematic and alternative strategies.[^esma] A retail "no-derivatives" mandate operates one layer up: it forbids the investor's *own account* from executing or clearing futures, options or OTC swaps, but buying a unit of a UCITS fund is buying a **transferable security with a retail ISIN**, not trading a derivative - even when that fund packs options and futures internally.[^pinsent] So the design bifurcates into two regimes, and naming them is half the work:

- **Regime A (indirect derivative integration).** The account holds zero derivatives but allocates to UCITS funds that use them internally - buffer ETFs, volatility-risk-premium and long-volatility funds, managed-futures UCITS. These are fully cleared, retail-distributed, and carry the derivative risk on the *fund's* balance sheet under UCITS counterparty caps.
- **Regime B (pure physical convexity).** The stricter reading, where even the underlying fund may hold no derivatives, so convexity must come entirely from physical, long-only assets that are structurally asymmetric or safe-haven under stress - currencies, physical precious metals, government-bond ladders.

The roster's tail tier wants *immediate* long gamma for the fast gap that the trend floor is too slow to catch (a retired pre-UCITS survey ran the same search across US-listed convex instruments and killed every candidate). Only Regime A can deliver that immediacy. Regime B reaches a *defensive anchor*, not a convex tail. The whole design therefore turns on which regime the mandate actually permits, and that is the investor's ruling to make before any instrument is chosen.

## Regime A: the genuine tail exists, but a data contract culls it to one

Regime A opens the European liquid-alternatives shelf, and on paper it is rich: defined-outcome "buffer" ETFs that buy a protective put-spread financed by writing calls;[^spqh] active VRP funds that sell index puts and recycle premium into deep-OTM puts and long VIX calls for positive convexity;[^empureon] and managed-futures UCITS that manufacture convexity from directional trends.[^dbi] The membership gates of [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]] still apply, but a coarser gate culls the shelf first: **can the instrument's crisis behaviour be measured on our daily harness at all?** The operating discipline here is that a strategy earns its label on a realized stream, not a brochure.

On that gate most of the shelf fails (data verified via yfinance, June 2026):

- The buffer ETFs are too young. Global X's S&P 500 Quarterly Tail Hedge UCITS (`SPQH`) lists from 2023-02 and has lived entirely inside a melt-up - **it has never seen a crash on the harness**.[^spqh] Its protection is also a capped, quarterly-reset *buffer* (the -3% to -12% band), re-exposing the holder below the band, and it is swap-replicated against a single counterparty (Citigroup) - counterparty risk on the very hedge held for a systemic event.
- The managed-futures UCITS are either too young (the iMGP DBi UCITS ETF lists 2025-04) or, where they have a decade of history (Winton Trend 2018, Quantica 2015, Amundi Metori Epsilon),[^metori] they are *slow* trend - the lagged long gamma the trend floor already supplies. Adding them re-buys the floor's own pole; they are excluded on redundancy, not on quality.
- The long-volatility mutual funds (Amundi Funds Volatility World, since 2007; One River Dynamic Convexity) do carry crisis history - Amundi Volatility World returned roughly +22% in 2020 and stayed positive in 2018 and 2022 - but they are SICAV mutual funds, not exchange-traded, so clean daily NAV is hard to ingest, and they are *actively vol-timed* (they take short-volatility exposure when implied vol is high), which is why they bleed in calm years (2017 -16.9%, 2023 -8.9%) and only modestly gain in crises.[^amundivol] They are manager bets, not a clean convex stream.

What survives the data gate as both *genuinely fast* and *backtestable* is a single instrument family: the **UCITS VIX-futures ETF**. The Lyxor/Amundi S&P 500 VIX Futures Enhanced Roll UCITS ETF (`VOOL` on Xetra, `LVO` on Borsa Italiana, EUR-denominated) lists from **2013-01**, covering Feb-2018, Q4-2018, Mar-2020 and 2022, and it printed roughly **+294% by 18 March 2020** from its February low.[^allseasons] It is exchange-traded, retail-buyable across the EEA, and holds its derivatives internally - the textbook Regime A instrument, and the one the rest of the shelf was supposed to improve upon but cannot, because they trade history for either softness (buffers) or discretion (active vol funds).

## VOOL is the UCITS VIXY: an old verdict, now testable

The catch is that `VOOL` is not a new mechanism - it is the UCITS wrapper around exactly the instrument prior research already killed. A constant-maturity short-dated VIX-futures position loses on the order of 30% a year in equilibrium, the variance risk premium paid to volatility sellers, and the tracking ETPs realize calm-year decays far worse;[^erakerwu] Whaley's standing verdict is that they are "virtually guaranteed to lose money through time."[^whaley] The "Enhanced Roll" construction shifts dynamically along the VIX curve to mute contango, but it cannot remove a premium that is structural, not an inefficiency. So the prior is strong that `VOOL` reproduces the VIXY result: a spectacular fast-crash spike sitting on a calm-year bleed too large to carry at any weight that moves the tail.

The advance is therefore not the instrument but the *epistemics*. The original VIXY verdict was inherited from US data the EU-retail book could never hold; `VOOL` is holdable, EUR-denominated, and ten years deep, so the spike-versus-bleed exchange rate can be measured directly on our own harness across all four in-window crises and judged against the trend floor, rather than assumed. A kill here would be ours, on our instrument, at our weights - which is worth more than a borrowed kill. And the one structural difference from VIXY is favourable for us: `VOOL` is EUR-quoted, so for a euro book its crisis spike arrives in the numeraire we actually spend.

## Regime B: the physical anchor is Swiss-franc-and-gold-led, not dollar-led

If the mandate forbids even internal derivatives, the tail degrades to a physical defensive anchor, and the search overturned the common instinct that the dollar carries it. The dollar's left-side smile has been compromised into what one desk calls a "structural smirk": the USD *depreciated* through recent equity drawdowns (the April-2025 tariff shock), driven by a US net-foreign-debt position near 90% of GDP, foreign liquidation of US tech in risk-off, prohibitive hedging costs that left allocations unhedged, and reserve de-dollarisation away from Treasuries.[^smirk][^berenberg] The instrument-level consequence is measured: the EUR-unhedged short-Treasury line (`IBC1`, history to 2005) returned roughly **-7.77% in EUR in 2025**, and by early 2026 the stock-bond correlation had flipped positive again, so Treasuries stopped hedging at exactly the moment they were needed.[^quantdecoded]

The academic record promotes two other havens above the dollar. The Swiss franc is, over 1999-2021, **superior to gold** as a defensive asset for global equity portfolios - delivering over 80% variance reduction at a *negative* hedge ratio, meaning a CHF-hedged book can hold *more* equity, and at a cheaper hedging cost than gold.[^chfgold] Gold, in turn, is the most regime-robust single leg, the one asset that protects in *both* deflationary crashes and inflationary/sovereign-debt cycles, and the only one passing the crisis-and-inflation test simultaneously in the 2026 stagflation-adjacent regime.[^quantdecoded][^cheema] But neither is unconditional: the Swiss franc hedged the GFC and European stress yet *failed* in the COVID-2020 scramble for some European investors; gold was "very risky" in parts of COVID and was sold in the March-2020 dash for cash; the dollar broke in 2025; the yen broke in 2022 under yield-curve control.[^cheema][^siema] Each canonical haven has a regime that turns it off.

That is the construction lesson, and it is the same one [[havens-at-regime-turning-points]] reaches from the other side: do not pick the haven, and do not momentum-rotate between them (rotation holds yesterday's regime through the flip). Hold an **always-on basket of imperfect havens whose failure regimes do not coincide** - gold and the Swiss franc as the two anchors, an optional short-dated unhedged USD leg for the growth-shock cell the other two are weakest in - fixed-weight and continuously rebalanced. The euro numeraire makes the FX convexity visible in the series - invisible to a dollar-denominated book - and the deep FX history (EUR/CHF and EUR/USD to 2003, gold to 2011) makes the whole basket backtestable through the GFC.

## The defensive-anchor ceiling, and why a tail still needs Regime A

The physical basket reaches the *signs* of the tail/defensive gates - negative bear-regime beta, non-negative crisis-conditional return, positive skew - but not the *strongly* magnitudes. It loses less; it does not gain a lot. The dash-for-cash failure caps it: in the fastest growth shock on record even gold and Treasuries were sold at the equity low before they worked, which is precisely the immediacy a physical haven cannot guarantee. So the constraint box does not forbid a tail; it forces a clean choice. A genuine immediate-gamma tail is reachable only in Regime A, and within Regime A only `VOOL` is both fast and testable - at the cost of a structural bleed. A pure-physical anchor is cheap, derivative-free and robust - at the cost of being a defensive sleeve, not a convex tail, and one that partly overlaps the floor it is meant to supplement.

## Monetization is the rebalancing rule, not a timing call

Whichever leg is chosen, the standing tail-hedging objection holds: over a full cycle the hedge drags, and its value is not standalone return but the structural risk-overweight in the core that its drawdown protection licenses.[^gstail] The mechanism that converts a bleeding hedge into compounding is *monetization* - selling the hedge into the spike and buying risk at the trough - and a fixed-weight, continuously rebalanced sleeve **is** that rule automatically: holding the weight forces selling the convex leg as it spikes and the book falls.[^soa] This matters more under our constraints because we cannot discretionarily trade options around the position; the rebalance is the only lever, and the literature's warning is that missing it is expensive (Invesco's April-2020 failure to execute a scheduled rebalance during the COVID dislocation cost an estimated $105m).[^soa] A constant-mix rebalance is itself a concave, short-strangle-like strategy whose premium is positive in range-bound markets and negative in trends; pairing it with a convex sleeve and monetizing at the trough is what turns the pair positive across the cycle.[^soa] The roster's "deploy tail episodically" prescription survives only in its weak form: size the sleeve small and let the rebalance monetize it, because every *trigger* to size it up fired post-shock on daily bars.

## Limitations

- The structural-smirk thesis rests on 2025-2026 episodes (the April-2025 tariff drawdown, the 2026 positive stock-bond correlation) - an n of one or two, the familiar few-windows fragility of any regime-break claim. It may be regime-specific rather than a permanent break; the dollar leg is demoted, not deleted, for that reason.
- `VOOL`'s ten-year history contains only two genuine fast crashes (2018, 2020) plus the 2022 grind it should *lose*. Everything inferred about the spike-versus-bleed exchange rate rests on that thin crisis count, and the VIX-ETP roll regime varies enough year to year that a weight sized on the average bleeds more in heavy-contango years.
- The Swiss-franc-superiority result is measured on monthly data over 1999-2021 via DCC hedge ratios; whether the advantage survives at daily frequency, net of the cost of holding CHF through a UCITS instrument (most clean CHF exposure for a euro investor is a CHF government-bond or money-market UCITS, whose FX is the convex part and whose carry is currently negative), is unverified and is itself a feasibility question.
- The active long-vol funds (Amundi Volatility, One River) were excluded on data-ingestion and manager-discretion grounds, not on merit; if a clean daily NAV series can be sourced, Amundi Volatility World's decade through every crisis would be a stronger backtest than `VOOL`'s and deserves a revisit.
- Regime A still carries counterparty risk inside the wrapper (UCITS caps OTC exposure at 10% of NAV per counterparty and requires daily collateral, but the cap is not zero), which is most dangerous in precisely the systemic event the sleeve is bought for.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

These are a bake-off: two EUR-denominated candidate tail legs, both backtestable across Feb-2018 / Q4-2018 / Mar-2020 / 2022, judged against the trend floor on the convexity-per-bleed and crisis-conditional gates of [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]] and [[measuring-crisis-alpha]]. Per-run keep/kill thresholds are pre-registered in the aegis run diary before each launch.

- [ ] **The testable rented-vol tail (Regime A).** A small fixed-weight `VOOL` sleeve, continuously rebalanced (the weight is the monetization rule), added to the trend floor, improves the held-out fast-crash splits (2018, 2020) by enough to clear the campaign's standing VIXY gate (2018 split improvement >= +0.3 over the floor-0 control AND composite >= control - 0.05 AND neither calm split drops > 0.3), at a weight in {0.02, 0.05}. Pre-registered prior from the pre-UCITS VIXY-floor kill ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084627805837Z_etf_aegis_v22_vixyfloor|v22]]): this most likely *fails* as VIXY did (the spike does not carry the bleed) - but the kill is now ours, on a holdable instrument, and a pass would be the first genuine immediate-gamma tail the UCITS box admits.
- [ ] **The physical defensive anchor (Regime B).** An always-on Swiss-franc-and-gold basket (EUR/CHF + `SGLN`, fixed weights, rebalanced) clears the *defensive* gates (negative bear-regime beta, non-negative crisis-conditional return) but **not** the *strongly* tail magnitudes - confirming it as an anchor, not a convex tail - and improves the floor's transition-window drawdowns at lower calm-year bleed than `VOOL`.
- [ ] **Does the dollar leg still earn its place.** Adding a small short-dated unhedged USD leg (`IBC1`/`IB01`) to the CHF+gold basket helps the growth-shock cell (2020) but *hurts* 2022 and 2025-style rate/tariff shocks - testing whether the structural smirk has demoted the dollar from anchor to optional, or removed it entirely.
- [ ] **Bake-off: tail versus anchor on the roster's own bar.** Ranking the floor-plus-`VOOL` pair against the floor-plus-physical-basket pair by held-out Calmar and crisis-conditional return decides which leg "earns its bleed" - the roster's open *Target* hypothesis - and whether the UCITS tail tier is a rented-vol tail, a physical anchor, or (most likely) a small blend of both.
