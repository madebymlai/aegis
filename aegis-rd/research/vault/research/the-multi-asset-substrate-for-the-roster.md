# The Multi-Asset Substrate for the Roster: What to Use

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> "Which asset class is best" is the wrong question for the [[the-tiered-strategy-roster|tiered roster]]: the answer is a *structure*, not a class - one broad, non-equity-tilted, cross-asset futures book traded long *and* short, from which each tier draws a different slice. Multi-asset wins, but only in long/short trend form: the same commodities and currencies that are estimation-risk-*harmful* as passive longs become the convex legs once a trend signal sits on them, and the breadth that pays is the number of *independent* macro bets (mid-teens), not the ticker count.

## Which asset class is the wrong question

The roster's organizing move is that an allocator needs an *order of operations*, not a count of mechanisms.[[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] The same move applies to the universe. Asking "stocks or bonds or commodities, single-asset or multi-asset" invites a horse-race between classes, when the choice is set by two axes the class label hides: the *role* an exposure plays on the convexity axis, and the *form* it is held in (passive long versus long/short trend). Get those two right and the asset class falls out; get them wrong and no class is the answer, because the same commodity book is a portfolio poison held one way and the convex core held the other.

So this note does not pick a winning asset class. It establishes the one substrate every tier shares, shows that the crisis convexity the roster is built around lives in the *non-equity* legs of that substrate, resolves the apparent contradiction that broad multi-asset diversification often fails to pay, and maps each tier to the slice it draws. The deep construction of each slice already lives in its own note - the trend universe in [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]], the income pole in [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]], the tail in [[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]]; the job here is the integrating map and the "what to use" verdict.

## One substrate serves every tier

The substrate is a broad cross-asset futures book - equity indices, government bonds and short rates, developed and emerging currencies, and physical commodities - held long or short and risk-weighted so no single class dominates. This is the managed-futures universe, and the case for it as a portfolio's core diversifier is old and well-replicated. Lintner's 1983 study established that adding managed futures to stocks and bonds improved the efficient frontier materially;[^lintner] the century-long reconstruction finds time-series momentum on this universe positive in 8 of the 10 worst drawdowns of a 60/40 portfolio over 1880-2016.[^century] A peer-reviewed stochastic-spanning test makes the static version of the point: stock-bond portfolios are *spanned* by - cannot replicate - portfolios augmented with individual commodity futures, FX and real estate, and the spanning benefit is strongest in down markets.[^spanning] More than one class is not decoration; it expands the reachable frontier, most where it is wanted.

The substrate is also *shared*: one non-equity-tilted book sources more than one tier at once. Carry, the floor's concave income pole, is a premium in all nine of the asset classes the same futures book spans, so the long/short trend sleeve and the carry sleeve are read off the same instruments with different signals.[^carry] That is why the floor can be built as a single book rather than two disjoint ones, and why the breadth argument below binds the whole roster, not just its trend tier.

## Crisis alpha is in the non-equity legs

The sharpest "what to use" finding is also the least intuitive. The convexity the roster's floor and target tiers exist to supply does *not* come from short-equity positions in a crash. It comes from the rates, currency and commodity legs.

Man Group's decomposition of trend returns through equity crises is explicit: most would expect sustained equity weakness to pay through short-equity positions, but historically the crisis alpha has come from fixed income, commodities and currencies.[^truthortrend] Fidelity's attribution reaches the same place from a different method - most of the crisis-alpha character and most of the return of a trend strategy come from "beta timing" across the four non-equity-dominated classes, with the cross-sectional equity component a minority of the risk.[^fidelity] ThinkNewfound supplies the mechanism: a diversified book's left-tail payoff is generated when cross-asset correlations crash to extremes in stress, letting the notional in short-equity / long-bond / short-commodity trades far exceed what an equity-only book could carry at the same risk target - and, crucially, the effect requires *enough other markets to be trending at once*, which is a property of breadth, not of the equity leg.[^newfound] The peer-reviewed CTA-positions study closes the loop: crisis alpha originates from two sources, diversification across many futures markets (gains in non-crisis sectors offset the crisis sector) and fast exposure reduction in the crisis sector itself, not from a directional equity short.[^crisismyth]

The construction consequence is direct: tilt the substrate *away* from equity. Adding correlated equity sectors raises the ticker count while diluting the convex tail, because an equity-dominated book collapses onto a single market factor and a lagged trend signal on it realizes the short-gamma opposite of what it is meant to (the substrate requirement, argued in [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]]). The convexity is in the legs that decorrelate from equities, so those are the legs to weight.

## Only as long/short trend: passive multi-asset is no free lunch

The case for multi-asset has a credible opposing literature, and ignoring it would make the recommendation wrong. Naive breadth often does *not* pay. A peer-reviewed study finds that adding five alternative asset classes (real estate, commodities, hedge funds, emerging markets, private equity) to a stock-bond book was *harmful* for US investors over 1997-2015, and that for commodities the harm persists even when the credit crisis is excluded - the cause is estimation risk, which grows with the number of noisy-input classes.[^harmful] Morningstar's repeated finding is that an eleven-asset diversified book lost to plain 60/40 on the Sharpe ratio over most rolling ten-year windows since ~2005, because the diversifiers cut risk but not enough to clear their return give-up, and correlations converge in exactly the crises diversification is bought for.[^morningstar] Practitioner work converges on a low ceiling for *passive* streams: as few as three to five lowly-correlated return streams capture most of the achievable risk-adjusted gain.[^alphaarch]

These do not contradict the substrate case; they pin down its *form*. The harmful-diversification result is about holding alternatives as **passive longs**, where each class contributes a noisy mean estimate and a long-only beta that re-correlates in a crash. The crisis-alpha result is about holding the *same* classes under a **long/short trend or carry signal**, where the position flips to short in a downtrend and the payoff is convex rather than collinear. Gresham's identity makes the gap quantitative: the correlation between two trend P&Ls scales as the *square* of the underlying-market correlation, so a trend overlay roughly halves the effective beta each market carries to the common factor (≈0.16 versus ≈0.4) and a simple trend book is more diversified than an equity book of the same name count.[^gresham] Passive multi-asset and long/short multi-asset are different objects: the first is the one the counter-literature finds harmful, the second is the one the crisis-alpha literature finds convex. What to use is the second.

The same identity carries the warning that breadth is not free even in trend form: pile in too many markets and the book drifts back toward one concentrated macro bet, so quality of orthogonality beats quantity of names.[^gresham] That is the bridge to the ceiling.

## The role-by-role map

With the substrate and its form fixed, each tier draws a named slice, and each slice has its own note.

- **Floor, convex pole (trend).** Non-equity cross-asset futures - the rate curve, real rates, the dollar and crosses, broad and single commodities - long/short. Source the convexity where equities are not. Construction and the breadth ceiling: [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]].
- **Floor, concave pole (carry/income).** Carry pays across every class, but the negative skew that makes it trend's true mirror lives in **FX and commodities** (and, duration-stripped, in **credit**), not in equity or bond carry - so a generic all-asset carry basket comes out near-symmetric and only half-concave.[^carry] Source the pole to the mechanism: [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]. The variance risk premium is the strongest single concave source.
- **Target, tail (immediate gamma).** Not "more multi-asset": a thin, regime-robust slice. The physical anchor is **Swiss-franc-and-gold-led**, not dollar-led (the franc beats gold as a defensive asset over 1999-2021 at a cheaper hedge ratio;[^chfgold] gold is the most regime-robust single leg and the only major asset passing crisis *and* inflation tests in the 2026 stagflation-adjacent regime, while Treasuries have been failing the safe-haven test on a positive stock-bond correlation[^safehaven]). True immediate gamma needs a rented-vol leg held episodically: [[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]].
- **Expansion, market-neutral.** Off the skew axis, breadth-hungry, and therefore the most substrate-dependent: it wants a wide cross-section to rank and is starved by a thin one. It belongs last and is breadth-gated, not foreclosed, at a ten-instrument universe.[[the-tiered-strategy-roster]]

The single thread through the map: the convex tiers want the substrate tilted *away* from equity, and the concave/tail tiers want it sourced to a *specific* mechanism (funding-unwind skew for carry, regime-uncorrelated havens for tail), not spread evenly across classes.

## Breadth saturates in the mid-teens

Multi-asset is best, but it stops paying sooner than the ticker count suggests. By Grinold's law risk-adjusted skill scales as $IR \approx IC \times \sqrt{BR}$, with breadth measured in *independent* bets;[^grinold] under equicorrelation a book of $N$ assets at average correlation $\rho$ carries $N_{\text{eff}} = N / (1 + (N-1)\rho)$ independent bets,[^resolve] so the lever is $\rho$, not $N$. A fifty-name equity-sector book at $\rho \approx 0.85$ collapses to roughly one bet; a cross-asset book at $\rho \approx 0.20$ keeps several from the same count. The practitioner consensus that three to five lowly-correlated streams capture most of the gain[^alphaarch] and Gresham's "too many markets returns you to a concentrated bet"[^gresham] both point at a low-teens ceiling, and our own breadth-saturation run located the knee concretely: trend quality peaks near twenty names (Tier 1) and *degrades* through the refinement tiers as the added names re-correlate with equities (see [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]] and [[runs/atalanta/2026-06-13]]). What to use is a mid-teens-to-twenty-name book of orthogonal macro axes, not the widest available list.

## What to actually use

For an EU-retail book that cannot trade futures directly, the substrate is reachable through wrappers, not only by building it. The convex floor is available off-the-shelf as a managed-futures UCITS: the iMGP DBi Managed Futures UCITS ETF (`DBMF`) is Europe's first such ETF, replicating leading CTAs across equities, bonds, FX and commodities;[^dbi] Quantica (≈110 markets across five classes), Winton Trend and Amundi Metori Epsilon (45+ futures) are genuine long/short cross-asset CTAs in UCITS form.[^quantica] A note of caution that follows directly from the crisis-alpha finding: the Article-8 *ex-commodities* variant of `DBMF` strips one of the very legs that supplies crisis alpha, so it is the wrong variant for the floor pole. A custom book on the same axes is built natively from single-axis UCITS ETPs (the tiered list in [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]]); the physical tail anchor is a fixed-weight Swiss-franc-and-gold basket ([[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]]). The wrapper changes; the substrate - non-equity-tilted, cross-asset, long/short - does not.

## Limitations

- **The passive/trend distinction is the load-bearing claim and rests on a comparison across literatures, not a single controlled study.** The harmful-diversification evidence is on passive longs;[^harmful] the crisis-alpha evidence is on long/short trend.[^truthortrend][^crisismyth] No one paper runs both arms on an identical universe and cost model, so the cleanest confirmation is our own pipeline (first hypothesis below), not a citation.
- **Crisis alpha from the non-equity legs is regime-conditional.** ThinkNewfound's own caveat is that the payoff requires enough markets to trend simultaneously, which past crises delivered but future ones need not.[^newfound] The most influential supporting decompositions are from CTA managers (conflict of interest);[^truthortrend] the peer-reviewed CTA-positions study is the neutral backbone.[^crisismyth]
- **Multi-asset's Sharpe verdict depends on the objective.** On *unconditional* Sharpe, plain 60/40 has beaten broad diversification since ~2005;[^morningstar] the substrate earns its slot on crisis-conditional and drawdown-path terms, not raw Sharpe. An allocator optimizing unconditional Sharpe in a calm regime will not choose it.
- **The breadth ceiling is reasoned, not universally measured.** The mid-teens figure comes from average-correlation arithmetic and one breadth-saturation run on our trend book; it has not been measured at the whole-roster (multi-tier) level.
- **Carry's concave skew can switch off.** G10 FX carry flattened after 2008 as rate differentials compressed, so the FX-sourced skew the income pole depends on is itself regime-dependent (detailed in [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]).

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [ ] **Trend, not breadth, is what makes multi-asset pay in crisis.** On our cross-asset book, a long/short trend overlay clears the crisis-conditional gate where an equal-risk *passive-long* basket of the identical legs does not - isolating *form* (long/short) from *breadth* as the source of crisis alpha, and confirming the resolution of the harmful-diversification counter-case on our own data.
- [ ] **Crisis alpha is in the non-equity legs.** Attribute the floor sleeve's crisis-window P&L by asset-class leg; the rates, FX and commodity legs supply the positive crisis return and the equity-index leg supplies little or only a short-equity sliver - reproducing the Man/Fidelity decomposition on our universe and justifying the non-equity tilt empirically rather than by citation.
- [ ] **Passive cross-asset breadth is estimation-risk-harmful below the trend line.** A naive mean-variance multi-asset allocation underperforms a fixed-weight stock/bond core on held-out Sharpe at our universe size, while the long/short trend book on the same assets does not - the controlled version of the harmful-diversification result.
- [ ] **One substrate sources both floor poles.** A single non-equity cross-asset book simultaneously yields the convex (trend) and concave (carry) sleeves at near-zero pole correlation, confirming the shared-substrate claim (partially seen in the floor runs - [[runs/floor/2026-06-14]] - to be re-tested as an explicit single-book construction).
