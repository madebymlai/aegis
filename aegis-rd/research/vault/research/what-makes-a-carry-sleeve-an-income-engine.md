# What Makes a Carry Sleeve an Income Engine

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> The income-engine shape a carry sleeve is hired for - steady premium in calm, a *budgeted* loss in the tail - is destroyed by the objective used to select it: the Sharpe-maximizing payoff is, by mathematical construction, "regular modest profits punctuated by occasional crashes", so Sharpe-ranking a short-gamma config sweep selects the candidate that best hides its tail. The manipulation-proof MPPM fixes the *gaming* but is still not the ranker, because it is blind to the pole's *portfolio* job: **rank on the composite** - the pole's manipulation-proof certainty-equivalent contribution to the blended two-pole book ($\Delta\hat\Theta$, which prices income, tail, and crisis-correlation to trend in one number) - and use the single-book measures as *gates*: a robust (L-moment) skew for family membership, a multi-month tail budget for survivability, a downside-correlation ceiling to trend for the crisis-conditional guard. Skew is a mandate the pole must hold, never an objective to maximize (maximizing depth buys uncompensated crash). Source the signal from spread *richness* (the value half of carry), because the raw spread *level* is mostly repriced credit beta. And distrust every daily statistic of an income stream, including the correlation guard itself: smoothed credit marks flatter them all, which is why the gates live on multi-month windows and the stream's autocorrelation (or Doubt Ratio) is a standing diagnostic. And conditioning a credit sleeve is two decisions, not one: whether to scale exposure, and which risk - default or liquidity - the tail is actually pricing once stress arrives.

The trend article ([[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]) ended in a selection rule: a convex sleeve ranked on Sharpe silently becomes the least convex book, so it must be ranked on its hired shape. This article is the mirror for the concave pole, and the trap here is worse. For trend, Sharpe-ranking merely *neglects* the hired shape; for carry, Sharpe-ranking *is the attack surface*. A short-gamma sleeve does not drift away from its job under a Sharpe objective - it over-performs its job, hiding ever more tail to buy ever more smoothness, and the optimizer rewards every step. Any pipeline that ranks its carry sweep on Sharpe - the default objective most sweeps start from - is selecting the roster's income pole ([[the-tiered-strategy-roster]], [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]) by exactly the objective the literature says it can game.

## Sharpe is the adversary's objective, not the evaluator's

The result is old and exact. Goetzmann, Ingersoll, Spiegel and Welch derive the payoff that maximizes the Sharpe ratio when returns can be shaped freely (with options or dynamic trading): it has a truncated right tail and a fat left tail, and they state the mechanism plainly - "expected returns being held constant, high Sharpe ratio strategies are, by definition, strategies that generate regular modest profits punctuated by occasional crashes".[^gisw] That is not a description of a pathological corner case; it is a description of a premium-collection sleeve. Spurgin had already shown the trade empirically: an out-of-the-money put-writing overlay smooths measured Sharpe upward with a two-in-three chance of surviving three years before the tail ever prints.[^spurgin] Leland closes the theory from the pricing side - mean-variance measures systematically *reward* short-gamma payoffs and penalize long-gamma ones even when true risk-adjusted alpha is zero.[^leland]

The reason this bites us specifically is that a parameter sweep is an optimization over payoff shapes. Eling and Schuhmacher's well-known counter-result - that across 2,763 live hedge funds, Sharpe and twelve alternative measures produce virtually identical rankings (98 of the Sharpe top-100 are also in the Omega top-100) - is real, and it is the best opposing source to this article's whole argument.[^eling] But its universe is a *cross-section of funds nobody optimized against the measure*; the authors attribute the result to hedge-fund returns being approximately elliptical, in which case the first two moments carry the ranking. A config sweep whose objective is Sharpe is the adversarial case their finding does not cover: the optimizer's job is precisely to find the non-elliptical candidate the measure cannot see. The mechanism also operates at the blend level: calm-drawdown-path-dominated objectives (UPI, Sharpe) over-reward carry's calm smoothing and over-buy the concave pole in a skew-neutral blend - a measured instance is recorded in the floor hypothesis trail of [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]].

Three further failures stack on top for this sleeve in particular. Lo shows that the Sharpe ratio's own sampling machinery breaks under the serial correlation typical of premium-collection streams - naive annualization can overstate a fund's annual Sharpe by up to 65%, enough to reshuffle rankings on its own.[^lo] The serial correlation itself is not incidental to credit: Getmansky, Lo and Makarov show that the autocorrelation of income-style streams is chiefly a symptom of *smoothed marks* - illiquid holdings priced by extrapolation report returns smoother than the economic truth, understating volatility and inflating Sharpe, worst in exactly the fixed-income categories a credit sleeve lives in.[^glm] And Bailey and Lopez de Prado show that selecting the best of N swept configurations inflates the expected maximum Sharpe mechanically, with fat-tailed and negatively-skewed winners inflating most - their Deflated Sharpe Ratio exists to correct exactly the "pick the sweep's Sharpe-winner" procedure we run.[^dsr]

## The manipulation-proof shape

The same Goetzmann et al. paper that names the disease supplies the cure. A performance measure is manipulation-proof when no payoff-shaping can raise the score without adding genuine expected utility; they prove the measure is essentially unique, and it is a power-utility certainty equivalent computed on the realized return stream:[^gisw]

$$\hat{\Theta} = \frac{1}{(1-\rho)\,\Delta t}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\frac{1+r_t}{1+r_{f,t}}\right)^{1-\rho}\right)$$

$\hat{\Theta}$ reads as the annualized risk-free-excess growth rate a CRRA evaluator with relative risk aversion $\rho$ would accept as certain in exchange for the stream - income net of the tail it hides, in one number. It needs nothing beyond the net return series, it has no threshold or horizon knob, and its single parameter is interpretable (they suggest evaluators in the $\rho \approx 2$-$4$ range). A day approaching total loss drives the score to $-\infty$: the measure prices the left tail the way the sleeve's owner actually experiences it, rather than averaging it away. This is the concave pole's analog of the trend pole's `trend_convexity_payoff` - a benchmark-free ranker that scores the *hired shape* - and it satisfies the same stabilizer discipline (a smooth utility-weighted mean, no cutoff to choose, low estimator variance) that made the crisis-payoff metric split-stable.

The measure is neither exotic nor untested. Morningstar's risk-adjusted return - the engine under its star ratings - is an MPPM variant with $\rho$ near 3, so the functional form has been ranking retail funds in production for two decades.[^mppmtail] Its tail pricing has been quantified: a Cornish-Fisher analysis finds the MPPM penalty rises nearly linearly with negative skewness (about 3% of certainty-equivalent per unit of skew in the base case) and steepens sharply with volatility and $\rho$ - so the measure does, mechanically, what this section hires it for.[^mppmtail] And its empirical validation ran on exactly the hostile terrain that matters here: on TASS hedge-fund data (skewed, fat-tailed), the MPPM ranks more truthfully than the conventional measures, and its authors extract a byproduct worth stealing - the **Doubt Ratio**, the risk aversion implied by comparing MPPM values across $\rho$, whose extreme values flag return streams that have been smoothed or manipulated (it flags Madoff).[^bki] For a credit sleeve whose marks can smooth mechanically ([^glm], below), that is a purpose-built trust check, not a curiosity. The pair has since been used in a peer-reviewed setting: hedge-fund portfolios formed on Sharpe and information ratios select funds whose performance is *persistently doubtful* by the Doubt Ratio, while portfolios formed on the excess MPPM carry far less doubt - the measure pair performing, in a journal, exactly the separation it is hired for here.[^gps]

One boundary keeps the enthusiasm honest: manipulation-proof *evaluation* is not manipulation-proof *certification*. Foster and Young prove that no compensation or screening mechanism built on track records - MPPM-based ones included - can separate skilled managers from lucky mimics without also deterring the skilled.[^fosteryoung] Translated to a sweep: the MPPM stops a candidate from *gaming* the score, but it cannot certify that the winner's score is skill rather than best-of-N luck - which is exactly the residual job of the Deflated Sharpe Ratio trust check.[^dsr]

Why not the more familiar alternatives? The moment-corrected Sharpe family (the Pezier-White adjusted Sharpe, $ASR = SR[1 + \tfrac{S}{6}SR - \tfrac{K-3}{24}SR^2]$) looks like the cheap fix, but Smetters and Zhang show Taylor-expansion corrections of this kind can misrank, destabilize, and even diverge outside near-normal territory - fine as a screen, not as the arbiter.[^smetters] The tail-ratio family (Omega, CVaR-based STARR and Rachev ratios) aims at the right economics but ranks on tail quantiles a daily sample estimates poorly: Varga-Haszonits and Kondor prove estimation error under downside measures diverges at a critical parameters-to-sample ratio, exactly the small-crisis-count regime a 2-year split lives in.[^kondor] The division of labor that follows the vault's existing pattern: **rank** on the smooth, low-variance utility ($\hat{\Theta}$), **gate** on the tail budget measured where a short-gamma blowup actually plays out - the left decile of overlapping multi-month compounded windows, band-averaged over the same 2-6 month horizons the trend metrics use so no single window is load-bearing - and **trust-check** the sweep winner post hoc with the Deflated Sharpe Ratio,[^dsr] the concave mirror of ranking trend on the point estimate and reporting the block-bootstrap CI.

## The metric to rank on: single-book gates, composite ranker

The manipulation-proof measure fixes the *gaming* problem, and it is still not the metric to rank a carry sweep on - because it is a function of the carry stream alone, and the pole's actual job is relational. The pole is hired to pay income in calm and to place a budgeted loss where the trend pole is *up*, so the book's compounded path is smoother than trend's alone. No statistic of the carry stream by itself can see that placement: two candidates with an identical multiset of daily returns - identical mean, vol, tail, skew, and MPPM - can differ entirely in whether their losses land on trend's worst days or its best, and only a portfolio-aware metric separates them. This is not hypothetical: a standalone-MPPM-ranked champion can quietly defect - reading a healthy standalone MPPM while its correlation to trend flips positive and its skew shrinks toward zero (see the hypotheses below). The lesson generalizes past the ranker A/B: manipulation-proofness protects against payoff-shaping *within* a stream; it does not protect against selecting a stream whose diversification value is gone ([[the-skew-is-the-product]]).

So the selection is two-staged, and only the second stage is the true objective. First, **single-book gates** shortlist candidates a per-candidate metric can compute in the sweep: the family-membership skew gate (below), the tail budget, and standalone MPPM as an income floor. Then the **composite ranker** orders the shortlist by the pole's contribution to the blended *book*.

The composite ranker is the manipulation-proof certainty-equivalent of the two-pole book, scored as a delta against trend alone:

$$\Delta\hat{\Theta} = \hat{\Theta}\big((1-w)\,\tilde r_{\text{trend}} + w\,\tilde r_{\text{carry}}\big) - \hat{\Theta}\big(\tilde r_{\text{trend}}\big)$$

where each leg $\tilde r$ is scaled to the book's mandated volatility, $w$ is the fixed carry weight (0.40, the floor rule), and $\hat\Theta$ is the same MPPM used standalone. Four properties make this the right allocator metric, each with support:

- **Manipulation-proof at the level that matters.** Goetzmann et al. prove $\hat\Theta$ is the essentially-unique measure no payoff-shaping can game, and note the case is "particularly compelling" for nonlinear payoffs;[^gisw] applied to the *book*, the carry pole cannot raise its score by any shaping that hides tail from the book - only by genuinely improving the book's utility-weighted growth.
- **It is the marginal portfolio contribution, formalized.** $\Delta\hat\Theta$ is exactly Tasche's marginal risk contribution $\rho(X) - \rho(X - X_i)$ (with $\rho$ the negative certainty equivalent) - the "with-minus-without" the total-portfolio-approach literature demands ("there are no buckets").[^tasche][^tpa]
- **It prices income, tail, and crisis-correlation in one number.** Power utility ($\rho > 1$) is convex in losses, so it weights the book's joint-loss states most; a pole whose losses fall in trend's *gains* never deepens the book's tail and scores high, while one that co-crashes with trend deepens it and scores low - the crisis-conditional correlation gate, priced smoothly and without a threshold. Certainty equivalent is the performance measure that dominates Sharpe precisely under the skewness this sleeve carries.[^brito][^skewaware]
- **It ranks placement, not scale.** Each leg is pinned to the book vol before blending, so the carry pole's own volatility - a mandate, not a free knob - cannot buy a higher score; the blend's volatility then floats with the correlation structure, which is the diversification signal. This closes the ranker A/B failure directly: MPPM on a free scale axis ranks estimation noise, but on a leg-vol-pinned composite it ranks the shape and placement it is meant to.

A credit pole can pass its standalone income measure and still **subtract certainty-equivalent from the blended book** (see the hypotheses below) - the composite metric states in one number what standalone ranking cannot see.

The relational guard is reported alongside, because a single sample's $\Delta\hat\Theta$ could be luck: the pole's **downside correlation** to trend - its correlation measured on trend's worst-decile days (Ang, Chen and Xing's downside correlation, the conditional measure that captures asymmetry an unconditional coskewness misses).[^acx] Full-sample correlation is not enough: Page and Panariello show it systematically hides crisis co-movement,[^pp] and a concave pole can read a mildly positive full-sample correlation to trend while its downside correlation runs several times higher - co-crashing far worse than the headline number admits (a measured instance is in the hypotheses below).

Smoothed marks bias even the guard. The Getmansky-Lo-Makarov result cuts one level deeper than Sharpe inflation: a smoothed stream's *contemporaneous betas and correlations are biased toward zero*, with the true co-movement displaced into lagged terms - so stale credit marks make a carry pole look more diversifying than it is, precisely on the statistic hired to catch co-crashing.[^glm] This is the econometric justification for a prescription the vault already runs on other grounds: measure the gates on overlapping multi-month compounded windows (temporal aggregation re-absorbs the lagged co-movement the daily numbers hide), and treat the sleeve's first-order autocorrelation - or its Doubt Ratio[^bki] - as a standing diagnostic rather than a footnote.

Two deliberate non-choices. We do **not** rank on skew depth directly: maximizing negative skew is maximizing insurance sold, and an optimizer pointed at "most negative" will buy *uncompensated* crash risk - the worst trade available. Skew is a mandate the pole must satisfy, not an objective to push. And we do not rank on coskewness with the market: it is priced (Harvey-Siddique put the premium near 3%/yr) but, as they stress, "very challenging to measure," so it stays a diagnostic, not a ranker.[^hs] The recent tail-hedge-allocation framing names the same division of labor from the CVaR side: a hedge sleeve's quality is not one-dimensional but a bundle of conditional convexity, tail-event reliability, non-stress carry, and drawdown persistence - a scalar objective aggregates them, and the gates keep each honest.[^cvarhedge]

### The skew gate, robust edition

The family-membership requirement - a concave pole must carry materially negative skew or it is not short-gamma - was gated on the third-moment (Pearson) skew, which is the worst available estimator of it. Moment skewness is an average of *cubed* deviations, so a single crash quarter dominates it; Kim and White show it is "extremely sensitive to single outliers," and the amplification is by construction (the cubing).[^kw] The damage is visible in practice: on a short-gamma stream measured over a few dozen quarters, the moment estimate can overstate the robust one severalfold while agreeing with no other skew estimate computed on the same stream (a measured instance is in the hypotheses below; the composite-level reading is [[the-skew-is-the-product]]).

The gate now uses the **L-moment skewness** ($\tau_3 = l_3/l_2$), a linear combination of order statistics: it is bounded in $(-1,1)$, exists whenever the mean does, and - being linear in the data rather than cubed - a lone outlier moves it only linearly. Hosking's L-moments have far lower bias and sampling variance than conventional moments, and Bastianin's Monte Carlo over financial and macro series finds L- and trimmed-L-moment estimators deliver the lowest RMSE across distributions; Groeneveld-Meeden's integrated-quantile (Bowley-family) skewness is the even-more-robust cross-check.[^hosking][^bastianin][^gm] It is measured on the same overlapping multi-month compounded windows as the tail budget, band-averaged - the concave signature lives at the multi-month horizon, not the day - so the estimator is both robust *and* aimed where the crash asymmetry actually is. The gate is two-sided: deep enough to be genuinely short-gamma, and survivable per the tail budget.

### Where each piece runs

The single-book pieces - MPPM income floor, robust L-skew gate, tail budget - are per-candidate metrics and can run inside a sweep. The composite ranker cannot: a per-candidate metric sees only its own book, so $\Delta\hat\Theta$ and downside correlation belong at the portfolio layer, scored over the shortlist where the trend pole's aligned returns are in hand. Wiring $\Delta\hat\Theta$ *into* a sweep as a first-class ranker requires the partner pole's returns as a stored fixture - the one honest coupling (the allocator metric is explicitly relative to the current partner lock, and must be regenerated when that lock moves).

## Strong carry is richness plus quality, not spread level

The metric fixes selection; the signal itself has a parallel defect, and the credit literature has just relitigated it. The raw credit premium is modest: duration-hedged US investment-grade credit earned about 137bp per year over 1936-2014 (Sharpe ~0.37) and high yield about 248bp over 1988-2014 (Sharpe ~0.26) - real (the excess is not spanned by the term and equity premia; the intercept survives at ~7bp/month) but the weakest of the classic premia on a raw basis.[^ar] And the cross-sectional "carry" version of the signal - buy the highest spread - is mostly beta: AQR's own decomposition reports corporate-bond carry with a 0.90 correlation to the aggregate credit market and five times the volatility of value for comparable return.[^aqrstyle] Quoniam's independent replication is blunter still in high yield, where OAS-based carry and value factors print *negative* alphas - the heterogeneous HY universe prices spread level as compensation, not mispricing.[^henke]

The sharpest recent evidence goes one layer deeper, and it now has a peer-reviewed spine. Dickerson, Mueller and Robotti showed in the Journal of Financial Economics that the flagship corporate-bond factor model (Bai-Bali-Wen 2019, same journal) fails replication once lead/lag data errors are corrected - the original factors carried a look-ahead misalignment for most of their sample, the authors have since *retracted* the paper, and the corrected data leave the plain bond-market factor unbeaten by any challenger except, marginally, traded liquidity.[^dmr] The 2026 sequel by Dickerson, Robotti and Rossetti industrializes the audit: a 108-factor corporate-bond zoo in which, after correcting the same two bias families (a shared noisy price in both the sorting signal and the return denominator, and look-ahead in ex-post filtering), *most published corporate bond factors are spanned by the bond-market factor* - and the survivors are primarily **credit-spread-based value signals**, not spread-level carry.[^drr] The April 2026 revision puts magnitudes on the purge: the short-term reversal premium falls from -0.99% to -0.09% per month once the shared-noisy-price bias is corrected (over 90% of the documented effect was bias), six-month momentum's 0.30% monthly premium is entirely an artifact of asymmetric ex-post winsorization, and credit-spread-based value keeps roughly half its premium and stays significant - the only signal family that survives with its economics intact.[^drr] The credit factor zoo is far thinner than its publication record suggests. The constructive statement of the same result is Israel, Palhares and Richardson: a long-only portfolio tilting the same universe by carry *plus* defensive (low leverage, low duration, high profitability) *plus* momentum *plus* value earned 5.26% net of costs at Sharpe 1.03 versus the cap-weighted benchmark's 0.69 - with defensive, momentum and value showing stronger return associations than carry alone.[^ipr]

For a sleeve like this the translation is direct. The defensible half of the signal is spread **richness relative to its own history** - a value signal in the Dickerson-Robotti-Rossetti sense - not the spread level. Anything that leans on raw level is leaning on the 0.90-correlated beta half.[^aqrstyle] And the cheapest untested lever is a quality tilt: the factor evidence says a defensive leg (shorter duration, higher quality inside HY) adds more than a second helping of carry does.[^ipr][^henke]

## Timing pays in the tail; sizing lags it

The conditioning evidence, which is FX-based for want of a credit literature, splits cleanly along a line worth respecting. *Regime avoidance* has real support: excluding carry positions during high-FX-volatility regimes (alone, or jointly with extreme rate differentials) beats the unconditional carry trade on mean, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, Sharpe and holding-period return in virtually every setting Mulder and Tims test, 25 countries, 1985-2015.[^mt] The mechanism is the one Christiansen, Ranaldo and Soderlind measure with a smooth-transition regression: carry's equity beta is regime-dependent and *rises sharply in the volatile regime* - the strategy is most exposed exactly when exposure is most expensive.[^crs] Against this stands Asness's factor-timing caution: contrarian, valuation-based timing of the factor itself is "deceptively difficult", and its backtested gains mostly vanish once implemented as a tradeable strategy.[^asness] The reconciliation is that the two claims time different things - avoiding a measurably hostile regime for a crash-prone trade is not the same act as tactically fading a factor's valuation - but a carry sleeve should treat conditioning as an unproven transfer to credit until tested. The credit-specific evidence has since begun to arrive, and it lands on both sides of the line. On the exposure side, a direct volatility-management test on bond portfolios finds the Moreira-Muir result transfers to exactly one corner of credit: the volatility-managed high-yield portfolio beats its unscaled counterpart on Sharpe and spanning alpha, while investment grade shows no benefit and the *isolated* credit-spread factor is made worse - and the high-yield improvement arrives by reducing or eliminating the negative skew, which for this pole is the familiar warning that the timing gain is paid for out of the skew inventory.[^ferreira] On the identity side, a Markov-switching decomposition of spread dynamics shows the regime does not merely scale credit's beta - it *rotates the driver*: in calm markets spreads move on credit and rates, and in high-volatility regimes market-wide illiquidity supersedes both as the dominant spread factor.[^fischerstolper] Conditioning a credit sleeve is therefore not one decision but two: whether to scale exposure (the FX-style question), and which risk the tail budget is actually pricing once stress arrives (a liquidity claim, not a default claim).

Sizing deserves the same honesty. Realized-vol scaling (a trailing-vol exposure scalar) raises average risk-adjusted return across factors, carry included,[^mt] but a trailing-vol estimator is coincident-to-lagging with respect to the regime that matters: the beta spike Christiansen et al. measure arrives with the volatility, so the scalar de-risks *after* the first leg of a gap move, never before it.[^crs] The sleeve should keep the scaling for what it does (drawdown-path smoothing, average Sharpe) and stop expecting it to do what it cannot (protect the first crash day). Fast-tail protection is the trend pole's and tail sleeve's job - the roster's division of labor, restated from the concave side.

## Limitations

- The best opposing source is genuinely good: Eling-Schuhmacher's rank-correlation result says measure choice rarely matters on live fund cross-sections.[^eling] This article's rebuttal - that a Sharpe-objective sweep is the adversarial case their elliptical universe excludes - is an argument from mechanism, not a measured demonstration on our book. The metric A/B hypothesis below is the measurement.
- MPPM's $\rho$ is a knob. Rankings are reported to be fairly stable across $\rho \in [2,5]$,[^gisw] but that stability should be verified per sweep (a cheap sensitivity sweep, not a new estimator) - and the $\rho$-spread itself is informative: a large implied-risk-aversion divergence is the Doubt Ratio's smoothing flag.[^bki]
- Manipulation-proofness has a proven boundary: it protects the *score* from payoff-shaping, but no track-record mechanism can certify that a high score is skill rather than luck (Foster-Young), so the MPPM ranking and the DSR luck-check are complements, never substitutes.[^fosteryoung]
- The 2026 Dickerson-Robotti-Rossetti zoo paper is not yet peer-reviewed (fate re-checked 2026-07-10: still a working paper, April 2026 revision), but its foundation now is: the JFE 2023 replication result and the consequent Bai-Bali-Wen retraction are settled record.[^dmr] What remains provisional is the zoo's own new bias taxonomy, not the direction of the finding.[^drr]
- The new credit-conditioning evidence is mixed-pedigree: the volatility-managed-credit test is a supervised master's thesis (Barroso-supervised, directionally consistent with the peer-reviewed FX and momentum analogues), and the driver-rotation result is a central-bank discussion paper; treat both as directional until journal versions exist.[^ferreira][^fischerstolper]
- Every daily-frequency statistic of a credit stream - Sharpe, vol, skew, and the downside-correlation guard itself - is potentially flattered by smoothed marks; the multi-month windows mitigate the bias but do not measure it, and the sleeve's own smoothing profile has not been estimated.[^glm]
- Several load-bearing construction sources carry aligned COI (AQR papers marketing multi-factor credit, Quoniam running credit-factor funds); the raw-premium figures and the manipulation-proof theory are the COI-free backbone.[^gisw][^ar]
- McLean-Pontiff's post-publication decay (~58% average) applies to every headline Sharpe above; the IPR 1.03 should be read as an upper bound, not an expectation.[^mp]
- FX carry conditioning may not transfer to credit; the timing hypothesis below is an extension of the literature, not a replication.
- The composite ranker $\Delta\hat\Theta$ is explicitly relative to the current trend lock: it must be recomputed when that lock moves, and a single window's value is a point estimate (report a block-bootstrap CI across splits and require a plateau, not a spike, exactly as for the standalone MPPM). $\rho$ and the blend weight $w$ are pinned by mandate; their sensitivity is a trust check, not a free axis. The metric measures marginal contribution at a *fixed* weight, not the optimal weight - the weight question is the floor's, argued separately.

- [ ] **Quality beats more carry.** Adding a defensive leg (short-duration HY or quality-screened HY UCITS ETF, instrument to be verified investable) to the IHYU+LQDH book lifts income-per-tail (`carry_income_utility` up at flat-or-shallower `carry_tail_budget`) more than raising `carry_gain` does - the IPR/Henke defensive-dominates-carry claim on our universe.[^ipr][^henke]
- [ ] **Richness is the live half of the signal.** A sweep that leans exposure on spread richness (current construction) beats an otherwise-identical sweep leaning on raw spread level, on held-out utility - the Dickerson-Robotti-Rossetti value-vs-carry split reproduced at the timing margin.[^drr]
- [ ] **Stress rotates the driver to liquidity.** Regress the credit sleeve's returns in high-vol windows on credit-spread changes and a tradable liquidity proxy (ETF premium/discount, bid-ask); pre-register that the liquidity term's explanatory share rises materially versus calm windows - the regime-rotation result on our instruments.[^fischerstolper] If it does, the tail budget is pricing a liquidity claim, and the sleeve's stress losses should be modeled and gated as such.
- [ ] **Vol-regime avoidance transfers to credit (or does not).** A high-vol mute on the credit book should improve held-out skew and utility as it does for FX carry;[^mt] pre-register the Asness-side null that its gains vanish net of forfeited calm-period income.[^asness] **Superseded before testing, 2026-07-03** ([[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]]): convergent evidence shows de-risking into declines is a skew-*raising* operation - a mute would flip the concave pole toward the convex shape the trend sleeve already owns, dissolving the floor's skew budget. A timing mute belongs in a separate convex-budgeted sleeve, never inside this one.
