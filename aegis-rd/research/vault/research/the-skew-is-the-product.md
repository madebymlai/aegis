# The Skew Is the Product

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> A concave income pole's negative skew is not a risk to be minimized - it is the inventory the sleeve sells, and the portfolio's reason for hiring it. The literature converges on an uncomfortable regularity: every lever that improves carry standalone (vol-targeting, skew-based selection, defensive tilts, drift-band rebalancing) buys the improvement by liquidating that inventory, because the income and the crash are separable enough that an optimizer will separate them. Credit's crash-beta even has a precise address - duration-times-spread in the BB-B belt, not "high yield" as a label. The fix is architectural, not parametric: score and gate the pole on its contribution to the composite (crisis-conditional correlation to the convex pole, hard skew floors), and treat rebalancing cadence as a payoff-shape lever, not a fee lever.

This article exists because of a measured failure. On 2026-07-03 the credit-carry pole was upgraded by everything the standalone evidence supported - a defensive maturity tilt, an EM local leg, live drift-band mechanics - and every standalone metric improved. The same day's floor re-verification ([[runs/floor/2026-07-03]]) showed the upgrade had quietly dissolved the pole's portfolio identity: quarterly skew shrank from -1.83 to -0.49 and correlation to the trend pole flipped from -0.11 to +0.17. The pole became a better sleeve and a worse teammate. The question this article answers from the literature: was that bad luck, or a law? It is much closer to a law - and the mechanisms are specific enough to act on.

## Income and crash are separable - that is the trap

The premium and the crash are not the same thing, and the quantitative literature keeps finding the crash to be the smaller part. Jurek hedges G10 carry with out-of-the-money options and finds crash risk accounts for at most about a third of the excess return - crash-neutral carry stays significantly profitable (3.2-5.3% per year, t > 3).[^jurek] His follow-up decomposition of the pricing kernel sharpens it: the skewness-specific component is on the order of 10-15% of the FX carry premium, with the bulk being compensation for variance generally.[^jurekxu] Bekaert and Panayotov make the separability constructive: G10 carry portfolios that simply exclude the prototypical crash currencies (AUD, JPY, NOK) - their "good carry" - earn *higher* Sharpe ratios with flat-to-positive skew, which they read as invalidating crash-risk explanations of the premium at large.[^bp] Torok reaches the same place through options: selecting currencies on option-implied skewness produces a skew-neutral carry with similar returns, positive realized skew, and a slightly higher Sharpe.[^torok]

So a config sweep *can* find income without the crash - that is exactly the problem. If the crash component is a minority of the return, an optimizer pointed at any standalone objective (Sharpe, income utility, income-per-unit-tail) faces a cheap trade: sell the skew, keep most of the income, collect a better score. What the standalone objective cannot see is that the crash component is not dead weight - it is the entire source of the pole's value to a two-pole book. Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt show carry's losses cluster across asset classes precisely in global recessions, a co-movement "obscured when considering monthly returns";[^kmpv] Lettau, Maggiori and Weber show the market beta of high-carry positions is concentrated *conditional on bad states* - a downside-risk CAPM prices carry across currencies, equities, options, commodities and sovereign bonds jointly.[^lmw] Those bad-state exposures are what a crisis-convex trend sleeve is short of, and they are exactly what "good carry" constructions shed. Bekaert-Panayotov never test whether good carry still diversifies a trend book - our floor verification is, in effect, that missing test, and the answer was no.

The framing that survives: for this pole, the negative skew is the product. Income is what the pole is paid *in*; crash absorption is what it is paid *for*. An objective that rewards shedding the second while keeping the first is asking the sleeve to defraud its employer.

## Credit's crash-beta has an address: DTS

For a credit implementation the mechanism is unusually concrete. Structurally, a corporate bond is riskless debt minus a put on the firm's assets - Merton's model makes "long credit = short equity put" a construction, not a metaphor.[^merton-av] The empirical hedge ratios confirm it with a precision rare in this literature: combining Merton and Geske, the theoretical sensitivity of credit spreads to equity put options is statistically indistinguishable from one (coefficients 0.94-1.12 across ratings), and the magnitude of that short-put exposure is wildly rating-dependent - roughly 0.26bp for AAA-AA credits against 8.6bp for BB-B.[^merton-av] The short-put character of credit lives almost entirely in the crossover belt. Campbell and Taksler supply the volatility side: idiosyncratic equity volatility explains as much cross-sectional variation in bond yields as ratings do.[^ct]

The exposure metric that tracks this is duration-times-spread. Ben Dor et al. show spread changes are linearly proportional to spread *level* - wide bonds move proportionally more - so a book's exposure to a systematic credit shock is spread duration multiplied by spread, not spread duration alone; a 1-year bond at 500bp carries the same spread risk as a 5-year bond at 100bp.[^dts] DTS is therefore the pole's crash-beta dial. Read our own construction through it and the 2026-07-03 skew erosion stops being mysterious: the defensive tilt swapped broad HY (~4y spread duration at HY spreads) for a 0-5yr bucket, cutting the book's DTS roughly in half; the rate-hedged IG leg contributes spread duration at IG spreads and near-zero short-put convexity by rating.[^merton-av] The pole's income survived because most of the premium is not crash rent (previous section); its skew did not, because the skew *is* the DTS. One boundary: at the distressed bottom (CCC), spreads decouple from the systematic factor into idiosyncratic default outcomes, so DTS-seeking should stop at the B/BB belt rather than reaching for the widest spreads - which the fool's-yield evidence in [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] independently supports.

The design consequence is a genuinely useful separation: **income and crash-beta get their own axes.** Size the credit legs so the book's DTS is pinned (the skew mandate), and let the optimizer improve income *at that DTS* - tilts that cut crash-beta then have to buy proportionally more of the shorter bucket instead of silently shrinking the product. This is the concave mirror of pinning `vol_target` by mandate and sweeping shape only ([[what-makes-a-carry-sleeve-an-income-engine]]).

## Every standalone improvement spends skew

The regularity generalizes beyond selection. Vol-targeting - our own exposure stage - is the canonical case. On momentum, Barroso and Santa-Clara's risk management lifts Sharpe from 0.53 to 0.97 precisely by truncating the left tail (skew -2.47 to -0.42).[^bsc-ref] Applied to carry itself, the effect is stronger: a vol-managed global carry portfolio raises Sharpe by ~40% while realized skew *flips sign*, from -0.37 to positive.[^almeida] Moreira and Muir's general result - scaling factor exposure by inverse trailing variance raises Sharpe across factors *including* FX carry - carries a footnote that matters for us: the improvement requires timing on the stream's *own* realized vol; timed on a common (equity-driven) volatility factor, the carry alpha disappears entirely.[^mm] So the three big standalone levers - vol-targeting exposure, skew-based selection,[^bp][^torok] and defensive credit tilts (previous section) - all improve the sleeve's standalone statistics through the same channel: they reduce or reverse the negative skew. No study we found improves carry's standalone risk-adjusted return while *keeping* its skew. Sharpe-gaming needed payoff-shaping freedom ([[what-makes-a-carry-sleeve-an-income-engine]]); skew-shedding needs much less, because it is not an attack on the measure - it is the honest optimum of every standalone objective.

For this pole the practical reading of Moreira-Muir is therefore double-edged: the vol-target earns its keep on average return, but every unit of drawdown-smoothing it buys comes out of the same skew inventory, and the sleeve's owner - the composite - never voted on the sale.

## The rebalancing band is a payoff-shape lever

The subtlest skew leak came from what looked like a pure execution decision. Rebalancing toward fixed weights is mechanically a short straddle on the relative value of the book's assets: Granger, Greenig, Harvey, Rattray and Zou show fixed-weight rebalancing "induces negative convexity by magnifying drawdowns when there are pronounced divergences," with the rebalancing premium as the compensation.[^rebrisk] Willenbrock identifies the diversification return - the compounding edge of a rebalanced book over buy-and-hold - as coming from exactly those contrarian trades; a drifting book earns none of it.[^wb] The complement: letting weights drift is an embedded momentum position. Hoffstein states it plainly - "by allowing our portfolio to drift, our portfolio will naturally tilt itself towards the out-performing asset... drift serves as an interesting amplifier to the momentum signal."[^hoffdrift] Rattray, Granger, Harvey and van Hemert quantify the stakes on a 60/40: the monthly-rebalanced book's GFC drawdown was 1.2x (five percentage points) worse than buy-and-hold from the same start, and a trend overlay is the convex mirror of the rebalancing short straddle.[^stratreb]

Now read our live-mechanics decision through that lens. Moving the carry book from daily rebalancing to a 10%-up/20%-down drift band did two things at once: it gave up the rebalancing premium's concave harvest, and it blended a quiet long-trend tilt into the carry stream - the drifting winners ride. A concave pole that drifts is partially a trend follower; the corr-to-trend flip to +0.17 and the skew compression are the two visible symptoms of one mechanism, and the literature predicts both signs. The band was adopted as a fee lever (254 trades/yr to ~3 on a book where order minimums bite), and at the *sleeve* level that arithmetic is right. At the *composite* level the band's cost is the pole's role, which no per-sleeve fee comparison prices. The middle path the literature offers is tranching - "rebalance a little but frequently," correcting a fraction of the drift each period[^hoffdrift] - which keeps most of the contrarian (concave) trade flow at a bounded trade count. For this pole, rebalancing cadence must be treated as a swept *shape* axis with the skew measured per cell, never as an execution detail bolted on after selection.

## Select the pole where it works: in the composite

Everything above says the pole's identity cannot be protected by any standalone statistic - so the selection machinery has to see the portfolio. Three gates have direct literature support. First, *conditional* correlation, not full-sample: Page and Panariello show full-sample correlations systematically hide crisis co-movement - US/non-US equity correlation is -17% conditional on the best 1% of months and +87% conditional on the worst 1% - and conclude flatly that "full-sample correlations are misleading."[^pp] A carry pole must clear its corr-to-trend ceiling *measured in the composite's stress states*, where the diversification is actually being bought. Second, marginal contribution as the score: the total-portfolio-approach literature formalizes "evaluate every investment by its marginal impact on total fund risk and return - there are no buckets,"[^tpa] which for a sweep means ranking cells by the composite's utility delta with the candidate in it, not by the candidate's own utility. Third, a tail-contribution budget: the Euler decomposition of portfolio risk (and its CVaR version) prices each sleeve's share of the composite left tail, the formal version of Ilmanen's dictum that bearing *small* insurance risks is well rewarded and bearing large ones is not.[^ins-ref]

One honest complication: the thing the gates defend is itself regime-dependent. Trend's crisis alpha requires time to reposition - in fast shocks "trend-following lacks sufficient time to adapt and the crisis alpha characteristic disappears"[^man-quality] - so the anti-correlation the carry pole is gated against is an emergent property of two conditional processes, not a physical constant. That argues for measuring the gates over multiple stress windows (and for the tail tier's existence), not for abandoning them.

## Limitations

- The "every standalone improvement spends skew" claim is an empirical regularity assembled from adjacent results (momentum vol-management, carry vol-management, skew-based FX selection, credit tilts), not a theorem. The carry-specific skew-flip evidence is the weakest link in pedigree: a supervised master's thesis,[^almeida] consistent with the peer-reviewed momentum analogues but not itself peer-reviewed.
- The DTS framework is practitioner research (Lehman/Barclays and Robeco, who market DTS-based products), though its core proportionality finding is 560,000+ bond-months and the framework is embedded in third-party risk systems.[^dts]
- Man Group's positioning-conditional trend numbers (crisis windows entered long vs short bonds) could not be verified before exa's rate limit; only the qualitative fast-shock claim is cited.[^man-quality] Related: we found no canonical published number for unconditional trend-carry correlation - the mechanism evidence is rich, the coefficient is not in the literature we swept.
- Bekaert-Panayotov and Torok do not test whether skew-lite "good carry" retains diversification value against a trend book; that inference is ours, supported so far by one measured instance (our own floor verification).
- Several agent-collected sources were verified by full fetch, the rest re-verified by us independently; the Baz et al. cross-strategy correlation tables and the Strategic Rebalancing band-width exhibits remain unread (rate limit) - the band-width magnitude question is answered qualitatively ("threshold rules slightly less potent" than trend-informed delay), not numerically.[^stratreb]

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [ ] **The composite-gated re-rank.** Re-rank the live-mechanics `carry_sota.yaml` sweep under the floor diary's gates made hard: quarterly skew <= -0.5 on the aligned window, corr(trend) <= 0 full-sample AND in the composite's worst-decile months (the Page-Panariello gate). Pre-register: deeper-DTS cells (IHYU-bearing) reclaim the championship, and the winner's floor composite at w=0.40 degrades less vs trend-only than the current champion's does ([[runs/floor/2026-07-03]] baseline).
- [ ] **Rebalancing cadence is a shape axis.** On the fixed champion cell, sweep cadence {daily, tranched 1/5 daily, band 0.05/0.10, band 0.10/0.20} and measure per-cell qskew, corr-to-trend, income, and net-of-fixed-fee utility. Pre-register the literature's signs: tighter cadence deepens skew and lowers corr-to-trend (the rebalancing short straddle) at higher fee cost; select by composite delta net of fees, expecting tranching to dominate both extremes.[^rebrisk][^hoffdrift]
- [ ] **Pin DTS, not just vol.** Add a book-level DTS estimate (spread proxy x spread duration per leg) and re-run the construction sweep with DTS pinned at the incumbent book's level, so defensive tilts must buy more short-bucket exposure instead of shrinking crash-beta. Pre-register: at pinned DTS the defensive tilt's utility edge shrinks but the skew survives (qskew stays <= -0.5).
- [ ] **The composite-delta ranker.** Score sweep cells by the delta in composite (trend + candidate at fixed w=0.40, vol-normalized) MPPM utility rather than standalone utility - the TPA principle as a ranker[^tpa] - keeping the standalone gates as sanity floors. If the composite-delta winner equals the composite-gated standalone winner, the cheaper gates suffice; if not, the ranker earns its complexity.
- [ ] **(Instrument-level, joint with [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]].) Pure-spread and explicit-insurance legs restore the skew.** A CDS-carry leg (protection-seller iTraxx Crossover, DBXM) and/or an AT1 leg (XAT1) - income that is undilutably crash-rent - deepen the pole's qskew past the -0.5 gate at flat-or-better income where the cash-bond defensive tilt could not.
