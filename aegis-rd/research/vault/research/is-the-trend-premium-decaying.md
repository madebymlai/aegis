# Is the Trend Premium Decaying

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> The 2023-2025 trend drawdown is environmental weather - a run of correlated V-reversals in a low-dispersion tape - not structural alpha decay: the premium's autocorrelation substrate is intact across 330 years and the live index survived 25 post-publication years including its best-ever 2022, the genuine decay is confined to fast/short signals and crowded equity-momentum, and the one structural headwind that does bind a thin retail book is universe capacity, not signal erosion - which is an argument about *breadth and sizing*, not about whether trend still works.

A trend sleeve that has just printed its second-worst drawdown in a quarter-century invites the question of whether the edge is gone. Three different claims hide inside that one question, and they have different answers. "The signal has stopped predicting" (alpha decay) is the strong claim and the evidence is against it. "The environment has been hostile" (bad weather) is the weak claim and the evidence is for it. "A thin retail book cannot harvest what is left as efficiently as a diversified program" (capacity) is a third claim, separate from both, and it is the only one that binds here. Separating them is the whole job, because the remedy for each is different: weather needs patience, decay needs retirement, and capacity needs breadth or a smaller allocation. This article argues the realized cause is overwhelmingly weather plus a capacity tax, with a narrow band of genuine decay that the slow non-equity sleeve mostly sidesteps.

## The environmental case

The cleanest evidence that the recent run is cyclical is that it looks exactly like the prior cyclical runs. AlphaSimplex catalogues every SG Trend drawdown greater than 10% since 2000: sixteen of them, arriving roughly every eighteen to twenty-four months, and for most episodes the recovery was *shorter* than the drawdown.[^alphasimplexcycles] The Liberation-Day-era drawdown (roughly -20 to -22% from May 2024 through May 2025) is the second-deepest in that sample, but its shape - depth, frequency, and the positive one- and two-year forward returns that have followed prior troughs - sits inside the historical distribution rather than outside it.[^alphasimplexcycles] Cambridge Associates reaches the same verdict from the macro side: the 2023-2025 tape was "characterised by abrupt reversals, range-bound price action, and a lack of persistent trends," and those conditions "are not without precedent."[^cambridge]

The mechanism of the losses is also diagnostic, and it is the whipsaw mechanism, not a decay mechanism. The damage came from a short sequence of identifiable correlated reversals - the March-2023 SVB rate snap-back, the August-2024 yen-carry unwind, the April-2025 tariff V - each a fast reversal of a position the slow signal had correctly held, the same reversal-sign loss analysed in [[trend-following-in-whipsaw-regimes]]. Rob Carver's 2025 dissection sharpens the distinction directly: he asks whether the degradation is a *cohort* effect (a particular vintage of markets decaying), an *instrument-decay* effect (individual markets losing trend), or an *environmental* effect, and finds the signature of the environment - all speeds and most markets weaken together in the same windows, which a per-instrument or per-cohort decay cannot produce.[^carverenv] The practical consequence he draws is the one that matters for diagnosis: because the cause is the environment, it cannot be fixed by dropping markets or changing the signal lookback - only by breadth and by waiting.

## The durability case

Underneath the weather, the substrate the premium rests on is one of the most persistent facts in markets. Schmidhuber and Safari analyse roughly 330 years of data across asset classes and find a statistically significant trending regime on horizons from a few hours to a few years; the persistence coefficient has declined modestly over decades but remains positive and is consistent with the order-1 Sharpe ratios trend programs target.[^schmidhuber] That the autocorrelation is small but real, and present across centuries and asset classes, is exactly what a durable risk-and-behavioural premium should look like, and it is not the profile of a data-mined artifact.

The premium also survives the test that kills most published anomalies: out-of-sample, post-publication, live, and net of costs. Academic predictors lose on the order of a quarter of their alpha out-of-sample and over half after publication ([[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] cites the same base rate via McLean-Pontiff), yet the live SG Trend Index has compounded positively across the 25 years since time-series momentum was formalised, including +20.9% in the 2008 crash and its best-ever +27.4% in 2022.[^alphasimplexcycles] Trend and carry are repeatedly among the minority of factors that clear large-scale replication audits, where the median anomaly does not (the replication evidence is in [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]]). And the crisis-alpha *mechanism* has been shown directly on live CTA data rather than asserted: the gains come from diversification across many simultaneously-trending futures plus exposure reduction inside fifteen days, not from a fragile directional call ([[measuring-crisis-alpha]] and [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]] both lean on the same Asif et al. result). For the specific substrate this book trades, the durability is sharper still: variance-ratio-conditioned momentum in *commodity* futures outperforms naive time-series momentum and survives transaction costs, out-of-sample testing, and data-snooping controls.[^mehlitz]

## Where decay is real

The honest reading is not that nothing has decayed. Three things have, and naming them prevents the lazy generalisation to "trend is dead."

First, *fast and short-horizon* trend has genuinely lost efficacy as markets grew more efficient and execution-sensitive - the multi-decade decline is clearest at the three-day-to-two-month end, and is the reason this campaign killed its own fast leg ([[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]). Second, the *medium* 60-to-125-day band is now the structurally weakest horizon, contributing noise rather than risk-adjusted return since roughly mid-2022 (the barbell-redundancy evidence in [[trend-following-in-whipsaw-regimes]]). A slow ~190-to-250-day sleeve sits in neither decayed zone, which is the central reason it remains defensible. Third, *equity-momentum crowding* accelerated after 2015 as factor ETFs democratised the trade, and one game-theoretic study finds a hyperbolic Sharpe decay correlated with factor-ETF volume growth.[^crowding] The same study carries a counter-intuitive twist that cuts against the usual crowding fear for *trend*: crowded momentum exhibits *lower* crash probability than uncrowded, because more participants reinforcing the same directional move is stabilising for a trend-following position rather than destabilising the way it is for a mean-reverting one.[^crowding] Crowding is a real headwind on the cross-sectional equity-momentum trade; it is not obviously one on cross-asset time-series trend, and may even be benign there.

The net of all three is a lower steady-state Sharpe than the early backtests advertised - the marginal Sharpe of the live trend index now sits well below the 0.7-plus of the founding papers - but a positive, persistent one, concentrated at the speeds and in the asset classes the decay has spared.

## The headwind that actually binds: capacity, not decay

For a three-to-twenty-name UCITS ETF book, the binding constraint is not whether the signal works but how much of the surviving premium the wrapper can capture. A small, ETF-constrained program can trade only a fraction of the markets a fully-diversified futures CTA reaches, and the practitioner estimate of the resulting drag is on the order of a Sharpe deterioration of ~17% and a return drag of roughly -1.5 to -4% per year relative to an unconstrained implementation.[^quanticacap] This is structural, not cyclical: it does not improve in good trend years. It also explains the 2023-2025 cross-section, where the managers who held up were the ones with the broadest universes (idiosyncratic markets like cocoa, the yen-carry leg, and election-driven rates were where the trends were), exactly the breadth a thin book cannot reach.[^quanticacap]

This relocates the entire improvement question. The thin book's problem is not a decaying edge to be filtered or re-signalled; it is a breadth ceiling to be respected. The actionable response is the one [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]] already argues - occupy as many *orthogonal* macro axes as the wrapper allows and stop - paired with sizing the sleeve to its honest, lower steady-state Sharpe rather than to the backtest's, and sizing it *counter-cyclically* (up after extended drawdowns, where the forward distribution is favourable, down after outsized years).

## Limitations

- The environmental-not-decay verdict leans on sources that sell trend (AlphaSimplex, Quantica, Man); the best-insulated legs are Carver (no aligned product) and the 330-year academic autocorrelation result, and the conclusion is built to rest on those.[^carverenv][^schmidhuber]
- The crowding/alpha-decay inversion (crowded momentum crashes *less*) is a single game-theoretic working paper and is stated as suggestive, not settled.[^crowding]
- "Lower but positive steady-state Sharpe" is a directional claim; the exact post-decay magnitude on a EUR-base, three-name, slow book is unmeasured and is precisely what the live sleeve has to earn, not assume.
- The capacity-drag figure is a practitioner estimate for a multi-billion program; the *direction* transfers to a small book (a thin universe cannot capture breadth-sourced premium) but the *magnitude* does not transfer linearly to a retail-scale book, where impact is negligible but the breadth ceiling still binds.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [ ] **Counter-cyclical sleeve sizing beats constant weight.** Sizing the trend sleeve up after extended SG-Trend-style drawdowns and down after outsized years (a slow, drawdown-aware sleeve weight, not a signal change) improves the roster's held-out Calmar versus a fixed sleeve weight - the portfolio-level analogue of the patience premium, tested on our own multi-sleeve book.
- [ ] **The slow non-equity sleeve is decay-insulated where fast/medium are not.** Re-running the speed sweep on the live universe, the ~190-250d band holds its held-out Sharpe across 2018-2025 sub-periods while a fast (~20-40d) and a medium (~60-125d) leg degrade in the post-2022 sub-period - confirming on our data that the decay is horizon-localised, not universal.
- [ ] **Commodity memory-conditioning survives where naive TSMOM weakens.** A variance-ratio (autocorrelation-aware) gate on the commodity legs (IGLN/ICOM) holds up out-of-sample net of retail costs where the unconditioned slow signal weakens, per Mehlitz-Auer on our two commodity names.
