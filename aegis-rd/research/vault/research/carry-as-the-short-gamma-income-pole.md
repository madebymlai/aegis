# Carry and the Short-Gamma Income Pole

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> The floor's concave half is an income engine that earns by selling insurance: it collects a premium in calm and gives it back in dislocation. Carry is its canonical form, but the negative skew that makes it the true counterpart to a trend sleeve lives in *currencies and commodities*, not in equity or bond carry - so a generic cross-asset carry basket is near-symmetric and only half the job. The pole's defining payoff is sourced where funding-unwind dynamics bite, and its cardinal sin is renting that payoff in a form that blows up faster than it pays.

## The pole the trend sleeve cannot fill

A trend sleeve is long gamma: it bleeds a little in calm and pays out in dislocation. Held alone it is an expensive hedge with no engine to fund it. The floor is finished only when a second sleeve sits opposite it on the convexity axis (the argument in [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]]) - short gamma, negatively skewed, paid to carry the risk the trend sleeve is paid to shed. That sleeve is the income engine: it compounds the premium that structurally accrues to sellers of crash risk, and it is the counterparty whose losses in the tail are the trend sleeve's gains. The roster names this the hardest piece to source and the feasibility gate for the whole construction, precisely because a concave sleeve that does not pay in calm is just a worse long position.[[the-tiered-strategy-roster]]

Carry is the obvious candidate. In its general form - the return an asset earns if its price does not move - carry has been shown to predict returns within and across every major asset class, with a diversified cross-asset carry portfolio earning a high Sharpe ratio that survives published transaction-cost estimates.[^carry] The premium is not a publication artifact: a 153-factor replication across 93 countries places carry among the factors whose out-of-sample evidence strengthens rather than decays under a Bayesian correction,[^jkp] and a separate reconstruction running back to 1800 finds a multi-asset carry premium significant in both the original and the long pre-sample period.[^gfp] Carry pays, and it keeps paying.

## Where the negative skew actually lives

Paying is necessary but not sufficient. The pole's job is to be *concave* - to absorb the loss in the tail - and that is a statement about skew ([[skewness-in-asset-returns]]), not about average return. Here the evidence is sharper than the general carry literature lets on, and it is the single fact that should govern how the sleeve is built.

The crash skew of carry is overwhelmingly a *currency* phenomenon. Sorting currencies by interest-rate differential, the high-yield (investment) currencies that earn the carry are exactly the ones that crash, and the funding currencies that finance it are positively skewed; across currencies, average skewness falls almost linearly with the average interest-rate differential, a relationship that explains the great majority of the cross-sectional variation.[^bnp] Commodity carry carries some of the same negative asymmetry, through the inventory and convenience-yield channel that links a backwardated term structure to scarcity and stress.[^carry][^ghr] But equity carry is *positively* skewed and bond (term-structure) carry is close to symmetric, so a diversified all-asset carry portfolio comes out with skewness near zero - lower-tailed than passive equity, not more.[^carry] The convexity article reached the same conclusion from the allocation side and flagged it as a sourcing constraint;[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] the carry literature confirms it from the inside.

The consequence for construction is direct and it mirrors the substrate lesson the trend pole already learned. Just as a trend sleeve realizes long gamma only on a non-equity book whose moves are sustained trends, a carry sleeve realizes the *concave* signature only where funding-unwind dynamics bite - in FX and, more weakly, commodities. Build the concave pole from bond or equity carry and it may pay, but it will not carry the negative skew that makes it the trend sleeve's true counterpart; it will be a second income stream of the wrong shape. The pole must be sourced to the mechanism, not assembled from whatever carries.

## Why someone keeps paying, and the unresolved part

A short-gamma premium is credible only if the buyers on the other side cannot easily leave. For carry the standard account is funding liquidity: investment currencies are held by leveraged players whose positions unwind together when funding dries up, so the losses arrive all at once and coincide with spikes in volatility and the unwinding of crowded trades.[^bnp] The seller is paid the rest of the time for standing in front of that unwind. This is the same bargain Ilmanen describes across asset classes - buying crash insurance is poorly rewarded, selling it is paid - which is why the concave pole is an income engine at all.[^ins]

What is *not* settled is whether the premium is compensation for the crash or simply mispriced volatility insurance, and the disagreement is worth stating because it bears on how the sleeve should be hedged. Jurek hedges currency carry with out-of-the-money options and finds that crash risk accounts for at most about a third of the excess return - crash-neutral carry stays significantly positive, so the premium is mostly *not* a payment for the crash.[^jurek] Caballero and Doyle run the comparison the other way and find carry returns move with a short-volatility (VIX-rolldown) position closely enough that controlling for it absorbs most of the carry alpha - on their reading carry is disguised short volatility, and the premium evaporates when volatility normalizes.[^cd] Both are credible; they disagree on the central question. The honest synthesis is that carry is *at least partly* a short-volatility premium and partly something else, and a sleeve that hedges its tail will keep some of the return and shed some of the risk - but how much of each is genuinely uncertain.

## Selling insurance, seen directly

The cleanest live instance of the concave pole is the one that sells the insurance explicitly. The variance risk premium - the gap between option-implied and subsequently realized variance - is reliably positive, so a seller of variance earns a premium that standard factor models cannot explain, because variance spikes exactly when the market falls and buyers will overpay to hedge that.[^cw] That premium predicts market returns at the quarterly horizon, which is the signature of a priced risk rather than a free lunch.[^btz] Realized in an index, it looks like the put-writing strategies whose long-run record is the concave pole in miniature: a cash-secured S&P 500 put-write earns an equity-like return at roughly two-thirds the volatility and a higher Sharpe ratio, with a shallower maximum drawdown than the index in 2008 - and a monthly return skewness far more negative than the market's, the quantitative fingerprint of a short-gamma payoff.[^put] The down-beta of such a strategy exceeds its up-beta: it gives back in the tail what it collected in the calm, which is the concave shape stated as a number.

These figures come from index back-tests, several of them exchange-funded, and flatter a live implementation that bears fees and slippage (see Limitations). But the *shape* is not in doubt and is corroborated by the conflict-free academic work on the premium itself.[^cw][^btz]

## The rented-premium trap

The concave pole has a failure mode exactly dual to the trend pole's. The trend sleeve's mistake is to *rent* its convexity from listed volatility, paying a roll decay that is itself the premium the concave sleeve collects.[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] The concave sleeve's mistake is to harvest that same premium in a form whose tail is unbounded and leveraged. Selling variance through a levered, mechanically-rebalanced product converts the modest negative skew of a collateralized put-write into a path that can lose almost everything in a single session: the inverse-volatility ETPs that imploded in February 2018 fell on the order of 96% in one day when the rebalancing flow they generated amplified the very spike they were short.[^volmageddon] The premium was real; the wrapper was lethal. The rule that generalizes is the income-side complement to the trend pole's: harvest the short-gamma premium in a *collateralized, unlevered* form sized to the loss the book can survive, and treat any structure that must trade in the direction of the shock to maintain its exposure as a different and far more dangerous instrument. The pole is supposed to lose money in the tail - that is its job - but it must lose a budgeted amount, not its whole capital.

## Limitations

The negative-skew sourcing claim is the load-bearing one and it rests primarily on a single study's cross-asset skew table,[^carry] corroborated for currencies by the carry-crash literature.[^bnp] Treat the sign pattern - FX strongly negative, commodities weakly negative, equity positive, bond near zero - as well-supported, but any specific skew magnitude as suggestive.

Several of the strongest performance figures carry conflicts of interest. The put-write record is exchange-funded and based on hypothetical, gross-of-cost index returns;[^put] much of the carry-survives-costs evidence is from managers who run the factors.[^carry][^jkp][^gfp] The peer-reviewed variance-premium work is the conflict-free backbone for *why* the premium exists;[^cw][^btz] the headline Sharpe ratios are not.

Carry's premium is regime-dependent. There is credible evidence that G10 FX carry flattened markedly after 2008 as rate differentials compressed, with the decline attributed to a *disappearance* of the downside risk rather than to a crash - which, if right, means the very skew the pole is sourced for can switch off in a low-differential regime.[^fan] A sleeve built today on the assumption that FX carry is reliably concave inherits that regime risk.

And the carry-equals-short-vol question is genuinely open.[^jurek][^cd] If Caballero and Doyle are closer to right, much of what looks like a carry premium is a volatility premium already captured by the put-write sleeve, and stacking both would be selling the same insurance twice - the within-pole redundancy that [[short-horizon-reversal-in-small-cross-sections]] raises for reversal applies here too.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [ ] **Source the skew to FX and commodities.** Build the concave sleeve from FX and commodity carry proxies and verify on our own universe that the realized stream carries the negative-skew / non-positive-$\beta_2$ signature, and that a bond/equity-carry-weighted version does *not* - confirming the sourcing constraint before trusting skew as the sleeve's verification signature. **Now investable, and first test failed 2026-07-03**: [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] identifies EM local-currency sovereign bonds (UCITS tracker IEML) as the long-only claim on the FX-carry premium *with its negative skew attached* (BIS mechanism evidence; Burger-Warnock skew documentation); tested as the `fx_weight` axis of the `carry_sota.yaml` sweep and **killed on the 2018-2025 window** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03#20260703T174905661970Z_demeter_eu_credit_carry_sota - the construction sweep|diary]]) - the leg diluted income-per-tail at every weight; the sourcing claim itself (FX carries the skew) is not refuted, but on this window the skew arrived without the premium. Re-entry: dollar-weakening regime or longer sample. Instrument re-check 2026-07-03: dedicated FX-carry UCITS ETPs survive only as ~10m-AUM zombies (Xtrackers DBX1AY, 2008 vintage), so EM local bonds remain the sole practical claim - the sourcing constraint is unchanged.
- [x] **Carry passes the mirror gate.** Confirm that a carry/short-gamma candidate's frozen stream passes the Carry/Mean-Reversion family gate (quarterly skew negative, $\beta_2 \le 0$, crisis-conditional return negative, bear-regime beta non-negative, moderate positive $\beta_1$) over a 36-60mo window, the mirror image of the trend gate in [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]]. **Confirmed 2026-06-13** (diagnostic, slow distribution-carry c252/vol63): quarterly skew -0.13, $\beta_2 \le 0$, crash-conditional -0.16%, bear-beta +0.12, $\beta_1$ +0.09 (sign-correct but below the 0.20 default); the negative skew is mild because the measurable carry is bond/credit/EM/FX-heavy. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#carry/yield diagnostic - the pivot (diagnostic)]].
- [x] **It pays in calm.** Verify the income-engine requirement directly: the concave sleeve earns positive held-out return in calm splits, since a short-gamma sleeve that does not compound the premium in quiet markets has no reason to exist. **Confirmed 2026-06-13:** the carry book locked at positive held-out Sharpe ~0.79 with 5/5 positive splits, where the price-reversion sleeve paid the premium (negative drift) - carry collects it. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#20260613T145955024575Z_demeter_carry]].
- [x] **The variance risk premium is the strongest concave pole, and it is trend's mirror.** Test whether selling variance directly ("Selling insurance, seen directly" above) carries a *deeper* concave signature than distribution-yield carry, and whether it is the structural opposite of the trend pole rather than merely a stronger carry. **Confirmed 2026-06-13** (diagnostic, `demeter.vol_carry`: VIX-term-structure short-vol signal, short-VXX execution, vol-targeted to 10%): quarterly skew **-1.18** and crisis-conditional **-0.95%** (vs distribution carry's -0.13 and -0.16% at matched vol - roughly 9x the skew and 6x the crisis loss), positive drift (Sharpe +0.57), bear-beta +0.10, $\beta_1$ +0.28; robust across six param cells. Trend is mechanically *long* variance, so short variance is its exact mirror on the convexity axis - the floor becomes a long-variance / short-variance barbell. The Treynor-Mazuy $\beta_2$ stays small only because it measures convexity in $SPY^2$ while VRP's concavity lives in the *vol* factor (captured by quarterly skew and the crisis-conditional). VRP is promoted to the champion concave pole; distribution carry is demoted to a milder secondary source. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#vol-carry gate - the variance risk premium (diagnostic)]]. **Now investable, 2026-07-03** ([[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] addendum): covered-call UCITS ETFs (JPM JEGP ~1.2bn, Global X QYLD ~650m) sell index optionality in a collateralized, unlevered wrapper - the harvest-not-rent form this article requires; no put-write UCITS exists. Identity caveat before any test: a covered-call book's crash factor is *equities*, so its corr-to-trend may violate the composite gates of [[the-skew-is-the-product]] - admissible only through the floor-level test, never on standalone income-per-tail.
- [ ] **Floor pays.** Size a trend (long-gamma) and carry (short-gamma) composite so full-sample net skew is near zero and test whether it beats either sleeve alone on held-out crisis-conditional return at matched volatility - the core two-pole claim shared with [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] and [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]].
- [ ] **Harvest, do not rent.** Compare a collateralized, unlevered short-gamma construction against a levered/listed-volatility version of the same premium on a payoff-per-survivable-loss basis, confirming that the concave pole's losses stay budgeted rather than open-ended.
