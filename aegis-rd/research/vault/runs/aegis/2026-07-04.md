# 2026-07-04

## Goal

H1 of [[monetizing-the-tail-sleeve]]: does a drift band alone (the allocator's tail-vs-core
band, layer 1) cut the tail leg's execution-cost drag without giving up crash-day capture?
New strategy `aegis.tailOverlay` (LVO at a sub-1.0 target weight, remainder cash - the weight
can finally drift, so the band gates), config `tail_band.yaml`. The band pair is a portfolio
knob, so it loop-edits across launches (the destsweep pattern): B0 control 0/0 (daily
constant-mix), B1 0.01/0.01, B2 0.01/0.05 (harvest-fast/re-fund-slow), B3 0.025/0.10.
`hedge_weight` {0.02, 0.05} sweeps in-grid. Component parity verified before launch:
`tailOverlay` at hedge_weight 1.0 reproduces `heldHedge` byte-for-byte.

## Runs

### tail_band B0 (control, band 0/0)

- **Hypothesis**: the daily-rebalanced sub-1.0 tail weight reproduces the P2 static-overlay
  execution profile on the real engine - high fill count (~251 trades/split) and a fee drag
  in the ~6%/yr region at w=0.05 on the 5k book - establishing the baseline the banded cells
  must beat. Crash-day capture should scale ~linearly with hedge_weight (sleeve +2.6%/day
  held-out -> book ~+0.13%/day at w=0.05, ~+0.05%/day at w=0.02).
- **Keep/kill criteria** (pre-registered, before launching, for the WHOLE B0-B3 protocol):
  - **keep**: some banded cell (B1-B3) cuts the tail leg's fee drag to <= 2%/yr (vs B0)
    AND retains >= 80% of B0's held-out crash-day capture at the same hedge_weight
    AND does not worsen held-out total return vs B0 (the band may not deepen the bleed).
  - **kill**: every banded cell loses > 20% of crash-day capture, or none halves the fee drag.
  - Expectation on record: the band fixes EXECUTION cost only; the ~-23%/yr roll bleed is
    structural (Bernstein's caveat in the article) and stays until H2 (profit-multiple
    harvest) / H3 (expected-carry throttle). Judged at book level; never on sleeve-standalone
    Sharpe.
- **What changed since previous run**: new component `aegis.tailOverlay` v1.0.0 (heldHedge
  shape scaled to hedge_weight, remainder cash); everything else (window 2013-2025, LVO.XAMS,
  fees, splits, crash_day_return ranker) mirrors `tail_vool.yaml` and its fresh lock
  `20260704T143038842848Z` for comparability.
- **Result** (`20260704T174335304443Z_aegis_tail_band`, 9 splits, 0 degenerate): held-out
  means - w0.05: 116 trades/split, **314 EUR fees/split (~6.3%/yr on the 5k book)**, total
  return **-7.25%/split**, crash-day +0.106%/day, maxDD 8.0. w0.02: 116 trades, 314 EUR,
  -6.67%/split, +0.027%/day. The fee floor pays ~1.25 EUR on a near-zero drift correction
  every bar regardless of weight - fees are ~6pp of the ~7pp loss; the instrument bleed
  contribution at w=0.05 is only ~-1.1%/yr. Baseline established exactly as hypothesized.
- **Verdict**: baseline recorded (control, no keep/kill of its own) - the drag P2 charged to
  the static overlay is mostly EXECUTION, not instrument, at this book size.

## Day summary

H1 answered in one session: **the drift band alone converts the tail sleeve from
execution-dominated to bleed-dominated.** The daily-rebalanced control (B0) loses
~7.25%/split at w=0.05, of which ~6.3pp is the fixed-fee floor paying 1.25 EUR on a
near-zero drift correction every bar - P2's static-overlay drag was mostly execution, not
instrument, at this book size. One weight-point of tolerance (B1) collapses that to ~0.1%/yr
in fees, and crash-day capture RISES to ~120% of the control because the spike drifts
unharvested until +1pp instead of being trimmed daily. What remains, -1.05%/split at w=0.05,
is almost exactly w x the -23%/yr roll bleed: the structural cost H2 (profit-multiple
harvest) and H3 (expected-carry throttle) exist to attack, exactly as pre-registered.

Two design laws for the overlay came out of the degenerate cells: (1) **the re-fund band
must sit strictly below the target weight** - the DriftBand contract gates the initial open
through the same inclusive gate (has_realized dropped by design), so band_down >= w0 never
opens the position and the run reads as an all-zero stream; (2) the engine's degeneracy
detector did NOT flag those all-zero streams (excluded_degenerate 0) - a zero-trade
flat-line candidate should probably count as degenerate.

H2 and H3 closed the day with the same shape of answer from two directions: **the
monetization overlay IS the asymmetric drift band - every signal layered on top of it
died on pre-registered criteria.** H2 (profit-multiple harvest): no multiple improves
total return, because the band's trim side already performs the constant-mix
monetization (M=2 sells the big spike too early; M=3/5 barely fire). H3 (expected-carry
throttle): the fast carry states that catch the COVID onset whipsaw the fee floor to
death, and the slow state that trades cheaply is late at the onset and halves the crash
capture - only 1 of 3 lookbacks even cut the bleed (spec-luck). The mechanism both kills
share: H1's band already collapsed the sleeve's carryable cost to ~1%/yr at w=0.05, so
any overlay signal must beat a very small number net of its own whipsaw, fees and
capture losses - and neither the position's own P&L path (H2) nor its own carry state
(H3) clears that bar on this window. What survives of the article's design: the
state-conditioning thesis is dead at sleeve level in price-only form; the band asymmetry
(harvest-fast/re-fund-slow) and the never-zero floor are the whole sleeve-level rule.
