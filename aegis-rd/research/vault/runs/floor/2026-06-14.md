# 2026-06-14

## Goal

Re-test the roster's load-bearing claim **"Floor pays"** ([[the-tiered-strategy-roster]],
line 77) with a **duration-stripped credit pole**. The 2026-06-13 runs left it
**iterate/partial-keep**: distribution-carry was *refuted* (realized skew ~0, too weak to
anchor a skew budget); VRP *fixed* the concavity (qskew −1.18) but VRP is **not
UCITS-retail-tradable** (short-vol ETPs are banned for retail). Open question: can the
concave pole be re-sourced as something **live, long-only, EU-retail** that recovers VRP's
concavity *and* makes the floor pay?

## Runs

**Result** (same trend pole both rows; only the concave pole swaps):

- **Result**: the duration strip **clears every defect the refutation pinned**. The pole is
  now a genuine concave anchor (qskew **−0.75 → −1.83**, deeper than VRP's −1.18); the
  skew budget balances at a real **w\* = 0.60** (crosses zero, *not* the degenerate ~0.99
  that refuted distribution-carry); poles orthogonal (corr **−0.11**); the held-out pole
  Sharpe is **+0.70 → +1.17** (`aerd` A/B, `carry_duration.yaml` vs `carry.yaml`). Against
  the same 20-name trend the composite lifts across the board: Sharpe **0.62 → 0.90**, UPI
  **0.91 → 2.17**, multi-window crisis **−0.95% → +5.56%**. The single driver is **2022**:
  the duration-laden pole lost −7.9% (rates up *and* spreads wide), the rate-hedged pole
  made +5.5% (spread only).
- **But it still does NOT pass the literal *beats-both-poles* bar.** Composite crisis
  **+5.56% < trend-only +7.86%**; composite maxDD **−26.6%** is *worse* than trend-only
  **−17.0%** (the deep −1.83 skew deepens the blended drawdown, since credit crises coincide
  with equity crises — short-gamma loses exactly when trend is needed, structural). Held-out
  leave-one-crisis-out: composite beats both **0/2**; rolling worst-day crisis-conditional
  median trend −0.03% / carry −0.46% / composite −0.26% (composite loses on the worst day,
  as the roster already flags).
- **The bottleneck moved.** Not carry-too-weak (fixed) but **trend-too-strong**: the new
  20-name held-out-winner trend pole is an exceptional crisis performer (qskew **+2.36**,
  +7.86%), so *any* short-gamma carry can only dilute its crisis return and deepen DD. VRP
  "paid" on 2026-06-13 only because that run's trend was weaker (+4.68%). Improving the trend
  pole **raised the bar** the floor has to clear.

## Day summary

The duration-stripped credit pole is the **UCITS-tradable answer to VRP's defect** — it
recovers (exceeds) VRP's concavity while staying long-only, retail, and live-2026, and it
fixes the sizing degeneracy that refuted the original credit/distribution pole. As a
*portfolio*, it lifts the floor's calm-market efficiency hard (UPI 0.91 → 2.17) and turns the
composite's crisis return positive (−0.95% → +5.56%). It does **not** beat the now-very-strong
20-name trend pole in crises, and it does not reduce the worst drawdown — which is the
roster's own division of labor (trend = drawdown payer, carry = income engine), not a failure
of the pole. **Verdict: iterate/partial-keep — same shape as VRP, now UCITS-tradable.** The
"Floor pays" hypothesis stays open, but its blocker is now the *bar* (a crisis-convex trend
pole), not the *concave sleeve* (sourced).

## Addendum — the blend objective: skew-neutral over-buys carry (drop it)

After the trend pole was finalized (single lb252 + turnover buffer; [[runs/atalanta/2026-06-14]]),
re-examined the floor's `w*` RULE itself — currently **skew-neutral** (net qskew=0). Swept four
objectives (skew-neutral / UPI-max / crisis-max / equal-risk) on the live floor (buffered pole +
`carry_duration`), London Tier-1 and the long US-proxy. Harness `/tmp/floor_wstar_objective.py`
(+ `_longwindow`).

| window | rule | w* (carry) | UPI | crisis% | maxDD% | qskew |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| London 2018-24 | skew-neutral | 0.59 | 2.38 | +6.20 | −26.2 | +0.02 |
| London 2018-24 | UPI-max | 0.59 | 2.38 | +6.28 | −26.0 | +0.09 |
| London 2018-24 | **w≈0.40 (knee)** | 0.40 | 2.08 | **+8.72** | **−16.9** | +2.14 |
| US-proxy 2015-24 | skew-neutral | **0.15** | 0.04 | +0.31 | −23.9 | −0.00 |
| US-proxy 2015-24 | UPI-max | 0.70 | 0.89 | −0.40 | −20.2 | −0.89 |
| US-proxy 2015-24 | equal-risk 0.50 | 0.50 | 0.50 | +0.03 | **−15.5** | −0.56 |

Findings:
- **Skew-neutral and UPI-max BOTH over-buy carry.** At their weight (London 0.59) the "floor" has
  a **deeper drawdown than the trend pole alone** (−26.2 vs −17.0) and dilutes crisis capture
  (+6.20 vs +8.76) — the carry's own credit drawdowns (−31.6 standalone) dominate once you hold
  ~60%. UPI/Sharpe are **calm-drawdown-path-dominated**, so they over-reward carry's calm
  smoothing and under-count the rare crisis drawdown — the same failure mode the *pole* ranking
  fix (Sharpe→UPI) addressed, reappearing one level up.
- **The skew-neutral RULE is window-unstable** — w* = 0.59 (London) vs **0.15** (US-proxy) on the
  *same poles*, because it keys off the trend pole's *measured* qskew (+2.41 vs +0.25, the proxy
  understatement). A weight that swings 0.15↔0.59 on a noisy skew estimate is not trustworthy.
- **A fixed, light, equal-risk-ish weight (~0.40–0.50 carry) is the robust answer.** It gives the
  best drawdown on *both* windows (−16.9 @0.40 London, −15.5 @0.50 proxy), recovers crisis capture
  on the real book (+8.72), keeps the floor **net-convex** (qskew +2.14) — carry as an income
  overlay (UPI 1.14→2.08), NOT a skew-canceller. The long window validates the *direction* (light
  carry → better drawdown; UPI over-buys); the crisis-recovery *magnitude* is only readable on the
  real London book (proxy trend too weak).

**Revised floor rule:** weight the poles at a **fixed ~0.40 carry (net-convex)**, not skew-neutral
and not UPI. A drawdown-payer floor should *keep* its convexity (that IS the protection) and use
carry as a bounded income overlay. This supersedes the skew-neutral `w*` used in runs 1–4.
