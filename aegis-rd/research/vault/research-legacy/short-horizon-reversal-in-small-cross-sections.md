# Short-Horizon Reversal in Small Cross-Sections

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> Short-horizon reversal is compensation for absorbing transient order flow, but that does not make every contrarian rule a concave income sleeve. The classic close-to-close signal has decayed, its net profitability depends on construction and cost assumptions, and its shared crash state with carry is a plausible funding-liquidity inference rather than an established distributional fact. In ETFs the cleanest evidence is a one-day market-price reversal supplied by authorized participants, with no forecast of the underlying basket. That points first to reversal as an execution overlay - delay liquidity-demanding trades - and only second to a standalone sleeve.

## The other way to be short gamma

[[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole|Carry]] is one route to the floor's concave pole; short-horizon reversal is the other mechanism the roster names for that slot.[[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] Buying recent losers and selling recent winners over days to a few weeks has earned a premium since it was first documented: weekly winners and losers reverse the following week, and a monthly contrarian strategy long past losers and short past winners earned around 2.5% a month gross over 1934-1987.[^lehmann][^jegadeesh] The question for the floor is not whether reversal exists but whether it is *concave* - whether it belongs on the short-gamma side with carry - and whether it survives the two things that kill it: costs and thin cross-sections.

## Reversal is paid to provide liquidity

The economic content of reversal is liquidity provision. When a wave of order flow pushes a price away from fundamentals, the contrarian who takes the other side is paid to absorb the imbalance, and the price reverts as the pressure clears. Nagel makes the mechanism precise: short-term reversal returns are compensation for supplying liquidity, they are highly time-varying, and they are *predictable by the VIX* - expected returns and conditional Sharpe ratios of reversal strategies spike during financial turmoil, when liquidity suppliers withdraw and the price of immediacy jumps.[^nagel] That is the same crisis-timing signature carry has, and it places reversal firmly on the concave pole: it earns most by selling immediacy-insurance precisely when that insurance is dearest, which is when the market is falling.

The modern lineage makes the counterparty explicit. A multiperiod model with short- and long-horizon noise traders plus active investors who underreact to information they did not produce generates short-run reversal that transitions gradually into momentum; across US and international markets, reversals are larger where noise trading is greater and weaker after earnings announcements, where price moves contain more information.[^jeglsubtit] The premium exists because non-informational demand must be warehoused and because the active side cannot instantly distinguish a transient flow from news. Reversal against a price move caused by information is not liquidity provision - it is simply being wrong early.

ETFs now supply a direct test of that mechanism in the instrument class this article needs. Authorized participants and market makers can meet excess ETF demand by selling shares before creating them, an "operational short" that accounts for roughly two-thirds of ETF short interest in the study's sample. Operational shorting predicts a next-day reversal in the ETF market price, particularly for non-equity ETFs and equity ETFs whose baskets are much less liquid than the wrapper, but it does not predict the underlying basket's NAV return.[^evans] The other side is largely retail order flow; the intermediary earns for bridging a temporary wrapper-level imbalance, not for discovering that an asset class is fundamentally mispriced.

This is why reversal is a candidate short-gamma mechanism rather than a long-gamma one. A liquidity provider is structurally on the wrong side of a trend that keeps going and the right side of an overreaction that snaps back. In a sustained dislocation - the regime the long-gamma trend sleeve is built for - the reverter keeps stepping in front of a move that does not reverse, and loses. But mechanism is not measured shape: position limits, exit rules and the split between the liquidation and repair phases can change realized skew, so concavity remains an empirical gate.

## The cost wall

Reversal's defining problem is that its turnover is enormous and its gross edge sits close to the cost line. Using live institutional trade data, short-term reversal has among the smallest break-even capacities of the major anomalies: where value and momentum survive at tens of billions of dollars, reversal's break-even fund size is about $9 billion in the United States and $13 billion globally - far below the corresponding size, value and momentum capacities.[^fim] Daily and weekly reversal profits collapse once bid-ask spreads are charged, because a large part of the raw signal is bid-ask bounce rather than alpha, and the residual concentrates in exactly the illiquid names where it is most expensive to trade.[^acg] The naive strategy is, for practical purposes, unimplementable.

That conclusion is disputed at small scale. De Groot, Huij and Zhou exclude expensive small caps and reduce turnover through portfolio construction, reporting 30-50 basis points per week net of modeled trading costs; the live-trade study still finds reversal the least scalable anomaly and little helped by cost optimization.[^degroot][^fim] These are not clean replications of each other: one asks whether a carefully constructed small strategy can clear estimated costs, the other estimates capacity from nearly $1 trillion of institutional trades. The defensible conclusion is conditional, not universal: reversal can clear the cost line in liquid equities at modest size, but a gross backtest and a generic 10-basis-point haircut do not establish that it does.

What survives is the *conditioned* version. Restricting reversal to large-cap, liquid names and neutralizing the short-term industry and factor momentum that fights it roughly doubles the risk-adjusted return and lifts it above the cost line; a composite of short-term signals traded with low-turnover buy-and-hold rules retains a meaningful net alpha where the naive single signal does not.[^residual][^stsignals] By 2024 the classic effect had vanished in most tested regions, while a version neutralized against short-term industry and factor momentum retained more than twice the risk-adjusted performance; its authors nevertheless recommend combining reversal with other short-term signals to make implementation viable.[^revtrend] All three favorable studies are Robeco-authored, so they form one practitioner evidence cluster, not three independent confirmations. The edge lives in universe, horizon, turnover control and what is neutralized - not in the sign of last month's return alone.

## The stronger use is execution, not another sleeve

Liquidity has two dimensions that prescribe different holding periods. In a 2024 international study, higher volatility produces stronger but faster reversals because inventory risk is high, while lower turnover produces slower, more persistent reversals because intermediaries must warehouse inventory longer.[^dai] A fixed lookback across instruments ignores the mechanism: the horizon should shorten as volatility rises and lengthen as turnover falls. More importantly, the authors' practical prescription is to use reversal as a rebalancing screen - postpone a trade that would demand expensive liquidity, but only until the expected concession has decayed - rather than incur new turnover to harvest the signal as a standalone book.[^dai]

That use defines errors out of existence. A sleeve must overcome spread, impact, borrow, breadth and crash-correlation gates before it adds anything to the roster. An execution overlay acts only when the book already intends to trade, so it can improve the entry price without creating an extra position or turnover budget. It also matches the ETF evidence: an operational short forecasts the wrapper's next-day price correction but not the basket's return, exactly the kind of temporary dislocation that should alter *when* an order is placed rather than *what* the portfolio owns.[^evans]

## It crashes with carry, not against it

The more serious objection is diversification. A second concave sleeve is only worth holding next to carry if it loses at *different* times; if it loses in the same unwinds, the book has simply doubled its short-gamma position under two labels - the redundancy trap the convexity argument warns against.[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] The August 2007 quant meltdown supplies the strongest direct case: a crowded, levered market-neutral contrarian book lost heavily over three days as funds deleveraged into each other, then rebounded once forced selling cleared.[^kl]

But one episode does not establish that reversal and carry crash together as a general law. After broad market declines, equity liquidity deteriorates most when funding is tight and liquidity commonality rises across industries; reversal returns then become stronger for up to two weeks, which is compensation available *after* intermediaries retreat.[^hkv] Carry crashes are likewise tied to funding-liquidity contractions, but joining those two literatures is our own synthesis: it implies shared state exposure, not identical timing. A reversal rule may lose while forced flow continues and earn during the subsequent repair. The redundancy claim therefore stays a hypothesis to measure on realized streams, not a settled reason to reject the sleeve.

## Thin cross-sections

Reversal is a cross-sectional bet: it ranks instruments against each other and trades the spread. That makes it breadth-hungry, and a floor universe of a dozen-odd asset-class instruments is thin on the axis reversal needs. The same breadth arithmetic that constrains relative momentum applies here - a handful of correlated risk assets collapse to few independent comparisons, so a rank among them is unstable.[[relative-vs-absolute-momentum-in-small-universes]] Richards documents slow multi-year winner-loser reversal among national equity indices, but that result does not validate a daily or weekly rule on a small ETF book.[^richards]

The cross-asset evidence changes the object rather than solving the breadth problem. A working paper across equity-index, rate, commodity and currency futures finds conventional close-to-close reversal mixed by asset class, while buying low overnight returns and selling high overnight returns for the following intraday session is positive across all four; using volume-weighted prices, its reported annualized Sharpes range from 0.58 in rates to 1.67 in equity indices, before a full implementation-cost audit.[^dellacorte] The result suggests that decomposing the return clock can matter more than extending the lookback, but it remains a daily, high-turnover working-paper strategy on broad futures cross-sections. It is evidence for a different mechanism-aware candidate, not proof that twelve correlated ETFs provide enough breadth.

## Limitations

The liquidity-provision account now transfers to ETFs, but only in a narrow form: operational shorting predicts a next-day reversal in the ETF market price, not in the underlying NAV, and the effect is strongest where wrapper and basket liquidity differ.[^evans] That does not validate a generic close-to-close asset-class reversal sleeve or the VIX-conditioned equity result on a small ETF universe.

Much of the "conditioned reversal survives costs" evidence comes from a single asset manager that sells short-term-signal strategies, which is a conflict of interest on the favorable conclusion.[^residual][^stsignals] The cost-failure result for naive reversal is the more independently corroborated half.[^fim][^acg]

The independent large-cap net-profit result uses modeled and broker-estimated costs, while the contrary capacity paper uses proprietary live institutional trades; scale, sample and cost methodology differ, so neither number transfers directly to a retail ETF book.[^degroot][^fim]

The crash-correlation claim rests on one direct reversal episode plus a composite inference from separate reversal and carry liquidity literatures.[^kl][^hkv] The inference is untested: reversal may lose during forced liquidation and earn during the repair, so contemporaneous and lagged joint-tail behavior must both be measured before calling it redundant with carry.

No kept primary source establishes a minimum viable cross-section for reversal. The breadth floor is transferred from portfolio breadth arithmetic and is explicitly a hypothesis, while the only cross-asset reversal study kept is an unrefereed working paper with no publication after a decade.[^dellacorte]

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **Reversal is concave on our universe.** A cross-sectional dollar-neutral reversion stream on the floor universe shows the concave *shape* (negative quarterly skew, $\beta_2 \le 0$, negative crisis-conditional return, non-negative bear-regime beta) - it sits on the concave pole. **But shape is not the pole (2026-06-13):** its drift is negative, so it *pays* the premium rather than collecting it, and it fails the income requirement. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#own-gamma + mirror-gate (diagnostic)]].
- [x] **The cost line is the gate.** Confirmed 2026-06-13: a naive short-horizon reversion sleeve loses net at a 10bps round-trip on our book (Sharpe negative across all lookbacks, more negative for the higher-turnover cross-sectional form), so the gross reversal premium sits below the cost line exactly as the literature warns - turnover control is a build requirement, and on a trend-selected substrate the gross signal is on the wrong side besides. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13|2026-06-13]].
- [ ] **Reversal does not diversify carry.** Measure the realized-return correlation and the joint crisis-conditional behavior of a reversal sleeve and a carry sleeve; if they lose together in the same unwinds, the floor's concave slot holds one mechanism, not two, and the second adds redundancy rather than diversification.
- [ ] **Reversal improves execution before it earns a sleeve.** On trades already demanded by a slow parent strategy, delay entries that fight a volatility-and-turnover-calibrated reversal screen for at most the estimated decay horizon. Pre-register zero added orders, lower implementation shortfall net of missed-move opportunity cost, and no deterioration in the parent strategy's held-out return; otherwise the screen is timing noise, not liquidity capture.[^dai][^evans]
- [ ] **The horizon must follow the liquidity state.** A reversal signal whose holding period shortens with volatility and lengthens as turnover falls beats fixed 1-, 5- and 21-day horizons net of identical costs; expected direction follows inventory risk and inventory duration, and is refuted if the adaptive rule only shifts turnover or fails adjacent-horizon sensitivity.[^dai]
- [ ] **Breadth floor.** Test whether cross-sectional reversal ranks carry information at the floor universe's size or are mostly noise, against the independent-bets count, before trusting any single-spec reversal result (the spec-luck discipline from [[relative-vs-absolute-momentum-in-small-universes]]). Treat overnight-to-intraday reversal as a separate candidate from close-to-close reversal rather than pooling their evidence.[^dellacorte]
