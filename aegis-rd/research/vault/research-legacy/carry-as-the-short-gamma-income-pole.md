# Carry and the Short-Gamma Income Pole

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> The floor's concave half is an income engine that earns by selling insurance: it collects a premium in calm and gives it back in dislocation. Carry is its canonical form, but the negative skew that makes it the true counterpart to a trend sleeve lives in *currencies* - and only construction-dependently in commodities - not in equity or bond carry, so a generic cross-asset carry basket is near-symmetric and only half the job. Explicit volatility carry offers the cleanest concavity, but covered calls and cash-secured puts are mostly equity exposure, cleaner variance trades carry harder margin and tail problems, and recent evidence says the historical option alpha has approached zero. The pole's defining payoff must therefore be isolated, collateralized, sized to a survivable stress loss, and verified in recent data rather than inferred from a legacy index.

## The pole the trend sleeve cannot fill

A trend sleeve is long gamma: it bleeds a little in calm and pays out in dislocation. Held alone it is an expensive hedge with no engine to fund it. The floor is finished only when a second sleeve sits opposite it on the convexity axis (the argument in [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]]) - short gamma, negatively skewed, paid to carry the risk the trend sleeve is paid to shed. That sleeve is the income engine: it compounds the premium that structurally accrues to sellers of crash risk, and it is the counterparty whose losses in the tail are the trend sleeve's gains. The roster names this the hardest piece to source and the feasibility gate for the whole construction, precisely because a concave sleeve that does not pay in calm is just a worse long position.[[the-tiered-strategy-roster]]

Carry is the obvious candidate. In its general form - the return an asset earns if its price does not move - carry has been shown to predict returns within and across every major asset class, with a diversified cross-asset carry portfolio earning a high Sharpe ratio that survives published transaction-cost estimates.[^carry] The premium is not a publication artifact: a 153-factor replication across 93 countries places carry among the factors whose out-of-sample evidence strengthens rather than decays under a Bayesian correction,[^jkp] and a separate reconstruction running back to 1800 finds a multi-asset carry premium significant in both the original and the long pre-sample period.[^gfp] The anchor without a fund attached is Lustig, Roussanov and Verdelhan: sorting currencies by interest differential isolates a single global "slope" factor that accounts for roughly 70% of the cross-sectional variation in currency excess returns, and the premium survives transaction costs - carry is a priced, robust feature of the data, not a manager's backtest.[^lrv] Carry pays, and it keeps paying.

## Where the negative skew actually lives

Carry is not inherently short gamma. Carry is an accounting characteristic, while concavity is a payoff property. Some carry constructions are negatively skewed; others are symmetric, positively skewed, or mainly compensated beta. This article is therefore about selecting a concave carry sleeve for one portfolio role, not a claim about the whole carry family. See [[carry-is-not-one-premium]] for the broader taxonomy.

Paying is necessary but not sufficient. The pole's job is to be *concave* - to absorb the loss in the tail - and that is a statement about skew ([[skewness-in-asset-returns]]), not about average return. Here the evidence is sharper than the general carry literature lets on, and it is the single fact that should govern how the sleeve is built.

The crash skew of carry is overwhelmingly a *currency* phenomenon. Sorting currencies by interest-rate differential, the high-yield (investment) currencies that earn the carry are exactly the ones that crash, and the funding currencies that finance it are positively skewed; across currencies, average skewness falls almost linearly with the average interest-rate differential, a relationship that explains the great majority of the cross-sectional variation.[^bnp] Commodity carry carries some of the same negative asymmetry, through the inventory and convenience-yield channel that links a backwardated term structure to scarcity and stress.[^carry][^ghr] Neither of those legs now rests on one table: the FX skew survives even dollar-neutral construction - Daniel, Hodrick and Lu strip the dollar exposure out of G10 carry and the negative skew and downside beta remain, so the asymmetry belongs to carry itself rather than to an embedded dollar bet[^dhl] - and in commodities the skewness cross-section is directly priced, a factor long the most negatively-skewed and short the most positively-skewed contracts earning about 8% a year, independent confirmation that commodity-futures asymmetry is a real, compensated property.[^fpskew] But equity carry is *positively* skewed and bond (term-structure) carry is close to symmetric, so a diversified all-asset carry portfolio comes out with skewness near zero - lower-tailed than passive equity, not more.[^carry] A second, independently constructed decomposition now brackets that table: at matched volatility across four asset classes, carry's monthly skew reads deeply negative in FX (-1.0 to -1.2 across both cross-sectional and time-series implementations) and positive in equities (+0.2) - but *positive* in commodities (+0.2 to +0.6) and sign-unstable in rates (+0.36 cross-sectional, -0.58 time-series), where Koijen et al. read -0.40 and near-zero respectively.[^bazdissect] The two decompositions agree exactly where the mechanism story lives (FX negative, equity positive) and disagree where it is weakest: the commodity and bond skew signs are construction-dependent, not facts of the asset class. The convexity article reached the same conclusion from the allocation side and flagged it as a sourcing constraint;[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] the carry literature confirms it from the inside.

The consequence for construction is direct and it mirrors the substrate lesson the trend pole already learned. Just as a trend sleeve realizes long gamma only on a non-equity book whose moves are sustained trends, a carry sleeve realizes the *concave* signature only where funding-unwind dynamics bite - reliably in FX, and only construction-dependently in commodities. Build the concave pole from bond or equity carry and it may pay, but it will not carry the negative skew that makes it the trend sleeve's true counterpart; it will be a second income stream of the wrong shape. The pole must be sourced to the mechanism, not assembled from whatever carries.

## Common versus asset-specific carry crashes

FX funding unwinds, commodity scarcity, credit defaults, rate repricing, and option-volatility explosions can occur in different states and may diversify one another. They become a common carry crash when funding, margin, dealer balance sheets, crowding, and forced deleveraging synchronize liquidation. Sleeve selection should therefore test both asset-specific tail states and portfolio-level co-crash exposure; a shared negative skew sign is not enough.

This distinction limits the pole claim. A portfolio role can require concavity without assuming every constituent loses in the same crisis. Conversely, low ordinary correlation does not establish tail diversification. Conditional correlation, co-expected-shortfall, funding-currency exposure, and liquidation capacity belong beside standalone skew.

## Why someone keeps paying, and the unresolved part

A short-gamma premium is credible only if the buyers on the other side cannot easily leave. For carry the standard account is funding liquidity: investment currencies are held by leveraged players whose positions unwind together when funding dries up, so the losses arrive all at once and coincide with spikes in volatility and the unwinding of crowded trades.[^bnp] That mechanism no longer hangs on one affiliated study: an intermediary-based model with the same shape - global interest-rate volatility tightens FX dealers' constraints, and the forced unwinding delivers the loss - prices 92% of the cross-sectional variation in carry and momentum returns,[^zeng] and the asset-pricing side closes the loop, with high-rate currencies loading negatively on innovations in global FX volatility while funding currencies hedge it,[^mss] and the whole forward-discount cross-section priced by a global *downside* market factor whose estimated premium matches the one in equities - the income is a rent on downside beta, earned in currencies at the same price as everywhere else.[^dob] The seller is paid the rest of the time for standing in front of that unwind. This is the same bargain Ilmanen describes across asset classes - buying crash insurance is poorly rewarded, selling it is paid - which is why the concave pole is an income engine at all.[^ins]

What is *not* settled is whether the premium is compensation for the crash or simply mispriced volatility insurance, and the disagreement is worth stating because it bears on how the sleeve should be hedged. Jurek hedges currency carry with out-of-the-money options and finds that crash risk accounts for at most about a third of the excess return - crash-neutral carry stays significantly positive, so the premium is mostly *not* a payment for the crash.[^jurek] Caballero and Doyle run the comparison the other way and find carry returns move with a short-volatility (VIX-rolldown) position closely enough that controlling for it absorbs most of the carry alpha - on their reading carry is disguised short volatility, and the premium evaporates when volatility normalizes.[^cd] Burnside, Eichenbaum, Kleshchelski and Rebelo push the crash story furthest: hedging the carry trade with at-the-money currency options leaves a risk-adjusted payoff statistically indistinguishable from zero, which they read as a peso problem - the feared event being a state of very high marginal utility rather than a very large in-sample loss.[^bekr] Jurek attributes that gap to construction (at-the-money hedges are the most expensive insurance available, and hedging each leg separately through dollar pairs pays for dollar protection the trade does not need), but the split is methodological and unresolved. All three are credible; they disagree on the central question. The honest synthesis is that carry is *at least partly* a short-volatility premium and partly something else, and a sleeve that hedges its tail will keep some of the return and shed some of the risk - but how much of each is genuinely uncertain.

## Selling insurance, seen directly

The cleanest live instance of the concave pole is the one that sells the insurance explicitly. The variance risk premium - the gap between option-implied and subsequently realized variance - is reliably positive, so a seller of variance earns a premium that standard factor models cannot explain, because variance spikes exactly when the market falls and buyers will overpay to hedge that.[^cw] That premium predicts market returns at the quarterly horizon, which is the signature of a priced risk rather than a free lunch.[^btz] Realized in an index, it looks like the put-writing strategies whose long-run record is the concave pole in miniature: a cash-secured S&P 500 put-write earns an equity-like return at roughly two-thirds the volatility and a higher Sharpe ratio, with a shallower maximum drawdown than the index in 2008 - and a monthly return skewness far more negative than the market's, the quantitative fingerprint of a short-gamma payoff.[^put] The down-beta of such a strategy exceeds its up-beta: it gives back in the tail what it collected in the calm, which is the concave shape stated as a number.

The conflict-free record corroborates the shape and supplies the persistence mechanism. Santa-Clara and Saretto find short S&P 500 option strategies earn Sharpe ratios well beyond what pricing models warrant - and then show why competition does not close the gap: margin requirements cap how many options a seller can write, and margin calls force positions closed precisely when they are losing, so the arbitrage capital that would compress the premium is structurally rationed (a 10%-out-of-the-money put program's Sharpe falls from 0.30 to 0.10 once margins bind).[^scs] The counter deserves equal weight: benchmarked against option-pricing models rather than CAPM alphas, Broadie, Chernov and Johannes find the famous out-of-the-money put returns are not even inconsistent with Black-Scholes - so the premium should be sized as fairly-priced insurance plus frictions, not as free money.[^bcj]

These figures come from index back-tests, several of them exchange-funded, and flatter a live implementation that bears fees and slippage (see Limitations). But the contractual *shape* is not in doubt and is corroborated by the conflict-free academic work on the premium itself.[^cw][^btz] Its realized skew over one short product history is a different claim. Long-horizon skew is noisy, horizon-dependent, and starved of non-overlapping observations; a crash-free sample can therefore report flat or positive skew for a strategy whose payoff remains contractually concave.[^neubskew] Realized skew says which states occurred in the sample, not whether a written put ceased to be short gamma.

## Which collateralized wrapper owns the premium

A covered call is not a clean variance sleeve. Its payoff decomposes into equity exposure, short-volatility exposure, and an equity-reversal exposure created as option delta changes. In the S&P 500 history studied by Israelov and Nielsen, equity supplied most risk and return; short volatility contributed less than 10% of risk despite a realized Sharpe near 1, while the reversal component contributed roughly one-quarter of risk with little return.[^covered] The result is affiliated, but its attribution warning is load-bearing: a covered-call ETF can look like successful volatility carry because equity rose.

Cash-secured put-write and covered-call payoffs at the same strike and maturity are linked by put-call parity. The roughly 1.1% annual return gap between the Cboe PUT and BXM indexes over 1986-2015 should not be read as a distinct put premium; expiration-morning exposure and index construction explain the apparent violation.[^parity] Collateral choice still matters operationally, but "covered call versus put-write" is mainly a question of implementation, liquidity, tax, and timing conventions once equity exposure is matched. It also matters to measured profitability: charging the path-dependent funding cost of required collateral can turn apparently high put-writing alpha negative.[^collateralerror] A fully funded ETF avoids a naked seller's margin call, but attribution must still separate collateral return and wrapper drag rather than label the total residual "option income."

Delta-hedged calls or puts, short straddles, and variance swaps move closer to the intended volatility factor by reducing equity delta. They do not become interchangeable. A comparison using S&P 500 options from January 1996 through June 2021 identifies three separate design problems: payoff shape, leverage normalization, and finite maturity. Strategies with equivalent frictionless exposures differ after bid-side execution and Cboe margin rules; shorter contracts offer more factor exposure and liquidity but roll more often, while variance swaps give direct exposure but can suffer extreme squared-payoff losses.[^harvest] Covered calls and put-writes are therefore easier to collateralize but impure; delta-hedged options and variance swaps are purer but operationally harsher.

VIX-futures carry is a fourth object. It harvests changes in implied volatility and roll-down along the VIX curve, not necessarily the difference between option-implied and expected realized variance.[^harvest] A positive backtest in a short-VIX proxy cannot validate the claim that an option variance risk premium was harvested. It validates an implied-volatility term premium with its own path, rebalance, and product risks.

## The premium may have compressed

The historical evidence no longer supports assuming a stationary option alpha. Dew-Becker and Giglio compare traded options with dynamically replicated synthetic options and find traded-option alphas statistically indistinguishable from zero over roughly the last 15 years. Delta-hedged puts and straddles have produced approximately zero alpha since 2010, while the gap between VIX and realized volatility has also narrowed.[^vrpdecline] Their intermediary model reads the old premium as compensation created by option-market frictions, which fell as hedging became cheaper and new sellers entered.

A separate tradable-options construction reaches a compatible but broader conclusion. Across options on twenty futures over 2006-2020, most assets show significant variance premia, but the S&P 500 premium is not statistically different from zero once the March 2020 variance loss is included; excluding that single month changes its average from roughly -11.9% to -22.9%.[^crossvrp] The premium may persist outside US equity index options even if the canonical S&P 500 harvest has weakened, and one crisis observation can dominate the estimate precisely because that is the risk being sold.

This does not prove short volatility has zero expected return. Negative market beta and disaster exposure can still command compensation, and a 2024 no-declared-COI study finds that adding a variance-risk-premium factor explains the apparent alpha of cash-secured put-write portfolios.[^putvrp] It does change the burden of proof: the sleeve must show recent net income beyond cash and equity beta, then show that the remainder loads on a measured variance-risk-premium factor. The VRP loading is the intended premium; the final intercept is unexplained return, not "option income." A legacy 1986-start index is supporting history, not an expected-return estimate.

Recent implementation evidence also prevents a clean “dead premium” verdict. A peer-reviewed study using one-minute S&P 500 index-option data from 2018-2023 reports superior risk-adjusted returns for several systematic writing and hedging specifications after transaction costs, with Black-Scholes-Merton hedges generally safer than Variance-Gamma hedges and an intraday 130-minute hedge interval offering its best cost-risk compromise.[^recentwrite] That is one short, specification-rich study and does not overturn the low-alpha evidence. Together, the papers say construction and sample choice now determine the answer; neither unconditional promotion nor unconditional rejection is defensible.

## Trend is a delayed offset, not a contractual hedge

Short volatility and trend occupy opposite sides of convexity only after the trend signal has time to change position. Puts protect sudden gaps directly; trend has historically worked better in prolonged declines and can remain long risk through the first shock.[^aqrtail] The combined floor must therefore be tested by crash speed. A slow trend sleeve may offset a 2000-2002 or 2022-style grind while failing to fund the margin call created by a one-day volatility explosion. Liquidity and collateral must bridge that timing gap without forced selling.

## The rented-premium trap

The concave pole has a failure mode exactly dual to the trend pole's. The trend sleeve's mistake is to *rent* its convexity from listed volatility, paying a roll decay that is itself the premium the concave sleeve collects.[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] The concave sleeve's mistake is to harvest that same premium in a form whose tail is unbounded and leveraged. Selling variance through a levered, mechanically-rebalanced product converts the modest negative skew of a collateralized put-write into a path that can lose almost everything in a single session: the inverse-volatility ETPs that imploded in February 2018 fell on the order of 96% in one day when the rebalancing flow they generated amplified the very spike they were short.[^volmageddon] The premium was real; the wrapper was lethal. The rule that generalizes is the income-side complement to the trend pole's: harvest the short-gamma premium in a *collateralized, unlevered* form sized to the loss the book can survive, and treat any structure that must trade in the direction of the shock to maintain its exposure as a different and far more dangerous instrument. The pole is supposed to lose money in the tail - that is its job - but it must lose a budgeted amount, not its whole capital.

## Limitations

The negative-skew sourcing claim is the load-bearing one, and the second decomposition redraws its map. The FX leg is now triply sourced - the carry-crash literature, the dollar-neutral construction, and an independent decomposition reading -1.0 to -1.2[^bnp][^dhl][^bazdissect] - and the equity-positive sign is confirmed by the same second table (+0.2).[^bazdissect] The commodity and bond legs went the other way: the second decomposition reads commodity carry skew *positive* (+0.2 to +0.6, against Koijen et al.'s -0.40) and bond carry sign-unstable across implementations, so those two signs are construction-dependent, and the commodity leg's concavity rests on the priced skewness cross-section[^fpskew] and the inventory channel,[^ghr] not on a robust realized-skew fact. A sleeve that needs guaranteed negative skew has exactly one reliable substrate: currencies. Treat any specific skew magnitude as suggestive.

Several of the strongest performance figures carry conflicts of interest. The put-write record is exchange-funded and based on hypothetical, gross-of-cost index returns;[^put] much of the carry-survives-costs evidence is from managers who run the factors.[^carry][^jkp][^gfp] The peer-reviewed variance-premium work is the conflict-free backbone for *why* the premium exists;[^cw][^btz] the headline Sharpe ratios are not. The no-COI half of the ledger has since thickened - the carry premium and its downside-risk pricing are anchored by unaffiliated top-journal work,[^lrv][^mss][^dob] and the put-write shape by Santa-Clara and Saretto - but that same literature cuts against implementability: the margin frictions that preserve the premium also bind any seller trying to harvest it,[^scs] and Broadie, Chernov and Johannes caution that the raw put returns may not be mispriced at all.[^bcj]

The wrapper comparison adds its own evidence limits. The cleanest covered-call decomposition and put-call-parity diagnosis come from an option manager with a direct product conflict.[^covered][^parity] The seven-strategy comparison is a working paper, not peer-reviewed, and its 1996-2021 conclusions depend on modeled option execution, margin, and variance-swap replication.[^harvest] Dew-Becker and Giglio provide the strongest current counter, but their zero-alpha conclusion is about abnormal return after beta adjustment, not a claim that selling disaster risk is costless or cannot earn a risk premium.[^vrpdecline] Our hierarchy of wrappers is therefore a synthesis to test, not a settled ranking.

The post-2010 evidence is internally conflicting. Heston and Todorov's cross-asset result is still a working paper and its S&P 500 estimate is dominated by March 2020,[^crossvrp] while Wysocki and Ślepaczuk's favorable 2018-2023 result searches across models, hedge frequencies, and sizing methods in a short sample.[^recentwrite] Neither provides a direct live-fund estimate for a fully collateralized UCITS wrapper. That gap belongs in implementation notes, not in the generic claim.

Carry's premium is regime-dependent. There is credible evidence that G10 FX carry flattened markedly after 2008 as rate differentials compressed, with the decline attributed to a *disappearance* of the downside risk rather than to a crash - which, if right, means the very skew the pole is sourced for can switch off in a low-differential regime.[^fan] A sleeve built today on the assumption that FX carry is reliably concave inherits that regime risk. Two additions bound the claim from both sides. An independent monetary-conditions decomposition confirms the post-2008 flattening: the pre-crisis channel (expansive US policy compressing currency risk premia) stopped transmitting at the zero lower bound, and crisis/ZLB-era net carry returns turned negative.[^falconio] And the August 2024 yen-carry unwind - roughly 40 trillion yen ($250bn, likely an underestimate) of yen-funded positions unwinding in days, the funding currency spiking while equities crashed - demonstrates the regime is cyclical rather than permanent: once policy divergence re-widened rate differentials in 2022-2024, the premium and its crash both came back at full size.[^bis2024] The regime lever is therefore observable (differential width), not a mystery - which the re-entry hypothesis below operationalizes.

And the crash-share question is genuinely open - now three ways.[^jurek][^cd][^bekr] If Caballero and Doyle are closer to right, much of what looks like a carry premium is a volatility premium already captured by the put-write sleeve, and stacking both would be selling the same insurance twice - the within-pole redundancy that [[short-horizon-reversal-in-small-cross-sections]] raises for reversal applies here too. If Burnside and co-authors are right, the premium is payment for a state the sample never shows, and no realized-stream verification - including ours - can distinguish it from alpha.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **Source the skew to FX and commodities.** Build the concave sleeve from FX and commodity carry proxies and verify on our own universe that the realized stream carries the negative-skew / non-positive-$\beta_2$ signature, and that a bond/equity-carry-weighted version does *not* - confirming the sourcing constraint before trusting skew as the sleeve's verification signature. **Now investable, and first test failed 2026-07-03**: [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] identifies EM local-currency sovereign bonds (UCITS tracker IEML) as the long-only claim on the FX-carry premium *with its negative skew attached* (BIS mechanism evidence; Burger-Warnock skew documentation); tested as the `fx_weight` axis of the `carry_sota.yaml` sweep and **killed on the 2018-2025 window** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03#20260703T174905661970Z_demeter_eu_credit_carry_sota - the construction sweep|diary]]) - the leg diluted income-per-tail at every weight; the sourcing claim itself (FX carries the skew) is not refuted, but on this window the skew arrived without the premium. Re-entry: dollar-weakening regime or longer sample. Instrument re-check 2026-07-03: dedicated FX-carry UCITS ETPs survive only as ~10m-AUM zombies (Xtrackers DBX1AY, 2008 vintage), so EM local bonds remain the sole practical claim - the sourcing constraint is unchanged.
- [x] **Carry passes the mirror gate.** Confirm that a carry/short-gamma candidate's frozen stream passes the Carry/Mean-Reversion family gate (quarterly skew negative, $\beta_2 \le 0$, crisis-conditional return negative, bear-regime beta non-negative, moderate positive $\beta_1$) over a 36-60mo window, the mirror image of the trend gate in [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]]. **Confirmed 2026-06-13** (diagnostic, slow distribution-carry c252/vol63): quarterly skew -0.13, $\beta_2 \le 0$, crash-conditional -0.16%, bear-beta +0.12, $\beta_1$ +0.09 (sign-correct but below the 0.20 default); the negative skew is mild because the measurable carry is bond/credit/EM/FX-heavy. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#carry/yield diagnostic - the pivot (diagnostic)]].
- [x] **It pays in calm.** Verify the income-engine requirement directly: the concave sleeve earns positive held-out return in calm splits, since a short-gamma sleeve that does not compound the premium in quiet markets has no reason to exist. **Confirmed 2026-06-13:** the carry book locked at positive held-out Sharpe ~0.79 with 5/5 positive splits, where the price-reversion sleeve paid the premium (negative drift) - carry collects it. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#20260613T145955024575Z_demeter_carry]].
- [x] **The variance risk premium is the strongest concave pole, and it is trend's mirror.** Test whether selling variance directly ("Selling insurance, seen directly" above) carries a *deeper* concave signature than distribution-yield carry, and whether it is the structural opposite of the trend pole rather than merely a stronger carry. **Confirmed 2026-06-13** (diagnostic, `demeter.vol_carry`: VIX-term-structure short-vol signal, short-VXX execution, vol-targeted to 10%): quarterly skew **-1.18** and crisis-conditional **-0.95%** (vs distribution carry's -0.13 and -0.16% at matched vol - roughly 9x the skew and 6x the crisis loss), positive drift (Sharpe +0.57), bear-beta +0.10, $\beta_1$ +0.28; robust across six param cells. Trend is mechanically *long* variance, so short variance is its exact mirror on the convexity axis - the floor becomes a long-variance / short-variance barbell. The Treynor-Mazuy $\beta_2$ stays small only because it measures convexity in $SPY^2$ while VRP's concavity lives in the *vol* factor. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#vol-carry gate - the variance risk premium (diagnostic)]]. **Revised 2026-07-11:** this confirms payoff identity, not current expected-return superiority. Post-2010 evidence demotes VRP from champion to challenger pending the recent-net-premium test. The earlier claim that no put-write UCITS existed was false; [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] now identifies UBS IE00BLDGHT92 as the cleanest listed wrapper candidate. Covered-call UCITS remain composite-gated equity-income products rather than clean VRP implementations.
- [ ] **Floor pays.** Size a trend (long-gamma) and carry (short-gamma) composite so full-sample net skew is near zero and test whether it beats either sleeve alone on held-out crisis-conditional return at matched volatility - the core two-pole claim shared with [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] and [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]].
- [ ] **Harvest, do not rent.** Compare a collateralized, unlevered short-gamma construction against a levered/listed-volatility version of the same premium on a payoff-per-survivable-loss basis, confirming that the concave pole's losses stay budgeted rather than open-ended.
- [ ] **Differential width gates the FX-carry re-entry.** The EM-local leg was killed on 2018-2025 as skew-without-premium, with re-entry pinned to "dollar-weakening regime or longer sample"; the regime evidence above makes the gate concrete. Build a rate-differential-width state (EM local yield minus DM funding yield, percentile over a trailing decade) and pre-register: the leg's held-out income-per-tail measured only in wide-differential states beats the all-state estimate that killed it, and the 2022-2024 window - where differentials re-widened and the 2024 unwind proved the crash was re-armed[^bis2024] - is the in-sample demonstration. Kill if wide-differential conditioning still shows income arriving without the premium.
- [ ] **Income is a downside-beta rent.** Regress each concave candidate's frozen stream on the market and on its downside (conditional) beta at the sleeve's horizon: the downside-risk pricing result[^dob] predicts that income per unit of tail scales with the downside loading. Two candidates with equal Sharpe but different downside beta are not equal - prefer the one paid more per unit of loading, and treat income the loading does not explain as fragile (either alpha or a premium the sample cannot price).
- [ ] **Separate equity beta from volatility carry.** For every collateralized option proxy, attribute excess return in layers: cash/collateral, matched equity and downside beta, a measured variance-risk-premium factor, then timing/reversal and wrapper costs. Call the VRP loading the intended option premium and the final intercept unexplained return - never infer "option income" from an equity-only regression residual. Keep the candidate only if recent net VRP compensation is positive after the full COVID episode, rather than relying on headline yield or realized skew from a crash-free product history.
- [ ] **Compare wrappers at equal survivable loss.** Compare covered call or cash-secured put, delta-hedged option, and VIX-term-structure implementations at equal stress loss, not equal notional or calm volatility. The purer volatility trade should earn more of its return from the intended factor, while the collateralized equity wrapper should require less emergency liquidity.
- [ ] **Trend must bridge the actual crash speed.** Test the floor separately on one-day gaps, one-month shocks, and multi-quarter bears. Require prefunded collateral for the gap state; do not credit slow trend with protection before its signal can turn.
- [ ] **The post-2010 premium must exist net.** Freeze a recent-only evaluation and test option or volatility carry after bid-ask, product fees, financing, equity beta, and the full COVID episode. Kill the explicit-volatility priority if the recent net premium is statistically and economically indistinguishable from zero.
