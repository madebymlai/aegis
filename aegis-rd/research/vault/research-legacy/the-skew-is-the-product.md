# The Skew Is the Product

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> A concave income pole's negative skew is not a risk to be minimized - it is the inventory the sleeve sells, and the portfolio's reason for hiring it. The literature converges on an uncomfortable regularity: every lever that improves carry standalone (vol-targeting, skew-based selection, defensive tilts, drift-band rebalancing) buys the improvement by liquidating that inventory, because the income and the crash are separable enough that an optimizer will separate them. Credit's crash-beta even has a precise address - duration-times-spread in the BB-B belt, not "high yield" as a label. The fix is architectural, not parametric: score and gate the pole on its contribution to the composite (crisis-conditional correlation to the convex pole, hard skew floors), and treat rebalancing cadence as a payoff-shape lever, not a fee lever. The regularity now has a peer-reviewed carry instance (timed carry flips skew -0.76 to +0.97), the trend-carry correlation a published unconditional number (+0.24, commodity-concentrated - mild, which is why the gate must be conditional), and the joint crash states a named factor: innovations in global equity correlation price carry and momentum together.

Upgrade a concave carry sleeve by every lever the standalone evidence supports - a defensive maturity tilt, a diversifying local-market leg, band-based rebalancing mechanics - and every standalone metric can improve while the sleeve's portfolio identity quietly dissolves: its negative skew compresses and its correlation to a convex trend sleeve flips positive, exactly the two properties that made it worth holding next to trend in the first place. The pole becomes a better sleeve and a worse teammate. Is that bad luck, or a law? The literature says it is much closer to a law - and the mechanisms are specific enough to act on.

## Income and crash are separable - that is the trap

The premium and the crash are not the same thing, and the quantitative literature keeps finding the crash to be the smaller part. Jurek hedges G10 carry with out-of-the-money options and finds crash risk accounts for at most about a third of the excess return - crash-neutral carry stays significantly profitable (3.2-5.3% per year, t > 3).[^jurek] His follow-up decomposition of the pricing kernel sharpens it: the skewness-specific component is on the order of 10-15% of the FX carry premium, with the bulk being compensation for variance generally.[^jurekxu] Bekaert and Panayotov make the separability constructive: G10 carry portfolios that simply exclude the prototypical crash currencies (AUD, JPY, NOK) - their "good carry" - earn *higher* Sharpe ratios with flat-to-positive skew, which they read as invalidating crash-risk explanations of the premium at large.[^bp] Torok reaches the same place through options: selecting currencies on option-implied skewness produces a skew-neutral carry with similar returns, positive realized skew, and a slightly higher Sharpe.[^torok] Daniel, Hodrick and Lu push the separability inside the portfolio itself: decomposing G10 carry, the dollar-neutral component carries the deep negative skew, the risk-factor exposure and the downside risk, while the diversified dollar-carry component earns a *higher* Sharpe with minimal skew and no measurable downside exposure - the return and the crash literally live in different legs of the same trade.[^dhl] Their drawdown anatomy adds a measurement warning the rest of this article leans on: carry's negative skew at monthly and longer horizons comes largely from time-varying autocorrelation - persistent sequences of daily losses rather than single-day jumps - so a short-horizon skew estimate understates the tail, and the pole's shape has to be read over multi-month windows.[^dhl]

So a config sweep *can* find income without the crash - that is exactly the problem. If the crash component is a minority of the return, an optimizer pointed at any standalone objective (Sharpe, income utility, income-per-unit-tail) faces a cheap trade: sell the skew, keep most of the income, collect a better score. What the standalone objective cannot see is that the crash component is not dead weight - it is the entire source of the pole's value to a two-pole book. Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt show carry's losses cluster across asset classes precisely in global recessions, a co-movement "obscured when considering monthly returns";[^kmpv] Lettau, Maggiori and Weber show the market beta of high-carry positions is concentrated *conditional on bad states* - a downside-risk CAPM prices carry across currencies, equities, options, commodities and sovereign bonds jointly.[^lmw] Those bad-state exposures are what a crisis-convex trend sleeve is short of, and they are exactly what "good carry" constructions shed. Bekaert-Panayotov never test whether good carry still diversifies a trend book - that is precisely the missing test, and on at least one measured instance the answer was no (see the hypotheses below).

The framing that survives: for this pole, the negative skew is the product. Income is what the pole is paid *in*; crash absorption is what it is paid *for*. An objective that rewards shedding the second while keeping the first is asking the sleeve to defraud its employer.

## Credit's crash-beta has an address: DTS

For a credit implementation the mechanism is unusually concrete. Structurally, a corporate bond is riskless debt minus a put on the firm's assets - Merton's model makes "long credit = short equity put" a construction, not a metaphor.[^merton-av] The empirical hedge ratios confirm it with a precision rare in this literature: combining Merton and Geske, the theoretical sensitivity of credit spreads to equity put options is statistically indistinguishable from one (coefficients 0.94-1.12 across ratings), and the magnitude of that short-put exposure is wildly rating-dependent - roughly 0.26bp for AAA-AA credits against 8.6bp for BB-B.[^merton-av] The short-put character of credit lives almost entirely in the crossover belt. Campbell and Taksler supply the volatility side: idiosyncratic equity volatility explains as much cross-sectional variation in bond yields as ratings do.[^ct]

The exposure metric that tracks this is duration-times-spread. Ben Dor et al. show spread changes are linearly proportional to spread *level* - wide bonds move proportionally more - so a book's exposure to a systematic credit shock is spread duration multiplied by spread, not spread duration alone; a 1-year bond at 500bp carries the same spread risk as a 5-year bond at 100bp.[^dts] The proportionality no longer rests on its inventors: an independent replication on 3.8 million Korean bond-months confirms both the spread-proportional volatility and DTS's forecasting edge over plain spread duration in a market the framework was never fitted to.[^jangkang] The honest counter on record is operational rather than statistical - DTS confounds exposure with risk, so a book's measured DTS spikes *after* spreads widen, a procyclical dial that reads highest right after the crash[^winston] - but for this pole the procyclicality cuts the other way: pinning DTS forces the book to re-buy crash-beta as spreads compress, which is exactly the concave discipline the pin exists to enforce. DTS is therefore the pole's crash-beta dial, and it explains why a defensive maturity tilt is not a free lunch: shortening spread duration while spread level holds roughly constant is, mechanically, a DTS cut, which can preserve most of the income (because most of the premium is not crash rent, per the previous section) while erasing the skew, because the skew *is* the DTS (a measured instance of this mechanism is in the hypotheses below). One boundary: at the distressed bottom (CCC), spreads decouple from the systematic factor into idiosyncratic default outcomes, so DTS-seeking should stop at the B/BB belt rather than reaching for the widest spreads - which the fool's-yield evidence in [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]] independently supports.

The design consequence is a genuinely useful separation: **income and crash-beta get their own axes.** Size the credit legs so the book's DTS is pinned (the skew mandate), and let the optimizer improve income *at that DTS* - tilts that cut crash-beta then have to buy proportionally more of the shorter bucket instead of silently shrinking the product. This is the concave mirror of pinning the volatility target by mandate and sweeping shape only ([[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]]).

## Every standalone improvement spends skew

The regularity generalizes beyond selection. Vol-targeting - a standard exposure-sizing stage - is the canonical case. On momentum, Barroso and Santa-Clara's risk management lifts Sharpe from 0.53 to 0.97 precisely by truncating the left tail (skew -2.47 to -0.42).[^bsc-ref] Applied to carry itself, the effect is stronger: a vol-managed global carry portfolio raises Sharpe by ~40% while realized skew *flips sign*, from -0.37 to positive.[^almeida] The sign-flip now has a field-journal instance: timing the carry trade with its own forward discount and trailing global FX volatility (a continuous risk-adjusted-return scalar) lifts the Sharpe from 0.76 to 1.07 while the skewness swings from -0.76 to +0.97 - the better the timing, the more completely the concave inventory is liquidated.[^dupuy] Moreira and Muir's general result - scaling factor exposure by inverse trailing variance raises Sharpe across factors *including* FX carry - carries a footnote that matters for us: the improvement requires timing on the stream's *own* realized vol; timed on a common (equity-driven) volatility factor, the carry alpha disappears entirely.[^mm] So the three big standalone levers - vol-targeting exposure, skew-based selection,[^bp][^torok] and defensive credit tilts (previous section) - all improve the sleeve's standalone statistics through the same channel: they reduce or reverse the negative skew. The spend is dose-dependent rather than binary - Daniel, Hodrick and Lu's mildly risk-rebalanced carry buys its performance improvement while skew only compresses (from about -0.5 to -0.3, still negative), where aggressive inverse-own-variance timing flips the sign outright[^dhl][^almeida] - but no study we found *deepens* the skew while improving the standalone statistics; the dial turns one way. And there is a theorem-shaped reason to expect exactly that. Firoozye and Koshiyama derive the first four moments of any linear-filter dynamic strategy on Gaussian returns in closed form and show positive skewness is an *essential* feature of every positive-Sharpe dynamic strategy: the moment a static premium stream is wrapped in a responsive sizing rule, the wrapper drags the payoff toward the dynamic-strategy shape - positive skew - and away from the concave inventory this pole is hired to hold.[^fk] Sharpe-gaming needed payoff-shaping freedom ([[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]]); skew-shedding needs much less, because it is not an attack on the measure - it is the honest optimum of every standalone objective, with the moment algebra on its side.

For this pole the practical reading of Moreira-Muir is therefore double-edged: the vol-target earns its keep on average return, but every unit of drawdown-smoothing it buys comes out of the same skew inventory, and the sleeve's owner - the composite - never voted on the sale.

## The rebalancing band is a payoff-shape lever

The subtlest skew leak came from what looked like a pure execution decision. Rebalancing toward fixed weights is mechanically a short straddle on the relative value of the book's assets: Granger, Greenig, Harvey, Rattray and Zou show fixed-weight rebalancing "induces negative convexity by magnifying drawdowns when there are pronounced divergences," with the rebalancing premium as the compensation.[^rebrisk] Willenbrock identifies the diversification return - the compounding edge of a rebalanced book over buy-and-hold - as coming from exactly those contrarian trades; a drifting book earns none of it.[^wb] The complement: letting weights drift is an embedded momentum position. Hoffstein states it plainly - "by allowing our portfolio to drift, our portfolio will naturally tilt itself towards the out-performing asset... drift serves as an interesting amplifier to the momentum signal."[^hoffdrift] Rattray, Granger, Harvey and van Hemert quantify the stakes on a 60/40: the monthly-rebalanced book's GFC drawdown was 1.2x (five percentage points) worse than buy-and-hold from the same start, and a trend overlay is the convex mirror of the rebalancing short straddle.[^stratreb]

Read a fee-motivated cadence change through that lens and it stops looking like an execution detail. Moving a carry book from daily rebalancing to a wide drift band does two things at once: it gives up the rebalancing premium's concave harvest, and it blends a quiet long-trend tilt into the carry stream - the drifting winners ride. A concave pole that drifts is partially a trend follower; a corr-to-trend flip toward positive and skew compression are the two visible symptoms of one mechanism, and the literature predicts both signs (a measured instance is in the hypotheses below). The band is typically adopted purely as a fee lever - cutting trade count on a book where order minimums bite - and at the *sleeve* level that arithmetic is right. At the *composite* level the band's cost is the pole's role, which no per-sleeve fee comparison prices. The middle path the literature offers is tranching - "rebalance a little but frequently," correcting a fraction of the drift each period[^hoffdrift] - which keeps most of the contrarian (concave) trade flow at a bounded trade count. For this pole, rebalancing cadence must be treated as a swept *shape* axis with the skew measured per cell, never as an execution detail bolted on after selection.

## Select the pole where it works: in the composite

Everything above says the pole's identity cannot be protected by any standalone statistic - so the selection machinery has to see the portfolio. Three gates have direct literature support. First, *conditional* correlation, not full-sample: Page and Panariello show full-sample correlations systematically hide crisis co-movement - US/non-US equity correlation is -17% conditional on the best 1% of months and +87% conditional on the worst 1% - and conclude flatly that "full-sample correlations are misleading."[^pp] A carry pole must clear its corr-to-trend ceiling *measured in the composite's stress states*, where the diversification is actually being bought. Second, marginal contribution as the score: the total-portfolio-approach literature formalizes "evaluate every investment by its marginal impact on total fund risk and return - there are no buckets,"[^tpa] which for a sweep means ranking cells by the composite's utility delta with the candidate in it, not by the candidate's own utility. Third, a tail-contribution budget: the Euler decomposition of portfolio risk (and its CVaR version) prices each sleeve's share of the composite left tail, the formal version of Ilmanen's dictum that bearing *small* insurance risks is well rewarded and bearing large ones is not.[^ins-ref]

One honest complication: the thing the gates defend is itself regime-dependent. Trend's crisis alpha requires time to reposition - in fast shocks "trend-following lacks sufficient time to adapt and the crisis alpha characteristic disappears"[^man-quality] - so the anti-correlation the carry pole is gated against is an emergent property of two conditional processes, not a physical constant. The unconditional coefficient confirms how little the full sample sees: the broadest published estimate puts the carry-momentum correlation at +0.24 in time series (+0.15 cross-sectional), near zero on most style/asset pairs and concentrated in commodities, where low inventories raise carry and momentum together.[^bazdissect] And the stress states now have a named factor: innovations in global equity correlation carry a robust negative price of risk and price the carry and momentum cross-sections *jointly* (cross-sectional fit near 90% in FX) - both strategies' paid legs lose when correlation spikes, so the two are not opposite bets against a correlation shock but two claims written on the same calm; the momentum leg there is cross-sectional FX rather than cross-asset time-series trend, so the transfer is by analogy.[^baeelkamhi] That argues for measuring the gates over multiple stress windows (and for the tail tier's existence), not for abandoning them.

## Limitations

- The "every standalone improvement spends skew" claim is an empirical regularity assembled from adjacent results (momentum vol-management, carry vol-management, skew-based FX selection, credit tilts), backed by the moment theorem for the dynamic-overlay case[^fk] but not itself a theorem. The carry-specific *sign-flip* no longer rests on a master's thesis: the timed-carry flip is published in a field journal,[^dupuy] with the thesis as the pure-inverse-vol variant,[^almeida] the momentum analogues for the flip direction,[^bsc-ref] and Daniel-Hodrick-Lu for the mild-dose compression.[^dhl] One reading caution survives: the published flip times on expected return per unit of risk (forward discount over volatility), not volatility alone, so the cleanest peer-reviewed instance bundles two timing signals.
- The DTS framework is practitioner research (Lehman/Barclays and Robeco, who market DTS-based products), though its core proportionality finding is 560,000+ bond-months and the framework is embedded in third-party risk systems.[^dts] An out-of-market academic replication now exists,[^jangkang] and the sharpest critique on record concerns procyclical exposure accounting, not the proportionality itself.[^winston]
- Man Group's positioning-conditional trend numbers (crisis windows entered long vs short bonds) could not be verified to a primary document; only the qualitative fast-shock claim is cited.[^man-quality] The trend-carry coefficient gap is now partly closed - the broadest published estimate is mildly positive (+0.24 time-series)[^bazdissect] - but it is a Man Group working paper's number, and no independent estimate exists for a cross-asset TSMOM-versus-carry pairing specifically. Measured directly, an apparent trend-carry co-crash can decompose to a single fast-crash window read at daily frequency, with the pairing diversifying at monthly frequency - the post-mortem of one such episode is [[carry-diversifies-only-a-divergent-trend]].
- Bekaert-Panayotov and Torok do not test whether skew-lite "good carry" retains diversification value against a trend book; that inference is ours, supported so far by a single measured instance (see the hypotheses).
- The Baz et al. correlation tables are now verified to the primary document (the +0.24/+0.15 figures above);[^bazdissect] the Strategic Rebalancing band-width exhibits remain unverified - the band-width magnitude question is answered qualitatively ("threshold rules slightly less potent" than trend-informed delay), not numerically.[^stratreb]

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **The composite-gated re-rank.** Re-rank the live-mechanics `carry_sota.yaml` sweep under the floor diary's gates made hard: quarterly skew <= -0.5 on the aligned window, corr(trend) <= 0 full-sample AND in the composite's worst-decile months (the Page-Panariello gate). Pre-register: deeper-DTS cells (IHYU-bearing) reclaim the championship, and the winner's floor composite at w=0.40 degrades less vs trend-only than the current champion's does ([[runs/floor/2026-07-03]] baseline). **Tested 2026-07-04, verdict pivot** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-04#the re-run cascade - composite re-rank of the 27-cell grid (diagnostic)|cascade]]): all 27 cells scored on the composite $\Delta\hat\Theta$ against the trend pole (dividends seeded) - **the gate promotes nothing**. Every cell has $\Delta\hat\Theta < 0$ (subtracts book CE) and downside correlation to trend in [+0.38, +0.49] - not one cell diversifies. The pre-registration was half right (the standalone-ranked champion's carry_gain=2 lean is composite-*negative*, and dropping it plus the defensive tilt is income-best) and half wrong (no cell is a floor partner at all, deeper-DTS included - because on this universe/window credit co-crashes with the trend book's risk legs, the regime-conditional limit made general). Pivot: the pole is a standalone income sleeve here, not a floor partner; the floor's case must be argued on a trend-winter window, not this one.
- [ ] **Rebalancing cadence is a shape axis.** On the fixed champion cell, sweep cadence {daily, tranched 1/5 daily, band 0.05/0.10, band 0.10/0.20} and measure per-cell qskew, corr-to-trend, income, and net-of-fixed-fee utility. Pre-register the literature's signs: tighter cadence deepens skew and lowers corr-to-trend (the rebalancing short straddle) at higher fee cost; select by composite delta net of fees, expecting tranching to dominate both extremes.[^rebrisk][^hoffdrift]
- [ ] **Pin DTS, not just vol.** Add a book-level DTS estimate (spread proxy x spread duration per leg) and re-run the construction sweep with DTS pinned at the incumbent book's level, so defensive tilts must buy more short-bucket exposure instead of shrinking crash-beta. Pre-register: at pinned DTS the defensive tilt's utility edge shrinks but the skew survives (qskew stays <= -0.5).
- [ ] **The composite-delta ranker.** Score sweep cells by the delta in composite (trend + candidate at fixed w=0.40, vol-normalized) MPPM utility rather than standalone utility - the TPA principle as a ranker[^tpa] - keeping the standalone gates as sanity floors. If the composite-delta winner equals the composite-gated standalone winner, the cheaper gates suffice; if not, the ranker earns its complexity.
- [ ] **Correlation innovations date the joint-loss states.** Compute a rolling average pairwise correlation across the book's risk legs and its month-over-month innovation; pre-register that the composite's worst-decile months cluster in top-quartile correlation-innovation months, and that the downside-correlation gate evaluated on exactly those months is at least as strict as the current worst-decile-of-trend conditioning - the correlation-innovation state variable as an operational gate window.[^baeelkamhi]
- [ ] **(Instrument-level, joint with [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]].) Pure-spread and explicit-insurance legs restore the skew.** A CDS-carry leg (protection-seller iTraxx Crossover, DBXM) and/or an AT1 leg (XAT1) - income that is undilutably crash-rent - deepen the pole's qskew past the -0.5 gate at flat-or-better income where the cash-bond defensive tilt could not.
