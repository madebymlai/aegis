# No UCITS Route to Cross-Asset Carry

> [!note] Status
> INSTRUMENT SURVEY and forced implementation decision, 2026-07-17. Construction work under our mandate, not generic research: the durable mechanism lives in [[carry-is-not-one-premium]], the pairing logic in [[carry-diversifies-only-a-divergent-trend]]. Identity facts verified against issuer documents, audited reports and the Central Bank of Ireland register on 2026-07-17. Contains a **correction** to [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]].

> [!abstract] Decision
> **No current UCITS route.** A transparent, predominantly non-equity, cross-sectional carry strategy spanning FX, sovereign rates and commodities is not presently obtainable by a small portfolio, as a single fund or as an assembly. The sovereign-rates sleeve has no UCITS implementation at all - the one credible index (FTSE Nomura CaRD) is long-only and unwrapped. The FX sleeve is now extinct: the last dedicated FX-carry UCITS ETF has been liquidated. The commodity sleeve exists and is healthy, but it implements a *calendar-spread* signal, not the cross-sectional high-minus-low curve carry the target specifies. Cross-asset carry should stay a research hypothesis, not a roster member.

## Answer

The target - systematic, long-short, cross-sectional carry, long high-carry and short low-carry *within* each of FX, sovereign rates and commodities, risk-normalized across the three, free of momentum, value, defensive, equity, credit and volatility-carry contamination - exists as an index. It does not exist as a UCITS product.

**Confidence is split, and the split matters:**

- **High** that no *verifiable* faithful route exists today. Each of the three sleeves fails for an independent, document-backed reason (below). This is not a close call.
- **Medium** that no faithful route exists *at all*, because exactly one finalist survives identity verification but cannot be looked through: **J.P. Morgan Cross Asset Carry Strategy** is a genuine UCITS whose rules are not public. Under the brief's own gate it is an **unverifiable implementation**, and it is the runner-up.

The decisive difference between the verdict and the runner-up is evidentiary, not economic: the JPM fund is the only object in the landscape that could in principle be the target, and no public document establishes that it is. **The exact disclosure that would reverse this conclusion** is the PassIM Structured Funds plc supplement for the J.P. Morgan Cross Asset Carry Strategy sub-fund, together with the rulebook for its reference index, Bloomberg ticker `XRJPDKAB`.

## Evidence

### The three objects must stay separate

The brief's discipline is the finding, not a preamble. Three products in this survey carry the word "carry" in their legal name; **none** of them implements the strategy the name denotes, and two of them implement something the target explicitly excludes. Marketing language, prospectus language and audited holdings diverge at every step. Keep the academic strategy, the wrapper's actual rules, and our allocation to it in separate columns - the taxonomy discipline of [[carry-is-not-one-premium]] is what makes the divergences visible.

### The FX sleeve is extinct, not merely small

The Deutsche Bank currency-factor shelf was the only dedicated cross-sectional FX-carry implementation in a UCITS wrapper. Its rule was exactly right: the Xtrackers Currency Carry UCITS ETF 1C (LU0328474126, WKN DBX1AY, TER 0.30%, launched 25 January 2008, Luxembourg, synthetic funded swap) tracked the Deutsche Bank Currency Carry (EUR Hedged) index, "purchasing 3-month forward contracts on three G10 Currencies in jurisdictions with high interest rates and selling 3-month forward contracts on three G10 Currencies in jurisdictions with low interest rates".[^dbx1ay] That is the Lustig-Roussanov-Verdelhan forward-discount sort, wrapped, hedged to EUR, at 30bp.

It is gone. justETF now carries the liquidation banner on the fund and reports 0.00% across every trailing and calendar period.[^dbx1ay] The same banner appears on all four siblings: Currency Momentum (LU0328474043), Currency Valuation (LU0328473748) and Currency Returns (LU0328474472, LU0511381047, LU0511381559).[^dbdead] The whole shelf died together, which is what a provider does when a product line fails rather than what a data vendor does when it mislabels one row.

**This corrects [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]]**, which recorded on 2026-07-03 that DBX1AY was "technically live but ~EUR 10m AUM after 18 years" and therefore "the last dedicated FX-carry UCITS ETP, practically untradable at retail scale". The instrument is no longer merely untradable; it has been wound up. The note's operative conclusion (EM local-currency bonds remain the sole practical FX-carry claim for a long-only book) is unaffected and if anything strengthened.

The only survivor with an FX-carry benchmark is the **First Trust FactorFX UCITS ETF** (Class A USD IE00BD5HBQ97 / FTFX; Class B GBP IE00BD5HBR05; Class C EUR Hedged IE00BD5HBS12 / FXEU; Ireland; TER 0.75%; launched 27 July 2017 / 29 November 2017). It fails the style gate on its own documents. It is *actively managed*, uses the Bloomberg G10 Carry Index "for performance purposes only" and "is not managed in reference to the Benchmark", and in 2022 added a second benchmark blending 50/50 G10 carry with an **8-currency emerging-market** carry index.[^ftfx-kiid] At launch First Trust described it plainly: the fund "applies two risk premia - value and momentum - to the carry trade", going long undervalued *and* higher-yielding currencies while seeking positive-momentum currencies.[^ftfx-launch] Momentum and value are out of scope for the target by construction, EM adds an unbudgeted risk factor, and the vehicle is tiny (roughly EUR 2m to 4.8m per class).[^ftfx-je] It is an FX multi-factor fund, not an FX carry sleeve.

### The sovereign-rates sleeve has never existed in a UCITS

This is the load-bearing failure. The academic object is well specified and strong: Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt define bond carry as yield pick-up plus roll-down and report a Sharpe of 0.68 for a long-short portfolio buying the US Treasury maturities with the highest carry per unit duration and selling the lowest; Martens and co-authors extend the curve-carry strategy to ten developed markets and find a global factor Sharpe of 0.69 with low cross-country correlation, whose alpha survives controls for the market, value, cross-sectional momentum, TSMOM and betting-against-beta.[^martens] Notably, cross-sectional momentum and value do *not* work in government bonds while carry does - so the rates sleeve is the one place where the target's purity requirement is not merely a preference but matches where the evidence actually is.

No UCITS implements it. The closest index is the **FTSE Nomura Carry and Roll Down (CaRD) World Government Bond Index Series**, jointly built with Nomura, base date 31 December 2006, monthly optimisation that maximises the index's 12-month expected carry and roll down subject to duration and country-weight constraints on the FTSE WGBI universe.[^card] Three things disqualify it as our sleeve, and the rulebook says all three:

1. **It is long-only.** "Leverage and negative weights are not allowed in the optimisation."[^card] There is no short leg, so it is not cross-sectional carry in the long-short sense; it is a maturity-bucket tilt inside a long book. FTSE Russell knew exactly what it was doing: its own research traces the CaRD formulation to Kikugawa et al. (2017), who tested carry-and-roll-down on JGBs as a **long-short** strategy with an interest-rate-matching constraint, and then states that it explores the same concept "albeit on a long-only basis" for the global sovereign series.[^lseg-carry] The provider deliberately shipped the long-only variant of a long-short academic strategy, because that is what its index-fund clients can hold. That is the wrapper layer selecting against us in one sentence.
2. **It retains full universe duration.** The optimiser constrains modified duration to equal the base universe's, country by country in the LO variants.[^card] It therefore *keeps* the WGBI's directional rates beta, which is precisely the exposure that overlaps the trend book we are trying to diversify.
3. **No UCITS wraps it.** LSEG reports the series benchmarks roughly USD 2.7bn of assets;[^lseg-card] that is Japanese institutional mandate money, and no fund in this sweep tracks it in a UCITS.

A duration-neutral, cross-sectional sovereign carry sleeve is simply absent from the European retail shelf. Since it is one of three mandatory components, its absence is not "contamination" to be quantified - it is a missing third of the strategy, and it alone forecloses the assembly route.

### The commodity sleeve is real, healthy, and implements a different signal

The **UBS CMCI Commodity Carry SF UCITS ETF USD acc** (IE00BKFB6L02, WKN A2PRV7, ticker UEQC, Ireland, launched 16 January 2020, TER 0.34% flat fee, EUR 159m AUM, synthetic fully-funded plus total-return swap with UBS AG, collateralized in G10 government bonds, supranationals and cash) is a genuine, liquid, savings-plan-eligible UCITS with no minimum subscription.[^ubs-je][^ubs-fs] It is the only sleeve in this survey that passes identity, feasibility and liquidity together.

It fails carry-definition fidelity. Its index - UBS CM-BCOM Outperformance Strategy Index ex-Precious Metals 2.5 Leveraged Net of Cost Total Return, Bloomberg `COSIXPLT` - "is long the CMCI BCOM Index and short the BCOM Index, excluding precious metals and with a 2.5x leverage", capturing "the outperformance of the CMCI rolling methodology vs a traditional front month rolling future methodology".[^ubs-fs] Both legs hold the *same commodities*. The spread is which point of each curve you own. That is a roll-methodology or congestion trade, not the cross-sectional signal the target specifies, which ranks commodities *against each other* and is long the backwardated and short the contangoed. The KID says so in the issuer's own words: the index "provides an exposure to the difference in performance of two commodity indices. Its objective is therefore not to directly track movements in commodity markets."[^ubs-kid] It is commodity-market-neutral by construction and carries embedded 2.5x leverage.

The WisdomTree Enhanced Commodity Carry product (CRRY, XS3022291473) is the same species and fails a prior gate. Its BNP Paribas Enhanced Commodity Carry ER index (`BNPIF73P`) extracts the premium "from the difference in the carry cost between the long and short positions in futures contracts of **the same set of underlying commodities** with different maturities", resetting long and short exposures to +300% and -300% monthly.[^crry] Again a calendar spread, and levered three times over. More decisively: it is an **ETC**, not a fund. WisdomTree's own factsheet states "This ETP is not a UCITS product" and that WisdomTree "is not promoting or marketing" these ETPs to retail clients; "UCITS eligible" means a UCITS *may hold it*, not that it *is* one.[^crry] The brief's exclusion of notes and certificates bites exactly here, and the "UCITS eligible" label is the trap it was written to catch.

The distinction is not pedantry. Our own taxonomy already fixed it: "Expected carry is not realized roll return", and "cross-sectional carry is not time-series carry - they differ in beta, breadth, and crash exposure" ([[carry-is-not-one-premium]]). Bloomberg and GSAM keep the two apart inside a single index: their Commodity Carry Index equal-risk-weights a **Curve Strategy** (short the nearby, long the 3-month deferred, for all commodities - the calendar spread the UCITS products sell) against a **Backwardation Strategy** (rank commodities by the nearby-to-12-month-deferred price ratio, long the top third, short the bottom third - the cross-sectional signal the UCITS products do not).[^bgscmc] The UCITS shelf sells one of those two and calls it carry.

### The single-vehicle route: a fund named Carry that has never been cross-asset carry

**Ailis Risk Premia Carry** (LU1640502628, Class I) is a Luxembourg SICAV qualifying as a UCITS under Part I of the 2010 Law, managed by Fideuram Asset Management (Ireland) dac with investment management delegated to Fideuram Asset Management UK Limited. Its KID says it "is focused on the Carry Risk Premia strategy" and will "exploit Risk Premia across a globally diversified range of assets, such as equities, government bonds, money market instruments, and corporate fixed income securities, commodities and currencies", actively managed, no benchmark, mainly via unfunded total-return swaps on indices.[^ailis-kid] On the label this is the closest thing in Europe to a single-style cross-asset carry UCITS.

The audited reports demolish it, twice, in opposite directions.

**In 2020** the fund was a volatility book. The FY2020 annual report attributes its losses to "the Volatility Carry, Dividend Carry, Credit Carry and Rates Carry strategies", noting "Volatility Carry strategy was hit as Volatility Index ('VIX') spiked to 82, therefore hurting short volatility strategies".[^ailis-2020] The FY2019 report lists portfolio changes including "Volatility Carry exposure was halved" and "Volatility Hedge exposure was reduced significantly", with performance "driven mainly by the Equity Volatility and Commodity Volatility exposure".[^ailis-2019] Systematic short volatility and dividend carry are disqualifying for the target; credit carry is contamination, not the sovereign-rates sleeve.

**In 2023** the fund was a single levered commodity swap. The FY2023 annual report states the sub-fund "was re-launched in the last quarter of 2022", is "invested via Excess Return swap in the BofA Merrill Lynch Commodity Carry Excess Return Strategy (MLCXSX6L Index) with a leverage of approximately 5 times", that its "performance was driven mainly by natural gas exposure", and that "nearly half of the Sub-Fund's gross exposure is in agricultural commodities", with cash in money-market EGBs. Average leverage as sum of notionals was 440.44%, under a VaR approach.[^ailis-2023]

So a fund whose prospectus promises carry across six asset groups was, on audited evidence, first a short-volatility and dividend book and then a five-times-levered single-asset-class commodity swap whose returns came from natural gas. It also **changed mandate materially** in Q4 2022, breaking historical comparability, and Class I is "reserved to institutional investors".[^ailis-kid] It fails style purity, asset-class coverage, rule stability and small-portfolio feasibility independently. It is a negative control, and a vivid one.

### The runner-up: verified wrapper, unverifiable strategy

**J.P. Morgan Cross Asset Carry Strategy** is real and it is UCITS. The Central Bank of Ireland register records it as sub-fund reference **C533831**, approved **24 September 2024**, status "UCITS Investment Company sub-fund", within the umbrella **PassIM Structured Funds plc** (C43424, authorised 15 November 2006), management company Carne Global Fund Managers (Ireland) Limited (C46640), depositary BNP Paribas SA (C26888).[^cbi-c533831] PassIM's own reports confirm the umbrella "was incorporated under the laws of Ireland on 2 November 2006, as an undertaking for collective investment in transferable securities pursuant to the UCITS Regulations", with seventy-three sub-funds in operation, investment-managed by J.P. Morgan Mansart Management Limited.[^passim-sar][^passim-pro] A distributor listing shows a EUR Share Class (acc) with a **EUR 10,000** minimum, and a sibling **J.P. Morgan Cross Asset Defensive Strategy** approved alongside it.[^unicaja] The same umbrella already runs a **Cross Asset Trend Strategy** (C405027, approved 17 February 2020), around EUR 259m.[^edgefolio] A trend sleeve and a carry sleeve on one UCITS platform is, on paper, the pairing this book wants.

It cannot be looked through. The only public description of the strategy is a Bloomberg profile: the fund "tracks the performance of the J.P. Morgan (XRJPDKAB) Index which aims to provide a synthetic exposure to a diversified basket of systematic strategies", classified as objective **Dynamic Allocation**, asset-class focus **Mixed Allocation**.[^bbg-jpcacea] "Diversified basket of systematic strategies" and "dynamic allocation" are not descriptions of a pure cross-sectional carry sleeve; they are the vocabulary of a multi-strategy book with changing weights, which the brief excludes as a proxy. But that is inference from a vendor summary, not proof. **State, Action and Exit cannot be answered from any document I could obtain. Under the brief's gate this is an unverifiable implementation**, and reputable sponsorship does not cure that.

There is a specific reason to withhold benefit of the doubt, and it must be labelled carefully. J.P. Morgan's published house taxonomy files things under "Carry" that the target excludes. In the strategy guide for its Alternative Index Multi-Strategy 5 index, the carry style comprises eight strategies including an "Equity Value Carry strategy [that] goes long the MSCI Daily Value Total Return Gross World Index and short the MSCI Daily Total Return Gross World Index" - that is **value**, filed under carry - and an "Equity Small Cap strategy [that] goes long an index that tracks Russell 2000 futures contracts and short an index that tracks S&P 500 futures contracts" - that is **size**, filed under carry. Its "Bond Carry Long strategy entails borrowing at short-term interest rates and investing the proceeds in two longer dated bonds", which is directional duration, not a cross-sectional sort. Only its FX Carry strategy (long the three highest-rate G10, short the three lowest) matches the target, and its Commodity Carry strategy is again a roll-methodology spread, long a proprietary backwardation-selecting JPM index and short the S&P GSCI Excess Return Index.[^jpm-aims5]

**That evidence is about a different index (`AIJPM5UE`) of an older vintage, not about `XRJPDKAB`, and I do not claim otherwise.** It establishes only that this house's "carry" label has historically covered equity value, equity size and directional bond duration. It raises the burden of proof on the label; it does not discharge our burden of verification. Hence: runner-up, unverifiable, not rejected.

### The target exists as an index, and is investable - outside UCITS

The economic target is not hypothetical. The **Bloomberg GSAM Cross Asset Carry Index** (`BGSXAC`) "provides equal risk weighted exposure to the carry factor in commodities, currencies and bonds using futures and forwards", combining the Bloomberg GSAM Bond Futures Carry, Commodity Carry and FX Carry indices, rebalanced quarterly to equal risk using five years of monthly returns, base date May 1999.[^bgsxac] Non-equity, three sleeves, explicit risk normalization, rules-based, reproducible: this is the brief's specification, written by someone else, with a 27-year history.

It is wrapped - in a **US ETF**. Return Stacked's RSBY uses a 100% BGSXAC / 100% Bloomberg Short Treasury portfolio as the model for its "futures yield" leg.[^rsby] A US 1940-Act ETF is excluded by the brief and unavailable to an EU retail book. The gap is therefore not a gap in strategy research, index engineering, or even product engineering. **It is a gap in the European wrapper layer specifically**, which is exactly why the answer to the core question is no while the answer to "does this strategy exist and can anyone buy it" is yes.

### Small-portfolio feasibility, for completeness

Feasibility does not rescue anything here, but the brief asks and the answers are asymmetric. UBS UEQC is exchange-traded with no minimum and EUR 159m of assets - genuinely holdable in coarse increments, and the only finalist that is.[^ubs-je] First Trust FXEU is exchange-traded but at EUR 2m to 4.8m per class carries real closure and spread risk.[^ftfx-je] Ailis Class I is institutional-only.[^ailis-kid] The JPM fund's EUR 10,000 minimum is not exchange-traded and is material against a small book.[^unicaja] The one vehicle a small portfolio can comfortably hold is the one that implements the wrong signal in one of three asset classes.

## Disagreements

**Academics and allocators do not disagree about carry. They disagree about wrappers, and that is our question.**

The academic camp is consistent and strong. Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt establish carry as a common, ex-ante observable predictor across asset classes; Martens and co-authors independently confirm global curve carry at Sharpe 0.69 with alpha surviving factor controls.[^martens] Nothing in this survey challenges the premium.

The practitioner and allocator camp is blunt that the premium has not reached investors. bfinance reports that the average ARP manager lost **11.35% through H1 2020** and **4.14% per year over the preceding three years**, that the manager universe went from a few pioneers in 2012 to 55 in 2018 and back to 47 with roughly USD 70bn, and - critically for us - that "the vast majority of ARP managers use the Carry factor", always as one factor among several.[^bfinance] Antti Suhonen (MJ Hudson, Aalto) puts it hardest: "many real-world alternative premia have never reached the level of performance shown in academic research of hypothetical past returns", and his decomposition finds **up to two-thirds** of diversified ARP returns explained by traditional asset classes - "Alternative risk premia is not in a world of its own".[^ipe][^risknet] Research Affiliates recorded that for the eleven months to November 2018 the carry substrategy returned **-9% to -10%**, its worst in a decade, in the first year of twelve where carry, value and momentum all lost together.[^ra-arp] Artur Sepp argues the products are selection artefacts: teams run backtests "until a specific parametrization produces a satisfactory Sharpe", then market it; and the bank ARP index behaves as if it "sells 3 puts to buy 5 calls".[^sepp]

Two refinements keep this honest rather than merely damning. Nick Baltas (peer-reviewed, FAJ 2019) shows crowding is not uniformly corrosive: **divergence** premia such as momentum underperform after crowded periods, while **convergence** premia such as value tend to *outperform* as they transition into larger flows.[^baltas] Carry sits nearer the convergence pole, so a blanket "carry has been arbitraged away" claim is not supported by the best crowding evidence. And the 2018-2020 ARP disaster indicts *multi-style, volatility-selling, equity-heavy* books - precisely the products our target excludes - so it is weak evidence against pure non-equity carry specifically.

**Where the evidence lands:** the allocators win the implementation question, which is the only question this brief asks. The academic Sharpe is an upper bound on what a wrapper delivers, not a forecast, and the wrapper layer is where this strategy dies in Europe. The practitioner blog evidence converges independently from the retail side: "retail-accessible products mostly do not implement the signal ... long-short commodity ETFs are scarce and most charge fees that erode the academic Sharpe", with the closest retail proxies being diversified managed-futures funds that "bundle equity and bond signals as well".[^qdecoded] That is the same conclusion this survey reached from the registers, and it is the anonymous-but-well-argued practitioner corroboration the brief asked to be labelled as such.

## Limitations

- **The Xtrackers liquidation rests on one data provider.** justETF shows the liquidation banner and 0.00% across all periods for LU0328474126 and the same banner on all four siblings, which is internally consistent and cross-checked across five share classes.[^dbx1ay][^dbdead] I did **not** find the primary DWS termination notice for this specific ISIN; the DWS notices I retrieved cover 2026 LSE delistings and the termination of an unrelated sub-fund. Treat the liquidation as high-confidence but primary-source-pending. It does not change the verdict: at EUR 10m after 18 years the fund failed the small-portfolio screen either way, per [[the-ucits-constrained-carry-sleeve]].
- **The JPM finalist is unresolved, by design of the evidence and not by choice.** Wrapper, domicile, UCITS status, manager, depositary and approval date are verified from the CBI register; the strategy is not verified at all. Do not read the AI Multi-Strategy 5 taxonomy as proof about `XRJPDKAB`.
- **The Ailis look-through is current to FY2023.** I did not obtain FY2024 or FY2025 reports. A fund that re-launched once can re-launch again; the mandate-instability finding is itself the durable point.
- **No negative search proves absence.** "No UCITS wraps BGSXAC or FTSE Nomura CaRD" is a claim about a wide sweep of issuer sites, justETF, Fundsquare, Morningstar and the CBI register, not a claim about every Luxembourg and Irish sub-fund in existence. Private-placement and distributor-specific sub-funds like the JPM one are exactly the class that hides from screeners - which is how the runner-up was nearly missed.
- **Saturation defence.** Three WIDE-NARROW rounds. Round 3 surfaced no new *kinds* of candidate: every new hit was another multi-style ARP fund (Systematica, Aegon, GS Multi Asset Factor Opportunities, Barclays ARP Select), another commodity roll-enhancement product (L&G Multi-Strategy Enhanced Commodities, WisdomTree WCOA), or another volatility-carry vehicle (SG29 MAS Rates Volatility, STEP Premium). The recurring negative - "carry is present, but as one style among four, or in one asset class, or as a roll spread" - repeated without exception. I stopped when the failure mode stopped changing.

## Open questions

1. **The reversal document.** Obtain the PassIM Structured Funds plc supplement for J.P. Morgan Cross Asset Carry Strategy and the `XRJPDKAB` rulebook, via `Mansart.Funds@jpmorgan.com` or jpmorganmansart.com. If the index is genuinely three-sleeve, cross-sectional, non-equity carry with fixed rules, the verdict flips to "single faithful vehicle" and the EUR 10,000 minimum becomes the only obstacle. If it is a dynamic multi-style basket, the verdict hardens and this note is finished. This is the highest-value next action in the whole survey.
2. **Primary confirmation of the DBX1AY termination** from DWS, closing the one secondary-source dependency.
3. **Does any UCITS wrap `BGSXAC` or the FTSE Nomura CaRD series?** Both are the right economics; both appear unwrapped in Europe. A negative from the index providers would be decisive rather than inferred.
4. **Ailis FY2024/FY2025 reports** - is it still the 5x commodity swap, and does any non-institutional class exist?
5. **The honest fallback.** If the JPM fund disappoints, the question worth asking is not "which impure carry product do we buy" but "does the ordinary-market companion seat need carry at all" - [[carry-diversifies-only-a-divergent-trend]] already found the existing pole diversifies the trend book at monthly frequency, and [[insurance-linked-securities-as-the-orthogonal-income-pole]] offers a payer that is not financial at all.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [ ] **The commodity calendar-spread sleeve is not a carry proxy (negative control, cheap).** Pre-register that UBS UEQC's return stream is uncorrelated with a cross-sectional commodity curve-carry signal built on our own data, confirming that the roll-methodology spread and the high-minus-low sort are different trades rather than two labels for one. Kill the "buy UEQC as the commodity carry sleeve" idea on a positive-but-weak correlation, not on the label.
- [ ] **A two-sleeve assembly cannot stand in for three.** If the JPM route fails, test whether any FX-plus-commodity pair reproduces `BGSXAC` risk and shape without the rates sleeve. Expect no: the rates sleeve carries the lowest cross-country correlation in the Martens evidence, so dropping it should cost more diversification than its risk weight implies.[^martens]
- [ ] **Shadow-track `BGSXAC` before buying anything.** Build the cross-asset carry stream from our own futures/forward data against the published index as a specification check, per the shadow-challenger discipline of [[fx-carry-is-not-yet-a-small-account-demeter-replacement]]. This is a research stream, not a roster candidate, and must not be given a production config.
