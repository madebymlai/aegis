# The UCITS-Constrained Carry Sleeve

> [!note] Status
> INSTRUMENT SURVEY, mostly tested; demoted from `research/` on 2026-07-09 and re-audited 2026-07-11. The re-audit corrected a material false negative: a put-write UCITS ETF does exist. IBKR paper-account tradability was verified read-only on 2026-07-11; live-account eligibility and point-in-time market liquidity still require re-verification before acting. This is construction work under our mandate rather than generic research; durable mechanisms live in [[the-skew-is-the-product]] and [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]].

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> Under a long-only, no-derivatives, EU-retail constraint the carry pole's credible upgrades sort into two kinds: legs that deepen income-per-tail while *keeping* the pole's negative skew (a short-maturity tilt inside HY; an EM local-currency leg as the only investable FX-carry skew source), and tilts that quietly dissolve the pole's identity (a trend gate flips its skew convex; a fallen-angel tilt smuggles back the rates duration the sleeve exists to exclude). The quality edge inside HY is largely the fallen-angel technical wearing a different label - and since no BB-only USD HY UCITS fund exists, it is bought through maturity-bucket index choice or not at all.
>
> **Caveat**: a defensive tilt can pass every standalone test and still fail the pole - measured in a blended-book composite it cuts duration-times-spread and with it the crash-beta the pole is hired for. The mechanism and the fix (pin DTS, gate on the composite) are in [[the-skew-is-the-product]]; the investable levers that carry undilutable crash-rent are in the addendum below.

The concave income pole has a metric article ([[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]]) and a sourcing article ([[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]); this one is about *construction* - which levers strengthen a sleeve that must stay long-only, physically-replicated, and UCITS-wrapped, the constraint set that already forced the tail tier's redesign in [[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]]. The governing rule carries over from the roster ([[the-tiered-strategy-roster]]): the pole is hired for negative quarterly skew, near-zero rates beta (orthogonality to the trend book's duration), and calm income. Any lever is judged twice - does it add income per unit of budgeted tail, and does it leave the pole's identity intact? Several popular levers pass the first test and fail the second, which is the finding this article exists to record.

## The defensive tilt is a maturity bucket, not a rating screen

The cleanest crisis evidence for defensive construction inside HY is the 2022 sell-off decomposed by maturity: issues with 0-5 year maturities returned -5.65% while 5-8 year returned -12.62% and >8 year -18.35%, against -11.10% for the broad index - and CCC-rated paper underperformed at -15.63% with the top ten worst issuers alone accounting for ~40% of the rating bucket's loss.[^ct22] The year was rate-driven, which is exactly the point for us: short-maturity HY is the long-only approximation of the duration strip, cutting the rate factor without derivatives. The pull-to-par mechanism (default risk decays as maturity approaches, and HY spread curves are often too flat to pay for extension) is the practitioners' account, and a European short-maturity implementation outperformed its broad benchmark in the 2022 drawdown (-4.3% vs -6.0%) while roughly matching it in the 2023 recovery.[^ctmat] The caveat the vendors state less loudly: the tilt gives up the recovery convexity - the weakest issuers bounce hardest in the rally phase, so the defensive book lags exactly when the pole's losses are being repaired. That is an acceptable trade for a sleeve whose job is budgeted bleed, but it must be priced on our own window, not taken from marketing decks whose "X% of return at Y% of drawdown" figures visibly drift year to year.

On the rating axis, the underlying anomaly is real but its interpretation is contested. Verdad's long-sample decomposition (developed-market corporates ex-financials, 1997-2019) finds risk-adjusted return *peaks at BB* and declines monotonically through B and CCC - "fool's yield": beyond a threshold, default losses overtake the coupon and realized return falls below the yield at purchase.[^verdad] Their later work sharpens the mechanism: B and CCC price returns are persistently negative and never reclaim pre-drawdown levels, so the quoted yield systematically overstates the achievable return in a way BB yield does not.[^verdad2] But an index-level decomposition attributed to Bloomberg's research group reportedly finds ~90% of the BB bucket's edge over adjacent buckets is the fallen-angel and rising-star *technical* (forced flows around the IG/HY boundary), not an independent quality premium - a claim we could not verify to a primary document and hold as provisional (see Limitations). The practical conclusion does not depend on resolving it: **no BB-only or quality-screened USD HY UCITS ETF exists** (the BB-only fund is US-domiciled '40 Act; the quality-tiered UCITS funds are EUR-denominated), so the only investable expression of "defensive inside HY" is the short-maturity bucket - iShares SDHY (iBoxx USD Liquid HY 0-5 Capped, since 2013) or PIMCO STHY (ICE BofA US HY Constrained 0-5yr, since 2012), both LSE-listed.

## Fallen angels: a real premium the mandate cannot hold

The fallen-angel reversal is among the better-documented technicals in credit: downgraded-to-HY bonds underperform matched HY around the downgrade (forced selling by capital-charged insurers and IG-mandated funds) and then reverse for up to two years, with the recovery proportional to the initial underperformance.[^bendor] The forced-selling mechanism is confirmed by independent insurer-level work, which also bounds it - the abnormal-return gap between constrained and unconstrained holders is modest and fully reverts within months, a bounded mean-reversion, not a crash premium.[^ache] And the premium has *not* decayed at the index level: FTSE Russell's post-Covid sequel finds fallen angels carrying a superior Sharpe ratio to both IG and HY since 2020, while stating plainly that "the cliff-edge effect of the formal downgrade from IG to HY is diminishing" post-Dodd-Frank as downgrades are pre-priced and mandates get flexible - the index-level premium persists even as its textbook mechanism thins, and the fallen-angel share of US HY has meanwhile fallen to a 23-year low near 3%.[^ftse25]

So why reject the tilt? Because the vehicle fails the identity test. Fallen angels are downgraded *investment-grade* issuance - long original maturities - so the FA index runs roughly 6.7-7 years of duration against ~3-4 for broad HY. A long-only UCITS wrapper has no way to strip it: the rate-hedged wrapper exists for IG (LQDH) but not for HY or FA, and the whole point of the duration-stripped pole's redesign was to kill the rates factor that overlaps the trend book. A fallen-angel leg would re-import, unhedged, the exact exposure the sleeve was rebuilt to exclude - at 2016-inception instrument history (iShares WING) that barely predates our window. The premium is real; the mandate cannot hold the vehicle. A short-maturity fallen-angel index would resolve the conflict, and none was found to exist.

## The trend gate would un-hire the pole

Tactical credit - gating the HY leg to cash or Treasuries on a moving-average or momentum signal - has genuine peer-reviewed support: a simple MA overlay on OAS-sorted HY and IG portfolios produces abnormal returns that survive transaction costs and an Asness-Moskowitz-Pedersen factor control, robust across formation windows.[^br18] It also has an unusually honest independent decomposition: across ~1,800 specifications, the timing edge concentrates almost entirely in 2000-03 and 2008-09 - strip those two windows and annualized return falls from 2.9% to 1.6%, of which roughly half is just carry differential harvesting, leading the authors to doubt there is "sufficient juice to squeeze" net of costs outside systemic crises.[^hoffstein]

But the disqualifying evidence is about shape, not returns. De-risking into declines is mechanically a skew-raising operation: the same construction applied to momentum cuts the strategy's maximum drawdown from -97% to -45% and roughly doubles Sharpe precisely by truncating the left tail,[^bsc] and the momentum-crash literature frames such risk management as converting a negatively-skewed stream into a neutral-to-positive one.[^dm] The trend article reached the identical conclusion from the opposite direction - trend-following's positive skew is *manufactured by* exactly this respond-to-price mechanism ([[what-makes-a-trend-sleeve-convex]]). Layer a trend gate on the carry pole and the book's two poles converge on the same shape: the concave income anchor becomes a weak convex hybrid, the floor's skew budget loses its short leg, and the roster owns two long-gamma sleeves again - the construction error [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] opens by warning against. No source directly measures trend-gated HY skew against buy-and-hold HY (a literature gap), but the mechanism evidence is convergent and includes top-journal work, so the prior is strong: **a timing mute belongs in a separate, convex-budgeted sleeve, never inside this one.** This also supersedes the vol-regime-mute hypothesis pre-registered in [[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]] - the FX evidence for regime avoidance is real, but on this pole the cure amputates the payoff shape the sleeve is hired for.

## EM local currency: the investable FX-carry skew

The sourcing article's central unfinished business is that carry's crash skew lives in *currencies*, which a no-derivatives retail book cannot hold directly. EM local-currency sovereign bonds are the loophole: BIS work shows local-currency sovereign yields and spreads move with the bilateral dollar exchange rate - the bond return genuinely embeds the FX risk premium rather than sitting beside it,[^bis474] with the post-2013 "risk-taking channel" strengthening the linkage (a stronger dollar tightens EM local financial conditions and widens local spreads together).[^bis775] The distributional signature is documented independently: EM local-currency bond returns carry high variance and *negative skewness* pronounced enough to measurably deter US investor participation - the crash asymmetry is real and priced, not an artifact of one episode.[^bw] Year-level index behavior confirms FX as the asymmetric driver: the GBI-EM's bad years are its currency years (2013, 2015, 2018), and the clean in-window illustration is the taper tantrum, where local-currency EM fell ~10% while developed HY fell low single digits. For a pole hired to be short-gamma, this is the right kind of ugly: a UCITS local-currency tracker (iShares IEML, since 2011) is a long-only, physically-replicated claim on the FX-carry premium *with its skew attached* - the first investable test of the sourcing article's "source the skew to FX" hypothesis.

Two disciplines on sizing it. First, its duration is EM local rates, not the DM duration the trend sleeve rides - so it does not break rates-orthogonality in the sense that matters, but it is not duration-free either. Second, the diversification it adds is calm-market only: the definitive cross-asset carry study finds carry strategies' worst drawdowns cluster across asset classes in global recessions even though their unconditional correlations are low,[^kmpv] and EM sovereign risk has grown *more* entangled with the US credit factor since the GFC, not less.[^bis541] The leg should lift income and deepen skew at modest weight; it must not be sized as if it hedged the crash - in the states the skew budget prices, all the carry legs go down together, which is precisely what the pole is paid for.

## Addendum (2026-07-03): levers whose income cannot shed its skew

Measured in the blended book, the cash-bond levers above optimize the pole out of its role: every defensive improvement is a DTS cut in disguise ([[the-skew-is-the-product]]). A second instrument sweep therefore asked a different question - not "which cash-bond tilt pays best per unit of tail" but "which UCITS instruments pay income that is *structurally* crash-rent", so the skew cannot be tilted away without exiting the position. Four candidates survive verification, one gap is confirmed, and one old kill is reaffirmed.

**Pure credit spread, sold as protection: DBXM.** The Xtrackers II iTraxx Crossover Swap UCITS ETF (LU0290359032, TER 0.24%, launched 2007, ~50m AUM, synthetic/funded swap, Xetra-listed) holds the protection-*seller* side of the iTraxx Crossover 5y CDS index: its return is cash rate plus CDS running spread, minus spread-widening and defaults.[^dbxm] This is the duration-stripped credit-carry exposure the pole has been approximating with SDHY+LQDH, delivered directly - no rates leg to hedge, no maturity bucket to defensively tilt, income and crash-beta fused in one instrument at the exact BB-B belt where credit's short-put character concentrates ([[the-skew-is-the-product]]). Costs of admission: small AUM, synthetic counterparty structure (the rented-premium warning of [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]] applies in milder form - this one is unlevered and collateralized), an unconfirmed LSE listing, and 2020/2022 drawdown figures still unverified. The Tabula/Janus Henderson iTraxx Crossover ETFs that once competed here were liquidated in 2021-22;[^tabula] DBXM is the last pure-spread vehicle standing.

**Subordination as crash-rent: AT1s and corporate hybrids.** The Invesco AT1 Capital Bond UCITS ETF (EUR-hedged Dist class XAT1, IE00BFZPF439, TER 0.39%, ~440m AUM) buys the bank-capital layer that is contractually first to absorb stress - its March 2023 stress test is on the public record (a ~13% drawdown around the Credit Suisse AT1 write-down, with only ~2.9% direct CS exposure).[^at1] The income is high because the skew is real and event-shaped; no defensive tilt inside the wrapper can remove it. The Invesco Euro Corporate Hybrid Bond ETF (EHBD, IE00BKWD3966, TER 0.39%, EUR-native) is the same theme one notch milder in non-financial corporates.[^ehbd] Sizing discipline: AT1 events cluster with credit crises (that is the point), and the instrument is a single-sector bet - a modest satellite leg, sized off the pole's tail budget, never a core holding.

**Correction (2026-07-11): collateralized put-write UCITS exists.** The UBS Euro Equity Defensive Put Write SF UCITS ETF (IE00BLDGHT92; Xetra `UIQ4`; launched 8 July 2020) synthetically tracks a rules-based strategy that sells listed four-week, 97%-strike European puts on the Euro Stoxx 50 and holds a notional cash exposure linked to the three-month German Bubill rate.[^ubsput] It is fully funded through a total-return swap collateralized with G10 government, supranational bonds, and cash. At end-January 2026 it reported EUR 140.24m assets, a 0.21% flat fee, 0.05%-0.06% ex-post tracking error, and five-year net performance of 7.58% annualized at 3.85% volatility. Those figures begin after the March 2020 crash and come from UBS, so they are implementation facts, not an edge estimate.

This is a cleaner hypothesis test than a covered-call ETF because its explicit components are cash return plus short-put payoff rather than long equities plus short calls. It is not pure delta-hedged variance: it retains Euro Stoxx 50 downside beta, sells a fixed 3% OTM strike, and introduces UBS swap-counterparty and synthetic-replication risks. Registration is broad but the January 2026 factsheet states Italy is institutional-only. The running IBKR Gateway nevertheless resolved the ISIN on 2026-07-11 as contract `437843482`, symbol/local symbol `E50PW`, primary exchange `EBS`, with SMART routing through `IBIS`/Xetra, Frankfurt, SIX, Gettex and others. A one-share non-transmitting What-If validation on the connected paper account returned `PreSubmitted`, no warning and no IBKR error. This confirms paper-account order eligibility, not live-account PRIIPs permission. Bid-ask spread, market depth, live-account eligibility, and the index's pre-launch backfill remain preconditions for a run.

**Covered-call UCITS remain equity-income products, not clean VRP sleeves.** JPMorgan's JEGP (IE0003UVYC20, 0.35%) actively selects a defensive global equity portfolio and sells out-of-the-money MSCI World calls; its May 2026 commentary reports 7.94% since inception against 22.65% for MSCI World, with the option overlay hurting during the rally.[^jegp] Global X QYLD (IE00BM8R0J59, 0.45%, about USD 646m at January 2026) synthetically tracks Nasdaq-100 plus successive one-month at-the-money calls. XYLU (IE0002L5QB31, 0.45%, about USD 142m) does the same on the S&P 500, with a disclosed -0.07% swap fee at January 2026.[^ccall] These are transparent expressions of capped equity beta. The 2026 facts strengthen the previous rejection: distribution yield should not be treated as standalone volatility alpha.

The implementation ordering is now: UBS put-write as the cleanest listed collateralized candidate, covered calls as rejected equity-income proxies for this role, and VIX-futures carry as a different and more path-fragile implied-volatility term premium. UBS is a research candidate, not a promotion, because the generic evidence finds post-2010 S&P 500 option alpha weak and because the accessible product sells European rather than US equity crash risk.

**Correction (2026-07-04): cat bonds are investable at the fund level, and are a pole in their own right.** The earlier "watchlist, not investable" read looked only at the *ETF* (KRC CATB/C47B, IE000UWJUW87, TER 1.28%, launched Dec 2025, still ~$13m and shadowed by the ESMA eligibility question).[^catb] At the *fund* level the category is large: the UCITS cat-bond sector held ~$19.12bn end-2025 (~31% of outstanding cat-bond risk capital), with weekly/fortnightly-liquid institutional strategies (Twelve $4.55bn, Schroder GAIA $4.05bn, Fermat, Icosa, Plenum, Franklin K2, Leadenhall). Because a cat bond's crash factor is meteorological, not financial, it is not really a *credit*-carry leg at all - it is a distinct, orthogonal concave pole that solves the floor's co-crash from the income side, developed in [[insurance-linked-securities-as-the-orthogonal-income-pole]]. The ETF stays a watch item; the funds are deployable now.

**Reaffirmed**: no rate-hedged HY UCITS and no BB-only USD HY UCITS exist (2026 re-check); dedicated FX-carry UCITS ETFs survive only as ~10m-AUM zombies from 2008 (Xtrackers DBX1AY), so EM local bonds remain the sole practical FX-carry claim.[^fxzombie]

## Limitations

- The short-duration-HY and CCC-avoidance evidence is practitioner-grade throughout - no peer-reviewed paper isolates either as a standalone anomaly, every vendor cited sells the product, and headline capture ratios drift between editions of the same claim. The 2022 maturity-bucket decomposition and Verdad's long-sample rating curve are the least conflicted pieces.[^ct22][^verdad]
- The claim that ~90% of the BB quality edge is fallen-angel/rising-star technicals is secondhand (attributed to Bloomberg index research; no primary document verified). If true it merges the quality and fallen-angel levers into one mechanism; the investable action (maturity-bucket choice) is unchanged either way.
- The trend-gate skew-destruction verdict is a mechanism inference from adjacent literatures (risk-managed momentum, trend construction), not a direct measurement on gated HY - strong convergent prior, unmeasured instance. A cheap diagnostic on our own data could close it, but is low priority since both the returns decomposition and the shape argument point the same way.[^hoffstein][^bsc]
- No independent net-of-cost backtest of an EM-local UCITS leg over 2018-2025 exists; we build our own (the run this article seeds).
- Post-publication decay applies to every premium here (the fallen-angel case shows a mechanism can thin while its index-level premium persists - the reverse can also happen).[^ftse25]
- The addendum's instrument facts (tickers, ISINs, TERs, AUM, listings) were verified against issuer/justETF pages on 2026-07-03, but crash-behavior numbers are patchy: DBXM's 2020/2022 drawdowns and the AT1 ETF's March-2020 drawdown could not be pinned to a primary document (research hit a fetch rate limit), and the Credit Suisse-episode figures are secondhand press numbers. Build drawdowns from our own data before sizing any new leg.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **The defensive tilt pays per unit of tail.** Shifting the HY bucket from broad toward short-maturity should raise held-out income-per-tail without deepening the tail budget. **Confirmed standalone, then amended in composite, 2026-07-03** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03|construction sweep]], [[runs/floor/2026-07-03|floor diary]], [[the-skew-is-the-product]]): the standalone confirmation held, but the floor composite showed the same shift is a duration-times-spread cut that shaves the pole's crash-beta along with its tail. Income economics stand; promotion needs a composite gate with duration-times-spread pinned.
- [x] **The EM local leg deepens the concave signature.** A modest local-currency sovereign sleeve should lift held-out income-per-tail and move the pole's skew more negative - the first investable test of "source the skew to FX" from [[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole]]. **Tested 2026-07-03** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03|construction sweep and live-mechanics re-sweep]]): utility-negative on a daily-rebalanced book, but flips to a live champion - shallower tail, income-neutral - once a drift band cuts turnover to a few trades a year. The leg earns its place as a tail-shallower under banded mechanics, not as an income source; the skew-deepening claim holds only in that conditional form.
- [x] **Upgrades must beat the incumbent, shape-ranked.** The sweep's champion must dominate the nested incumbent on held-out income-per-tail without deepening the tail budget, ranked on a shape-only grid per the ranker A/B lesson in [[what-makes-a-convergent-sleeve-an-income-engine]]. **Confirmed 2026-07-03** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03|diary]]): the champion clears both bars on a plateau, not a spike.
- [ ] **(Negative control, low priority.) Trend-gating the credit legs flips the pole's skew.** If ever run: pre-register that a MA-gated variant *raises* quarterly skew toward zero-or-positive and fails the concave family gate of [[verifying-strategy-family-membership]] - confirming the identity argument above on our own data rather than by mechanism inference.
- [ ] **The pure-spread leg restores the skew (DBXM).** Adding a protection-seller iTraxx Crossover leg (DBXM) to the book deepens held-out qskew past the -0.5 gate and holds corr-to-trend <= 0 at flat-or-better `convergent_income_utility` - crash-rent income the defensive axis cannot tilt away. - [x] **The subordination leg pays event-shaped crash-rent (AT1s).** A modest AT1 leg should lift income and deepen skew with correlation-to-trend intact; kill if income arrives without deepened skew. **Killed 2026-07-03** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-03|diary]]): with coupons fully credited, every weight loses held-out utility and income to the control - the instrument is fine, the book's vol-target sizing taxes the high-vol leg away. - [x] **(Composite-gated only.) The covered-call leg is tested in the floor, not standalone.** A VRP leg via covered-call UCITS may deepen skew while raising correlation-to-trend, since its crash factor is equities; admissible only through the composite gates of [[the-skew-is-the-product]]. **Tested 2026-07-04, rejected (this window)** ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-04|VRP probe]]): downside correlation to trend is positive - it co-crashes, the equity-crash-factor overlap flagged above. **Correction 2026-07-11:** the claim that no survivable put-write UCITS wrapper existed was false; UBS IE00BLDGHT92 now receives the separate clean-wrapper hypothesis below.
