# Carry Diversifies Only a Divergent Trend

> [!note] Status
> SETTLED on our book (2026-07-04); demoted from `research/` on 2026-07-09. This is a project post-mortem - the co-crash decomposition of our own floor's poles, with our run numbers load-bearing throughout - not generic research. The generic lessons live in articles: TSMOM-not-safe-havens is in [[divergent-vs-defensive-crash-resistance]] and [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]]; the monthly-frequency measurement rule and the orthogonal-income target are in [[insurance-linked-securities-as-the-orthogonal-income-pole]] and [[the-skew-is-the-product]].

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> A concave income pole's value to a two-pole book is not a property of the income pole alone - it is a property of the *pairing*, and it is conditional on the convex pole being a genuinely divergent, long-straddle payoff (futures time-series momentum), not a static long-beta "safe-haven" basket. Passive long-duration, long-gold and long-credit-protection are unreliable crisis hedges; a trend-follower that can go short is orthogonal to carry in calm markets and profits in the shocks that sink it. The literature result is general; our own application of it carried a trap. Our convex pole *is* a divergent TSMOM, and once the co-crash is decomposed, the existing carry pole already diversifies it at the horizon the floor operates (ex-fast-crash contribution +0.007, monthly correlation +0.081) - the alarming daily -0.009 / +0.42 headline was a single-fast-crash, daily-frequency artifact. So the operative lesson is narrower than "suspect the convex pole": measure the pairing at the right frequency and let the tail tier own fast crashes, before concluding either pole is broken.

This article exists because of a second measured failure, one level up from the first. [[the-skew-is-the-product]] diagnosed why a carry pole kept shedding its skew: every standalone lever sells the inventory the pole is hired to hold. The fix is a composite ranker that scores the pole on its manipulation-proof certainty-equivalent contribution to the book, gated on crisis-conditional correlation to the convex pole ([[what-makes-a-carry-sleeve-an-income-engine]]). Pointed at a whole construction grid, that kind of metric can return an uncomfortable verdict: not one credit-carry cell diversifies the book, and neither does an equity variance-risk-premium pole, nor even a pure short-volatility pole - every investable concave source carries positive downside correlation to the trend pole. The natural reading is structural despair: carry drawdowns cluster in global recessions across asset classes ([[the-skew-is-the-product]]), so perhaps nothing can diversify a crisis book. That reading is often wrong, and the error hides in a term held fixed the entire time - the convex pole.

## The co-crash can be one fast crash measured daily, not a miscast pole

The convex pole in question need not be a static basket. Take the archetype: a vol-sized regression-slope TSMOM book, ranked on convexity rather than Sharpe, that *shorts* long-duration Treasuries on a confirmed downtrend (borrow-gated) and forgoes to cash when flat. That is already the divergent long-straddle the literature prescribes, not the safe-haven basket it is easy to mistake it for.

Yet measured at daily frequency over the full window, the credit-carry champion scored a certainty-equivalent contribution to a book with this pole of -0.009 - it appeared to *subtract* - at downside correlation +0.42. Decomposing where that co-crash lives settles it: only 14 of the trend pole's 177 worst-decile days fall in the March-2020 fast-crash window, but they carry almost the entire signal. Excluding that window, the downside correlation falls from +0.42 to +0.095, the full-sample daily correlation from +0.157 to +0.101, and the carry pole's contribution *flips positive to +0.007*; at monthly frequency the correlation is +0.081, and on the trend pole's worst-decile days overall the carry pole's mean return is flat (-0.0003), not negative.[^mfprobe] The +0.42 was never structural evidence about the pairing. It was a slow trend book (lookback 189) caught long risk in a fast crash it had no time to reposition out of - the "second responder" property - co-moving with credit for a few weeks, amplified by daily-frequency co-gapping (the "daily downside-correlation bias"). Outside that one crash, at the horizon the floor actually operates, **the existing carry pole already diversifies the existing trend pole.**

The external comparison still carries a smaller, real signal: against three independent broad managed-futures proxies the same carry pole contributes +0.013 to +0.032 with full-sample correlation near zero (a broad index proxy prints -0.006).[^mfprobe] A broad CTA is a marginally better partner than our narrow three-name pole - but the gap over our own pole's ex-fast-crash +0.007 is small, and it lives mostly in breadth and in fast-crash handling. That residual is a *speed* property of a deliberately-slow drawdown-payer, and covering fast crashes is the tail sleeve's mandate ([[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]], [[detecting-fast-crashes-from-price]]), not the trend pole's. So the fix is not to rebuild the convex pole; it is to measure the floor at the horizon it trades (monthly, on the composite CE contribution) and let the tail tier own the fast crash.

## Static safe-havens are the *unreliable* divergent

The taxonomy in [[divergent-vs-defensive-crash-resistance]] splits crash-resistance into edges that *gain* in a crash and edges that merely *lose less*. Long duration, gold, and long credit-protection are usually filed under the first heading - flight-to-safety - but the crisis-hedging literature is blunt that this filing is regime-fragile. Cook, Hoyle, Sargaison, Taylor and Van Hemert, studying every major crisis from 1985 to 2016, rank passive hedges by cost and reliability and conclude that holding safe-haven US Treasuries is "generally an unreliable crisis-hedge strategy, since the post-2000 negative bond-equity correlation is a historical rarity"; long gold and long credit-protection "sit between puts and bonds in terms of both cost and reliability."[^bsww] Passive safe-havens are hedges that happen to have worked in the equity crises of one monetary regime, and 2022 is the counterexample that shows the seam: an inflation/rates-up crisis in which the safe-haven basket was itself the epicentre.

A static safe-haven pole therefore fails as the floor's convex leg for two compounding reasons. It is an unreliable divergent (it does not gain in every crisis, and in a rate shock it is the crisis). And its losing states coincide with carry's losing states, because both are long duration-and-credit risk - so pairing them concentrates rather than diversifies. The pole looks convex on the 2008/2020 equity crashes and reveals itself as short the rate-shock state in 2022.

## The reliable convex pole is a long-straddle

The one hedge Cook et al. find reliable across regimes is not passive at all: futures time-series momentum, which "has parallels with long option straddle strategies, allowing it to benefit during extended equity sell-offs."[^bsww] The straddle analogy is not loose - it is Fung and Hsieh's foundational result that trend-following returns are replicated by portfolios of lookback straddles, i.e. the strategy is structurally long volatility and long gamma, with an option-like convex payoff that no linear combination of static asset exposures reproduces.[^fh] Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen extend the evidence across a century of markets: time-series momentum delivered positive average returns in every decade since 1880 with low correlation to traditional assets, and performed well in eight of the ten largest 60/40 drawdowns.[^hop] The mechanism behind the crisis alpha is dynamic, not allocational: studying daily CTA positions, the crisis alpha comes from cutting exposure to the crashing market fast (inside fifteen days) while staying long the markets that are gaining - a book that *repositions*, which a static basket cannot do.[^crisisalpha] That is why the divergence is reliable where the safe-haven's is not: the straddle is long the move, whichever way it goes, so it does not depend on 2022 rhyming with 2008.

This also dissolves the "structural despair" reading. Carry losses do cluster in recessions across asset classes - but a trend-follower is not a static long position in those asset classes; in a sustained recessionary trend it is *short* them and profits. The convex pole and the concave pole fail in disjoint states by construction: the concave pole gives back its premium in fast dislocations, and the convex pole harvests exactly those sustained dislocations. They are the two halves of a straddle-and-short-straddle pair, which is the entire logic of [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]].

## Why carry pairs with a straddle but not a safe-haven

The pairing works in the states that matter because a genuine trend book has near-zero unconditional correlation to carry and, crucially, is *not co-exposed* to carry's crash factor. In calm markets carry earns its premium and trend earns its slow momentum premium from unrelated signals - hence the ~0 full-sample correlation we measure. In a fast dislocation carry crashes and trend, having trailed the move, is positioned with it. The research on combining them at the signal level reaches the same conclusion from the other direction: Molyboga, Qian and He call carry and trend "a match made in heaven," and studies that let the relative weight on carry and momentum signals vary with recent performance find the combination generates roughly flat returns through the 2008 crisis when naive carry alone lost about a quarter of its value.[^ra][^cmom] "Should trend follow carry" - conditioning trend signals on carry - improves risk-adjusted returns most in exactly the asset where carry's contribution is largest (bonds), though the authors caution that carry-conditioning can dilute the pure crisis alpha, so the two are complements to be balanced, not merged.[^ra]

The safe-haven pole has neither property. Its correlation to carry is not zero-conditional-on-crisis - it is *positive* in the rate-shock state because it is long the same duration risk - and it cannot reposition, so it cannot manufacture the disjoint-states structure that makes the pairing convex. The lesson generalizes past our specific instruments: the concave pole's diversification is a joint property of the pair, and it is destroyed by choosing a convex pole whose crash factor overlaps the concave pole's, however "defensive" that convex pole looks in isolation.

## What this does and does not fix

It does not make the downside correlation vanish. On the shorter, 2022-dominated proxy windows the carry pole's correlation to a managed-futures pole on the pole's worst decile is still positive (+0.33 to +0.38); only the longest, full-window proxy roughly halves it to +0.19.[^mfprobe] What flips cleanly is the metric that actually governs promotion - the certainty-equivalent contribution - because it integrates the near-zero full-sample correlation and the carry income across the whole joint distribution, not just the worst decile. This argues for a gate correction: rank on the composite CE contribution (turns positive), and retire the hard "downside correlation <= 0" screen as both unreachable and unnecessary for a long-only book.

Unreachable, because Patton's result is that knowledge of asymmetric tail dependence is economically valuable for an unconstrained investor but "for short-sales-constrained investors the gains are limited";[^patton] a UCITS long-only pole cannot manufacture a negative crisis beta. Unnecessary, because the diversification value does not require negative correlation in the first place. The rebalancing premium between two equal-volatility poles has a closed form, `RP = sigma^2 (1 - rho) / 4`: it is positive for *any* correlation below one and grows as correlation falls, so a pole at rho ~ 0 already extracts close to the maximum premium the volatility budget allows - the negative-rho regime a long-only book cannot reach buys only a marginal increment on top.[^rpsynth] The right gate is therefore "composite CE contribution > 0 and near-zero unconditional correlation," measured at monthly rather than daily frequency (daily correlations are inflated by microstructure gapping and short-horizon autocorrelation, while the structural relationship the floor trades on lives at lower frequency).[^rpsynth] The concave side's job is orthogonal income, not an anti-trend - which is exactly the case for sourcing it from a crash factor that is not financial at all ([[insurance-linked-securities-as-the-orthogonal-income-pole]]).

Two open threads for implementation, neither of which changes the diagnosis. First, investability: the reliable convex pole is a futures TSMOM program, and accessing it under UCITS means a managed-futures UCITS wrapper rather than the static ETF basket we held - the instrument survey belongs in [[the-orthogonal-non-equity-trend-universe]] and is the natural next sourcing task. Second, architecture: whether the floor should run trend and carry as two poles rebalanced against each other, or fold carry in as a conditioning signal on the trend book ([[when-conditioning-pays]]), is the Research Affiliates question and is worth testing both ways.[^ra] What is settled is the term we had wrong: a carry sleeve that will not diversify the book is first evidence about the *convex* pole, and only second an instruction to re-tune the carry.

## Strategy hypotheses

- [x] **The convex pole is not the problem (P0, resolved by diagnosis not construction).** The convex pole is already a divergent TSMOM; the co-crash decomposed to a single fast crash, and ex-that-window the existing carry pole contributes +0.007 with monthly correlation +0.081 ([[runs/demeter/2026-07-04]]). No rebuild or re-source needed. A broad managed-futures pole is marginally better (+0.013..+0.032) but the gap is mostly fast-crash handling, which is the tail sleeve's job.
- [ ] **Gate recalibration (the actual fix).** Adopt "composite CE contribution > 0 and |correlation| < 0.10, measured at monthly frequency" as the floor-promotion gate, retiring the daily "downside correlation <= 0" hard screen as both unreachable long-only (Patton) and a fast-crash artifact. Keep the robust skew floor as the family-membership mandate, not a promotion objective.
- [ ] **Confirm the tail tier owns fast crashes.** The residual March-2020-style co-movement is a speed property of a deliberately-slow drawdown-payer; verify it is covered by the tail sleeve ([[the-ucits-constrained-tail-sleeve]]), not patched into the trend pole.
- [ ] **Two poles vs carry-conditioned trend.** Test the floor built as (a) trend and carry as separate poles rebalanced against each other vs (b) a single trend book with signals conditioned on carry ([[when-conditioning-pays]]); pre-register the composite CE and the crisis-window capture for each. Expect (a) to preserve more pure crisis alpha and (b) to raise calm-market Sharpe, per Research Affiliates.[^ra]
