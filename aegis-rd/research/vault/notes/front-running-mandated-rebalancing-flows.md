# Front-Running Mandated Rebalancing Flows

> [!note] Status

> [!abstract] Answer
> The open, mechanism-first sweep found exactly one alpha source that names its payer, fits a small account, does not lean on price continuation, and has both peer-quality evidence and independent replications: **the month-end price pressure of mandated institutional rebalancing and payment cycles, traded as a short-horizon stock-bond reversal**. Pensions, target-date funds and balanced mandates must sell the month's winning asset class and buy the loser on a schedule they admit they cannot change; the pressure predicts next-day stock and bond returns (~17 bp per one-standard-deviation signal) and reverts within two weeks.[^hmm] The same payer family surfaces independently in Treasuries, where the entire term premium concentrates in the last few days of the month and a long-Treasuries-only-then position earned a Sharpe near 1, driven by insurers and index funds buying month-end index additions,[^schwarz] and in corporate bonds, where index weight changes earn ~39 bp/month that vanishes the following month.[^ottonello] A long-only expression - hold the month's stock/bond underperformer for roughly the last five sessions of the month via two UCITS ETFs - is implementable at retail cost. **Confidence: medium.** The mechanism and payer are well documented across three independent academic teams and two practitioner replications, but the strategy paper is young and heavily downloaded, one practitioner counter-study finds no effect in 2020-2025 data, and every prior predictable-flow edge (index effect, Goldman roll, odd-lot tenders) died once crowded. The verdict is therefore *proceed to prospective empirical validation with a pre-registered decay monitor*, not allocate. Runner-up: crypto perpetual funding-rate carry - a genuine, named-payer transfer with a structural limits-to-arbitrage story,[^cryptocarry] but gated for this book by venue/counterparty risk, EU retail derivative access, and post-ETF basis compression. No other mechanism out of the twenty-eight mapped cleared the bar.

The assignment behind this article is [[the-tiered-strategy-roster|the roster's]] unfilled ordinary-market seat: a transparent, falsifiable partner for Atalanta, the slow, diversified, volatility-scaled non-equity trend sleeve. The previous search ([[accessible-ordinary-market-income-after-an-open-search]]) worked from strategy families and products and ended with three untested tracer lanes. This round reset the search at the level of economic mechanisms and payers, presupposing nothing about carry, convergence, skew, or asset class. Expectations and falsifiers were written down before the sweep (scratchpad pre-registration, 2026-07-17); the sweep ran wide-to-narrow across eight rounds - six WIDE sweeps plus two NARROW source-drilling passes, 24 search/fetch calls over ~140 documents - drawing on academic (SSRN, NBER, journals) and practitioner (blogs, replication sites, forum retrospectives) camps. Stopping rule: the final WIDE round added only dead or implementation-gated mechanisms (pre-FOMC drift, structured-product vol supply) plus corroboration of the winner in new markets, which is the saturation signature the methodology asks for.

## The job, defined without naming a solution

Atalanta's weaknesses are measured, not hypothesized ([[trend-following-in-whipsaw-regimes]], the 2026-06/07 run campaigns): it bleeds in quiet oscillation (repeated whipsaw around a flat latch), is wrong-footed by V-shaped reversals (the worst held-out split is a reversal window), and misses the first leg of fast gaps (2018-type, repositioning arrives days late). Five separate conditioning arcs (turn gates, ER gates, R^2 weighting, memory gates, speed blends) failed to repair these from inside the sleeve - the whipsaw cost of any filter exceeded its benefit on a thin book (graveyard, "can't filter your way out", five reproductions).

What repairs the weakness at the whole-portfolio level is therefore an *external* return stream with three properties: its trigger does not require directional persistence (so it can pay during oscillation); its holding period is short and calendar-bounded (so a reversal cannot wrong-foot it for months); and its driver is not equity beta, duration, credit or short volatility held statically (so it does not duplicate the roster's carry pole or the divergent seat itself). A calm-market income *shape* is not required; a flat-convexity stream that simply fires on a clock, independent of trend state, has equal or better marginal utility because it also pays during crises (a point the sizing literature makes about left-tail correlation, not smoothness[^quantica]).

Two meta-facts from the map discipline the whole search. First, the anomaly-decay literature says the average published predictor loses roughly half its return after publication, the decline is concentrated in cheap-to-arbitrage large-liquid expressions, and newer publications decay faster - every year of publication vintage adds about five points of Sharpe haircut.[^mclean][^falck] Only the US shows a reliable post-publication decline, which is weak comfort since every candidate here trades US assets.[^jacobs] Second, the three cleanest precedents of predictable-flow edges - index adds, the Goldman roll, levered-ETF resets - all died within roughly a decade of being documented, through better liquidity supply and "sunshine trading" rather than through the flows stopping.[^gs][^irwin][^daniel] Any flow-based winner must therefore carry its own decay monitor as part of the strategy contract.

## The winner: mandated-flow reversal at the month end

### Mechanism and evidence

Institutional portfolios bound to fixed stock/bond allocations - roughly $20 trillion across pensions, target-date funds and balanced mandates - mechanically sell the month's outperforming asset class and buy the underperformer, on calendar (month/quarter-end) or threshold (drift-band) rules. Harvey, Mazzoleni and Melone reconstruct both rules from nothing but trailing S&P 500 and 10-year Treasury returns and show the resulting signals predict next-day returns: a one-standard-deviation overweight-equity signal predicts equities about -17 bp and bonds about +2 to +4 bp the next day (t ~ 4), robust to momentum, reversal, macro and sentiment controls; the pressure peaks in the last week, reverts almost entirely within two weeks, is stronger into quarter-ends, extends to international equities, and appears in CFTC positioning (asset managers on the pressure side, leveraged funds partially on the other).[^hmm] A front-running portfolio - long the asset the rebalancers must buy, short the other, in futures - earns a Sharpe above 1 over 1997-2023.[^hmm]

Three independent lines corroborate the payer rather than the backtest. Etula, Rinne, Suominen and Vaittinen document the sibling payment-cycle channel in trade-level data: institutions are net sellers up to four days before month-end and buyers after, a one-standard-deviation fund outflow moves T-8..T-4 returns by -1.3 pp with a +0.9 pp rebound in T-3..T-1, and the reversal amplifies when funding is tight - published in RFS with ANcerno institutional trades, not index returns.[^etula] Graziani shows the aggregate S&P 500 reverts the month-end pressure over the following month, and that this time-series reversal survives transaction costs and out-of-sample tests precisely because it lives in liquid, high-priced stocks - the opposite of classic cross-sectional reversal.[^graziani] Kayaçetin (peer-reviewed, 2026) finds the turn-of-month effect persistent across three decades of international data with a hierarchical month -> quarter -> half-year buildup matching rebalancing mandates, and - against the efficient-markets prediction - at peak economic significance in the final five years of the sample.[^kayacetin]

The same payer leaves the same footprint in fixed income, measured by teams with no connection to the equity work. Schwarz documents that average excess returns on coupon Treasuries are positive and highly significant only in the last few days of the month - the entire term premium concentrates there - and that a long position held just for those days earned an annualized Sharpe around 1, with insurer transaction data showing life insurers buying the securities added to the benchmark index precisely on the month-end rebalancing date.[^schwarz] The New York Fed finds Treasury trading volume now *concentrates* on the last trading day of the month, which it attributes to the growth of index-benchmarked passive funds.[^dyer] In corporate bonds, benchmark weight changes - publicly computable in advance - earn 39.5 bp of alpha in the rebalancing month that collapses to insignificance the next month.[^ottonello] One payer, four markets (US equities, international equities, Treasuries, corporates), five independent data types (index returns, institutional trades, CFTC positions, insurer transactions, the payers' own statements). That breadth is what separates this mechanism from a single well-marketed backtest.

On the practitioner side, two independent replications reproduce the paper's strategy: one gets Sharpe ~0.94 on futures with the paper's construction,[^quantitativo] the other confirms the futures result and then re-implements it in SPY/TLT ETFs, reporting CAGR 15.6%, Sharpe 1.17, max drawdown -19.7%, equity beta 0.12 and monthly skew +7.1 over 2007-2025.[^qr] A third practitioner builds the long-only expression directly: determine the stock/bond underperformer around the 15th trading day, hold it for the last ~5 sessions, exit at month-end; 20 years, CAGR 7.9% pre-cost, Sharpe ~0.78, two losing years, best year 2008.[^valuelytica]

Why has competitive capital not closed it? Three named reasons, each with evidence. The payers say they cannot stop: in the authors' pension roundtable, funds acknowledge the predictability and call changing rebalancing policy harder than tasking their own alpha desk to trade around it.[^hmm] The natural arbitrageurs are misaligned: hedge funds face their own month-end redemption calendars and on average *contribute to* rather than absorb the pressure.[^etula] And the trade requires warehousing days of outright stock-bond risk, not riskless spread liquidity - unlike the calendar-spread Goldman roll or the index add, there is no synchronous "sunshine" auction where liquidity providers can queue.

### The strategy contract (research-grade, from the sources, no invented thresholds)

- **State.** Two observable conditions from public daily closes: (i) *calendar* - the final ~5 trading sessions of the month, weight rising into quarter-ends;[^hmm][^kayacetin] (ii) *threshold* - the drift of a synthetic 60/40 stock/bond portfolio from target since the last rebalance, averaged across drift bands 0-2.5% as in the paper.[^hmm] Signal = which asset the mandated rebalancers must buy, and how far weights have drifted.
- **Action.** Hold the under-weighted asset of the pair (the month's relative loser) for the pressure window. Full expression is long/short futures; the shorts-free retail expression is long-only in two UCITS ETFs (S&P 500; 20y+ Treasury), sized to the sleeve, one entry per month.[^qr][^valuelytica]
- **Exit.** Calendar-bounded: flatten at month-end or capture the documented first-days reversal, per the paper's last-day sign flip.[^hmm] No open-ended holding; maximum exposure ~5-7 sessions per month.
- **Payer.** Mandate-bound institutions (~$20T) paying ~5-15 bp/year of price impact for tracking-error minimization and month-end cash obligations - approximately $16bn/year in aggregate - documented in returns, institutional trades, CFTC positions and the payers' own statements.[^hmm][^etula]
- **Failure (pre-registered, causal).** (i) The rolling 36-month event-window response of the equity-bond spread to the Threshold and Calendar signals loses statistical significance - the exact death signature the index effect and Goldman roll displayed;[^gs][^irwin] (ii) a structural payer change: large-scale adoption of randomized/overlay rebalancing by TDF and pension complexes (observable in their own policy disclosures, which the L&G rebuttal shows is already the sales pitch of overlay managers[^lg]); (iii) live net returns below cash over a pre-declared two-year paper window after the fee floor. A six-month negative Sharpe is explicitly *not* a kill: two losing years occurred inside the 20-year long-only sample.[^valuelytica]

### Portfolio fit

Independence from Atalanta is structural, not just statistical: the signal is one-month *relative* stock-bond performance traded *against* its direction - anti-continuation at exactly the horizon where the trend sleeve is pro-continuation - held for days, flat ~75% of the time. Reported equity beta of the ETF expression is 0.12 with strongly positive skew,[^qr] the long-only variant's best year was 2008,[^valuelytica] and the mechanism keeps firing in quiet ranges (monthly stock-bond dispersion never goes away), in V-reversals (calendar-bounded holding cannot be wrong-footed for months), and in persistent crises (mandates keep rebalancing; Etula's reversals *widen* when funding is tight[^etula]). The one regime interaction to test rather than assume: the long-Treasury leg overlaps Atalanta's duration axis for a few days each month, and 2022-style joint stock-bond selloffs compress the pair's dispersion into a single falling axis. That is what the aligned-returns test is for.

Costs are the honest constraint at small scale, and they are parametric, not fatal: the long-only expression is ~24 orders/year; at IBKR's EUR 1.25 minimum that is ~EUR 30/year, or 1% on a EUR 3k sleeve and 30 bp on a EUR 10k sleeve, against a documented pre-cost CAGR of ~8% on deployed capital.[^valuelytica][^ibkr] The prior search demoted the unconditional turn-of-month hold on exactly this arithmetic; the difference here is the conditional cross-asset signal (which roughly doubles the per-event edge by choosing *which* asset to hold) and the compounding-portfolio framing the brief mandates. Capacity is the small account's advantage: the flow being front-run is tens of billions per month; the strategy's capacity constraint will never bind at our scale, and the institutions that could crowd it out face the redemption-calendar misalignment above.

### What this is not

It is not (b) compensated exposure wearing a costume: the return is conditional on a calendar/drift state, is contrarian to the month's winner (so it cannot be a static beta harvest), and the correct benchmark - continuously holding the same 50/50 ETF pair - has entirely different exposures on ~19 of 22 sessions a month. It is also not yet (a) *proven*: the core strategy paper is a 2025 working paper with 3,400+ SSRN downloads, a press release and multiple public replications, which by the decay literature's own arithmetic is the profile of a fast-decaying edge.[^falck] That is why the deliverable is a prospective validation with the decay monitor pre-registered, not an allocation.

## The runner-up: crypto perpetual funding carry, gated

The only other mechanism that cleared the mechanism-and-payer bar: holders of leveraged long crypto exposure pay shorts a funding rate every 1-8 hours to keep perpetual futures pinned to spot; delta-neutral long-spot/short-perp collects it. The payer is named (trend-chasing leveraged longs), the transfer is measured (carry has exceeded 40% annualized in demand spikes; funding runs 3-15% annualized in normal regimes), and the limits to arbitrage are structural rather than behavioral: regulatory segmentation keeps traditional balance sheets out, and the absence of cross-margining makes the short leg liquidation-prone - at 10x leverage the futures leg would have been liquidated in over half the sample months, so even patient capital cannot lean in at size.[^cryptocarry] Small scale is a genuine advantage here.

It fails this book's gates today, on three counts stated in the sources rather than assumed: venue/counterparty risk is the dominant realized failure mode (FTX took out basis traders in 2022, and withdrawal freezes recur);[^backquant] compliant EU retail access to perpetuals through a MiFID broker like IBKR does not exist, so the trade requires offshore venue accounts with the custody risk that implies; and the January 2024 US spot ETF measurably compressed the basis by reducing the very segmentation that fed it.[^cryptocarry] Verdict: genuine (a)-class mechanism, implementation gate currently failing, revisit only on a changed precondition (regulated EU perp access or a materially different venue-risk assessment) - the same shape as the [[no-ucits-route-to-cross-asset-carry|cross-asset carry conclusion]].

## Disagreements

- **Does the rebalancing pressure exist right now?** Harvey et al. find it through 2023 and argue their estimates are conservative;[^hmm] Kayaçetin finds the turn-of-month at peak significance in the most recent five years;[^kayacetin] but L&G Asset Management, analyzing their own practitioner data for January 2020 - June 2025, report *no observable relationship* between rebalancing estimates and subsequent returns, and argue CTA momentum demand now offsets rebalancing supply.[^lg] L&G sells rebalancing-overlay services, so the counter-evidence carries conflict of interest in both directions (the paper's second author is at Capital Group). The academic side currently has the better data and the peer-reviewed international corroboration; the practitioner null on the most recent window is exactly the kind of evidence the decay monitor exists to adjudicate. Unresolved; the prospective test settles it.
- **Is the turn-of-month effect alive at all?** Han, Han and Tian: gone entirely in the US after 2001, killed by cheap trading.[^hanhan] Kayaçetin: pervasive and persistent internationally, conditional on prior risk and rebalancing hierarchies.[^kayacetin] These are reconcilable (unconditional US market-level TOM vs conditional, cross-asset, internationally pooled pressure), but the disagreement is real and recorded, and it favors trading the *conditional cross-asset* expression over the naive "hold equities into month-end" one.
- **Are the aggregate costs (and thus the prize) overstated?** Kent Daniel's discussion accepts the predictability as "really nice" but argues the $16bn assumes all $20T rebalances in nearly the worst way, notes TDF holdings data show rebalancing spread over months (45% same-month), and asks the load-bearing question: every similar liquidity-provision profit pool was competed away - "If that isn't happening here, why not??".[^daniel] Our answer (redemption-calendar misalignment plus warehoused outright risk) is an argument, not a measurement.
- **Practitioner vs academic on where retail edge lives.** The practitioner camp converges on constraints - capacity, operational pain, mandate-driven forced flows - as the only durable retail hunting grounds,[^aligrithm] which is consistent with the academic flow-pressure evidence; but the same camp's live small-account records also show fee floors and sudden alpha death dominating outcomes at our scale, which is a warning against the *winner's* cost margin, not just the rejects'.

## Open questions

1. The aligned-returns question: same-date monthly returns of the long-only expression against Atalanta's held-out splits, specifically the reversal split and 2022 (joint stock-bond selloff) - conditional contribution, not unconditional correlation.
2. Whether the last-day sign flip (mean-reversion capture) survives the fee floor at our sleeve size, or whether the retail expression should take only the pressure leg.
3. Whether quarter-end amplification[^kayacetin] earns a sizing tier, or is spec-luck of the same kind v28 punished.
4. The L&G null: reproduce their 2020-2025 window on public data before trusting either side.
5. Whether the UCITS ETF pair (which trades European hours against US-close-anchored signals) loses a material fraction of the next-day effect to timing lag - the one implementation question with no source evidence either way.
