# Relative vs Absolute Momentum in Small Universes

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> The entire difference between time-series and cross-sectional momentum is the time-varying net-long exposure - in a long-only book, the cash gate - so relative selection adds asset-picking but zero crash protection (alone it retains full bear-market drawdowns), and at N=10 with five correlated risk assets the rank itself is mostly specification noise; the evidence supports relative selection only as a concentration overlay *inside* the absolute gate, with the 2023 hope (rotate into the one trending asset) weakly supported and confounded by spec luck.

The aegis champion is an absolute-trend book: each of the ten ETFs is held long when its own 252-day trend is positive and not otherwise. Its known chop cost has a precise anatomy - in the 2023 held-out year nine of ten tickers whipsawed while one (SPY) trended, and the per-asset gate paid the unwind on all nine for a -0.36 split Sharpe even after the breadth throttle ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T193500856144Z_etf_aegis_v10_breadth|v10]]). [[trend-following-in-whipsaw-regimes]] frames that bleed as the structural premium paid for crash convexity, and our v5/v6 kills confirmed that neither filtering nor speed diversification removes it. The intuition this article tests: relative momentum ranks assets against each other and concentrates in what *is* trending, so a top-k overlay should have spent 2023 in SPY instead of paying nine unwinds. The literature turns out to answer a sharper question first - what relative selection structurally can and cannot do in a long-only defensive book - and that answer constrains every hypothesis worth running.

One structural fact up front: the family already contains a cross-sectional sleeve. The haven rotator kept in [[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T183626383519Z_etf_aegis_v2_haven|v2]] is top-3 relative momentum within the haven basket. The open question is therefore not "should we try relative momentum" but whether relative selection earns a place on the *risk* side of the book, and whether it can substitute for - rather than sit inside - the absolute gate.

## One difference, not two signals

Time-series momentum entered the canon as its own anomaly: Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen found the sign of an asset's own past 12-month return predicts its next month across 58 futures contracts, with a diversified portfolio Sharpe above 1 and the claim that time-series momentum largely subsumes the cross-sectional kind (regressing one on the other left a 76 basis-point monthly alpha for time-series and none the other way).[^mop] Two later papers dismantled the "two signals" reading from different directions, and both survive scrutiny better than the original claim.

Goyal and Jegadeesh show the two strategies differ by exactly one term: cross-sectional momentum is a zero-net-investment rank bet, while time-series momentum mechanically takes on a time-varying net-long position - when most assets have been rising, the book is net long, and the net-long position grows with the lookback (from $0.12 per $1 at one month to $0.99 at sixty months in stocks).[^gj] Add that same time-varying market position to the cross-sectional strategy and the difference disappears in stocks; in the futures universe the leverage-matched cross-sectional strategy actually wins (21.55% versus 14.75% a year at the 12-month horizon under the original paper's volatility scaling). Their verdict on the subsumption claim: an artifact of comparing a scaled long-short book against an unscaled one. Huang, Li, Wang and Zhou reach the same destination through inference: asset-by-asset, 47 of 55 contracts show no significant own-history predictability, the pooled regression that carried the original result fails bootstrap critical values, and a strategy that simply goes long assets with positive *unconditional* historical means matches the time-series momentum strategy's performance - the profits are mostly harvested risk premia held through a net-long book, not 12-month timing skill.[^huang] Implementation comparisons land the same way: where time-series momentum beats cross-sectional in head-to-head tests, the mechanism identified is that its exposure varies with the state of the market while the cross-sectional book must keep digging for relative winners in weak markets.[^bird]

The translation to a long-only defensive book is direct and is the article's organizing fact. **The thing that makes time-series momentum special is precisely the cash gate** - the time-varying net exposure that took the book to havens and cash through 2022 and that whipsawed through 2023. Relative selection contributes the other component: cross-sectional asset-picking with no view on overall exposure. They are complements answering different questions (how much risk; which assets), not substitutes, and a "switch from absolute to relative" framing is a category error the rest of the evidence keeps confirming.

## Relative selection alone does not protect

The known failure of pure relative momentum - it stays long the least-bad asset in a bear - is quantified by its own best advocates, which makes the numbers hard to dismiss. Antonacci's module tables show relative momentum between US and international equities (1974-2011) earning 13.46% a year with a **-54.56% maximum drawdown - deeper than buying and holding the US index (-50.65%)**; adding the absolute filter (route to T-bills when the winner's own 12-month excess return is negative) lifts the return to 15.79% and cuts the drawdown to -23.01%.[^antonacci] All of the crash protection in dual momentum comes from the absolute leg; his own phrasing is that absolute momentum "does far more to lessen volatility and drawdown." Faber's relative-strength studies say the same thing twice over: top-k sector rotation since the 1920s beat buy-and-hold by 300-600 basis points a year while retaining **70-80% maximum drawdowns**, fixable only by an absolute overlay (a 10-month moving-average cash fallback) that roughly halves them;[^faber] and on a five-asset-class menu (1973-2009), top-3 relative selection alone drew down -39.84% with a -22.26% worst year, against -12.95% and -3.49% once the trend filter was added. Two independent practitioner datasets, both COI-flagged toward momentum, agreeing that relative selection is a return-adder and the absolute gate is the tail-cutter.

For our book the consequence is a pre-commitment: any relative overlay must live *inside* the absolute gate. A relative-only book is structurally always-invested; it would have re-imported the 2022 exposure the family exists to avoid. That claim is itself cheap to falsify on our splits, and a hypothesis below pre-registers it as a negative control.

## Dual momentum, graded honestly

Dual momentum - relative selection plus the absolute filter - is the canonical hybrid and the obvious anchor, so its evidence quality matters. The grade is mixed. The backtest is strong but practitioner-grade: index-level, pre-cost, monthly, with the 12-month lookback that happens to be the best-performing specification in-sample;[^svrn] the author sells books and a newsletter on the result, a direct conflict of interest. The published numbers - 17.43% a year with a -22.7% maximum drawdown for GEM over 1974-2013, 15.8% versus 11.4% for the S&P 500 with a third of its drawdown on the 1950-2018 extension - are replicable (an independent re-implementation got 15.2%) but carry three documented liabilities.[^gem] [^craftsman]

First, specification fragility. Changing the lookback from 10 to 9 months flips GEM's 2010 return from +12.2% to -9.31% - a 2,151 basis-point spread from one parameter notch on a three-asset menu - and the 10-year cumulative gap between adjacent specs ran to 103 percentage points.[^hoffstein] A 1,226-specification replication with block bootstrap could not reject equality between the published spec and the alternatives (the original beat them by 0.8% a year, ahead in only 61% of bootstrap draws), and the published spec's in-sample edge converges to the cross-spec median out-of-sample.[^craftsman] Antonacci's rebuttal concedes the quantitative point - his own comparison shows a lookback ensemble matching GEM's Sharpe and drawdown - and defends the single spec on priors and trading costs.[^whither]

Second, post-publication decay at the predicted rate. The base rate for published predictors is a 26% out-of-sample and 58% post-publication return haircut.[^mclean] GEM's tracked post-publication record (2014-2021): 5.89% a year at *higher* volatility than the backtest, with a **-33.7% maximum drawdown - eleven points deeper than anything in the 40-year backtest** (single independent tracker for the exact figures, but a second tracker independently logs the same -33.7% drawdown event).[^postpub] Whatever the true edge, the realized hybrid did not protect the way its backtest advertised.

Third, the defensive asset matters as much as the signal. In 2022 the tactical-allocation universe as a whole did its job on the equity leg (aggregate drawdown -12.1% versus -23.9% for the S&P 500) but split sharply on what it rotated *into*: strategies with rate-sensitive defensive assets drew down -17.3% versus -8.0% for those falling back to bills.[^as2022] GEM's bond fallback is exactly the rate-sensitive kind, and its post-publication drawdown includes the 2022 bond bear. Our family already learned this lesson from the haven side - the union basket exists because TLT alone fails 2022 - so the hybrid's fallback design imports a problem we have already solved structurally.

## What transition years actually pay

The 2023 hope needs splitting into a mechanism that is true and a payoff that is not well-evidenced. The true part: cross-sectional momentum's catastrophic episodes are a *short-leg* phenomenon. In the canonical momentum crashes, past losers rip during post-bear rebounds - the loser decile returned +232% in mid-1932 and +163% in March-May 2009 while past winners returned +32% and +8% - and the strategy that dies is the one short those losers.[^dm] A long-only top-k book holds no short leg, so it cannot crash that way; risk-managed momentum work confirms the tail is concentrated there.[^barroso] [^verdad] And because a relative-only book is always invested, it re-enters rebounds mechanically while the absolute gate waits for trend confirmation.

The not-well-evidenced part is that this re-entry advantage cashes out as transition-year protection. Daniel and Moskowitz's own data show the *long* winners leg underperformed the market for four years from the 2009 bottom - relative selection holds the defensive winners of the prior bear into the rebound, which is a lag, not a hedge.[^dm] In 2023 itself, trend followers had their year of the whipsaw (SG Trend Index -4.16%, BTOP50 about -2%),[^ttu] but the tactical-allocation universe - much of it momentum-based and always partially invested - was also merely "middling," with both momentum and trend flagged as strugglers; relative selection did not demonstrably harvest the SPY trend across tracked strategies.[^as2023] The cleanest live fund datapoint, a top-k global momentum rotation with a trend filter, returned +8.23% in 2023 against +26% for SPY (single source).[^gmom] Tactical funds in aggregate entered 2023 at roughly 40% equity with 29% cash and missed the recovery.[^morningstar] And the counter-cases cut directly against the hope: 2010 is the year a one-notch lookback change swung GEM by 2,151 basis points, and 2015-16 whipsawed asset-class rotation systems wholesale - in trendless, rotating markets the *ranks themselves* churn.[^hoffstein] The honest summary: relative selection changes the shape of transition-year pain from "pay every unwind" to "hold the wrong winner and lag," and which hurts less in any given year is substantially spec luck.

## What survives at N=10

Cross-sectional momentum at the asset-class level is real but conditional on the cross-section having something to say. In the broadest peer-reviewed test, momentum rank spreads earn a 0.73 Sharpe across 18 country equity indices and 0.63 across 27 commodities - but 0.34 across 10 currencies and **0.06 across 10 government bond markets**, statistically zero.[^amp] A multi-asset rotation study at N=12 found large and robust top-minus-bottom-quartile spreads (about 12% a year, 1986-2007, momentum plus value),[^blitz] so small-N is not disqualifying by itself. The discriminating variable is internal homogeneity: bond markets are a correlated block whose ranks carry no information, country indices are genuinely heterogeneous. Our universe is five havens with real cross-sectional dispersion (the v2/v17 rotation evidence) plus **five risk assets at high mutual correlation - much closer to the bond case**. Breadth arithmetic says the same: the fundamental law scales skill by the root of *independent* bets, and the defense of small-N tactical allocation explicitly relies on the assets being uncorrelated - five equity-linked ETFs at 0.8 pairwise correlation contribute roughly one to two effective bets, so a rank among them is mostly noise around a single common factor.[^resolvealpha] The portfolio-level momentum that does exist in correlated blocks runs through cross-serial covariance structure rather than own-trend - a fragile, regime-dependent mechanism, not a robust signal.[^lewellen]

The implementation costs at small N are quantified and material. Monthly top-1-to-3 rotation on ten assets turns over 281-378% a year, cuttable below 100% with a rank band (buy top 3, sell on dropping out of the top 5).[^faber] Naive equal weight over momentum winners at N=10 ran about 2.5x the volatility and over twice the drawdown of covariance-aware weighting of the same winners.[^aaa] And concentrated small-N rotation maximizes rebalance-timing luck - identical two-asset tactical strategies differing only in trade day diverged 150 basis points a year over 55 years, with single-year dispersion in the hundreds.[^timingluck] None of these kill the idea; all of them say a top-k overlay must be banded, risk-weighted, and judged against spec-luck noise floors rather than a single backtest path.

One external echo is worth flagging because it lands on the family's one confirmed chop lever: the small-universe systems that report the strongest crash protection (PAA, VAA) get it from *breadth* - the count of assets with negative momentum drives the cash fraction, replacing per-asset gates entirely.[^paa] [^vaa] That is structurally our breadth throttle, confirmed twice on our data ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T211807448501Z_etf_aegis_v16a_breadth_extended|v16]]). Their papers are heavily in-sample-optimized and COI-flagged, but the convergence - independent practitioners finding that universe-level breadth, not per-asset filtering, is the robust small-N defense - raises confidence that the throttle is the right abstraction, and suggests the relative-selection question should be tested *with* the throttle in place, not as an alternative to it.

## Limitations

- The 2023-specific claim rests on category-level and single-fund evidence; no public per-strategy print for a pure always-invested top-k on a 10-asset menu was found. Our own runs will generate the missing number, which is the point of hypothesis 3.
- GEM's exact post-publication figures come from one independent tracker (a second corroborates the drawdown); Antonacci does not publish a comparable net series. Treat 5.89%/-33.7% as approximately right, not exact.
- Nearly all cited evidence is monthly-cadence on index or large-ETF data; our book trades daily with a turnover gate. Monthly whipsaw and daily whipsaw are different animals, and the spec-luck findings (rebalance-day dispersion) say cadence itself is a loaded parameter.
- The TSMOM critiques (Goyal-Jegadeesh, Huang et al.) establish that time-series momentum is mostly net-long timing plus risk premia - they do not say the timing is worthless, and our own 2022 evidence says the gate earns its keep. The article's claim is about the *division of labor*, not about trend being dead.
- The breadth-math argument that within-risk-block ranks are noise is our inference from the correlation structure plus the bond-market null result; no source tests rank informativeness as a function of within-universe correlation directly. Hypothesis 3 is the direct local test.

Promotion blocked on the spec-luck floor (h4). Design note: the rank ran over the full core (havens included), so part of the gain is haven-tilting.*
- [x] **Relative-only negative control (pre-registered to fail)**: the same top-k selection with the absolute gate removed (always invested in the top-k risk assets). Expectation from Antonacci's and Faber's own tables: 2022 collapses. If it instead survives 2022 within 0.3 of the champion, the structural claim of this article is wrong on our data and the division-of-labor framing needs revisiting. *2026-06-11: PREDICTION FAILED AS WRITTEN, with a confound ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084826791853Z_etf_aegis_v27_relonly|v27]]): 2022 Sharpe survived (+0.73 vs champion +0.77) because our ungated rank ran over the FULL universe and rotated into the trending inflation havens - the rank substituted for the gate on a haven-containing universe. The drawdown did not survive (16.8% vs 6.4%), and the book is dead overall (composite -0.126). The division-of-labor claim holds for drawdowns and composites; on universes with a dispersive haven block, cross-sectional rank carries real slow-bear defense. The pure-risk-universe version of the claim remains untested here.*
- [x] **Rank-information control at N=5**: top-k selection among trend-positive risk assets versus equal weight over the same trend-positive set, identical elsewhere. Expectation from the breadth math and the bond-market null: indistinguishable (within 0.05 composite). If confirmed, the within-block rank carries no information on our data and the relative-momentum line closes cheaply; if refuted, hypothesis 1 gets priority. *2026-06-11: REFUTED in its null form ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084802915274Z_etf_aegis_v26_corek|v26]]): composite moves only 0.022 but per-split swings reach 1.49 (2019) - the rank is a large, systematic stress/calm trade, not noise. Caveat: the test ran on the full core (risk + havens), so it does not isolate the within-risk-block rank; the bond-market-null analogy for the pure risk block is still uncontradicted.*
- [ ] **Spec-luck floor before believing any winner**: run the rank lookback at {63, 126, 252} days and read the dispersion of the 2023 split across specs against the ensemble-of-ranks variant. This is selection-spec diversification, mechanically distinct from the graveyard-dead two-speed *signal blend* (v6 blended fast and slow TSMOM scores for the gate; here the gate stays single-speed and only the allocation rank is ensembled). If cross-spec dispersion exceeds the champion-to-candidate gap, no single-spec relative result should be trusted or promoted. *2026-06-11: NOW REQUIRED - v26's keep is blocked on this.
