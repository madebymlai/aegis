# Always-On Convexity from Listed Instruments

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> The UUP floor's lesson generalizes - in fast crashes, insurance that needs no trigger beats anything that needs detection - but among listed instruments passing our data contract, convexity per unit of calm-year bleed differs by an order of magnitude and is regime-specific: short-term VIX ETPs are the only instruments that print large gains at daily-bar scale in every fast crash including intraday-delivered 2018 (at a 50-77%/yr calm-year decay that is an equilibrium risk premium, not an inefficiency), put-ladder and anti-beta funds pay more slowly and cheaply, duration and managed futures fail the 2018 channel outright - so the run question is an insurance price war: which fixed always-on weight buys the most 2018 split per unit of composite cost, against the priced UUP floor as the incumbent.

The campaign's fast-crash hole has resisted every detection-based fix: fast-vol throttles, the overnight-gap trigger and the in-bar speed claim are all graveyard-dead because coincident signals arrive post-shock ([[detecting-fast-crashes-from-price]], [[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T205352247752Z_etf_aegis_v11_yzthrottle|v11]]-v15). The one survivor is insurance: an always-on 30% UUP floor halves the 2018 blowup at about 9% composite cost on the standard window ([[the-dollar-as-dual-regime-haven]], [[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T210311580510Z_etf_aegis_v14_floor_standard|v14]]). This article generalizes that survivor. If a permanent, trigger-free position is the only mechanism that works, the design question becomes which listed instrument delivers the most fast-crash payoff per unit of calm-year bleed - and the champion already owns 2022-style grinds, so the marginal instrument only has to own 2018.

The framing from [[divergent-vs-defensive-crash-resistance]] carries over: these are *defensive* convexity instruments (they pay because of what they hold, not because they adapt), and the relevant taxonomy is not "hedge vs no hedge" but which crash *shape* each instrument is short. Two axes organize everything below: speed (spike versus grind) and trigger (growth shock versus rate shock). 2018 was fast and rate-triggered - the hardest cell, and the one our book needs.

## The data-contract gate

The request's both-sides rule - yfinance daily history through 2017 and Interactive Brokers availability - is empirical, so it was checked directly (yfinance `period="max"`, June 2026). The popular managed-futures ETFs fail before any performance question: DBMF starts 2019-05, KMLM 2020-12, CTA 2022-03. Today's VXX also fails: the original note matured in January 2019 and the current Series B history starts 2018-01-25, missing Feb-2018 - and VXX is an unsecured Barclays note that demonstrated its structural hazard in March 2022, when Barclays over-issued $15.2bn beyond its shelf registration, suspended creations, and the note traded up to ~15% away from indicative value for months.[^vxxhalt] ETNs are out on tradability grounds alone.

What passes: VIXY (2011-01), VIXM (2011-01), TAIL (2017-04), BTAL (2011-09), EDV (2008-01), ZROZ (2009-11), WTMF (2011-01), plus the min-vol pair USMV/SPLV (2011). All are plain US-listed ETFs carried by IB; the VIX products additionally require IB's Complex or Leveraged Exchange-Traded Products permission (an onboarding step, not a blocker),[^ibclp] and an EEA-retail account cannot buy any US ETF without PRIIPs workarounds - an account-class dependency to resolve before live deployment. One practical artifact: VIXY has done six reverse splits (cumulative factor ~1/8000); adjusted feeds handle them, raw feeds show 4-5x discontinuities.[^splits]

## The ledger: what each survivor paid and bled

Three readings carry the article.

**Only the VIX ETPs print Feb-2018 at calendar granularity.** The put-ladder and anti-beta funds both netted ~0 for the month - their early-February gains round-tripped inside the V - while VIXY finished the month +47.9% because the *vol level* stayed elevated after the spike. The same asymmetry holds for the year: 2018 had two fast shocks and VIXY finished +66.8% while every other candidate was single-digit or negative. This is the in-window confirmation of a mechanism the literature states directly: a short-term VIX futures position is convex in the *speed* of the selloff, not its depth.[^erakerwu] [^whaley2013] One measurement caveat for the runs: the Feb-5-2018 ETP close (+34%) understates what the index delivered at the 4:15pm futures settlement (+97%); a daily-bar backtest sees the rest on the next bar.[^harwood] [^amf] The same episode's short-vol blowup (XIV losing >90% in a day) is the peer-reviewed evidence that the spike was real and intraday-delivered - exactly the channel the gap trigger missed in v15.[^augustin]

**The bleed is a priced risk premium, not a fixable inefficiency.** The constant-maturity 1-month VIX futures position loses about 30% per year in equilibrium - compensation paid to volatility sellers - and the ETPs tracking it have realized calm-year decays of -65% to -77% (own-calc 2012-2023, consistent with the published since-inception average of roughly -48%/yr).[^erakerwu] [^whaley2013] [^stockan] The VIX term structure sits in contango roughly 80% of days (more when VIX is low), which is the roll engine of the decay.[^simon] Expect no mean reversion in the cost: whatever sleeve weight the runs choose is a permanent tax paid for episode frequency.

**2022 is the failure regime for spike instruments and the payday for the grind instruments.** In 2022 the VIX never closed above 36.45 against a historical average of ~37 *for comparable drawdowns* - the grind starved the ETPs (VIXY -25.0%) and the put ladder (TAIL -13.15% official) while anti-beta (BTAL +20.90% official) and trend (SG Trend +27.3%) banked it.[^lavaca] [^tailsec] [^btalsec] [^hedgeweek] For our book this asymmetry is acceptable by construction - the champion's 2022 held-out is +0.84 - but it bounds the sizing: the sleeve must stay small enough that a grind year's bleed does not undo the champion's own grind-year edge.

## The candidates, graded

**VIXY (short-term VIX futures, the maximum-convexity end).** Structure: 1st/2nd-month VIX futures rolled daily to ~30-day maturity, an ETF holding futures (no issuer credit risk), 0.85% fee.[^proshares] Crash prints are the largest of any candidate by an order of magnitude (table above; Feb 19-Mar 18 2020: +405%). Decay is the largest too. The best pre-computed estimate of the exact mechanism we would run comes from Szado: adding a 5% one-month VIX futures sleeve to a 60/40 portfolio over 2006-2017 cut annualized return from 7.5% to ~4% and Sharpe from 0.65 to 0.54, in exchange for max drawdown improving from -34.8% to -27%; in 2008 alone the same sleeve turned -20.9% into -13.4%.[^szado2020] That study is Cboe-funded (COI) but honest about the drag, and its shape matches our v13/v14 result for the UUP floor: insurance helps the tail year and costs the composite. The counter-literature finds short-term VIX ETPs outright ineffective as buy-and-hold hedges because the roll cost dominates;[^deng] Whaley's verdict - "virtually guaranteed to lose money through time" - is the standing health warning.[^whaley2013]

**VIXM (mid-term VIX futures, the cost-matched middle).** Same structure at the 4th-7th month, roughly one-third the crash payoff at one-third-to-two-thirds the decay (own-calc), with two distinguishing facts: it *kept* its 2020 spike (calendar 2020 +72% vs VIXY's +10.5% - short-term decay gave the spike back within the year) and it was approximately free in 2022 (-0.7%). Whaley and the hedging-effectiveness literature both explicitly prefer mid-term over short-term for standing positions.[^whaley2013] [^deng]

**TAIL (put ladder on Treasury collateral).** ~1%/month budget of 0-30% OTM SPX puts laddered over months, ~90% in intermediate Treasuries, 0.59% fee.[^tailsec] It paid in every fast crash at quarter scale (Q4-2018 +14.56%, Q1-2020 +23.49%, both official "best quarter" prints) and bleeds about -8%/yr since inception (official since-inception -8.35%/yr through 2024). Its 2022 (-13.15%) is the instructive failure: both legs lost at once - the ~7.5-year-duration collateral fell with rates while the slow grind never spiked vol enough to pay the puts.[^tailsec] [^marketsent] As a single ticker it bundles put convexity with a duration bet we did not order; cheaper per year than VIXY, far less convex, and actively wrong in rate-shock grinds.

**BTAL (anti-beta market-neutral).** Long ~200 low-beta / short ~200 high-beta US stocks, sector-dollar-neutral, 1.40% net (1.65% gross) fee.[^agffact] The only candidate positive in *both* 2018 (+15.05% official) and 2022 (+20.90% official),[^btalsec] which makes it the only both-tails instrument in the set - but the payoff accrues slowly (Feb-2018 month ~0; it is a negative-beta carry position, not an option), the bleed is lumpy and regime-dependent (2023 -14.85%, and the trailing year to May-2026 is -38.7% in a high-beta melt-up),[^agffact] and the fund switched from passive index-tracking to an active rules-based strategy in February 2022, making pre-2022 history partly non-representative.[^btalsec] One honesty flag from our own graveyard: a BTAL floor re-imports short exposure through a fund wrapper after [[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T183709952487Z_etf_aegis_v3_dual|v3]] killed in-book shorts. The instrument is structurally different (sector-neutral beta spread, institutional shorting infrastructure, the low-beta anomaly as funding), so it is not a re-proposal of the dead idea - but the same whipsaw-plus-carry failure mode applies and the hypothesis below pre-registers it.

**EDV/ZROZ (long-duration zero-coupon Treasuries): rejected on regime.** They are ~1.5-1.8x duration-levered TLT - which the book already holds - and the discriminating variable for duration is the crash *trigger*, not its speed: bond betas to stocks flipped sign around 2001 and flip back when inflation dominates growth risk.[^cpv] [^aqrsbc] In Feb-2018, a rate-triggered fast crash, EDV fell *more than SPY* (-4.3% vs -3.6%); in 2022 it lost -39.2%. Its showcase windows (Q4-2018 +5.3%, Q1-2020 +29%) are growth-scare windows TLT already covers at lower amplitude, and even the showcase has an operational failure mode - the March-2020 dash for cash sold Treasuries hard at the equity low until the Fed intervened.[^duffie] Long-run cost of carry while waiting: EDV has returned ~2.6%/yr since 2007 with equity-scale volatility.[^vanguard] As 2018 insurance, duration is the wrong instrument *because* 2018 was a rate-shock year - the same reason every haven except the dollar failed it ([[crash-resistance-in-our-etf-universe]]).

**Managed futures: a double fail.** Data first - the credible vehicles (DBMF, KMLM, CTA) all post-date 2018. Mechanism second, and decisively: trend followers entered Feb-2018 long equities off the 2017 melt-up, and the SG CTA Index dropped more than 6% in that month, its worst since November 2001;[^rcm] the SG Trend Index finished 2018 at -8.1%, then its worst year on record.[^barclayhedge] CTA convexity is real but lives at monthly-and-slower horizons - measured at daily or weekly frequency it is flat to negative, because the system needs weeks to reposition ([[trend-following-in-whipsaw-regimes]] documents the same latency from the whipsaw side).[^sepp] Managed futures are *divergent* sustained-bear insurance (2022: SG Trend +27.3%[^hedgeweek]) - the year our champion already owns - and structurally cannot own 2018. WTMF, the one ETF with passing history, confirms the verdict live: roughly zero CAGR since 2011, negative in Feb-2018, Q4-2018 *and* 2022 (-6.5%, in the strategy's banner year), with a June-2021 redesign that breaks its own history.[^wtmf]

**Min-vol (USMV/SPLV) and quality: attenuation, not convexity.** Low-vol funds cushion grinds (SPLV 2022 -4.81%, Q4-2018 -5.26% official) but provided zero protection in the 2020 gap crash - both fell as much as the market or more (-33% to -36% across measurement bases, against the S&P's -33.8%) - because they stay fully long.[^splvsec] [^salt] QUAL has beta ~0.97 and lost more than the index in 2022. Fine funds; not insurance; excluded from the hypotheses.

## Sizing: when does a bleeding hedge raise compound growth

Both camps in the tail-hedging war accept the same identity - geometric growth ≈ arithmetic mean minus half the variance - and therefore both accept, in principle, that an instrument with negative standalone return can raise portfolio compound growth if it cuts variance (and the deepest drawdowns) enough.[^voltax] [^peters] AQR's own rebuttal concedes the channel in a footnote while disputing that real option pricing leaves any of it on the table.[^aqr24] For a small weight w added to portfolio P, the marginal condition follows from the identity: w·(mu_H - mu_P) + w·(sigma_P^2 - rho·sigma_H·sigma_P) > 0 - a very negative hedge mean can be carried by a sufficiently negative correlation and a sufficiently volatile hedge. No source states this two-asset form canonically (we derive it; the formal backbone is the peer-reviewed ergodicity result that expectation-reducing insurance can raise time-average growth[^peters]), and the rebalancing mechanism that harvests it is classical.[^shannon]

The empirical fight is then about magnitudes, and it is openly adversarial. The anti side: a put-protected S&P portfolio over 1986-2016 was matched on compound return by simply holding 36.5% equities and 63.5% cash, with uniformly better drawdowns at horizons beyond a few months (put overlay alpha about -1.8%/yr);[^israelov] every passive put variant tested over 1985-2020 bled in every decade despite 1987, 2008 and 2020;[^aqrtrend] the live tail-fund peer index has compounded at roughly -2 to -3%/yr since 2008, and a 95/5 SPX/tail-index mix added no terminal wealth over 2007-2020.[^aqrtrend] [^rzep] Buying financial catastrophe insurance is, on the broad record, poorly rewarded.[^ilmanen2012] [^litterman] The behavioral cost compounds the financial one: CalPERS terminated its tail-hedge program in October 2019, weeks before it would have paid out over $1bn - always-on insurance is hardest to hold exactly when it is about to pay, which is an argument for mechanical floors over discretionary ones, not for skipping the insurance.[^calpers] The pro side rests on Universa's fund-supplied numbers (a 3.33% tail sleeve claimed to lift portfolio CAGR; March 2020 reported as +3,612% *on premium spent* - about +12.8 points at portfolio level on the honest denominator) and is severely conflicted, but survived an adversarial audit-era review with the asterisk that the convexity is real and the denominator is marketing.[^universa] [^brown] The reconciliation the primary texts support: the anti-camp evidence is almost entirely *passive rolled puts held to expiry*; the pro-camp's mechanism requires deep convexity plus *monetization* - selling the hedge into the spike and rebalancing into risk at the trough. Held-to-expiry round-trips destroy even 70x payoffs.[^bhansali]

That reconciliation matters for us because **a fixed-weight, continuously rebalanced floor is itself an automatic monetization rule**: as the hedge spikes and the book falls, maintaining w forces selling the hedge high and buying risk low - mechanically the Bhansali prescription without discretion (our inference, not a sourced claim). The v13 UUP floor already behaves this way. The candidate arithmetic, at the crudest level (own synthesis, to be priced properly by the runs): a 5% VIXY sleeve costs roughly 3.3-3.9 points/yr in calm years and would have contributed roughly +3.3 points in 2018 (+3.8 in Q4 alone, against the champion's -11.7% year) and +10 points in Q1-2020; Szado's 12-year net for the same mechanism was about -3.5 points/yr of return for 8 points of max-drawdown relief.[^szado2020] The incumbent to beat is priced: the UUP floor buys +0.46 of 2018 split Sharpe for ~9% composite ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-10#20260610T210311580510Z_etf_aegis_v14_floor_standard|v14]]). Whether any VIX-ETP weight beats that exchange rate is exactly what the pre-registered runs decide.

## Limitations

- The window table is own-calc from yfinance adjusted closes; official NAV figures, where they exist, agree within market-price noise, but the Q4-2018 and VIXM episode numbers have no independent published source. The Feb-5-2018 settlement-time artifact means daily-close backtests slightly understate (and lag by one bar) the worst-day ETP move.[^harwood]
- VIX ETP history starts 2011, so the sample contains exactly two fast-crash episodes (2018, 2020). Everything inferred about payoff frequency rests on n=2; the few-windows statistics and bootstrap cautions of [[measuring-crisis-alpha]] apply in full, and v20/v21's lesson - this grid's regularization may mute both the benefit and the harm - cuts both ways.
- The decay numbers span regimes: VIXY's calm-year decay ranged from -27% (2024) to -77% (2012). A sleeve sized on the average bleeds more in heavy-contango years. Conversely the equilibrium-premium reading means no part of the cost should be expected to improve.[^erakerwu]
- COI density in this literature is extreme on both sides: Cboe funds the pro-VIX study, AQR sells the competing alternatives, Universa and LongTail Alpha sell the hedges, issuers wrote the fund documents. The article leans on regulator/peer-reviewed anchors (AMF, FAJ, JFE, JPE) for every load-bearing mechanism claim, but most exact payoff figures originate with interested parties or our own computation.
- BTAL's strategy changed in February 2022; its both-tails record straddles two different funds in one ticker. TAIL and BTAL also carry single-manager/single-fund idiosyncratic risk that index-tracked instruments do not.
- IB availability holds at the category level (all candidates are US-listed ETFs, which IB carries wholesale), but per-ticker contract pages were not individually verifiable at sweep time; VIX products need the complex-product permission and EEA-retail accounts face the PRIIPs block on US ETFs - live-deployment dependencies outside backtest scope, to be settled with a TWS paper-account check before any live run.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **VIXY floor (the maximum-convexity candidate)**: final book = (1-w)·champion + w·VIXY, w in {0.02, 0.05}, continuously rebalanced (the fixed weight is the monetization rule). On the extended 2016-start window, mirroring v13's gates: keep if the 2018 split Sharpe improves by >= +0.3 over the in-run w=0 control AND composite >= control - 0.05 AND neither calm split (2019, 2021) drops more than 0.3. Kill if no w clears the 2018 gate or every improvement costs more composite than the UUP floor's exchange rate (+0.46 of 2018 per ~0.11 composite); the head-to-head against the v13c floored variant is the decision output, not just the gate. *2026-06-11: KILLED ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084627805837Z_etf_aegis_v22_vixyfloor|v22]]). w=0.05 clears the 2018 gate (+0.38) but composite goes 0.151 -> -0.001 and both calm splits collapse (2019 -1.51, 2021 -0.43); w=0.02 buys +0.07. Strictly dominated by the UUP floor (-1.09 / 0.255).*
- [x] **VIXM floor (the cost-matched variant)**: same design and gates with w possibly larger (decay is one-third to two-thirds of VIXY's). Expectation: smaller 2018 dent, much smaller calm-year drag, ~flat 2022; if VIXM passes gates VIXY fails, the convexity-per-bleed frontier bends toward mid-term and the sleeve should be built there. *2026-06-11: KILLED ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084651789718Z_etf_aegis_v23_vixmfloor|v23]]). Cost asymmetry confirmed (composite 0.129-0.146 vs VIXY's collapse) but best 2018 improvement +0.07 - convexity shrinks faster than cost; the frontier flattens rather than bending. Only split helped: 2020 (+0.71 -> +1.18), the multi-week crash, consistent with the speed taxonomy.*
- [ ] **TAIL floor (bundled puts-plus-duration)**: same gates, plus a pre-registered 2022 watch: TAIL's -13% grind-year print must not degrade the 2022 split by more than 0.1 (the champion's purpose year). Expectation: passes Q4-2018/2020 channels at lower cost than VIXY but fails to move Feb-2018 at daily granularity (its month netted to zero) - if so, that localizes the put ladder's value to multi-week crashes only. *2026-06-11: NOT TESTABLE on our window - TAIL history starts 2017-04 (verified via vbt YFData pull), so with the 252-day warmup the book cannot emit weights before mid-2018 and the 2017/2018 splits are uncovered. Not killed; would need a shorter-warmup design to test.*
- [x] **BTAL floor (the both-tails candidate, graveyard-adjacent)**: same gates. Pre-registered flags: this re-imports short-beta exposure through a fund wrapper after v3 killed in-book shorts - the kill rationale (whipsaw plus carry in calm years) predicts the 2019/2021/2023 splits are where it dies; and the February-2022 strategy change means any pre/post-2022 behavior difference must be inspected before trusting the 2022 print. If it passes everything, it is the only single-instrument answer to both tails and deserves priority over the VIX pair. *2026-06-11: KILLED as insurance ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084715455371Z_etf_aegis_v24_btalfloor|v24]]) - 2018 +0.10 vs the +0.3 gate, dominated by the UUP floor on both axes. Two surprises recorded: the predicted calm-year death did NOT happen (all calm splits within 0.11 of control), and w=0.05 mildly lifted composite (0.151 -> 0.181), 2018 AND 2022 together - a both-tails diversifier, not insurance, with the 2022 leg resting on post-redesign history.*
- [x] **Negative control - EDV floor (pre-registered to fail)**: same design. Expectation from the regime map: no improvement on Feb-2018 (duration fell with stocks in the rate-triggered crash), material damage to the 2022 split, modest help in Q4-2018/2020 that TLT already provides. If EDV *passes* the 2018 gate, the trigger-axis (growth vs rate) reading of this article is wrong on our data and the duration question reopens. *2026-06-11: CONFIRMED - failed exactly as predicted ([[runs/aegis/2026-06-11#20260611T084739213055Z_etf_aegis_v25_edvfloor|v25]]): 2018 +0.01 at both weights, 2022 damage monotone in the floor (-0.08/-0.20). The trigger-axis reading holds.*
