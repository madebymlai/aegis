# Short-Horizon Reversal in Small Cross-Sections

> [!abstract] One-line takeaway
> Short-horizon reversal is a second concave mechanism - paid to provide liquidity, earning most when fear is highest and losing in the same unwinds that break carry - so it does not diversify the carry sleeve, it doubles it. And its edge sits below the transaction-cost line for any naive implementation; only a conditioned, low-turnover version survives, and the breadth to rank a cross-section thins out fast at a dozen-odd instruments. It is a candidate concave sleeve with two hard gates to clear, not a free second income stream.

## The other way to be short gamma

[[carry-as-the-short-gamma-income-pole|Carry]] is one route to the floor's concave pole; short-horizon reversal is the other mechanism the roster names for that slot.[[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] Buying recent losers and selling recent winners over days to a few weeks has earned a premium since it was first documented: weekly winners and losers reverse the following week, and a monthly contrarian strategy long past losers and short past winners earned around 2.5% a month gross over 1934-1987.[^lehmann][^jegadeesh] The question for the floor is not whether reversal exists but whether it is *concave* - whether it belongs on the short-gamma side with carry - and whether it survives the two things that kill it: costs and thin cross-sections.

## Reversal is paid to provide liquidity

The economic content of reversal is liquidity provision. When a wave of order flow pushes a price away from fundamentals, the contrarian who takes the other side is paid to absorb the imbalance, and the price reverts as the pressure clears. Nagel makes the mechanism precise: short-term reversal returns are compensation for supplying liquidity, they are highly time-varying, and they are *predictable by the VIX* - expected returns and conditional Sharpe ratios of reversal strategies spike during financial turmoil, when liquidity suppliers withdraw and the price of immediacy jumps.[^nagel] That is the same crisis-timing signature carry has, and it places reversal firmly on the concave pole: it earns most by selling immediacy-insurance precisely when that insurance is dearest, which is when the market is falling.

This is also why reversal is short gamma rather than long. A liquidity provider is structurally on the wrong side of a trend that keeps going and the right side of an overreaction that snaps back. In a sustained dislocation - the regime the long-gamma trend sleeve is built for - the reverter keeps stepping in front of a move that does not reverse, and loses. The payoff is concave by construction.

## The cost wall

Reversal's defining problem is that its turnover is enormous and its gross edge sits close to the cost line. Using live institutional trade data, short-term reversal has among the smallest break-even capacities of the major anomalies: where value and momentum survive at tens of billions of dollars, reversal's net expected return is small and its break-even fund size an order of magnitude lower - the trading costs eat most of the gross return.[^fim] Daily and weekly reversal profits collapse once bid-ask spreads are charged, because a large part of the raw signal is bid-ask bounce rather than alpha, and the residual concentrates in exactly the illiquid names where it is most expensive to trade.[^acg] The naive strategy is, for practical purposes, unimplementable.

What survives is the *conditioned* version. Restricting reversal to large-cap, liquid names and neutralizing the short-term industry and factor momentum that fights it roughly doubles the risk-adjusted return and lifts it above the cost line; a composite of short-term signals traded with low-turnover buy-and-hold rules retains a meaningful net alpha where the naive single signal does not.[^residual][^stsignals] The lesson for the concave pole is that reversal is a real mechanism with a real edge, but the edge lives entirely in the construction - universe, turnover control, and what you neutralize - not in the raw signal. A sleeve that does not pay that construction tax will ship a backtest that the cost model erases.

## It crashes with carry, not against it

The more serious objection is diversification. A second concave sleeve is only worth holding next to carry if it loses at *different* times; if it loses in the same unwinds, the book has simply doubled its short-gamma position under two labels - the redundancy trap the convexity argument warns against.[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] The evidence says reversal and carry crash together. The August 2007 quant meltdown is the cleanest case: a crowded, levered market-neutral contrarian book lost a large fraction of its capital over three days as funds deleveraged into each other, then rebounded sharply once the forced selling cleared - a pure liquidity-unwind, the same funding-driven mechanism that drives carry crashes.[^kl] Reversal earns by providing liquidity and carry earns by bearing funding risk; both are short the same thing - the sudden withdrawal of liquidity in a deleveraging - so they are negatively skewed *in the same states of the world*. Stacking them deepens the floor's short-gamma tail rather than diversifying it.

## Thin cross-sections

Reversal is a cross-sectional bet: it ranks instruments against each other and trades the spread. That makes it breadth-hungry, and a floor universe of a dozen-odd asset-class instruments is thin on the axis reversal needs. The same breadth arithmetic that constrains relative momentum applies here - skill scales with the square root of the number of *independent* bets, and a handful of correlated risk assets collapse to one or two effective bets, so a rank among them is mostly specification noise.[[relative-vs-absolute-momentum-in-small-universes]] The horizon matters too: the slow, multi-year cross-sectional reversal documented among country-index baskets has failed out of sample even where it looked strong in-sample, while the surviving ETF-level reversal effects are fast (daily) and intra-asset rather than slow cross-sectional rankings.[^richards] A concave sleeve sourced from reversal on a small asset-class book is therefore betting on the fast, conditioned, low-turnover end of the family, not the slow cross-sectional end - and even then on a cross-section that may be too narrow to rank.

## Limitations

The liquidity-provision account of reversal is well-established for individual equities;[^nagel][^acg] its transfer to a small book of asset-class ETFs is an extrapolation. ETFs are baskets, their order-flow microstructure differs from single stocks, and whether the same VIX-predictable liquidity premium appears at the asset-class level is an open empirical question rather than a settled one.

Much of the "conditioned reversal survives costs" evidence comes from a single asset manager that sells short-term-signal strategies, which is a conflict of interest on the favorable conclusion.[^residual][^stsignals] The cost-failure result for naive reversal is the more independently corroborated half.[^fim][^acg]

The crash-correlation claim rests heavily on one episode (August 2007), albeit a well-studied one.[^kl] It is strong evidence that levered reversal and carry share a deleveraging tail, but a single crisis is not a distribution; the within-pole correlation should be measured directly on realized streams before treating reversal as redundant with carry rather than complementary.

## Strategy hypotheses this could seed

- [x] **Reversal is concave on our universe.** A cross-sectional dollar-neutral reversion stream on the floor universe shows the concave *shape* (negative quarterly skew, $\beta_2 \le 0$, negative crisis-conditional return, non-negative bear-regime beta) - it sits on the concave pole. **But shape is not the pole (2026-06-13):** its drift is negative, so it *pays* the premium rather than collecting it, and it fails the income requirement. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13#own-gamma + mirror-gate (diagnostic)]].
- [x] **The cost line is the gate.** Confirmed 2026-06-13: a naive short-horizon reversion sleeve loses net at a 10bps round-trip on our book (Sharpe negative across all lookbacks, more negative for the higher-turnover cross-sectional form), so the gross reversal premium sits below the cost line exactly as the literature warns - turnover control is a build requirement, and on a trend-selected substrate the gross signal is on the wrong side besides. See [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13|2026-06-13]].
- [ ] **Reversal does not diversify carry.** Measure the realized-return correlation and the joint crisis-conditional behavior of a reversal sleeve and a carry sleeve; if they lose together in the same unwinds, the floor's concave slot holds one mechanism, not two, and the second adds redundancy rather than diversification.
- [ ] **Breadth floor.** Test whether cross-sectional reversal ranks carry information at the floor universe's size or are mostly noise, against the independent-bets count, before trusting any single-spec reversal result (the spec-luck discipline from [[relative-vs-absolute-momentum-in-small-universes]]).
