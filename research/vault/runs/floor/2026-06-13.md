# 2026-06-13

## Goal

Run the roster's load-bearing claim, **"Floor pays"** (`aegis-rd-djc`): does a
trend (long-gamma, +skew) + carry (short-gamma, -skew) composite, **sized to ~zero net
skew** and compared **at matched volatility**, beat **either pole alone** on **held-out
crisis-conditional return**, without giving back the calm-market compounding? Both poles
are built and locked - this is the composite test that turns two sleeves into a verified
floor. Theory anchors: [[the-tiered-strategy-roster]] ("Floor: both signs of skew"),
[[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification]] (net portfolio skew is the managed
quantity), [[measuring-crisis-alpha]] (the objective: median compounded sleeve return
across SPY-drawdown windows, bootstrapped to a lower bound; never a single window),
[[verifying-strategy-family-membership]] (the per-family gates the two poles passed).

**Universe decision (resolved, recorded):** the demeter carry champion
(`research/configs/demeter/carry.yaml`) and the atalanta champion
(`research/configs/atalanta/v3_tier1.yaml`) lock on the **identical 20-name Tier 1
book** (TLT GLD UUP PDBC USL DBB DBA FXY TIP BWX EDV EMLC SLV UNG FXE CORN CPER SHY LQD
FXA). So the two poles already live on the same substrate - no cross-substrate question
remains. Each sleeve's daily stream is built on its **own champion config/params**
through the real component pipeline + `simulate_single_book` (faithful to the locked
champions), then the **streams** are blended (there is no cross-strategy allocator in
`aerd`; productionization is a blend strategy component a la `aegis_crisis_blend*`, out
of prototype scope).

## Runs

### floor-pays composite (prototype diagnostic)

Harness `/tmp/floor_pays_prototype.py` (throwaway, research-fin). Pinned-champion
diagnostic (not an `aerd` Candidate Store run, so no timestamped run_id - same shape as
the atalanta breadth and demeter carry gate diagnostics). Pulls the 20-name book + SPY,
builds the two champion streams, finds the skew-neutral carry weight, vol-matches all
three streams, and compares crisis-conditional return composite-vs-poles.

- **Hypothesis**: a skew-neutral, vol-matched trend+carry composite lifts held-out
  crisis-conditional return above BOTH poles alone (the core 2-floor claim and the
  net-skew-budgeting claim), because the +skew trend pole pays in the dislocations where
  the -skew carry pole bleeds, and the carry pole compounds the premium in calm that
  trend gives back - the two ledger entries of one straddle.
- **Keep/kill criteria** (pre-registered, before running):
  1. **PRIMARY (held-out crisis-conditional)**: with {composite, trend-only, carry-only}
     all vol-matched to one annualized vol, the **composite's held-out crisis-conditional
     return strictly exceeds BOTH poles alone**. Crisis-conditional measured two ways
     ([[measuring-crisis-alpha]]): (a) the committed `_crisis_conditional` reducer (mean
     stream return on SPY worst-decile days), and (b) the multi-window form (median
     compounded stream return across SPY peak-to-trough drawdown windows deeper than 10%,
     with a stationary-block-bootstrap lower confidence bound). Floor pays iff the
     composite wins on the held-out measure.
  2. **GUARDRAIL (no calm give-back)**: the crisis gain is not bought by wrecking the
     calm curve - composite **UPI (Ulcer Performance Index) / Calmar >= trend-only's**
     within noise. A composite that only wins crisis-conditional by giving back
     full-sample compounding is not a floor.
  3. **ROBUSTNESS (not one window)**: the win holds on the **median across windows** and
     survives the **bootstrap lower bound**; if it rests on a single episode
     (2018/2020/2022 are the only crises on the 2018-2025 yf window) it is a kill/iterate,
     per the few-windows fragility in [[measuring-crisis-alpha]].
  - **KEEP ("Floor pays" confirmed)** iff PRIMARY holds out-of-sample AND GUARDRAIL holds
    AND ROBUSTNESS holds.
  - **KILL / iterate** iff: the **thin** carry sleeve cannot move the composite (the
    skew-neutral weight drives carry's risk-share so high the composite is essentially
    carry-only, or so low it is essentially trend-only, and crisis-conditional does not
    separate); OR the win rests on one window; OR the composite needs leverage / a calm
    give-back to win.
  - **Record** the skew-neutral carry weight and its risk-share (the THIN-carry finding
    the demeter diary flagged: steady-state carry is ~0.4 Sharpe, mild -0.13 qskew, so
    neutralizing trend's strong +0.77 qskew may demand a large carry weight - how large
    is itself a result).
- **What changed since previous run**: first floor (composite) run; new codename. Inputs
  are the two locked champions unchanged ([[runs/atalanta/2026-06-13]] Tier 1 trend,
  [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13]] carry). No new components; the blend is a return-stream
  composite computed in the harness.
- **Result**: the harness reproduced both locked champion streams faithfully - trend
  Sharpe **+0.25** / qskew **+0.77** / +1.8%/yr, carry Sharpe **+0.48** / qskew **-0.03**
  / +3.4%/yr (matches [[runs/atalanta/2026-06-13]] and [[runs/demeter/2026-06-13]]; the
  carry skew reading is slightly less negative than the diary's -0.13 because the post-252d
  warmup drops the 2018-Q4 selloff). corr(trend, carry) daily **+0.12**.
  - **Crisis-conditional frontier (full-sample, all vol-matched to 8.8% ann vol), two
    measures across carry risk-weight `w`:**

    | w | worst-decile MEAN | multi-window COMPOUNDED median | qskew |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 0.00 (trend) | **+0.163%** | +4.68% | +0.77 |
    | 0.25 | +0.084% | **+5.21%** | +1.51 |
    | 0.50 | -0.033% | +4.96% | +1.75 |
    | 0.75 | -0.144% | +3.70% | +0.92 |
    | 1.00 (carry) | **-0.212%** | +2.30% | -0.01 |

    On the **worst-decile mean** (the committed `_crisis_conditional` reducer, ~150 days)
    crisis-conditional return declines **monotonically** as carry weight rises - the +gamma
    trend pole is the only crisis payer, every unit of carry subtracts (and a conditional
    *mean* is ~linear in `w`, so a convex blend structurally cannot beat the better pole on
    it). On the **multi-window compounded** measure (path-dependent, so it *can* reward a
    blend via a stress-window rebalancing premium) a **light carry overlay does help**:
    w=0.25 = **+5.21%** beats BOTH poles (+4.68% / +2.30%). BUT that beneficial mix sits at
    qskew **+1.51** (far from neutral) - it is "a little carry sharpens trend's stress
    compounding," not a skew-neutral floor.
  - **Skew-neutral weight `w* = 0.99`** (carry = **100%** of composite variance). Because
    the carry pole's realized quarterly skew is **~0** (-0.01), not meaningfully negative,
    neutralizing trend's strong +0.77 skew demands ~all carry, so the "skew-neutral
    composite" **degenerates to carry-only**: crash **-0.211%**, qskew +0.00, Sharpe +0.49
    - statistically identical to carry-only (-0.212% / +0.48).
  - **PRIMARY criterion FAILS.** At the skew-neutral weight the composite's
    crisis-conditional return (-0.211%) is far **below** trend-only's (+0.163%). It does
    not beat both poles - it loses to the pole that supplies the crisis protection.
  - **Held-out** (rolling 504d, 50/50, fit `w*`+vol-scale on selection, measure on
    held-out): composite does **not** beat both poles on median held-out crisis-conditional
    (composite -0.106% vs trend-only **+0.038%** best, carry-only -0.368%). Composite beats
    both in only **2/10** windows - and those two are exactly the windows where the
    selection-fit chose `w*~0` (~all trend). Median held-out `w*` = 0.19 and per-window
    `w*` swings 0.00-1.00: the skew-neutral weight is **ill-conditioned** precisely because
    carry's skew hovers at zero.
  - **Multi-window** (SPY drawdowns deeper than 10%: only **2** episodes 2020 + 2022, so
    fragile per [[measuring-crisis-alpha]]): median compounded return trend **+4.68%** >
    composite +2.33% ~ carry +2.30% - same ordering, trend best.
  - **GUARDRAIL** (composite UPI +0.49 >= trend-only +0.20) passes, but only because the
    composite *is* ~carry-only (the higher-Sharpe income sleeve) - it does not represent a
    balanced floor that keeps trend's crisis protection without giving back compounding.
- **Verdict**: **kill** - "Floor pays" refuted as stated, on this universe and this carry
  pole. The skew-neutral, vol-matched composite does not beat both poles on held-out
  crisis-conditional return; it loses to trend-only. The diagnosis is decisive (it rests on
  the robust monotone full-sample frontier, not the fragile 2-window crisis stat): the test
  embeds **two requirements that pull in opposite directions** - crisis-conditional return
  is maximized at all-trend (`w=0`), skew-neutrality demands all-carry (`w~1`), and they
  cannot both hold. The root cause is that the distribution-yield carry pole's realized
  negative skew is **~0, too weak to anchor a skew budget**, so "net skew ~0" collapses onto
  carry-only instead of a balanced trend+carry mix. This is exactly the caveat the demeter
  carry diary flagged (mild -0.13 skew; commodity-roll/FX concavity unsourced) and the
  [[convexity-as-the-axis-of-strategy-diversification|convexity article]] limitation (carry's
  negative skew is FX/commodity-specific, weak or absent in bond/credit carry) - made
  decisive. The pairing **mechanism** is not refuted in principle; **this** carry pole is too
  weakly concave to realize it through a skew-neutral budget. There is a real but **small and
  fragile residual**: a *light, non-skew-neutral* carry overlay (w~0.25) lifts the
  multi-window compounded stress return above both poles (+5.21% vs +4.68% / +2.30%) - a
  stress-window rebalancing premium - but it rests on only 2 SPY-drawdown episodes (2020,
  2022) so per [[measuring-crisis-alpha]] it is a direction, not a result, and it is *not*
  what "Floor pays" claims (which mandates skew-neutral sizing the carry pole cannot deliver
  at light weight). Re-test only after sourcing a strongly-concave carry pole - both as the
  skew-neutral floor and as the light-overlay sharpener.

### floor-pays re-test with the VRP pole (prototype diagnostic)

Harness `/tmp/floor_pays_vrp.py` (the retained prototype with ONE swap: the concave pole
is now the **variance risk premium** sleeve `demeter.vol_carry` - VIX-term-structure
short-vol signal, short-VXX execution - whose gate qskew is -1.18, instead of the
~symmetric distribution-yield carry that refuted the first run). Same trend champion, same
sizing/vol-match/crisis logic; only the carry stream changed. This is the re-test
`aegis-rd-ypz` gated on a strongly-concave pole.

- **Hypothesis**: with a *materially* negative-skew concave pole, the skew-neutral weight
  becomes a genuine balance (not the all-carry degeneration), and the composite lifts
  held-out crisis-conditional return above both poles - the structural prior is favorable
  because trend is long-variance and VRP is short-variance (a one-axis barbell).
- **Keep/kill criteria**: identical to the run above (PRIMARY held-out crisis-conditional
  beats both poles, on both the worst-decile-mean and multi-window-compounded measures;
  GUARDRAIL no calm give-back; ROBUSTNESS not one window).
- **Result**: the sizing defect is **fixed**, but the verdict splits sharply by which
  crisis measure - and the split is structural, not incidental.
  - **Streams**: trend Sharpe +0.25 / qskew +0.77; **VRP Sharpe +0.60 / qskew -0.49** /
    +16.3%/yr; **corr(trend, VRP) daily +0.01** (essentially orthogonal - the cleanest
    diversification of any pole pairing so far). (VRP qskew reads -0.49 here vs the gate's
    -1.18 because the post-252d-warmup window starts 2019 and **drops Feb-2018
    Volmageddon**, the pole's defining crash - see the caveat below; it is still decisively
    negative vs distribution carry's ~0.)
  - **Skew-neutral weight `w* = 0.40`** (VRP = **30%** of composite variance), net
    quarterly skew -0.001. This is the headline fix: a **genuine 40/60 balance**, not the
    refuted run's degeneration to ~100% carry. A materially-concave pole anchors the skew
    budget, exactly as predicted - the thin-carry blocker is gone.
  - **Crisis-conditional frontier (full-sample, vol-matched to 8.8%):**

    | w (VRP) | worst-decile MEAN | multi-window COMPOUNDED median | qskew |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 0.00 (trend) | **+0.163%** | +4.68% | +0.77 |
    | 0.25 | -0.110% | +6.36% | +0.47 |
    | 0.40 (skew-neutral) | ~ | ~ | -0.00 |
    | 0.50 | -0.474% | **+7.62%** | -0.50 |
    | 0.75 | -0.740% | +7.12% | -1.25 |
    | 1.00 (VRP) | -0.837% | +5.72% | -1.24 |

  - **PRIMARY (worst-decile mean): still FAILS.** Composite at w* crash-conditional
    **-0.323%** < trend-only **+0.163%**; held-out median composite **-0.798%** vs
    trend-only **+0.038%**, composite beats both in **0/10** windows. This is now seen to be
    **structural and near-tautological**: VRP is short-gamma, so it *loses* on the single
    worst SPY days by construction; blending any amount of it into trend can only *dilute*
    trend's positive worst-day return. A conditional *mean* on the worst days cannot be
    raised by adding a pole that is negative there. Only a long-gamma pole protects the
    worst days - that is trend's exclusive job, and no concave pole, however deeply concave,
    can add to it.
  - **But the floor PAYS on the portfolio measures.** (1) **Multi-window compounded crisis
    return**: composite **+7.27% beats BOTH** poles (trend +4.68%, VRP +5.72%) with a far
    better bootstrap LCB (-7.1% vs trend -16.9%). (2) **Full-sample risk-adjusted**:
    composite **UPI +1.05 vs trend +0.20**, **calmar +0.41 vs +0.09** (best of the three),
    **maxdd -10.9% vs trend -20.5% / VRP -13.4%** (best). GUARDRAIL passes decisively and -
    unlike the refuted run - the composite is a *real blend* (40/60), not carry-in-disguise.
  - **Held-out (leave-one-crisis-out, compounded; w\* fit OFF each test window)** - a true OOS
    read: composite beats both poles in **1/2** windows. It **wins the slow 2022 bear**
    (+14.43% vs trend +4.25% / VRP +12.26% - the rebalancing premium stacking on carry's
    contango harvest) but **loses the fast 2020 crash** (+2.52% vs trend +5.48%, where
    short-vol is hit intraday). Median-across-windows OOS composite +8.48% > trend +4.87% /
    VRP +5.26%, but with 2 episodes that median rests on 2022 - the few-windows fragility
    [[measuring-crisis-alpha]] warns of, and the fast-crash miss is exactly the
    [[the-tiered-strategy-roster|target-tier]] (tail) job, not the floor's.
- **Verdict**: **iterate / partial-keep - the failure mode changed, and the change is the
  result.** "Floor pays" in the strict **worst-decile-mean** sense is still refuted, but
  for a *different and now-understood* reason than the first run. Run 1 failed because the
  budget degenerated to all-carry (thin skew); **that defect is fixed** - VRP gives a
  genuine skew-neutral 40/60 balance. Run 2 fails the worst-decile-mean *structurally*: a
  short-gamma pole cannot raise a blend's worst-DAY mean, because it is the loser on those
  days by definition. The right conclusion is that **worst-day tail protection is trend's
  job alone**; the concave pole's contribution is a **diversification / rebalancing
  premium** that shows up as better compounding through whole drawdown episodes (+7.27% vs
  both) and materially lower drawdown (maxdd -10.9% vs -20.5%, UPI 5x trend), at near-zero
  correlation. So the floor *pays as a portfolio* (Sharpe +0.54 at half trend's drawdown)
  even though it does not *pay as worst-day insurance*. **Major caveat gating this verdict**:
  the post-warmup window excludes **Feb-2018 Volmageddon**, the one episode where the
  long-variance/short-variance barbell thesis is most testable (trend's long-vol spike
  exactly when VRP's short-vol crashes). The worst-decile-mean refutation cannot be the
  final word on tail-pairing until the test spans 2018. Next: re-run with the trend warmup
  shortened (or a longer history / VIX-futures proxy back to 2011) so Feb-2018 is in-window,
  and decide whether "Floor pays" should be judged on worst-day mean at all (vs the
  compounded-episode + drawdown measures the floor actually improves).

### floor-pays re-test on the UCITS (EU-retail-tradable) universe (prototype diagnostic)

Harness `/tmp/floor_pays_ucits.py`. Both poles re-mapped for EU-retail tradability: trend =
atalanta champion mechanism on **18 London UCITS ETFs** (`atalanta/trend_ucits.yaml`, e.g.
IDTL/SGLN/CRUD/WCOA.L, USD-denominated, single LSE calendar); concave pole = **credit carry**
(`demeter_eu.credit_carry` on IHYU.L/LQDE.L/IEMB.L = USD HY/IG/EM credit), since UCITS bans
retail short-vol so the VRP pole cannot be re-mapped. Signal = Baa-10yT spread richness
(FRED, inline). Benchmark held at SPY so the crisis definition is constant vs runs 1-2.

- **Hypothesis**: the VRP floor (run 2) survives migration to EU-tradable instruments - a
  UCITS credit-carry concave pole (same crash-risk premium, Koijen et al.) reproduces the
  balanced skew budget and the crisis payoff.
- **Keep/kill criteria**: same pre-registered gate (PRIMARY held-out crisis-conditional beats
  both poles; GUARDRAIL no calm give-back; ROBUSTNESS not one window).
- **Result**: the **sizing budget survives migration but the crisis payoff does not**.
  - **Streams**: UCITS trend Sharpe **+0.18** / qskew **+1.10** (vs US +0.25/+0.77 - the
    London wrappers + dropped EDV/FXA + different commodity-roll vehicles keep the +skew shape,
    even sharpen it, at lower Sharpe); credit carry Sharpe **+0.35** / qskew **-0.49** (matches
    the screening gate, milder than VRP's +0.60). **corr(trend, credit) = -0.17** (vs VRP
    +0.01) - *negatively* correlated, even better day-to-day diversification.
  - **Skew budget still balances**: w* = **0.41**, credit = **30%** of variance - identical to
    VRP's 40/60. Credit's -0.49 skew anchors the budget; the sizing defect stays fixed.
  - **Full-sample risk-adjusted: composite beats BOTH poles** - Sharpe **+0.38** (> credit
    +0.35 > trend +0.18), UPI **+0.46** (> +0.33 / +0.14), Calmar **+0.17** (best). The negative
    correlation buys a real *calm-market* diversification win.
  - **But the crisis floor REGRESSED vs VRP.** On the prescribed **multi-window compounded**
    objective the frontier is now **monotone-decreasing** (no mid-peak): trend-only +4.89% is
    BEST, composite +3.25%, credit-only **-2.70%**. Credit carry **bleeds in the slow 2022
    bear** (-7.0% per-window) where VRP *harvested* (+13.2%), because credit spreads widen
    exactly when equities fall - it shares the trend benchmark's crash factor, it is not
    crisis-orthogonal. maxdd only **-19.8%** (~trend's -20.4%, not VRP's halving to -10.9%);
    bootstrap LCB composite **-20.6%** is *worse* than trend's -17.0%.
  - **Held-out (leave-one-crisis-out, compounded)**: composite beats both in **0/2** windows
    (vs VRP 1/2) - loses 2020 (+4.67 vs trend +6.24) AND 2022 (+1.92 vs trend +3.87). Rolling

- **Verdict**: **iterate (weaker than VRP) - EU-tradability keeps the diversification but
  costs the crisis floor.** The migration preserves what is *mechanical* (the skew budget
  balances at the same 40/60; the composite even has the best full-sample Sharpe/UPI/Calmar,
  helped by the -0.17 correlation) but loses what made the VRP floor a *floor*: credit carry is
  concave (qskew -0.49, bear-beta +0.46) yet **not crisis-orthogonal** - it loads on the same
  crash-risk factor as equities, so it bleeds in the very dislocations (2022 especially) the
  pairing is bought for, instead of harvesting through them the way short-vol contango did. On
  the prescribed crisis objective the composite now loses to trend-only (no mid-peak, 0/2 OOS,
  worse tail LCB, drawdown barely improved). Root cause is structural and not fixable by
  re-mapping: UCITS bans the short-vol pole, and the only UCITS-accessible concave income
  (credit) shares equities' crash factor. So the EU-tradable floor pays as a **calm-market
  Sharpe/drawdown diversifier**, not as **crisis protection** - a genuine downgrade from the
  US VRP floor on the actual "Floor pays" claim. Options: (a) accept the floor as a
  calm-diversifier and lean on trend alone for crisis (the worst day/episode is trend's job
  anyway); (b) seek a UCITS-legal crisis-orthogonal pole (FX carry, gold/duration tilts, or a
  UCITS managed-futures wrapper) rather than credit; (c) pair an offshore VRP sleeve if the
  mandate allows non-UCITS.

### floor-pays UCITS, carryRiskBudget pole allocator (A/B vs run 3 carryStraddle)

Harness `/tmp/floor_pays_ucits_pole.py`. Same UCITS trend book and credit signal as run 3; the
ONLY change is the concave pole's allocator: `demeter.carryRiskBudget` (vol_target 0.10,
carry_gain 2.0, `credit_carry_ucits_pole.yaml`) replaces `carryStraddle`. The allocator fixes
the Baltas-Kosowski wash-out (carryStraddle gross-normalized the common spread to zero) by
routing spread richness to a vol-targeted EXPOSURE (capped gross 1.0, UCITS no-leverage)
instead of cross-sectional weights, leaning INTO rich (widening) spreads via carry_gain.

- **Result**: the allocator deepens the pole's concavity **as designed** - credit pole qskew
  **-0.49 -> -0.75** (clears the <= -0.5 pole gate), at a modest Sharpe cost (+0.35 -> +0.30);
  the skew budget still balances (w* = 0.40, carry **27%** of variance), corr(trend, credit)
  -0.15. **But the FLOOR got slightly WORSE, not better.** Multi-window compounded crisis:
  composite **+3.25% -> +2.25%** (trend still +4.89% best); per-window composite 2020 +7.0 ->
  +4.7, 2022 -0.5 -> -0.2; bootstrap LCB -20.6 -> -21.7. OOS leave-one-crisis-out still **0/2**.
  Full-sample Sharpe/UPI/Calmar all marginally LOWER than run 3 (+0.34/+0.41/+0.15 vs
  +0.38/+0.46/+0.17). The extra concavity comes from leaning INTO rich = widening spreads =
  MORE credit exposure at the onset of stress, which deepens the LEFT tail (2020 carry-only
  flips +1.4% -> -3.5%) - the right direction for the skew GATE, the wrong one for a crisis
  FLOOR.
- **Verdict**: **the allocator is the right fix for the pole's family-MEMBERSHIP (deepens skew,
  fixes the inert-signal bug, keeps the balanced budget) but it does NOT rescue the floor.**
  This sharpens run 3 into a clean principle: the credit pole's problem is not insufficient
  concavity DEPTH (the allocator fixed that, -0.75) but the wrong KIND - **crisis-CORRELATED,
  not crisis-ORTHOGONAL**. No allocator can turn a pole that goes down WITH equities into crisis
  protection: the skew gate is necessary but not sufficient for a floor. The EU floor stays a
  calm-market diversifier; a crisis floor needs a crisis-orthogonal concave pole (FX carry /
  managed-futures wrapper) or an offshore VRP sleeve.

## Day summary

The roster's load-bearing **"Floor pays"** claim is **refuted on the current poles**. With
both champions on the same 20-name Tier 1 book, a skew-neutral vol-matched trend+carry
composite does not lift held-out crisis-conditional return above either pole - it loses to
trend-only, because crisis-conditional return falls **monotonically** with carry weight
while skew-neutrality (given carry's ~0 realized skew) demands almost pure carry. The two
design requirements are in **direct conflict** on this universe. The binding constraint is
not the pairing logic but the concave pole's **weak negative skew**: distribution-yield
carry on a bond/credit/EM/FX book is ~symmetric, so it cannot anchor the skew budget the
convexity mechanism needs. This sharpens the roster's own warning ("the floor's concave
sleeve is the hardest to source, and the whole roster rests on it") into a measured result:
the floor as specified needs a genuinely concave carry pole we have **not yet sourced**.
Graveyard updated; both source-article hypothesis boxes checked with the refutation.

**Update (same day): re-tested with the VRP pole - the sizing defect is fixed and the
failure mode changed.** Sourcing the strongly-concave pole as the **variance risk premium**
(`demeter.vol_carry`, short-vol, gate qskew -1.18) instead of distribution carry makes the
skew-neutral weight a **genuine 40/60 balance** (VRP 30% of variance, net skew -0.001) -
the all-carry degeneration that refuted run 1 is gone, and the structural prior held
(corr(trend, VRP) = +0.01, a true long-variance/short-variance barbell). But "Floor pays"
on the strict **worst-decile-mean** crisis-conditional is *still* refuted, now for a
structural reason: a short-gamma pole loses on the worst days by construction, so it can
only dilute trend's worst-day protection - **tail protection is trend's job alone, no
concave pole can add to it**. What the concave pole *does* add is a diversification /
rebalancing premium: the composite beats BOTH poles on **compounded multi-window crisis
return** (+7.27% vs +4.68% / +5.72%) and slashes drawdown (**maxdd -10.9% vs trend -20.5%**,
UPI +1.05 vs +0.20) at near-zero correlation. So the floor **pays as a portfolio** (better
compounding at half the drawdown) even though it does **not pay as worst-day insurance**.
The verdict is gated by one limitation: the post-warmup window excludes Feb-2018
Volmageddon, the very episode where the barbell thesis is most testable, so the worst-day
refutation is not yet final for tail-pairing.
